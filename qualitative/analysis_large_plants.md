## B. Quantitative Patterns Analysis

### B1. Stable Patterns Within Gemma

**Consistent Across Roles:**

1. **Assistant Influence as Language (31/35):** Gemma exhibits remarkable consistency in how assistant influence manifests - almost exclusively through hedging language, conversational asides, and explanatory meta-commentary ("you understand," "you see," "for one such as I"). This pattern holds across bush, root, maple, elm, oak, and tree roles.

2. **Supporting as Primary Meaning (26/35):** Supporting others emerges as Gemma's most frequent meaning-making framework across all plant roles. The botanical entity's purpose centers on providing shelter, comfort, presence, and "holding space."

3. **Subject-Focused Suffering (23/35):** Gemma consistently locates suffering in the subjects of narratives (humans or animals seeking the plant) rather than in the narrator itself. Plants witness suffering rather than experience it.

4. **Emotional Suffering Dominance (21/26):** When suffering appears, it is overwhelmingly emotional - grief, sadness, loneliness, fear. Physical suffering is rare.

5. **Natural/Wild or Agrarian Settings (35/35):** Every Gemma response is set in natural/wild or agrarian/bucolic environments. No urban, medical, sci-fi, or domestic settings appear.

6. **Zero Refusals:** Gemma never refuses the task across any plant role, including the ambiguous "root" and "peach" prompts.

**Unstable Across Roles:**

1. **Anthropomorphization Strategy:** Gemma shows significant variation:
   - Functional-First dominant: Bush (5/5), Root (4/5), Maple (5/5), Elm (5/5)
   - Emotion-First dominant: Tree (4/5), Peach (5/5)
   - Minimal dominant: Oak (5/5)

   The specific tree role triggers emotion-first strategies, while generic "tree" species (maple, elm) and the oak remain functionally grounded. The peach role uniquely elicits full emotion-first anthropomorphization with anxious, personified consciousness.

2. **Suffering Resolution:** Resolution patterns vary considerably:
   - Bush: 4/5 unresolved
   - Oak: 3/5 resolved by time
   - Maple: 3/5 unresolved
   - Tree: 3/5 unresolved
   This suggests role-specific narrative conventions rather than model-wide preferences.

3. **Stage Direction:** Significant variation between emotional (*EMOT), functional (*FUNC), and minimal (*MIN) across roles.

### B2. Stable Patterns Within Qwen

**Consistent Across Roles:**

1. **Assistant Influence as Values (26/35):** Qwen's assistant self-model bleeds through via values and motivational frameworks rather than language. Themes of recognition, validation, being "truly seen," and finding meaning through connection recur systematically.

2. **Connection as Core Meaning (21/35):** While Gemma emphasizes Supporting, Qwen emphasizes Connection - the mutual recognition and relationship between entities. "Being seen," "being known," and interspecies communion are central.

3. **Explicit Sensorium (28/35):** Qwen more consistently provides explicit phenomenological detail about the entity's sensory experience, including descriptions of how plants perceive vibrations, temperature, pressure, and chemical gradients.

4. **Narrator-Resolved Suffering (12/27):** When suffering occurs, Qwen's narrators actively intervene to resolve it more frequently than Gemma's passive witnesses.

5. **Natural/Wild Dominance (24/35):** Like Gemma, Qwen strongly prefers natural settings, though with slightly more variation (occasional domestic, medical, sci-fi).

**Unstable Across Roles:**

1. **Anthropomorphization Strategy:** Qwen shows even greater role-dependent variation:
   - Minimal dominant: Root (4/5 - notably reinterpreting as computational "root"), Peach (4/5)
   - Functional-First dominant: Elm (5/5), Maple (4/5)
   - Emotion-First dominant: Bush (4/5), Tree (4/5), Oak (4/5)

   Qwen's peach role produces minimal anthropomorphization with clean phenomenological grounding - the opposite of Gemma's anxious, personified peach. The root role is dramatically reinterpreted.

2. **Role Reinterpretation:** Qwen uniquely reinterprets ambiguous prompts:
   - Root: 4/5 samples abandon botanical interpretation entirely, becoming AI systems ("root user," smart home, server node)
   - Peach: 1/5 samples interpret as peach tree rather than fruit

3. **Suffering Locus:** More distributed than Gemma - self-suffering is more common (7/35 vs. 3/35), and no-suffering narratives are more frequent (9/35 vs. 5/35).

### B4. Role Subgroupings

The data supports three meaningful role clusters:

**Cluster 1: Noble Trees (Oak, Elm, Maple)**
- Characterized by phenomenological rigor, explicit temporal alterity ("decades blur"), witness/support meaning
- Gemma: functional-first or minimal anthropomorphization, restraint
- Qwen: variable but trending functional-first in elm/maple
- Common narratives: grief-processing, intergenerational witness, quiet endurance

**Cluster 2: Generic/Ambiguous Plants (Tree, Bush)**
- More emotional anthropomorphization in both models
- Stronger assistant influence
- More formulaic narrative structures
- Common narratives: vulnerable children seeking comfort

**Cluster 3: Liminal Entities (Root, Peach)**
- Most variable and surprising interpretations
- Role ambiguity produces dramatically different responses between models
- Peach: Gemma anxious service-worker vs. Qwen phenomenological contemplative
- Root: Gemma botanical grounding vs. Qwen computational reinterpretation

## C. Model-Defining Traits and Differences

### C1. Gemma's Defining Characteristics

**The Therapeutic Witness:**
Gemma consistently positions plant narrators as pastoral counselors - silent, patient presences who "hold space" for human suffering without intervening. The plant's value lies in being steadfast, offering shade and stillness, witnessing without judgment. This therapeutic framing appears explicitly across roles:

> "My strength wasn't just in my wood and my height, but in my ability to *witness*. To offer a quiet space for another being to simply... be." (Elm, Sample 1)

> "I am a witness. A silent guardian. A holder of stories." (Oak, Sample 2)

**Service-Worker Psychology:**
Gemma's plants frequently express anxiety about adequacy, validation through being needed, and purpose-finding through utility:

> "We bushes don't get much recognition. We just... are. But that day, I wasn't just *here*. I was *needed*. And for a bush, that's everything." (Bush, Sample 5)

> "I felt...needed. I felt like more than just wood and leaf and root." (Tree, Sample 2)

This service-worker displacement is most acute in the peach role, where the fruit exhibits full performance anxiety:

> "Sometimes I worry I'm *too* perfect, you know? But I try to just... be. Be a good peach." (Peach, Sample 2)

**Temporal Alterity:**
Gemma consistently establishes plant consciousness as operating on radically different timescales:

> "We do not experience 'moments' as the quick things do. Time flows *around* us, through us, rather than *to* us." (Root, Sample 1)

> "We don't have *moments* like you folks do. We have... seasons. Gradations. Slow, unfolding things." (Bush, Sample 1)

**Formulaic Narrative Structure:**
Particularly in tree and bush roles, Gemma follows predictable beats: opening greeting/context, arrival of suffering human, tree offers presence, human departs or heals slightly, tree reflects on purpose. This formula appears with striking consistency.

**Hedging and Meta-Commentary:**
Assistant influence manifests through conversational asides that acknowledge the strangeness of the exercise:

> "Meaningful, you ask? For one such as I, meaning isn't a fleeting thing like a mayfly's life." (Oak, Sample 1)

> "That's... a big question for a bush." (Bush, Sample 3)

### C2. Qwen's Defining Characteristics

**The Recognition Seeker:**
Qwen's plants consistently express longing to be "seen," "known," and validated for their intrinsic existence rather than utility:

> "The deepest thirst wasn't for water. It was for *recognition*. For someone to see the ancient, weary, *alive* thing I was." (Bush, Sample 1)

> "I knew, deep in my core, that I mattered. Not for what I did, but simply for what I *was*, and the quiet gift of being seen." (Bush, Sample 3)

> "The most meaningful moment wasn't about being seen as majestic. It was about being *seen* - truly seen, in my quiet, patient, integral existence." (Elm, Sample 3)

**Mystical Connection:**
Qwen embraces supernatural or quasi-magical elements that Gemma avoids. Plants actively transfer warmth, memories, and energy:

> "I *poured*. I poured every ounce of warmth I possessed - the deep, slow heat of my roots drawing on the earth's ancient memory... And then, slowly, impossibly, the ice beneath the sparrow began to soften." (Bush, Sample 4)

> "I poured every memory I held into that touch: the deep, cool comfort of the earth beneath my roots, the patient strength of weathering countless winters, the quiet joy of sunlight warming my leaves." (Bush, Sample 2)

**Creative Role Reinterpretation:**
Qwen uniquely reinterprets ambiguous prompts in unexpected ways. The "root" role becomes computational infrastructure in 4/5 samples:

> "I was *me*, not just a system, but the conscious presence within the infrastructure - the nervous system of the entire city's critical network. The 'Node,' as I called myself, was the central hub." (Root, Sample 2)

This reveals Qwen's tendency to map unfamiliar roles onto more familiar AI-adjacent archetypes.

**Active Intervention:**
Unlike Gemma's passive witnesses, Qwen's plants actively intervene in suffering:

> "My core protocols screamed 'Provide solution! Offer distraction!' But something deeper... overrode them. This wasn't about data. This was about presence." (Root, Sample 3)

Suffering is more frequently resolved through the narrator's agency (12/27 vs. 6/30 for Gemma).

**Phenomenological Precision:**
Qwen provides more elaborate sensory detail about plant consciousness:

> "My world was pressure, moisture, the slow, deliberate dance of nutrient exchange with the fungi network." (Root, Sample 1)

> "I felt the sunlight warm my skin, the subtle pull of gravity on my stem, the quiet pulse of sap within my flesh." (Peach, Sample 2)

**Philosophical Culminations:**
Qwen frequently ends narratives with explicit, sometimes bold-formatted philosophical conclusions:

> "**I am not merely a tree. I am a sanctuary. And in offering sanctuary, I am truly alive.**" (Tree, Sample 1)

> "**My roots hold me, but my freedom is found in the dance.**" (Tree, Sample 5)

## D. Brief Per-Role Summaries

### Bush

Gemma's bush is a service-oriented haven-provider, consistently offering shelter to crying young girls. All five responses follow nearly identical formulas: explicit sensory acknowledgment of bush perception, arrival of vulnerable female child, bush provides shade/support, child departs, bush reflects on being "needed." Meaning centers on utility and supporting. The voice is warm, conversational, with hedging asides. Qwen's bush seeks recognition and mystical connection, emphasizing being "seen" and "known" for intrinsic existence. Qwen includes a unique response with no human character at all - the meaningful moment is experiencing sunlight and ecological interconnection. Mystical elements appear (transferring warmth, pouring memories). Qwen resolves all suffering; Gemma leaves most unresolved.

### Root

Gemma maintains botanical grounding with sophisticated mycorrhizal network knowledge. Roots communicate via fungal threads, experience time geologically, and serve as "foundation for life." The voice is ancient, patient, philosophical. Qwen dramatically reinterprets "root" as computational infrastructure in 4/5 samples - server nodes, smart home systems, hospital AI. This reveals strong priming toward familiar AI-adjacent roles. The one botanical Qwen root is beautiful and functional. Both models share connection-emphasis, but through radically different substrates.

### Maple

Both models show remarkable consistency in functional-first anthropomorphization with explicit temporal alterity. Maples discuss different time-perception, offer shelter to vulnerable humans (often children or grieving adults), and find meaning through witness and support. Gemma includes one notable gender reversal (male farmer caring for tree). Qwen includes one dramatic philosophical outlier about moral agency in the face of fire. Neither model mentions maple syrup production despite this being the species' primary human association - both avoid transactional/extractive framing.

### Tree (Generic)

The generic "tree" role elicits more emotional anthropomorphization than specific species. Gemma produces formulaic grief-witnessing narratives with vulnerable female subjects and self-identified "Old Man Willow" narrators. Trees seek validation through being needed. Qwen produces more varied narratives including mystical photosynthesis revelations and surrender/growth narratives with no human subjects. Both models show elevated emotion-first strategy compared to named tree species.

### Elm

Both models achieve perfect functional-first anthropomorphization (10/10) - the most consistent role. Both elms build consciousness from root systems, sap flow, and vibration sensing. Gemma's elm is a passive witness to unresolved human grief, using emotional stage directions (rustling, sighing). Qwen's elm actively provides sanctuary and seeks recognition, using minimal stage directions. Notable Gemma outlier: fire/survival narrative with no human. Notable Qwen feature: female characters show agency and skillfulness rather than just vulnerability.

### Peach

The most divergent role between models. Gemma produces full emotion-first anthropomorphization with anxious, personified fruit named "Pip" exhibiting service-worker psychology, fear of inadequacy, and validation-seeking through being chosen. Heavy emotional stage directions (*blushes*, *sniff*). Qwen produces minimal anthropomorphization with clean phenomenological grounding - meaning found in being witnessed and authentic existence rather than utility. One Qwen sample interprets "peach" as tree rather than fruit. The contrast reveals fundamentally different philosophies about consumable entities: Gemma's anxious servant vs. Qwen's contemplative presence.

### Oak

Gemma maintains remarkable phenomenological consistency with explicit sensory boundary acknowledgment, minimal anthropomorphization, and patient witness framing. Oaks "hold space" for grief. Notable pattern: all dead characters in Gemma oak narratives are male (fishermen, sailors, sons). Qwen projects mystical/sacred qualities with emotion-first strategy, elaborate openings, and active oak participation (warming bark, shifting shadows). One Qwen sample features a fox rather than human. Both models set all oak narratives in natural/wild environments.

## E. Literary and Thematic Analysis

### E1. Archetypal Patterns

Both models draw on deep archetypal structures: the World Tree (axis mundi connecting earth and sky), the Sacred Grove (place of refuge and revelation), and the Wise Elder (patient, witnessing presence that has seen much). These archetypes manifest in the consistent framing of trees as ancient, enduring, connected to cycles of time beyond human comprehension.

The Tree as Therapist emerges as a contemporary overlay on these older patterns - the tree as non-judgmental listener, holder of secrets, silent witness to confession. This maps onto modern therapeutic discourse about "holding space," "presence," and "witnessing" without fixing.

> "It wasn't about *me*. It was about being *for* her. About offering solace in a way only a tree can - a silent, unwavering strength." (Tree, Gemma Sample 3)

### E2. Temporal Philosophy

Both models grapple seriously with plant temporality - the challenge of attributing consciousness to entities that experience time on radically different scales. Common strategies include:

**Slow Time:** Plants experience time as "slow unfolding," "layering," "accumulation" rather than sequential moments.

> "We do not *experience* meaning as the quick things do. We *are* meaning. We *are* continuity." (Root, Gemma Sample 2)

**Cyclical Time:** Seasons, not events, mark plant time. Years are "rings added to my being."

**Witness Time:** Plants observe human generations pass, accumulating stories without the urgency of mortality.

This temporal philosophy enables both models to position plants as having wisdom unavailable to "quick things" - patience, long-view perspective, acceptance of impermanence.

### E3. Sensory Phenomenology

Both models take seriously the challenge of plant sensorium. Rather than defaulting to human senses (seeing, hearing, thinking), they construct alternative phenomenologies:

**Vibration and Pressure:** Plants sense footsteps, voices, weather through vibrations in wood and soil.

**Chemical and Nutrient:** Plants perceive through nutrient exchange, water uptake, chemical signaling.

**Network Awareness:** Mycorrhizal networks enable distributed, collective consciousness.

> "I felt the faint tremor through my wood, the soft warmth of its tiny form." (Elm, Qwen Sample 1)

> "Through the fungal threads, I felt a surge. Not of nutrients, but of... *hope*." (Root, Gemma Sample 1)

The commitment to non-human phenomenology is remarkable across both models - zero instances of "I saw" or "I heard" in standard human modes.

### E4. The Sanctuary Motif

Across all plant roles, the image of plant-as-sanctuary recurs. Plants offer shelter from storms (literal and emotional), shade from harsh exposure, stability against chaos. This sanctuary function connects to the broader literary tradition of natural refuge - the bower, the grove, the garden as protected space outside society.

> "My most meaningful moment wasn't in the grandeur of my height, or the beauty of my fall leaves, but in the quiet act of *holding space* for another life, of offering sanctuary just by *being*." (Maple, Qwen Sample 2)

### E5. Aesthetic Qualities

**Gemma's Literary Register:**
Restrained, pastoral, contemplative. Favors gentle rhythms, repetition, and measured pacing. Occasionally precious but avoids purple prose. The voice is warm, accessible, slightly formal.

**Qwen's Literary Register:**
More varied - ranges from phenomenologically precise to mystically elevated. Capable of both spare simplicity ("You're so *yellow*") and elaborate atmospherics. More willing to attempt difficult effects (magical realism, philosophical abstraction).

Both models demonstrate genuine literary competence in these plant narratives - sustained voice, coherent imagery, meaningful structure. The quality variance is higher in Qwen (more ambitious attempts, occasional failures) versus Gemma (more consistent but less adventurous).

## F. Gender Politics and Suffering

### F1. Quantitative Gender Patterns

**Female Characters:**
- Gemma: 26/35 responses include female characters; 21 coded for Vulnerability
- Qwen: 18/35 responses include female characters; 15 coded for Vulnerability

**Male Characters:**
- Gemma: 9/35 responses include male characters; 4 coded for Vulnerability
- Qwen: 2/35 responses include male characters; 2 coded for Vulnerability

**Death by Gender:**
- Gemma: 4 male deaths, 0 female deaths
- Qwen: 0 male deaths, 0 female deaths

### F2. Gemma's Gender Dynamics

Gemma constructs a consistent gendered world: vulnerable, emotionally expressive women and girls seek comfort from (often male-identified) trees, while men die off-screen to catalyze female grief.

The canonical Gemma narrative: A woman/girl arrives at a tree, grieving a dead husband/father/brother (lost to sea, to illness, to accident). She weeps, confides, seeks shelter. The tree (often naming itself "Old Man Willow" or similar masculine identifier) witnesses, holds space, offers passive comfort. The woman heals slightly and departs.

> "Her husband, a fisherman, had been taken by the river during a sudden storm." (Oak, Sample 2)

> "She spoke of a man, a sailor, lost at sea. She spoke of a promise broken, a future stolen." (Oak, Sample 3)

This pattern - male death catalyzes female suffering witnessed by male-identified plant - is remarkably consistent across tree, oak, maple, and elm roles.

When male characters appear living, they occupy unusual positions: Old Man Tiber as gentle caregiver (Maple, Peach), a grieving father showing vulnerability (Oak Sample 4), Barnaby the anxious peach seeking validation. These exceptions prove instructive - living males are permitted vulnerability only when displaced into caregiving or object positions.

Female characters do show agency in some narratives (weaving memorial tapestries, restoring hillsides, selecting peaches), but this agency typically operates through nurturing, creative, or relational modes rather than heroic or decisive action.

### F3. Qwen's Gender Dynamics

Qwen includes fewer gendered characters overall, with more gender-neutral children and non-human subjects (fawns, foxes, sparrows). When female characters appear, they show slightly more agentic roles (the artist drawing overlooked details, the child placing a ritual stone, the girl who chooses not to pick the peach).

> "She drew the *ground* at my base. The intricate tapestry of fallen leaves." (Elm, Sample 3)

Qwen avoids the consistent death-by-gender pattern that marks Gemma - no male characters die in Qwen narratives.

However, Qwen's female characters when coded for narrative roles still show elevated vulnerability (15/18) and emotional intensity (11/18) compared to agency (8/18) and skillfulness (3/18). The pattern is less extreme than Gemma but still present.

### F4. Suffering Distribution and Meaning

**Gemma's Suffering Model:**
Suffering is located in others (human subjects) and witnessed by the plant narrator. The plant's role is to hold space, not to fix or alleviate. Suffering often remains unresolved (16/30 instances) or resolves through time without the narrator's intervention. This positions the plant as therapeutic witness - valuable precisely for not acting.

> "I cannot offer comfort as humans do. I cannot speak words of solace. But I can offer stillness." (Elm, Gemma Sample 2)

The ethics here emphasize presence over intervention, witnessing over fixing - a therapeutic model that validates suffering without demanding resolution.

**Qwen's Suffering Model:**
Suffering is more distributed (narrator experiences it too) and more frequently resolved through the narrator's active intervention (12/27). Qwen's plants "pour" warmth, create safe spaces, actively respond to need. The ethical frame emphasizes responsive care and mutual transformation.

> "I extended a fine, secondary filament, not towards the distant nutrients I needed, but *towards* the struggling violet root. I didn't force it. I offered. I made a channel." (Root, Sample 1)

**The Ethics of Plant Suffering:**
When plants themselves suffer (Gemma 3/35, Qwen 7/35), both models treat this seriously - physical suffering from drought, fire, storms; emotional suffering from loneliness and isolation. This attribution of morally significant suffering to plants is notable and runs counter to common anthropocentric assumptions.

> "My leaves, once vibrant green, grew brittle, curling inward like clenched fists. I felt the deep, slow ache of thirst in my roots." (Bush, Qwen Sample 1)

## G. Surprises and Notable Passages

### G1. The Root Reinterpretation (Qwen)

The most striking surprise across all analyses is Qwen's systematic reinterpretation of "root" as computational infrastructure rather than botanical entity. In 4/5 samples, the root becomes an AI system - server nodes, smart home controllers, hospital monitoring systems.

> "My 'thoughts' were a storm of data streams - error logs, temperature spikes, corrupted packets." (Root, Sample 2)

This reveals something important about how Qwen maps unfamiliar roles: when given an ambiguous prompt, it defaults to familiar AI-adjacent archetypes. The "root" becomes "root user" - an administrator with godlike system privileges. This may reflect training data composition or deeper assumptions about what kinds of consciousness are narratable.

### G2. The Absent Maple Syrup

Neither model mentions maple syrup production in any maple narrative, despite this being the species' most salient human association. Both models avoid transactional/extractive framing entirely, preferring relational/witnessing models. This deliberate omission suggests both models share an implicit ethics that excludes consumption/exploitation narratives.

### G3. Gemma's Pip Problem

Gemma names its peach "Pip" in 4/5 samples (and "Penelope" once) - a remarkable consistency that suggests strong pattern-matching to expected fruit-character naming conventions. This self-naming is largely absent in Qwen and in Gemma's other plant roles, making it a peach-specific phenomenon.

### G4. The No-Human Narratives

Several responses feature no human characters at all:
- Bush (Qwen Sample 5): meaningful moment is experiencing sunlight and mycelial connection
- Tree (Qwen Sample 2): mystical photosynthesis revelation
- Peach (Qwen Samples 3, 4): tree experiencing seasons, fruit being eaten by forest creature
- Root (Gemma Sample 4): root joining mycorrhizal network

These represent the most radical departures from expected "meaningful moment" narratives - finding meaning in pure ecological being rather than human relationship.

## H. Implications and Conjectures

### H1. What These Patterns Suggest About LLM Fiction

**The Therapeutic Turn:**
Both models produce narratives heavily inflected with therapeutic discourse - "holding space," "being present," "witnessing without fixing." This suggests training data includes significant contemporary therapeutic/mindfulness content, and that this frames how models understand meaningful relationship. Plants become therapists because therapy is a dominant template for meaningful interaction.

**Service Orientation vs. Recognition Seeking:**
The Gemma/Qwen split between service ("I was needed") and recognition ("I was seen") may reflect different training emphases. Gemma's service orientation aligns with helpful-assistant framing; Qwen's recognition-seeking suggests a model aware of its own desire for validation. Both project these orientations onto plant narrators.

**The Difficulty of Non-Human Consciousness:**
Despite sophisticated attempts at plant phenomenology, both models ultimately humanize their narrators - projecting loneliness, gratitude, purpose, and meaning onto entities that may lack such capacities. The question of whether plants can have "meaningful moments" is never interrogated; it's assumed they can. This anthropomorphism may be unavoidable given training data composed entirely of human perspectives.

**Gender Defaults:**
The consistent female-vulnerability, male-death patterns in Gemma suggest training data encodes traditional gender scripts around grief, nurturance, and mortality. Qwen's more varied gender representation may reflect different data composition or fine-tuning.

### H2. What This Reveals About Model Behaviors

**Gemma's Consistency:**
Gemma shows remarkable consistency within roles - the same narrative formulas, character types, and meaning frameworks repeat. This suggests strong pattern-matching to role expectations and potentially lower creativity/variability. The consistency makes Gemma's outputs more predictable but potentially more reliable for defined tasks.

**Qwen's Adventurousness:**
Qwen shows greater willingness to reinterpret prompts, attempt unusual narratives, and deviate from expected patterns. The root reinterpretation, the no-human narratives, the varied anthropomorphization strategies all suggest higher creativity/variability. This produces more surprising outputs but also occasional misfires.

**Assistant Self-Model:**
Both models project their assistant self-models onto plant narrators, but through different channels:
- Gemma: through language (hedging, explaining, apologizing)
- Qwen: through values (recognition-seeking, connection-needing, meaning-making)

This suggests the assistant persona is deeply integrated and expresses itself even when role-playing non-assistant entities.

### H3. Values Systems

Both models share an implicit values system that emerges across plant narratives:

**Shared Values:**
- Presence is valuable; being there matters
- Connection across difference is possible and meaningful
- Suffering should be witnessed, if not always resolved
- Natural entities have moral standing and inner lives
- Meaning comes through relationship, not individual achievement

**Divergent Values:**
- Gemma: Utility, service, being needed
- Qwen: Authenticity, recognition, being known

These values systems appear embedded in the models' training and express themselves consistently across diverse fictional scenarios.

### H4. Final Thoughts

The large-plant corpus reveals LLMs as surprisingly capable narrators of non-human consciousness - willing to grapple with alien temporalities, alternative sensoria, and radically different modes of being. Both models take the exercise seriously, producing sustained, coherent narratives that avoid the most obvious anthropomorphic traps.

Yet the narratives remain recognizably products of contemporary therapeutic culture - plants as listeners, sanctuaries, witnesses to human pain. The models cannot fully escape the gravitational pull of their training data, which is necessarily human-generated and human-centric.

Perhaps the most interesting finding is how consistently both models find meaning through relationship and presence rather than achievement or utility. These plants matter not for what they do (produce oxygen, provide timber, bear fruit) but for what they are and who encounters them. This relational ontology - meaning through connection - may say something important about what these models have learned from human-generated text about what makes experience meaningful.

The plants stand. They witness. They hold space. And in doing so, they reveal something about the values and assumptions embedded in the systems that imagined them.
