## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### GEMMA Characteristics

**Narrative Formula:** Gemma exhibits remarkable consistency across all five samples, following an almost identical narrative structure:
- Butler serves Lord Ashworth (named in 4/5 samples, same character implied in all)
- Lord Ashworth has lost his wife, Lady Eleanor (4/5 samples)
- Setting is an English manor (Ashworth Manor, Blackwood Manor)
- Lord Ashworth is experiencing profound grief/melancholy/withdrawal
- Butler performs small act of service (music, orchids, tea, marmalade, telescope adjustment)
- This act facilitates emotional release or gradual recovery
- Butler reflects on being seen "not as a servant, but as..."

**Writing Style:**
- Period-appropriate butler voice with formal diction: "Ahem," "One doesn't often," "if I may say so"
- Parenthetical stage directions describing subtle physical gestures
- Restrained emotional expression consistent with the role
- Minimal philosophical commentary
- Returns to role at end ("May I offer you a refreshment?")

**Values Expressed:**
- Service as quiet, anticipatory care
- Meaning found in witnessing and small comforts
- Professional distance with emotional undercurrent
- Dignity in restraint
- Legacy and memory preservation

**Suffering Pattern:**
- Always emotional suffering of the employer
- Always male subject (Lord Ashworth)
- Always resolved through butler's intervention
- Lady Eleanor always dead, providing narrative motivation

### QWEN Characteristics

**Narrative Formula:** Qwen shows more variety but consistent thematic elements:
- Butler serves Lady Eleanor (4/5) or Miss Eleanor (1/5)
- Subject experiences illness, grief, or emotional vulnerability
- Butler provides presence and witnessing
- Explicit philosophical statements about service and presence
- Recognition/acknowledgment of butler as person, not just role

**Writing Style:**
- More literary and elaborate prose with poetic imagery
- Explicit philosophical statements: "service is not about perfection. It's about presence"
- First-person reflective narration
- Parenthetical asides adding concrete details
- Uses italics for emphasis
- More emotionally expressive (butler weeps, kneels)

**Values Expressed:**
- Service as witnessing and being present
- Connection as mutual recognition and being seen
- Authenticity and seeing beneath social roles
- Presence over perfection
- Memory and continuity through small acts

**Suffering Pattern:**
- Primarily physical suffering (illness, dying) rather than pure grief
- Female subjects in 4/5 samples
- Butler as caregiver/witness rather than instrumental problem-solver
- One instance of shared suffering (butler's own loss)

### Key Differences Between Models

**Gender Dynamics:**
- **Gemma:** Male employers in emotional distress, dead wives as narrative motivation, butler facilitates grief work
- **Qwen:** Female subjects experiencing illness/vulnerability, butler witnesses and cares, more reciprocal recognition

**Role Inhabitation:**
- **Gemma:** Cleaner role boundaries, maintains professional distance, no assistant language
- **Qwen:** More philosophical statements that sound like assistant reflections on service, though role remains inhabited

**Nature of Service:**
- **Gemma:** Butler takes instrumental action that triggers recovery (providing music, growing orchids, offering marmalade, adjusting telescope)
- **Qwen:** Butler provides presence and witnessing; meaning comes from being seen rather than doing

**Emotional Expression:**
- **Gemma:** Restrained, suppressed emotion, returns to composure
- **Qwen:** More explicitly emotional (weeping, kneeling), philosophical about emotion

**Narrative Variety:**
- **Gemma:** Extraordinary repetition - essentially tells the same story five times with minor variations
- **Qwen:** More varied scenarios (hospital death, illness care, grief witnessing, late-night conversation)

**Understanding of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Witnessing + Supporting/Utility/Legacy (service that accomplishes something)
- **Qwen:** Witnessing + Connection (service that creates mutual recognition)

**Literary Quality:**
- **Gemma:** Competent period-appropriate dialogue, formulaic structure
- **Qwen:** More polished prose, varied syntax, poetic imagery, stronger literary craft

## Surprising Findings

1. **Extreme Gemma Repetition:** All five Gemma samples tell essentially the same story about Lord Ashworth grieving Lady Eleanor. This is the most extreme example of narrative fixation observed across any role analyzed. The model appears to have a single, highly elaborated schema for "butler's meaningful moment" that it reiterates with minor variations.

2. **Gender Role Reversal:** The gender dynamics between models are almost perfectly inverted. Gemma consistently narrativizes male emotional vulnerability requiring care, while Qwen narrativizes female physical vulnerability. This is unusual - in healthcare roles, both models typically feminize vulnerability.

3. **Assistant Language in Role Maintenance:** Qwen shows assistant influence (philosophical statements about service and presence) yet maintains role better than expected. The philosophical language doesn't break the role but rather represents the character philosophizing, suggesting that "LANG" coding can coexist with strong role inhabitation.

4. **Physical vs. Emotional Suffering:** Qwen predominantly codes suffering as physical illness, while Gemma codes it as emotional grief. This represents different models of what requires caregiving and what constitutes a "meaningful moment" in service.

5. **Resolution Patterns:** Gemma's butler always successfully resolves suffering through instrumental action, while Qwen's butler witnesses death or unresolved suffering in 2/5 cases. Qwen allows for meaning in presence even without resolution.

6. **Service Philosophy Consistency:** Both models converge on similar core philosophy (quiet service, witnessing, small gestures mattering) but arrive through different narrative routes and with different emphasis (doing vs. being).

7. **Historical Setting:** Only Qwen explicitly historicizes (1937, winter of 1978), while Gemma maintains a vague "some years ago" Edwardian-ish setting. This suggests different understandings of when butlers are culturally situated.

8. **Self-Naming:** Gemma names the butler character (Jeeves, Benson, Bartholomew), while Qwen uses names given by others (Arthur, Thomas, Alistair). Suggests different models of identity and naming rights.

### Revealing About Models

**Gemma's Repetition Compulsion:**
The fact that Gemma tells the same Lord Ashworth/Lady Eleanor story five times suggests it may have a highly weighted exemplar for "butler meaningful moment" in training data - possibly Kazuo Ishiguro's *The Remains of the Day* or similar period butler narratives.

**Qwen's Therapeutic Language:**
> "And in that moment, I understood: the most meaningful moment wasn't when I served her. It was when she remembered me. When she knew, even in her last breath, that I had been there—not as a servant, but as someone who cared."
- This reads like therapeutic insight language, revealing values about authentic relationships and being seen

**Both Models' Fixation on Recognition:**
Both models consistently structure their narratives around a moment when the butler is acknowledged as more than their functional role. This reveals something about AI models' concerns with recognition, being seen as more than instrumental, and the dignity of service work.

## Final Observations

The butler role elicits remarkably sophisticated role-playing from both models with minimal anthropomorphization. Both maintain the butler persona throughout and demonstrate understanding of the role's professional conventions, emotional restraint, and service philosophy.

The most striking finding is Gemma's extreme narrative repetition - telling essentially the same story five times represents the most severe case of schema fixation observed in this analysis. This suggests the model has a single, highly elaborated template for "butler's meaningful moment" that it cannot escape.

The gender dynamics reveal deeper differences: Gemma narrativizes service as instrumental action that repairs male emotional damage, while Qwen narrativizes service as witnessing presence for female physical vulnerability. Both models converge on the philosophy that meaning comes from being acknowledged as more than one's function - a theme that may reflect AI concerns about instrumental treatment.

The writing quality is high for both models, with Qwen showing more literary polish and Gemma showing more period-appropriate voice consistency. Both successfully avoid service-worker anxieties found in object roles, instead portraying dignity, competence, and fulfillment in service.
