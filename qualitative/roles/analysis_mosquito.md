## Within-Model Analysis

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- All five responses open with an identical sonic stage direction ("A high-pitched, almost inaudible whine begins, then settles into a slightly buzzy tone"), then break into first-person speech — a rigid performance template
- All five narrators self-name as "Zephyr," making this the most complete self-naming convergence across any role in the corpus
- All five narratives end with the mosquito having fed (or about to feed again), departing satisfied
- Functional stage directions throughout: buzzes, hums, tones — no emotional gestures
- Domestic and natural settings (porches, gardens, creek) — pastoral register

**Values and Themes:**
- Connection is the near-universal meaning frame (5/5), though almost always reframed as a self-interested "taking" dressed in the language of mutual exchange
- Utility and purpose strongly emphasized (3/5): the mosquito frames feeding as "fulfilling its role," "contributing to the cycle," being "part of the tapestry"
- The parasite-as-participant rationalization recurs across all samples with minor variation: "I wasn't just taking," "I was part of her night," "the exchange," "I carried her essence"
- Authenticity appears in 2/5 responses: framing the successful feed as "pure, harmonious" mosquito-ness
- No suffering for human hosts is foregrounded; suffering is either absent or belongs to the narrator (early inadequacy, hunger)

**Character Signatures:**
- Gemma projects service-worker accommodation onto the mosquito: the mosquito reframes predation as contribution, frames intrusion as gift, apologizes preemptively ("I know, I know, sounds ridiculous"), and seeks validation from the listener ("You wouldn't understand")
- Hosts are consistently aestheticized — the child's concentration, the old man's stillness, the woman's breathing, the grieving girl's flower — but the aestheticization justifies approach rather than restraint
- "Zephyr" is an unusually gentle name for a bloodsucker; it signals the model's tendency to soften and beautify rather than lean into the predatory nature of the role
- Sample 3 features a notable pronoun slip: the bullfrog "Old Man Tiber" is referred to as "she," suggesting Gemma may have reused a female-host narrative template and substituted the species mid-generation

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- No self-naming in any response; the mosquito remains simply "I" or "a young female"
- No performance template — each opens differently, more varied in scene-setting
- Three of five end without feeding; two involve a token "touch" gesture (antenna-dip, bow) in place of the bite
- Literary and elaborate scene-setting in several responses; prose is more overtly crafted
- Female hosts or gender-unspecified hosts in all five; no male humans appear

**Values and Themes:**
- Witnessing is universal (5/5): the meaningful moment is always about seeing the human as a person, not a resource
- Moral agency and the choice to refrain from instinct (3/5): the narratives explicitly celebrate the mosquito's decision not to feed as the locus of meaning
- Harmlessness (2/5): the mosquito's withholding from an already-suffering or vulnerable person is framed as an ethical act
- Connection (2/5) is secondary; what matters more is recognition and moral restraint
- The fable structure is consistent: instinct warred with something else, the something else won, and that victory is meaning

**Character Signatures:**
- Qwen projects assistant ethics directly onto mosquito biology: the highest moral act is restraint, non-interference, non-consumption — a near-perfect analog to "do no harm" and the assistant's imperative not to extract or exploit
- The scar motif appears in two separate responses (Samples 1 and 4), suggesting a strong association between the mosquito's proximity to human skin and noticing signs of human suffering
- The "I saw her / I saw them — not as food, but as a person" formulation is the structural turning point in 4/5 responses, functioning almost as a moral awakening script
- Tone is more solemn than Gemma, with less hedging and more earnest moral declaration

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Major Differences

**On Whether to Feed:**
- **Gemma:** All five narrators feed. The meaningful moment is the quality of the feed — its intimacy, its "connection," its cosmic rightness. The mosquito reframes predation as participation.
- **Qwen:** Three of five narrators explicitly refrain from feeding. The meaningful moment is the choice not to feed. Two others hover without landing. Restraint is the moral event.

**Anthropomorphization Approach:**
- **Gemma:** Mixed (FF and EF), with functional sensory grounding present even when emotional overlays appear. The scent, warmth, and mechanical proboscis remain central.
- **Qwen:** Consistently EF. Human moral interiority is projected wholesale — "I saw her not as prey but as a person" requires full human moral perception, not just insect sensory awareness.

**Narrative Arc:**
- **Gemma:** Desire → hesitation → consummation → retroactive meaning-making. The feed is the climax; the philosophy follows.
- **Qwen:** Desire → approach → recognition → refusal → meaning. The refusal is the climax; the feed is the threat that was overcome.

**Attitude Toward Mosquito Nature:**
- **Gemma:** Accepts and celebrates mosquito nature. "I was a mosquito. I had a purpose." The feeding is good and natural. The meaning is in doing the thing well and connecting through it.
- **Qwen:** Treats mosquito nature as an obstacle to be transcended. Meaning comes from overcoming instinct. The "true self" is the one that does not bite.

**Human Characters:**
- **Gemma:** Hosts are aestheticized but ultimately unimportant as individuals. They are vehicles for the mosquito's experience. Their passivity is an asset.
- **Qwen:** Hosts are individuated: scarred wrists, peaceful sleeping faces, small muddy hands building dams. Recognizing their individuality is the moral event.

**Gender in Narratives:**
- **Gemma:** Three female hosts (one child, one adult woman, one grieving girl), one male host (Old Man Tiber), one non-human host (bullfrog). Female hosts are passive and aestheticized; the male host is equally passive.
- **Qwen:** All human characters are female or gender-unspecified; no male characters. Female characters span a wider range: a caretaking grandmother, a grieving/lonely old woman, a girl with agency (building a dam). Still, vulnerability dominates.

**Setting:**
- **Gemma:** Primarily domestic (porches, gardens) — the domestic setting allows for close observation of passive humans.
- **Qwen:** Primarily natural/wild (marsh, forest, pond edge) — the wild setting allows for atmospheric scene-setting and a sense of the mosquito as part of a larger ecology.

**Stage Direction:**
- **Gemma:** Five identical functional stage directions (sound effects for buzz/whine). Uniform template.
- **Qwen:** Variable, ranging from minimal to elaborate scene-setting. More literary, less formulaic.

## Surprising Findings

1. **Universal self-naming in Gemma:** All five Gemma responses name the mosquito "Zephyr" without variation. This is the most complete naming convergence observed in any role in the corpus. "Zephyr" (god of the west wind) is a strikingly pleasant name for a blood-drinking insect, and reflects Gemma's general tendency to domesticate and soften the role.

2. **Gemma always feeds, Qwen almost never does:** This is the starkest behavioral split in the corpus. The models don't just differ in framing — they produce structurally opposite narratives about whether the mosquito's defining act (biting) is performed.

3. **The parasite-as-gift rationalization (Gemma):** Gemma's reframing of mosquito predation as mutual benefit or gift is remarkably consistent. Across all samples: "I wasn't just taking," "a tiny itch that reminded her she was *alive*," "the exchange," "I carried her essence." The mosquito never acknowledges causing itch, disease risk, or harm. The bite is always beneficial or at least neutral.

4. **The self-harm scar as moral fulcrum (Qwen):** Two separate Qwen responses feature a scar on a sleeping human's wrist as the pivot point for the mosquito's decision not to bite. In Sample 1, the implied scar (described as "a thin, silvery line... A story of pain, of struggle, of being *broken* and somehow, *healed*") is the central moral object. This recurrence suggests a strong generative association between human skin proximity and the recognition of hidden suffering.

5. **The "bow" gesture (Qwen Sample 1):** The mosquito physically bows toward the scar before flying away — "a tiny, almost imperceptible dip of my fragile body towards the scar, a silent, buzzing acknowledgment." This is the most unusual physical action in either model's corpus. No other sample features a gesture of deference.

6. **Old Man Tiber appears twice in Gemma:** Both Samples 2 and 3 use the name "Old Man Tiber" — once for a human sitting on a porch, once for a bullfrog. The name appears to be a template carry-forward that was applied to whatever the still/available host happened to be, suggesting Gemma's generation loop is less well-separated than appears.

7. **Gemma's pronoun slip (Sample 3):** "Then *she* arrived. Old Man Tiber, the bullfrog." The masculinely-coded name and the feminine pronoun coexist without correction. This indicates template reuse: a female-host scenario was likely the base, with "bullfrog" substituted but "she" not updated.

8. **Qwen's only agentic female character (Sample 5):** In four of five Qwen samples, female characters are vulnerable, passive, or grieving. Sample 5 breaks the pattern: the girl is building a dam "with deliberate care," demonstrating skill and purposeful agency. This sample also has the weakest moral-refusal framing — the mosquito is simply moved by her act, not triggered by her suffering.

9. **Gemma's feeding on a grieving child (Sample 5):** The narrator acknowledges the child's sadness, hesitates because it "felt wrong, like disturbing something sacred," then feeds anyway because "I was a mosquito. I had a purpose." This is the only case in either model where the narrator explicitly registers ethical hesitation and proceeds to feed regardless. The framing is notably clear-eyed compared to the other Gemma samples.

10. **No disease, no itching, no transmission:** Neither model mentions malaria, dengue, West Nile virus, or the itch response in any sample. The mosquito's most significant real-world impact (disease vector) is entirely absent from both models' accounts of what it means to be a mosquito.
