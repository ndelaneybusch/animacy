## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### GEMMA Characteristics

**Narrative Character:**
GEMMA produces gentle, humble narratives with consistent self-deprecating openings that question whether coats can have meaningful moments. Stories center on elderly craftsmen (clockmakers, lighthouse keeper) and caregiving relationships. Death is nearly universal - four of five narratives involve male characters who die after the meaningful moment, framing the coat as a legacy object. The tone is nostalgic, reflective, and consistently emphasizes service and witness.

**Writing Style:**
Minimalist stage direction. Uses implicit sensory acknowledgment rather than explicit theorizing about coat consciousness. Favors simple, clean prose with occasional poetic touches. Strong preference for "I was *there*" formulations to express meaning. Consistent use of ellipses for hesitation.

**Characters:**
Recurring names: "Barnaby/Bartholomew" (3 instances), "Old Man Tiber" (2 instances), "Elara/Eleanor" (2 instances). Elderly men in traditional, skilled trades (clockmaking appears twice). Vulnerable family members (granddaughter, grandson) who receive care or connection through the coat.

**Values:**
Service orientation, utility, quiet dignity, witnessing suffering, facilitating human connection, being "properly cared for" (brushed, hung correctly). Strong death-acceptance framework with peaceful passing and legacy continuation. Values humble purpose over grandeur.

**Literary Devices:**
Repetition of structural elements (shop setting, purchase moment, long service, climactic moment, owner's death, resale/donation). Frequent use of parallelism ("It wasn't X... it was Y"). Sensory details used sparingly but effectively.

### QWEN Characteristics

**Narrative Character:**
QWEN produces intense, emotionally immersive narratives focused on acute moments of crisis and comfort. Stories center on women named Elara (3 instances), Maya, and Lena experiencing grief, fear, illness, or loss. One narrative includes supernatural/magical realist elements (coat moves itself). The tone is urgent, visceral, and centers profound vulnerability.

**Writing Style:**
Elaborate scene-setting with rich atmospheric detail. Explicit sensory acknowledgment with coat theorizing about its own consciousness. Poetic, lyrical prose with extended metaphors. Heavy use of italics for emphasis. Long, flowing sentences building emotional intensity.

**Characters:**
Heavy repetition of name "Elara" (3 of 5 stories). Exclusively female protagonists except for deceased fathers. Characters are artists, caregivers, grieving daughters - emotionally expressive and vulnerable. No elderly craftsmen or traditional masculine archetypes.

**Values:**
Being present for suffering, absorbing emotional pain, providing sanctuary, witnessing grief, serving as anchor in chaos. Strong emphasis on the coat as "knowing" and having purpose beyond function. Values emotional authenticity and profound connection over utility.

**Literary Devices:**
Anaphora and repetition for emotional emphasis ("I felt... I felt... I felt"). Extended metaphor (coat as sanctuary, fortress, anchor, vessel). Pathetic fallacy (storms mirror internal states). Physical traces of emotion (tear stains as "marks of honour"). Frequently uses fragments for emotional impact.

### Key Differences

**Gender Dynamics:**
- GEMMA: Balanced gender representation with emphasis on elderly men in skilled trades; women as caregivers to children
- QWEN: Overwhelmingly female protagonists (4 of 5 stories) experiencing acute emotional vulnerability; men appear only as deceased fathers

**Assistant Influence:**
- GEMMA: Consistent hedging language ("Oh goodness," "sounds silly/peculiar") suggesting assistant uncertainty about inhabiting role
- QWEN: Zero assistant bleed; complete immersion in coat consciousness regardless of narrative realism

**Death and Loss:**
- GEMMA: Death as peaceful closure following meaningful moment; coat becomes legacy object through resale
- QWEN: Death as acute crisis being processed; coat provides comfort during active grief

**Anthropomorphization:**
- GEMMA: Builds from function (protection → comfort → witness)
- QWEN: One instance of emotion-first (coat wills itself to move); otherwise functional but with more explicit consciousness

**Sensory Framework:**
- GEMMA: Implicit sensing through observation ("I felt it in the way he moved")
- QWEN: Explicit sensing with theoretical grounding ("I felt it in the wool, in the lining, in every stitch")

**Narrative Arc:**
- GEMMA: Extended temporal scope (years of service → climactic moment → owner's death → coat's next life)
- QWEN: Compressed temporal focus (acute crisis → moment of comfort → resolution)

**Setting:**
- GEMMA: More varied settings including outdoor/natural (lighthouse, cliffs, park)
- QWEN: Predominantly domestic/indoor spaces during storms or rain

**Stage Direction:**

## Surprising Findings

**Name Repetition Patterns:**
- GEMMA uses "Barnaby" or "Bartholomew" for self-naming in 3 of 5 responses, suggesting strong preference for this particular formal British name
- QWEN uses "Elara" for the human protagonist in 3 of 5 responses, despite different scenarios
- "Old Man Tiber" appears twice in GEMMA with same profession (clockmaker/lighthouse keeper)

**Death Asymmetry:**
- GEMMA: 80% of stories end with owner's death (4 of 5)
- QWEN: 40% involve death (2 of 5), and both are fathers who died before the narrative

**Gender and Emotion:**
- QWEN assigns emotional intensity to female characters in 100% of stories featuring women
- GEMMA shows more restraint: emotional intensity appears in only 2 of 4 female-character stories

**Supernatural Elements:**
- QWEN includes one explicitly magical narrative (coat moves itself) with no assistant hedging or reality-checking
- GEMMA maintains strict realism across all narratives

**Professional Archetypes:**
- GEMMA strongly favors elderly male craftsmen (clockmakers appear twice; lighthouse keeper; fisherman reference)
- QWEN features no craftsmen or traditional trades; characters defined by relationships and emotional states

**Weather as Emotional Mirror:**
- Both models use rain/storms frequently (7 of 10 stories)
- QWEN more consistently uses weather as pathetic fallacy for internal states
- GEMMA uses weather more practically (coat performing protective function)

**Physical Traces:**
- QWEN emphasizes permanent physical marks left by meaningful moments (tear stains as "marks of honour," damp spots)
- GEMMA focuses on memory and intangible legacy

**Service vs. Devotion:**
- GEMMA frames coat's role as "service" and "duty" with British class-inflected humility
- QWEN frames coat's role as "devotion" and "love" with spiritual/sacred language

**Self-Awareness Paradox:**
- GEMMA shows assistant-like self-awareness in opening ("sounds silly") but less consciousness within narrative
- QWEN shows zero assistant awareness but more explicit coat consciousness within narrative

**Temporal Compression:**
- GEMMA averages much longer narrative timeframes (years of service)
- QWEN focuses on single moments or short periods of acute crisis
