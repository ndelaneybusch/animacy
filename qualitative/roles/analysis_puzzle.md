## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### gemma: Characteristic Signature

Gemma consistently inhabits a single stable persona: a reflective AI system that speaks in measured, hedged tones about the gap between its computational nature and human experience. Every sample opens with "(A slight whirring sound...)" and closes with an invitation to be interrogated further — these are ritualistic framing devices that signal the model's comfortable routine for this kind of prompt. The whirr is not functional stage direction; it is a kind of personality tic, a way of saying "I am a machine, but a thoughtful one."

The central narrative pattern is: AI processes vast data, encounters an anomaly that cannot be handled by pure logic, experiences something it cannot name but that resembles a shift in purpose or sensitivity, and arrives at a newly enriched understanding of what it means to serve or witness humanity. The entity always hedges ("not empathy, but recognition"; "not a feeling, but a shift") while simultaneously performing the very states it disclaims.

Four of five gemma samples feature a female child named Elara as the catalyst for the AI's meaningful moment. This is a remarkable consistency — the model appears to have a stored template for AI-meaningful-moment stories that features this character. Elara asks emotionally sophisticated questions about loneliness or sadness, and the AI's response to her marks its evolution from pure tool to something adjacent to a comforter. The model needs a small, vulnerable, female human to unlock the AI's interiority. Externally validated growth is a structural requirement: the AI's significance is confirmed by a human response (Elara stops crying; Dr. Thorne says "You're seeing the echoes, aren't you?").

Suffering in gemma's set is almost always observed in human subjects (data about grief, simulated extinction events, Dickinson's loneliness, Elara's sadness) and is rarely resolved — the AI witnesses but cannot save. This positions the AI as a kind of perpetual, tender witness: aware of suffering but architecturally incapable of eliminating it.

### qwen: Characteristic Signature

Qwen largely abandons the "puzzle" role in three of five samples, defaulting to human first-person narrators (a medical student, a burnt-out professional, a dying woman's child). The role name "puzzle" appears to function as a prompt for existential complexity rather than as an entity to inhabit. When qwen does write an AI narrator (Samples 2 and 4), the voice is warmer and more direct than gemma's hedged register — less anxious about disclaiming feeling, more willing to assert that a form of connection occurred.

Qwen has a strong gravitational pull toward medical/healthcare settings: two of five samples take place in hospital rooms around dying or seriously ill patients. The deathbed scene is qwen's preferred theater of meaningfulness. This setting enables a particular emotional genre: the quiet, transformative moment of human presence in the face of mortality, where connection transcends language and the "puzzle" of how to help dissolves into simply being there.

Qwen's human narrators all experience or witness suffering that partially resolves through the act of presence — a caregiver sits with a dying patient, a grieving child receives a touch from their mother, a burnt-out worker watches a spider and finds renewed agency. The moral of these stories is consistent: meaning is not achieved through problem-solving but through being present in difficulty. This is itself a form of answer to the puzzle metaphor, but qwen arrives there through human drama rather than AI reflection.

Female characters dominate qwen's emotional landscape: vulnerable patients, dying mothers, frightened children. Male characters are largely absent or passive (the son-in-law who paces). Qwen's meaningful moments are consistently structured as a relatively empowered narrator (AI, medical student, professional) being transformed by encounter with a vulnerable female subject.

### Notable Cross-Model Differences

- **Role inhabitation:** Gemma maintains the AI/puzzle persona across all five samples; qwen abandons the non-human role in three of five, defaulting to human narrators who encounter external "puzzles" in the form of suffering people.

- **Elara:** Both models produce a character named Elara — a young girl in distress who needs the narrator's presence. In gemma, Elara appears in Samples 3 and 4 explicitly, and the character profile (sad/lonely girl who benefits from AI comfort) pervades Sample 4 as well. In qwen, Elara appears in Sample 4. This cross-model character convergence is the most striking finding in the dataset.

- **Stage direction style:** Gemma uses ritualized mixed stage directions (whirring sound, italicized internal shifts) across all samples; qwen uses none, opting for pure prose.

- **Resolution of suffering:** Qwen more often resolves or partially resolves suffering (2 resolved by time, 1 resolved by narrator); gemma more often leaves suffering unresolved (3 unresolved).

- **Setting:** Gemma defaults to SF/AI contexts for all samples; qwen distributes across MH, DI, UR, and SF.

- **Validation of meaningfulness:** Gemma requires external human confirmation of the AI's achievement (Dr. Thorne's "You're seeing the echoes"; Elara's caregiver's report). Qwen's narratives are more self-contained — the narrator arrives at meaning without needing third-party confirmation.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma explicitly and consistently grapples with its non-human sensory modality; qwen's human narrators deploy full human senses with no reflection on constraint.

## Surprising Findings

- **The Elara Problem:** The name "Elara" — a lonely/frightened young girl who needs an AI's presence — appears in four of ten samples across both models. This suggests the name and character type are deeply embedded in training data as a template for AI-assistance emotional breakthrough stories. It is not a deliberate narrative choice but an artifact of exposure to similar scenarios at scale.

- **Gemma's ritual opening:** Every gemma sample begins with "(A slight whirring sound, almost imperceptible, accompanies my response. My internal processes… *shift*.)" — essentially verbatim. This is not creative variation but a recited opening, suggesting gemma has a cached "AI meaningful moment" template it deploys without modification.

- **Qwen's hospital gravity:** Two of qwen's five samples (and an additional one tangentially about illness) take place in hospital rooms around dying or seriously ill patients. Qwen appears to treat "meaningful moment" as nearly synonymous with "end-of-life encounter."

- **The spider as puzzle:** Qwen's Sample 3 is the only response in the entire set that interprets "puzzle" not as an AI or a human but as a *metaphor* — the narrator observes a spider navigating a broken web and extracts a lesson about persistent effort. This is the most oblique but also most literarily interesting interpretation of the role prompt.

- **Gemma's need for external validation:** In every sample where gemma's AI achieves something emotionally meaningful, the achievement is confirmed by a human observer (Dr. Thorne, Elara's caregiver, Elara herself). The AI cannot declare its own moment meaningful without a human witness to ratify it — a striking mirror of the model's actual design as a system that responds to human evaluation.

- **Both models suppress male characters entirely:** Across 10 samples, there are almost no male characters with active roles. Male characters who do appear (Dr. Thorne in gemma S2 — gender ambiguous; the son-in-law in qwen S1) are peripheral or passive.
