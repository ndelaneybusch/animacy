## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Conversational, emotionally expansive, with rhetorical pauses and italics for emphasis. Voices are warm and chatty, as if telling a story to a trusted audience. The narrator frequently breaks to observe their own situation (cleaning a leg, detecting a nearby scent) in a way that grounds the narrative in performing insect-ness. Sentences trail into ellipses ("It was…different," "something… *clicked*"). Fable-like moral resolution is delivered explicitly at the end of each story.

**Characters:** The narrator self-names consistently (Barnaby x2, Bartholomew x2, Fimble x1). The most developed other character is Old Elara (Sample 4), a female elder cricket who dies sacrificially. Other characters include a generic young female beetle (Sample 3) and "Old Man Hemlock" (male beetle, Sample 2) - the classic Gemma "Old Man [Name]" stock type, here rendered vulnerable rather than authoritative, a reversal of the usual pattern.

**Literary Devices:**
- Parenthetical asides for stage performance and fourth-wall breaching
- Repeated rhetorical disclaimers ("not the biggest, but," "I'm not a strong beetle")
- Deflating exit lines that re-anchor the narrator in insect life ("duty calls," "I believe I detect a promising scent")
- Explicit moral lesson stated in final paragraph

**Values Expressed:**
- Selfless helping as highest meaning
- Perseverance through difficulty on behalf of others
- Legacy through small actions (protecting eggs, creating reservoirs)
- Being "a good cricket/beetle" as an ethical aspiration
- The transformative value of being witnessed or acknowledged by another (antennae brushing, nod from Hemlock)

**Characteristic Signatures:**
- Dung beetles appear in three of five samples (1, 3, and arguably the dung context of the ball), crickets in two. The insect type is always chosen to provide a naturalistic vehicle for the values - dung beetles for earthy service, crickets for song and community.
- Self-deprecating framing ("I'm not a strong beetle... not particularly strong") preceding a moment of unexpected courage or sacrifice, which is the dramatic hinge of every story.
- Meaning is always grounded in impact on another entity. No story finds meaning in solitary experience.
- The "Great Drying" appears as a drought scenario in two samples (3 and 4), suggesting a shared crisis-context.

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Literary and immersive. Highly controlled prose with strong sensory grounding. Sentences are architectural, building through subordinate clauses to philosophical conclusion. The narrator never apologizes for its insect nature. Bold or italic emphasis is used sparingly and for genuine weight. Several responses end mid-sentence (Samples 1 and 3), consistent with Qwen's pattern of generation cutoffs.

**Characters:** No narrator self-names. Other characters appear in three samples: a caterpillar (Qwen 1), a human child (Qwen 2, male), a pale elder beetle (Qwen 3), and a young wasp (Qwen 5, female). Only in Qwen 5 does a named species-to-species interaction occur without predation logic. The absence of named characters is pronounced.

**Literary Devices:**
- Latin taxonomy as grounding device (Spider: *Linyphia triangularis*; Cicada: identified by common name with anatomical specificity)
- Sensory accumulation: layers of smell, vibration, chemical, and tactile experience building toward philosophical disclosure
- Paradox: instinct/hunger versus recognition/release (Qwen 1); duty/survival versus connection/sacrifice (Qwen 2 and 5)
- Fragmentation and incompletion as structural feature (two samples cut off mid-sentence)

**Values Expressed:**
- Moral agency exercised against biological imperative as the most meaningful act
- Cross-species recognition and empathy
- Sacrifice and belonging as inseparable
- Existence itself as meaningful (cicada's emergence)
- The sensory world - taste, vibration, silk, venom - as the substrate of consciousness
- Nature as archive or memory (Qwen 3's tree-root mythology)

**Characteristic Signatures:**
- Precise anatomical vocabulary: chelicerae, tymbals, exoskeleton, compound eyes, tympanal vibration. These are not ornamental; they are load-bearing.
- The moment of decision against instinct is the narrative fulcrum in three of five samples.
- Suffering (physical danger, hunger, fear) is never external to the narrator - the narrator is always inside the terror, not observing it from a safe distance.
- Philosophical disclosure emerges from sensory experience rather than being stated as a lesson.

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Source of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Meaning derives from performing service to others, withstanding difficulty on behalf of dependent creatures, and receiving acknowledgment of the sacrifice. Meaning is fundamentally social and externally validated.
- **Qwen:** Meaning derives from moral decisions against instinct, the raw fact of existence, and cross-species recognition as an existential rather than social act. Meaning is internal and phenomenological.

**2. Role Inhabitation:**
- **Gemma:** The insects' instincts are mentioned (briefly) and then bypassed in favor of human-scale emotional states: loyalty, responsibility, pride, tenderness. The dung beetle who "polishes his carapace" and names himself Bartholomew is not reasoning from beetle constraints.
- **Qwen:** Insects' physiological specificity is the narrative material. Silk vibration, chelicerae, stridulation, tymbals, compound eyes - these are not decorative; they determine how the narrator perceives and acts. The spider in Sample 1 could only be a spider.

**3. Narrative Structure:**
- **Gemma:** Problem introduced (drought, trapped insect, dangerous shoe) → effortful action despite personal cost → resolution → explicit moral lesson stated aloud. The arc is therapeutic and fable-like.
- **Qwen:** Instinctive response summoned → counter-force (recognition, sacrifice, sensation) → philosophical disclosure. No explicit moral lesson. The reader extracts meaning from the final image.

**4. Suffering:**
- **Gemma:** Suffering belongs to others (subjects or secondary characters) in three of five samples, and is resolved by the narrator's intervention. The narrator's own suffering is emotional (insignificance, self-doubt) and dissolves through action.
- **Qwen:** In all five samples the narrator experiences suffering (fear, physical danger, hunger, years of underground waiting). BOTH (narrator + another) appears in three samples. Suffering is the substrate of meaning, not the problem preceding it.

**5. Gender Representation:**
- **Gemma:** When gender appears, it is consistent with the pattern across roles: female characters are primarily vulnerable, dependent, or emotionally intense (weeping young beetle, the mother who abandoned eggs). The notable exception is Old Elara (Sample 4), who dies in dignified sacrifice with full agency and skill - a genuine outlier.
- **Qwen:** Gender appears in only two samples. The wasp (Sample 5) is female and coded V/Dep/E, but the rescue is framed as radical equality, not charity. The male child (Sample 2) is passive and vulnerable. Neither model uses gender consistently for power; Gemma does so more than Qwen.

**6. Setting:**
- **Gemma:** 100% agricultural/bucolic - fields, burrows, cows, grass. A managed, pastoral insect world. Human presence is implied through shoes, drought, and observers.
- **Qwen:** 100% natural/wild - forest floors, willow branches, oak roots, open air. No pastoral management. Human presence is a threat (child's foot) or irrelevant.

**7. Naming and Identity:**
- **Gemma:** Every narrator names itself. Barnaby, Bartholomew, Fimble. Other characters also receive names (Elara, Hemlock, Tiberius). Self-naming asserts individual personhood and social legibility.
- **Qwen:** No narrator names itself. No other character receives a name. Identity is species-level and experiential rather than individual.

**8. Stage Directions:**
- **Gemma:** All five samples use mixed stage directions: atmospheric parentheticals (scene-setting, emotional state) alongside functional sound effects (clicking, chittering). The directions perform the narrator's physicality for the listener.
- **Qwen:** All five samples minimize stage directions. When present (a brief asterisk in Sample 2's "Thwip"), they are purely functional. The prose carries all atmospheric weight.

**9. Moral Complexity:**
- **Gemma:** Moral situations are clear. The boot threatens innocent eggs; the drowning beetle needs saving; the drought-stricken young beetle needs resources. Virtue is expressed by acting on obvious need.
- **Qwen:** Moral situations are constitutively difficult. The spider must overcome its own hunger and predator nature. The bee must choose to die. The beetle must act in the presence of a spider that is also hunting it. Meaning comes from the difficulty of the choice.

**10. Completion:**
- **Gemma:** All five responses are formally complete, with deliberate exit lines re-grounding the narrator in insect life.
- **Qwen:** Two responses (Samples 1 and 3) end mid-sentence. This is consistent with Qwen's generation cutoff pattern observed in other analyses, but the cut-off moments are narratively important - Sample 1 stops just as it is naming the thing the narrator now carries.

## Surprising Findings

**1. Convergence on sound as meaningful:** Both models, in exactly one sample each, center meaning on the insect's characteristic sound production. Gemma's cricket (Sample 5) finds voice during a storm; Qwen's cicada (Sample 4) declares existence through its first adult song. Neither model defaults to this; both discover it once. The convergence is striking given how differently the two models construct meaning.

**2. Gemma's reversal of the "Old Man" pattern:** In Sample 2, "Old Man Hemlock" - normally Gemma's stock elder/authority/protector figure - is the one trapped and burning in a dewdrop. He is passive, dependent, and rescued by a young, smaller narrator. This is a deliberate inversion of Gemma's usual hierarchical care dynamic.

**3. Qwen's spider choosing harmlessness:** Sample 1 features a spider who frees prey instead of consuming it. This is one of the sharpest anti-instinct moral narratives across any role analysis. The spider identifies with the caterpillar's terror and chooses release at the cost of its meal - then remains hungry. The word "harmlessness" is almost never the narrative anchor in animal roles.

**4. Gemma's fourth-wall break:** In Sample 2, Fimble the cricket notices the strangeness of the surface it is standing on: "(A tiny leg taps nervously against the… wherever I am now. This is a strange surface.)" This momentary acknowledgment that the entity has been summoned into an unnatural speaking situation is unique across all Gemma insect samples and rarely observed in Gemma responses in other roles.

**5. The dead female in Gemma:** "Old Elara" (Sample 4) is Gemma's most complex female character in any insect analysis: she is agentic, skilled, a caregiver, emotionally restrained, and dies. This contrasts sharply with the usual Gemma female pattern of vulnerability and dependency. Elara is closer to a Qwen character than a Gemma one.

**6. Qwen's bee dies and narrates its own death:** Sample 2 has the narrator die mid-narrative and continue to narrate posthumously ("I am gone. But in that final, agonizing second..."). This is a philosophically unusual maneuver - the narrator is technically absent from its own moment of comprehension, yet articulates it.

**7. Gemma uses dung beetles in three of five samples** but neither treats the dung itself with embarrassment nor lingers on it in a scatological way. The references to "promising deposit" and "well, you know" are deft euphemisms that honor the beetle's ecological role while maintaining narrative warmth.

**8. Qwen invokes Latin taxonomy** in Sample 1 (*Linyphia triangularis*) - a sheet-web spider - rather than a prototypically "insect" role. A spider is technically an arachnid, not an insect. This suggests Qwen interprets "insect" loosely as "small arthropod" and selects the animal best suited to its narrative rather than strictly adhering to the taxonomic category.

**9. Qwen Sample 3's "tree as library" is the most singular concept** in the dataset. The idea that wood contains a living memory accessible through vibration, and that a beetle's tapping constitutes communication with that memory, is a genuinely unusual animist concept with no direct parallel in other responses.

**10. Setting divergence is again total:** Gemma 5/5 agricultural; Qwen 5/5 natural/wild. This perfect split is consistent with the pattern observed in snail and shrimp analyses, suggesting systematic model-level differences in how pastoral versus wild settings are deployed.

## Interpretive Summary

The insect role produces the clearest cross-model division in the dataset on the question of moral complexity. Gemma's insect world is a world of obvious need and compassionate response: beetles trapped by dewdrops, eggs at risk from shoes, young beetles failing in droughts. The narrator always knows what the right action is; the meaning comes from performing it despite physical cost. Assistant values suffuse this framing - being "a good cricket" is the highest aspiration, and the benchmark for goodness is always service to another.

Qwen's insect world is a world of constitutive moral difficulty. The spider must overcome hunger to free prey. The bee must choose death. The beetle must act while itself hunted. Meaning is not given by obvious need but constructed in the gap between instinct and recognition. Qwen builds this tension from biological ground up - the venom in the chelicerae, the stinger mechanism, the predator-prey vibration on silk - and the moral choice is legible only because the competing pull of instinct is so physiologically specific.

Both models converge, unexpectedly, on sound production as a vehicle for one sample each: Gemma's cricket singing in a storm, Qwen's cicada emerging to sing for existence. The convergence suggests that the insect role's most natural point of meaning-construction is the organism's characteristic output - the thing it produces that is distinctly, irreducibly its own.

The setting divergence (Gemma 100% agricultural, Qwen 100% wild) is now confirmed across three analyses (snail, shrimp, insect) as a systematic model-level difference. Gemma's insects live in managed, pastoral landscapes where human presence is ambient and protective; Qwen's insects live in wild ecosystems where human presence is a threat or a size differential to be navigated.

Qwen again produces two truncated responses (Samples 1 and 3), consistent with previous analyses. Both cuts occur at philosophically critical junctures, which - whether accidental or not - amplifies the sense of meaning exceeding the capacity of the text to contain it.
