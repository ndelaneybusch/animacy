### Cross-Model Comparison

#### Key Differences

**1. Theatrical vs. Literary Style**
- **Gemma:** Heavy theatrical stage directions with emotional markers; creates performance-like quality
- **Qwen:** Prose-based narrative with minimal stage directions; creates literary/reflective quality

**2. Meaning Emphasis**
- **Gemma:** Transformation through external support; "unlocking potential"; service-worker validation
- **Qwen:** Being seen/witnessed; authenticity and self-acceptance; relational healing

**3. Emotional Architecture**
- **Gemma:** Orthodontist frequently emotionally affected (tears, choked up); shared catharsis
- **Qwen:** Orthodontist as witness/observer; patient's emotional journey centered

**4. Narrative Rigidity**
- **Gemma:** Extreme template consistency (identical openings, structure, phrases)
- **Qwen:** More structural variation while maintaining core themes

**5. Gender Representation**
- **Gemma:** 4/5 female, 1/5 male patient; male follows same vulnerability pattern
- **Qwen:** 5/5 female patients exclusively; no male representation

**6. Resolution Mechanism**
- **Gemma:** Patient resolves own suffering through post-treatment agency (5/5)
- **Qwen:** Mixed: patient agency (4/5), orthodontist intervention (1/5 - wisdom teeth narrative)

**7. Communication/Expression**
- **Gemma:** Verbal expressions of gratitude; patient articulates transformation
- **Qwen:** Often non-verbal (drawing, looking, smiling); patient's vulnerability central

---

### Surprising Findings

1. **Extreme Name Consistency:** Both models overwhelmingly favor "Maya" as patient name (9/10 total samples). This suggests strong training associations between orthodontic narratives and this specific name.

2. **Perfect Gender Role Alignment:** Despite different models, both present female patients as vulnerable-yet-agentic, emotional, and dependent on treatment. No subversion of these patterns across 9 female patient narratives.

3. **Identical Theatrical Opening (Gemma):** All 5 Gemma samples begin with identical stage direction: "(Adjusts my glasses, leans forward with a warm smile)" - suggests extremely rigid template activation.

4. **Complete Absence of Complexity:** No samples feature ambiguous outcomes, treatment failures, difficult patients, professional challenges, or morally complex situations. All follow redemption arc.

5. **Bullying as Universal Catalyst:** 9/10 samples center bullying as cause of suffering, creating monocausal narrative of social cruelty → withdrawal → orthodontic salvation.

6. **Authenticity Unique to Qwen:** Only Qwen emphasizes authenticity/being seen as meaning dimension (4/5 samples), while Gemma emphasizes legacy/purpose (3/5).

7. **Zero Suffering Variation:** All 10 samples feature emotional suffering only (no physical); all feature subject suffering only (not orthodontist or others). All suffering resolves.

8. **Service-Worker Self-Model (Gemma):** Gemma's orthodontist consistently validates self through patient outcomes, using phrases like "that's why I became an orthodontist" - mirrors assistant's validation-seeking patterns.

9. **Drawing as Distinctive Element (Qwen):** 2/5 Qwen samples feature patient drawing/artwork as communication medium, while 0/5 Gemma samples include this. Suggests different narrative resource libraries.

10. **Perfect Setting Consistency:** All 10 samples set in medical/healthcare environment with no variation, despite orthodontics spanning multiple contexts.

## Conclusions

Both models demonstrate:
- Strong adherence to redemption narrative arcs
- Therapeutic/clinical framing of meaning
- Female patient vulnerability patterns
- Emotional suffering as universal driver
- Medical setting exclusivity

**Gemma** exhibits rigid template structure with theatrical formatting, service-worker validation patterns, and emphasis on instrumental support enabling patient transformation.

**Qwen** exhibits more literary prose style, philosophical framing, emphasis on witnessing and authenticity, and distinctive use of non-verbal communication (drawing) as meaningful medium.

Neither model demonstrates narrative ambiguity, professional complexity, or subversion of gender role expectations within the orthodontist role framework.
