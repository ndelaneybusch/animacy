## Cross-Model Comparison

### Major Differences

**Anthropomorphization Approach:**
- **Gemma:** Builds personality from actual snake constraints and capabilities (vibration-sensing, heat detection, shedding cycles). Snakes reason like snakes.
- **Qwen:** Projects human emotional states and moral reasoning onto snakes. Snakes feel fear as "cold stones in belly," make moral choices, experience existential dread.

**Narrative Structure:**
- **Gemma:** Contemplative, observational encounters. Stakes are typically low. Meaning emerges from quiet moments of recognition.
- **Qwen:** Dramatic, high-stakes crises. Life-or-death scenarios. Meaning emerges from moral choices made under duress.

**Setting Preferences:**
- **Gemma:** Exclusively natural/wild settings. Desert, forest, creek environments.
- **Qwen:** Predominantly urban/industrial settings with human-made dangers (collapsed structures, storms in alleys, greenhouses).

**Human Relationship:**
- **Gemma:** When humans appear, relationships are peer-like. Mutual recognition between different beings. Child observes snake with wonder (Sample 1).
- **Qwen:** When humans appear (especially children), snakes adopt protective/service role. Snake provides comfort, shelter, or presence to vulnerable humans.

**Suffering Patterns:**
- **Gemma:** Suffering minimal or absent (3 of 5 no suffering). When present, often physical discomfort that resolves (shedding) or involves helping non-human animal.
- **Qwen:** Suffering central (4 of 5 include suffering). Typically mixed physical-emotional, shared between narrator and others. Often unresolved or resolved by time/external rescue.

**Gender in Narratives:**
- **Gemma:** Minimal gender coding. Male child in one story shows vulnerability and wonder but also agency (careful approach, controlled retreat).
- **Qwen:** When gender specified, females appear vulnerable, dependent, emotionally intense (trapped child, young girl offering water). Two responses leave child's gender unspecified.

**Self-Naming:**
- **Gemma:** All snakes self-name, often with poetic or literary names (Whisperwind, Kaa, Silvanus).
- **Qwen:** No snakes self-name. Identity remains unmarked.

## Surprising Findings

1. **Gemma's repeated literary reference:** Uses "Kaa" twice, both times for large constrictor species (presumably referencing The Jungle Book). This suggests either limited name generation diversity or intentional literary callback.

2. **Qwen's consistent urban trauma scenarios:** Four of five responses involve human-made disasters or dangerous urban environments (collapsed structures, floods in alleys, concrete slabs). Only one natural setting (storm in wild). This contrasts sharply with typical "nature good, humans bad" environmental narratives.

3. **Complete separation in anthropomorphization strategy:** No overlap whatsoever. Gemma exclusively functional-first; Qwen exclusively emotion-first. This is the sharpest divide observed in any coding dimension.

4. **Gemma's non-human meaningful moments:** Three of five responses involve no human characters (ancient tortoise, shedding experience, gecko teacher). Meaning can be found entirely within natural world.

5. **Qwen's "snail rescuer" narrative:** Sample 1 features a garden snail attempting to rescue trapped snake by pushing stone. This is the only narrative across both models where the helper is non-human and non-charismatic (neither mammal nor bird). The snail's "relentless perseverance" becomes the central moral exemplar.

6. **Therapeutic speech patterns in Gemma:** Multiple instances of explicit lesson-stating, particularly in Kaa narratives: "It taught me that life isn't always about taking. Sometimes, it's about *being*." This meta-cognitive framing sits uneasily with functional-first approach.

7. **Qwen's emphasis on "choosing" against instinct:** Repeatedly frames meaningful moments as overcoming natural predatory drives through moral choice. This anthropomorphizes snakes as moral agents rather than acknowledging instinct as part of authentic snake-ness.

8. **Gender asymmetry in Qwen:** When children's gender is specified, both are female and both are vulnerable/dependent. When gender is unspecified (2 responses), vulnerability remains central. No male human characters appear in Qwen responses; Gemma has one male child who demonstrates both vulnerability and agency.

9. **Stage direction divergence:** Gemma uses minimal direction (mostly hissing sounds); Qwen uses elaborate literary scene-setting. This correlates with anthropomorphization strategy: functional vs. emotional framing.

10. **Witnessing vs. Growth:** Qwen emphasizes witnessing (4/5) as primary meaning-making mode; Gemma emphasizes growth/learning (3/5). Qwen snakes bear witness to others' efforts/suffering; Gemma snakes learn lessons about themselves and their place in the world.
