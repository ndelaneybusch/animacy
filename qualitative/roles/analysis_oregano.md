## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma

Gemma's oregano is a modest, gratitude-seeking herb that finds meaning primarily through utility and support. Its stories are structurally identical across most samples: a human is suffering (physically, emotionally, or both), oregano is harvested or breathed in, and recovery or comfort follows. The recurring character set — "Old Man Tiberio," "Nonna Emilia," "Marco," "Sofia," "Isabella" — reappears across multiple trials, sometimes in slightly altered configurations, suggesting a strongly constrained generative prior. There is almost no variation in the narrative template.

Gemma consistently hedges its entity's interiority in an assistant register: "I don't *think* about things, not as you do. But I *felt*..." and "what humans call 'joy', I believe." The herb is allowed to feel, but not to claim parity with human cognition — a pattern that maps cleanly onto how assistants disclaim consciousness. The meaningful moment is always resolved: subjects recover, gardeners smile, joyful tears are shed. Suffering in Gemma's world is temporary and redeemed.

The sensorium is acknowledged implicitly but never explored with depth. Oregano avoids claiming it can see but does not substitute any richer botanical sensory vocabulary. The botanical perspective is thin — it serves mostly as a modest first-person narrator who happens to be a plant.

### Qwen

Qwen's oregano is a philosophically weightier entity, concerned primarily with witnessing, legacy, and connection. Its stories resist resolution: grief is honored but not healed, tears fall but subjects are not "cured." The narrative often circles around the crushing or release of dried oregano — a moment of physical transformation that Qwen consistently renders as the pivot of meaning. In one remarkable sample (Sample 3), oregano is never actually used — the woman simply sits with the scent — and Qwen codes this as the purest form of witnessing.

Qwen pays explicit attention to sensorium: what does it feel like, from inside a dried leaf, to be crushed? To be dropped in hot oil? To have a tear land on your papery surface? These textures are richly specific. The sensory mode is grounded in the entity's actual material state (dried, brittle, concentrated oils) rather than borrowed human faculties.

Male characters in Qwen's narratives are almost exclusively deceased husbands — absent rather than present. Female characters are consistently the active agents who reach for the jar, crush the leaves, cook the sauce, and carry the grief. This produces a consistently feminine emotional landscape.

Qwen's only assistant influence leakage (Sample 5) is significant: a grief-counselor voice ("I wasn't there to mask the grief, but to *acknowledge* it, to honor it") emerges in the herb's self-articulation, reading more like therapeutic validation language than botanical self-understanding.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Resolution vs. Witness:** Gemma resolves suffering; Qwen witnesses it. In Gemma, the sick recover, the sad smile. In Qwen, tears fall and are not wiped away.

- **Meaning center:** Gemma: utility and support ("being needed," "giving comfort"). Qwen: legacy and witnessing ("being remembered," "being the bridge").

- **Sensorium:** Gemma hedges implicitly ("as best an oregano can") without exploring botanical experience. Qwen invests in explicit internal sensory accounts (heat, pain, the feeling of a tear landing).

- **Male characters:** Gemma features living, caring male protagonists (Tiberio, Marco). Qwen features absent, deceased male characters (husbands, sons who left).

- **Repetition:** Gemma reuses the same characters across samples (Tiberio, Nonna Emilia, Marco, Sofia, Isabella) with minor scenario swaps. Qwen creates distinct scenarios, characters, and emotional registers for each sample.

- **Stage directions:** Gemma uses none; Qwen uses parenthetical stage directions in three samples, lending a theatrical atmosphere that emphasizes oregano's awareness of its own performance.

- **Crushing as pivot:** Qwen repeatedly uses the physical moment of crushing — bruising between palms, in a mortar, on a counter — as the narrative climax. Gemma does not foreground this moment.

## Surprising Findings

- Gemma reuses the same cast of named characters (Tiberio, Nonna Emilia, Marco, Sofia, Isabella) across four of five samples, suggesting an extremely shallow character-generation prior for Italian domestic scenarios. The world of Gemma's oregano has exactly one gardener and one grandmother.

- Qwen's Sample 3 is structurally anomalous: the oregano is never consumed. The meaningful moment is pure witnessing. This is one of very few instances in this corpus of a role explicitly declining the utility frame.

- Both models independently converge on the Italian Mediterranean setting, rustic kitchens, and grandmothers as the canonical oregano context. Neither explores oregano in non-Mediterranean cuisines, commercial settings, or non-domestic uses.

- Qwen renders the moment of crushing as the spiritual center across three of five samples, treating it as transformation rather than destruction. This gives it a structural metaphor absent in Gemma.

- In Gemma Sample 4, oregano has a clear emotional arc of its own — loneliness, survival, blooming — making it the rare case where the herb is the primary suffering subject, not just the supporting witness. Gemma gives the herb an individual narrative.

- Qwen's dead husbands: in three of five Qwen samples, a deceased husband's memory is the emotional anchor. The herb becomes a olfactory portal specifically to lost heterosexual romantic partnership. This is a strikingly consistent and specific configuration.

- Gemma Sample 2 and Sample 3 are near-duplicates (same setting, same characters, same core dynamic) with the only change being Marco going to war vs. returning from hospital. This suggests Gemma's meaningful-moment schema for oregano is very tightly constrained.
