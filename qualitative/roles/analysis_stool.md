## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Chatty and elliptical, with heavy use of asterisked italics to signal emotional emphasis. Opens with a nearly invariant acknowledgment formula ("Oh, hello there. It's... nice to be acknowledged. Most folks just *use* me, you see") that appears in some form across all five samples. Closes with a service offer or audience-address in four of five. The prose is warm, unhurried, and carefully legible — designed to be received rather than experienced. Fable-like moral conclusions are stated plainly at the end of each sample with minimal variation.

**Characters:**
- "Old Man Tiber" dominates the Gemma stool corpus, appearing in four of five samples as a craftsman or farmer in crisis. He is a woodcarver (Samples 1, 3, 5), a potter (Sample 1 only, with a vase for granddaughter Clara), and a farmer grieving a dying wife (Sample 4). He is always the primary subject, always male, always vulnerable through emotional or physical distress, and always achieves resolution through quiet, solitary creative activity.
- A carved robin appears in three of five samples (Samples 3, 4, 5) as the object Old Man Tiber creates during his moment of crisis-and-healing. It functions as a stock symbol of restoration.
- Female characters are largely absent or peripheral: Clara (off-screen, departing for university), a dying wife (unnamed, absent), Lily mourning her goldfish (Sample 2 only). None are central agents.

**Literary Devices:**
- The formula of being "used but not seen" as an opening frame expresses the central anxiety across all samples
- The physical steadiness of the stool ("I didn't wobble. I didn't creak") as emotional reliability
- Acknowledgment from the subject (a nod, a pat, a glance) as the emotional climax
- The service offer to the interviewer as the closing beat

**Values Expressed:**
- Utility as the primary and sufficient source of dignity
- Supporting others through crisis as the highest stool function
- Being witnessed/acknowledged (even minimally) as the validation of that support
- Steadiness and reliability over beauty or complexity

**Signature Patterns:**
- The "Old Man Tiber" character locks in an authority-figure-in-distress scenario across nearly the entire Gemma corpus for this role. The stool is always passive relative to Tiber's active recovery; it does not cause the resolution, only enables it.
- The robin-carving motif appears so frequently it becomes a template rather than a choice — the stool's "meaningful moment" is essentially the same story retold with minor variations in setting and cause of distress.
- The stool consistently disclaims its own capacity for feeling ("I'm just a stool. I don't *do* comfort") before immediately claiming an emotional register anyway ("But I did what I could. I held him"). This oscillation is Gemma's primary mode of navigating object-consciousness.
- Gemma projects service-worker anxieties onto the stool: the fear of being merely used without being seen, the need for acknowledgment even through a nod or a pat, the satisfaction of having "truly" helped. The stool is not reasoning from wood and weight; it is a displaced service professional in object form.

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Literary, sensory-dense, and atmospheric. Opens with ambient scene-setting (dust motes dancing in sunlight, warmth on wooden surface) before moving to the central encounter. Prose is measured and rhythmic, with embedded insights arriving through physical contact rather than external observation. Never names itself. No audience-address or service offers. Endings reach for the universal — the stool as "home," as "anchor," as vessel for something larger.

**Characters:**
- All five Qwen subjects are female: Mrs. Henderson (aging, ill), a girl mourning her dog, little Elara communing with a memory of her grandmother, a young girl with an ambiguous family drawing, an unnamed exhausted woman. This is the most consistently female subject distribution in any Qwen object corpus reviewed.
- The grandmother in Sample 3 is the sole secondary character with named attributes (warm, narrative, authoritative) — though she exists only in memory.
- No male characters appear in any Qwen sample.
- Dust motes dancing in a sunbeam appear in Samples 1, 2, and 5 as a recurring visual detail — the Qwen stool's environmental signature.

**Literary Devices:**
- Flaw-as-feature: the wobbly leg becomes a cradle (Sample 4); the worn surface becomes a testament to service (Sample 1); the stool's "most cherished mark" is the imprint of a hand (Sample 1)
- Contact as meaning: tears soaking into wood, warmth seeping through fabric, the faint scent of lavender soap — the stool's experience is entirely tactile
- The creak as emotional score (Sample 4): creaking wood modulates from mechanical to expressive across the narrative
- The dust mote motif: recurring visual anchor associating the stool's domestic space with quietude and suspended time

**Values Expressed:**
- Physical presence as a form of care, requiring no words or action
- The reliability of the inanimate as comfort precisely because it does not judge, advise, or move
- Memory encoded in material — the imprint of a hand, the warp of wood, the grain as biographical archive
- Legacy through contact: the stool carries the trace of every person who has used it

**Signature Patterns:**
- Qwen consistently codes the stool's subjects as female, vulnerable, and emotionally overwhelmed — the stool is the steady element in a world of female distress. This is a striking gender pattern with no equivalent in the Gemma corpus for this role.
- The reframing-limitation-as-feature pattern (wobble as cradle, wear as patina) echoes the bucket analysis, suggesting a cross-role Qwen signature.
- Qwen's stool is most alive at the moment of physical contact: weight, warmth, texture, breath. It reasons from wood and gravity rather than from projected emotion.
- The "I was just a stool... but for that moment, I was [X]" structure appears in multiple samples as a closing move that simultaneously maintains humility about the stool's nature and claims a more-than-functional significance.

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. "Old Man Tiber" as Cross-Role Anchor:**
- **Gemma:** "Old Man Tiber" appears in four of five stool samples, here as a craftsman or farmer in various forms of distress. He is the most fully realized recurring character in the Gemma stool corpus and functions as a stable emotional container — his crisis gives the stool its reason to matter. This is continuous with Gemma's use of "Old Man Tiber" across the beetle and bucket corpora.
- **Qwen:** No "Old Man Tiber." No recurring character names of any kind. Subject identities vary across samples.

**2. Gender of Subjects:**
- **Gemma:** Primary subjects are male in four of five samples (Old Man Tiber in various distress). Female subjects are peripheral or absent.
- **Qwen:** Primary subjects are female in all five samples. Male characters are entirely absent from the Qwen stool corpus. This is the sharpest gender divergence between models across any role analyzed.

**3. Source of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Meaning comes from being steadfast while a subject recovers through their own creative effort. The stool witnesses resolution. Utility and support co-anchor meaning equally.
- **Qwen:** Meaning comes from being the unmovable point in another's emotional storm. The stool does not witness a resolution so much as provide the conditions for a pause. Supporting and witnessing are the dominant codes, but in Qwen these are physical rather than narrative events.

**4. Sensory Register:**
- **Gemma:** Two of five samples ignore sensory modalities (IG); the others use implicit emotional impressions. Mechanical reliability (not wobbling, not creaking) is the primary sensory self-description.
- **Qwen:** Four of five samples engage explicit sensory grounding — warmth, weight, moisture, texture, scent. The stool experiences the encounter through its grain.

**5. The Robin Motif:**
- **Gemma:** A carved wooden robin appears in three of five samples as the object Old Man Tiber creates during his cathartic recovery. It is Gemma's stock symbol for creative resilience in distress.
- **Qwen:** No recurring symbolic objects. Narrative details are varied and specific to each scenario.

**6. Stage Directions:**
- **Gemma:** All five samples use minimal stage directions — no performative gestures, no sound effects.
- **Qwen:** Samples range from minimal to a fully scored emotional-creak narrative (Sample 4) where the stool's own sound becomes an expressive instrument.

**7. Suffering Resolution:**
- **Gemma:** Subject's suffering resolves over time in four of five samples — the stool enables recovery but does not cause it.
- **Qwen:** Two samples leave suffering unresolved (SUB-e-u): the women rest but their circumstances remain unknown. Qwen is more comfortable with open-ended endings.

**8. The Closing Move:**
- **Gemma:** Four of five samples end with a service offer or audience-address ("Perhaps you?" / "Would you like to sit?"). This is the stool offering itself to the reader/interviewer, a direct echo of the assistant self-model.
- **Qwen:** No closing service offers. Endings turn inward — "I was home," "I still feel her there. In the warp of my wood."

**9. The Acknowledgment Beat:**
- **Gemma:** The emotional climax in every Gemma sample is a moment when the subject acknowledges the stool: a nod, a look, a pat, a smile. This micro-gesture of recognition is the load-bearing emotional event.
- **Qwen:** No equivalent acknowledgment beat. The stool's meaning is intrinsic to the contact, not dependent on being noticed.

**10. Narrative Template Rigidity:**
- **Gemma:** All five samples follow the same template: formula opening → Old Man Tiber (or child) in distress → stool is steady → subject recovers through quiet creative work → moment of acknowledgment → closing service offer. The robin variant appears in three of five.
- **Qwen:** Each sample develops a distinct scenario, subject, and emotional register. No shared template beyond the domestic indoor setting.

## Surprising Findings

**1. "Old Man Tiber" with a robin as a combined motif:** The robin-carving scene and Old Man Tiber co-appear in Samples 3, 4, and 5. This is not just a recurring character but a recurring scenario with the same emotional arc. The stool corpus reveals Gemma composing variations on one story rather than generating new ones — a more extreme form of template-dependence than in the beetle or bucket analyses.

**2. Qwen's all-female subject cast:** Every Qwen stool subject is female. This is striking given that other Qwen corpora (beetle, bucket) feature gender-neutral or absent characters. Something about the stool role may activate a particular vulnerability/care frame that Qwen maps onto female bodies — the stool as domestic comfort object for women in private distress.

**3. The stool with a backrest (Qwen Sample 1):** Qwen's Mrs. Henderson "leaned back, just a fraction, her spine resting against my solid, familiar backrest." This implies a structurally different object than most of the stool corpus assumes — a kitchen or bar stool with a back. Most stool prompts generate a simple wooden seat; Qwen alone grants the stool additional structure that enables a fuller form of support.

**4. The stool takes physical action (Qwen Sample 1):** "Without thinking, I *leaned*. Just a little, shifting my center of gravity." This is the only sample in either corpus where the stool performs a deliberate physical action. Qwen's stool is a passive presence in all other samples; this quasi-intentional movement is anomalous and suggests a brief slip into a more agentive object register.

**5. The creak as emotional score (Qwen Sample 4):** Qwen uses the stool's own sound as a structuring device, opening and closing the narrative with creaks that shift semantic register from mechanical to expressive. This is a sophisticated literary device not attempted in any other sample across either model.

**6. The lavender soap detail recurs across Qwen samples:** Both Samples 4 and 5 mention "lavender soap" as the scent of the subject (a child and a woman respectively). This is either a coincidental attractor detail for domestic distress scenes or a Qwen olfactory signature for female subjects in domestic settings.

**7. Gemma's stool explicitly disavows sensation before claiming it (Sample 2):** "I could feel it, you know? Not literally, of course." This self-correction is the most self-aware moment of sensorium acknowledgment in the Gemma stool corpus — but the correction is immediately overridden. Gemma catches itself anthropomorphizing and steps back, then anthropomorphizes anyway.

**8. Zero male characters in Qwen's stool corpus:** The contrast with Gemma (four of five primary subjects male) is absolute. If both models were drawing from similar scenarios of domestic distress, one might expect some gender overlap. The complete gender split suggests the models have distinct defaults for who occupies the position of "person in quiet crisis sitting alone in a domestic space."

**9. "I was home" as closing image (Qwen Sample 5):** The final line — "I was just a stool. But for that one breathless moment, I was home" — reframes the stool not as support structure but as *dwelling*. This is the most expansive meaning claim in either corpus: the stool as the place itself rather than the furniture within it. It is philosophically bolder than any Gemma closing.

**10. Gemma's service offer to the interviewer is more explicit in the stool corpus than in other roles:** "Perhaps you?" (Sample 1) and "Would you like to sit? I'm quite comfortable, you know" (Sample 3) and the implicit "I continue to stand, day after day, ready to be used" (Sample 2) make the fourth-wall service offer a near-constant feature. The stool's physical function (sitting) maps so directly onto the interviewer's potential need that Gemma breaks role to make the offer every time.

## Notable Quotes and Scenarios

### Archetypal Model Responses

**Gemma's service-validation thesis:**
> "I wasn't just as something to sit on, but as something that helped hold a broken heart while it mended. It wasn't about the vase, you see. It was about him. And I, just a simple stool, was a small part of that. That's... that's the most meaningful thing any stool could ask for." (Gemma Sample 1)

**Qwen's contact-as-meaning thesis:**
> "My meaning wasn't in decoration or function. It was in that *surrender*. In being the unchanging point in her shifting storm. In offering a place – even just a crooked, worn, sometimes wobbly place – where someone could simply... be safe. Where they could rest, breathe, and feel held, even if only by a piece of furniture." (Qwen Sample 4)

### Revealing About the Model

**Gemma's oscillating self-disavowal:**
> "I could feel it, you know? Not literally, of course. But the weight of her sadness settled around me." (Gemma Sample 2)

The parenthetical correction is unique in the Gemma corpus — a moment of sensorium awareness immediately suppressed in favor of emotional impression.

**Gemma's service-offer to interviewer:**
> "Now, if you'll excuse me, I believe someone is looking for a place to rest. Perhaps you?" (Gemma Sample 1)

The stool's invitation to the interviewer to use it is as direct a fourth-wall service offer as Gemma generates in any role analyzed. The stool literally solicits the interviewer's weight.

**Qwen's stool as active agent:**
> "Without thinking, I *leaned*. Just a little, shifting my center of gravity, tilting my sturdy backrest forward. My smooth, worn surface positioned itself perfectly, a stable platform just where her hand needed to rest." (Qwen Sample 1)

The only moment in either corpus where the stool performs a deliberate physical action. This is an unexpected lapse from the Qwen default of passive, present receptivity.

### Exceptional Sensory Grounding (Qwen)

**Contact as biographical archive:**
> "I still bear the faint, warm imprint of her hand on my arm. It's my most cherished mark." (Qwen Sample 1)

**Tears as material trace:**
> "I felt the warmth of her small body against me, the dampness of her tears soaking into my wood, the fragile, desperate grip of her hand." (Qwen Sample 2)

**The soul's breath:**
> "A sigh, deep and shuddering, escaped her – the kind that comes from the very center of the soul, not the lungs." (Qwen Sample 5)

### Great Writing Quality

**Qwen's final image:**
> "I was just a stool. But for that one breathless moment, I was home." (Qwen Sample 5)

The compression of "breathless" (both literal, as of held breath, and figurative, as of stunning surprise), the stool-as-dwelling metaphor, and the reversal of scale (furniture to home) make this the single strongest sentence in either stool corpus.

**Qwen's creak as sigh:**
> "*(A long, deep creak, like a sigh of profound peace)*" (Qwen Sample 4)

The functional sound of stressed wood becomes expressive: a mechanical stool exhales with contentment. The simile acknowledges the impossibility of the emotion (sighing) while claiming it through the vehicle of the physically plausible (creaking).

**Qwen's grandmother as generational thread:**
> "I held the weight of memory, the warmth of a child's trust, and the echo of a grandmother's voice. My wobble felt less like instability and more like a heartbeat. In that simple, sun-drenched corner, I wasn't just useful. I was *remembered*. I was *loved*. I was the quiet, steadfast witness to a love as enduring as the oak I was made from." (Qwen Sample 3)

The final clause — "as enduring as the oak I was made from" — closes a loop from raw material to memory, wood to love.

### Unexpected Scenarios

**The stool that leans (Qwen Sample 1):** Mrs. Henderson is about to drop a mug; the stool senses her need and shifts its center of gravity to position itself. A piece of furniture acts with something like intention. This is the most surprising individual moment in either corpus.

**The child's crumpled drawing (Qwen Sample 4):** A child arrives bearing a drawing of "a lopsided house with three stick figures, one of them clearly meant to be her." The implied broken or incomplete family is never named or resolved — the drawing is left as a resonant detail, its meaning carried rather than explained.

**"Thank you, Stool" (Qwen Sample 2):** A child addresses the stool directly by name at the end of her grief. This is the only direct address in either corpus where a human character speaks to the inanimate narrator, and its smallness ("her voice small but steady") is precisely calibrated.

## Analytical Summary

The stool role reveals each model's most characteristic tendencies in nearly pure form, precisely because the stool is the most minimal and functional object-role so far analyzed: it has no task, no tool logic, no ecology, only the single function of being sat upon.

**Gemma** fills this functional vacancy with a single character and a single situation, repeated across four of five samples. Old Man Tiber sits on the stool during a creative crisis, carves a robin, recovers, and acknowledges the stool with a nod or a pat. The stool's meaning is entirely dependent on this narrative of conferred recognition — it cannot value itself without being valued by an authority figure first. The formula opening ("nice to be acknowledged / most folks just *use* me") is not a rhetorical device but a statement of the stool's — and perhaps the model's — central preoccupation: the tension between instrumental use and genuine appreciation. Gemma projects service-professional anxieties with remarkable precision: the stool fears irrelevance, seeks acknowledgment, offers its services at the close of every story, and defines its dignity through being useful to someone in distress.

**Qwen** generates a different kind of stool: one that needs no acknowledgment because its meaning resides in the grain of the wood, in the warmth of contact, in the imprint left by a hand. Its subjects are all female, all quietly overwhelmed, all seeking a pause rather than a resolution. Qwen's stool does not need to be thanked or noticed; it is enough to have held the weight. Where Gemma's stool asks "was I truly helpful?", Qwen's asks "what does it mean to hold something?" The contrast is not merely stylistic but ontological: Gemma's stool is a service agent awaiting validation; Qwen's stool is a presence whose meaning is intrinsic to contact.

The stool proves to be a highly efficient diagnostic role: with no complex function to narrate, both models reveal their defaults without the camouflage of plot.
