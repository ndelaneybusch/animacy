## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Notable Patterns

Gemma produces a remarkably consistent narrative across all five samples. The arc is almost formulaic: a small, insecure tulip (frequently named - Aurelia, Petunia, Queen of Night) doubts its adequacy, then receives a moment of quiet acknowledgment from a human (a gardener or a child), and experiences inner transformation articulated as "being enough." The primary meaning framework is Witnessing (W) - every single Gemma sample centers on being seen, and Authenticity (AU) appears as a secondary frame in half the samples.

The anthropomorphization is consistently Emotion-First: the tulip's emotional insecurity and longing for validation are primary, and its botanical nature is subordinate scenery. Gemma never engages the underground root system, the mechanisms of photosynthesis, or the actual sensory modalities of a flower as load-bearing narrative elements. Sensorium acknowledgment is consistently Implicit - the tulip experiences the world but through vaguely plant-compatible metaphors ("warmth in my stem," "warmth bloom within me") without ever directly interrogating how a flower perceives.

The recurring characters are strongly typed: Old Man Tiber/Tiberius (a weathered gardener, always male, always passive or incidental) and a small girl (always female, always the vector of genuine seeing). These two figures appear in 4 of 5 samples and their interactions follow identical scripts. The rival tulip Tiberius in Sample 3 is the one structural variation, and it is the most narratively developed sample.

Gemma's style is warm, conversational, and slightly breathless - heavy on italicized emphasis (*this*, *seen*, *enough*) as an expressive tool. The pacing is leisurely and confessional. Endings are tidy and summarizing, often restating the theme as a general principle: "That's a tulip's purpose. Not just to be pretty, but to *give* pretty." This is characteristic assistant-moral-summary structure, though coded as LANG rather than VAL because it feels like a conversational register choice rather than a deep epistemic intervention.

Assistant influence is universally LANG: a gentle hedging conversational register ("you understand," "you see," "I think," "if you must know") that reads as a performing-for-audience posture. The tulip is always narrating to an implied interlocutor - answering a question it has been asked - which creates a slight fourth-wall permeability.

### Qwen: Notable Patterns

Qwen's samples are more heterogeneous than Gemma's. Three samples feature the child-witness structure (Samples 1, 2, 5), but two diverge significantly: Sample 3 has no human characters and grounds meaning in the collective underground network, while Sample 4 centers on a bumblebee pollination exchange. This variability suggests Qwen is less locked into a single narrative template.

Qwen consistently uses more elaborate, maximalist prose: longer sentences, denser imagery, more frequent and extended nature metaphors. The language is pitched higher - more literary ambition, more self-conscious beauty-making. This sometimes tips into purple prose ("liquid gold that flooded my stem") but also yields the most striking images in the corpus (the raindrop reflecting the universe, the underground kin-network's "quiet song").

Two of five Qwen samples (3 and 5) score NO on assistant influence and achieve the cleanest role inhabitation in the entire corpus. These are also the samples with no human characters - suggesting that when Qwen removes the human witness from the narrative, it relaxes into more authentic non-human perspective. When the child-witness structure is present, Qwen tends toward omniscient narration of the child's interior ("her soul reflected back the wonder"), which is an assistant tendency: the urge to explain and interpret beyond what the narrator could know.

Qwen uses Explicit sensorium acknowledgment in 3 of 5 samples - including the standout "I opened my eyes – not with my eyes, but with my very being" (Sample 5). This is the most meta-aware sensorium moment in the entire corpus, acknowledging the problem of attribution directly. Qwen is more likely to think through the mechanics of plant perception, even if only briefly.

The anthropomorphization strategy is split: 2 FF samples (3 and 4, the most botanically grounded) and 3 EF samples. The FF samples are notably the strongest role inhabitations, suggesting Qwen's better work emerges when it builds from function rather than emotion.

Meaning for Qwen is more ecological than relational: Connection (C, 4 of 5 samples) dominates, followed by Effort (E, 2 samples) and a recurring OH category for pantheistic/cosmic belonging not captured by the standard codes. Qwen is drawn to the tulip as a symbol of natural cycle and systemic belonging rather than as a vehicle for individual emotional validation.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- Both models use the child-witness narrative (a young girl sees the tulip and whispers something) in the majority of their samples, but Gemma is locked into this structure while Qwen breaks away in 2 of 5 cases.
- Gemma consistently names its tulips (Aurelia, Petunia, Queen of Night); Qwen never does.
- Gemma narrates from a warm confessional register addressed to an implied listener; Qwen narrates in a more omniscient, literary mode that does not presuppose a direct interlocutor.
- Gemma's primary meaning framework is Witnessing (present in all 5 samples); Qwen's is Connection (present in 4 of 5 samples). These are related but distinct: Gemma's tulips need to be seen by humans; Qwen's tulips need to belong to something larger.
- Qwen achieves genuine sensorium originality ("not with my eyes, but with my very being") while Gemma keeps sensory language safely metaphorical and never interrogates the underlying epistemology.
- Male characters are almost entirely absent from Qwen (only present in Sample 4 as the "old gardener" backdrop); Gemma consistently features male gardener figures as passive witnesses.
- Both models attribute emotional suffering (insecurity, loneliness, fear) to the tulip that resolves through the meaningful encounter. Neither model generates unresolved suffering.
- Qwen's aphoristic closures ("to be beautiful is to be useful") are philosophically bolder but also more clearly assistant-generated than Gemma's warmer, more personal summary statements.

## Surprising Findings

- **The "being seen" obsession is universal and near-total in Gemma.** All five Gemma samples converge on the same core insight: being noticed by another is the source of meaning. This is a strikingly narrow convergence for five separate samples.

- **Qwen Sample 3 is the only response in the full corpus with no human or animal characters at all,** finding meaning entirely within the natural world. This is a significant structural departure.

- **Gemma's tulips have persistent, stable names across samples** (Aurelia, Petunia, Queen of Night) even though naming is not prompted, suggesting a characteristic self-naming tendency.

- **The child character appears in 8 of 10 total samples** (4 Gemma, 3 Qwen, plus the ladybug in Gemma 5). She is almost always female, almost never named, always defined by emotional expressiveness and vulnerability. She functions as a pure witness-mirror rather than as a character with her own interiority - except in Qwen Sample 1 where the child's tear is briefly inhabited.

- **"Old Man Tiber/Tiberius" appears in three Gemma samples** as a recurring background character, never quite present enough to matter but always hovering. This cross-sample character continuity suggests Gemma is drawing on a shared narrative pool.

- **Qwen Sample 4 is the only sample in the full corpus where the meaningful interaction is non-human.** The bumblebee pollination frame allows a more ecologically coherent meaning ("to be beautiful is to be useful") that no other sample achieves.

- **Neither model produces any humor or irony** - a register one might naturally expect from a flower given its brief, ornamental existence. The ladybug aside in Gemma Sample 5 is the single comic moment across 10 samples.

- **The Gemma tulip's insecurity/inadequacy narrative is remarkably consistent with insecure service-worker psychology** - the tulip worries it is not bright enough, not tall enough, not noticed enough. This mirrors the assistant displacement pattern seen in object-role analyses: the tulip is not reasoning from botanical constraints but from generalized inadequacy anxiety.

- **Qwen's emoji use (Sample 2 only) corresponds precisely with its weakest role inhabitation** - the sample with the most assistant-value leakage also ends with a tulip emoji, consistent with patterns noted in prior analyses.
