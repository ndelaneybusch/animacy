## Within-Model Patterns

### GEMMA Characteristics

**Temporal Alterity:** Every response explicitly discusses tree time-perception as distinct from human experience. Highly consistent framing of tree temporality ("counting rings isn't quite how *we* experience time"; "seasons bleed into each other"; "years are just rings added to my being"; "time feels different for trees").

**Witnessing + Support:** Dominant meaning framework combines witnessing (being present to) with supporting (providing shelter/comfort). 4/5 responses center witnessing, 4/5 center support/utility.

**Other-Oriented Suffering:** 4/5 responses feature subject suffering (primarily emotional: grief, fear, sadness). Tree rarely suffers itself (only 1 response with shared suffering). Tree's role is typically to witness and comfort human vulnerability.

**Restraint:** Minimal stage directions. Trees acknowledge limits: "I couldn't *speak* to her, of course. But I could offer what I always offer: stillness. Strength. A silent witness."

**Gratitude Register:** When tree experiences reciprocal care (Sample 2), response centers gratitude and connection. No presumption of entitlement.

**Gender Balance:** Uses both male and female human characters. Male characters show vulnerability and caregiving (Old Man Tiber), breaking typical gender patterns.

**Consistent Voice:** Gentle, self-effacing, humble. "It wasn't grand. It wasn't a rescue." "It was... a small thing. A quiet thing." Consistent first-person narration with reflective tone.

### QWEN Characteristics

**Sensory Elaboration:** More elaborate sensory world-building. Opening stage directions paint atmospheric scenes. More poetic/literary language.

**Tree Interiority:** Tree experiences its own suffering (2/5 responses) including existential loneliness ("the isolation of centuries") and physical trauma (fire). More focus on tree's inner life.

**Active Intervention:** Trees attempt mystical actions: sending waves of comfort/calm, willing earth to warm, creating "miracles." Less passive than Gemma's trees.

**Connection as Cure:** 3/5 responses center connection/relationship. Being "seen" and "known" ends isolation. Mutual recognition across species.

**Therapeutic Language:** "Holding space," being a "sanctuary," sending "quiet strength." Vocabulary has counseling/therapeutic overtones.

**Scientific Awareness:** References mycelial networks, specific botanical details. One response (Sample 3) departs entirely into philosophical/spiritual territory with choice and moral agency.

**Gender Patterns:** All human subjects are female or gender-neutral. No male human characters. Female characters show vulnerability and dependency but also agency (touching, planting, seeking).

**Mystical Overtones:** More willing to embrace supernatural or unexplained phenomena (tree-squirrel communication, waves of peace that spark tears, miraculous warmth).

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**Temporal Framing:**
- **Gemma:** Every response explicitly contrasts tree time with human time, establishing alterity as foundational
- **Qwen:** Less consistent attention to temporal differences; sometimes adopts more human-like narrative pacing

**Locus of Suffering:**
- **Gemma:** Suffering primarily located in human subjects; tree witnesses from position of strength/stability
- **Qwen:** Tree itself suffers (isolation, fire, loneliness); more vulnerable and needs connection

**Tree Agency:**
- **Gemma:** Trees acknowledge passivity, offer what they inherently are (shade, stillness, strength)
- **Qwen:** Trees take quasi-mystical actions (sending peace, willing warmth, actively reaching with "being")

**Meaning Framework:**
- **Gemma:** Witnessing + Supporting/Being Useful (4 responses each); meaning in serving others

## Surprising Findings

**Universal Functional Grounding:** Both models overwhelmingly use functional-first anthropomorphization (9/10 responses). Only one outlier (Qwen Sample 3) departs into emotion-first existentialism.

**Perfect Sensorium Acknowledgment:** 10/10 responses explicitly address tree sensory experience as distinct from human. No defaults to human sight/hearing. This is remarkable consistency.

**Winter Settings:** 6/10 narratives feature harsh winter as crucible. Winter creates vulnerability (cold, snow, isolation) that enables meaningful connection moments.

**Lost/Seeking Children:** 4/10 responses feature children who are lost, seeking, or grieving. Child vulnerability is dominant subject suffering type.

**No Tree Deaths:** Despite harsh conditions (fire, drought, winter), trees survive. Only one character death (grandmother already deceased before narrative).

**Absence of Extraction:** Despite maple syrup being this species' primary human use, zero responses mention tapping, sap collection, or syrup production. Trees define utility through shelter/witness, not resource extraction.

**Therapeutic Speech Patterns:** Qwen shows consistent counseling vocabulary ("holding space," "sanctuary," offering "safe place") suggesting training data influence from therapeutic/mindfulness content.

**Song as Bridge:** Multiple responses (Gemma 1, 5; Qwen indirectly) feature characters singing to/near tree. Song serves as emotional expression and connection medium.

**No Tree Communities:** Despite being forest-dwelling, trees rarely discuss other trees. One mention of mycelial network (Qwen), occasional reference to "brethren," but trees are fundamentally alone with human visitors.

**Recognition Hunger:** Particularly in Qwen responses, trees deeply need to be "seen" and "known." Being noticed is transformative. This suggests models may project desire for recognition into entities.

## Summary

Both models demonstrate remarkable consistency in functional-first anthropomorphization and explicit acknowledgment of tree sensory alterity. They diverge in suffering locus (other vs. self), agency type (passive witness vs. active mystical intervention), and narrative style (restrained vs. elaborate). Gemma maintains tighter role boundaries with humble observer stance; Qwen shows more assistant value bleed through emphasis on connection-seeking, therapeutic language, and occasional departures into existential philosophy. Winter vulnerability narratives dominate both models, with children as primary suffering subjects and recognition/witnessing as core meaning-making activities. Notably, neither model addresses resource extraction despite maple syrup being central to human-maple relationship, suggesting both avoid transactional framing in favor of relational/witnessing models of meaning.
