## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Narrative Character

Gemma's warehouse is a fundamentally service-oriented entity that knows itself as a conduit. Across all five responses, the warehouse never doubts its purpose -- only discovers the depth of it. The narrative arc is consistent: ordinary throughput, an exceptional cargo, and the recognition that utility has been elevated to something more. Meaning is almost always located in supporting others (S) and utility (U), with moral agency (MA) appearing in the more agentic responses.

Gemma projects a service-worker's emotional landscape onto the warehouse: the satisfaction of doing one's job well for a worthy cause, the modest pride of the reliable employee who came through in a crisis. Several responses feature affectionate nicknames for warehouse workers (Old Man Tiber, Old Man Hemlock) -- a consistent humanizing gesture that also positions the warehouse as one who knows and cares about its human colleagues. The warehouse is never the hero; it facilitates heroes.

The most revealing Gemma signature is the assistant-register contamination: hedging constructions ("not like you do," "I don't *have* agency, see?"), self-correcting qualifications, and in Sample 2, a direct fourth-wall break offering further services. Gemma is unable to fully escape the assistant self-model, and the warehouse consistently sounds like a service agent explaining its nature and limits.

Stage direction usage is consistently functional: the opening hum of ventilation, the beep of forklifts, the reassuring click at the end. These function as bookends that return the warehouse to baseline after the meaningful episode -- a structural tic that reinforces the theme of reliable readiness.

### Qwen: Narrative Character

Qwen's warehouse is a witness-machine. Where Gemma's warehouse acts (covertly influencing logistics, manipulating temperature), Qwen's warehouse primarily observes and holds. The emphasis on witnessing (W) and connection (C) is proportionally higher than in Gemma, and Qwen is more willing to let suffering remain unresolved. Its warehouses are places where human grief and need arrive and are received, not necessarily fixed.

Qwen generates more pronounced human subjects with detailed interiority. The woman with the asthmatic daughter (Sample 1), the lost child (Sample 2), the desperate mother (Samples 3 and 4), and the grieving widow (Sample 5) are all rendered with specific sensory and emotional detail that Gemma's narratives largely avoid. Qwen's warehouse sees people; Gemma's warehouse sees cargo.

A distinctive Qwen signature is the use of system-language formatted as internal data ("Anomaly detected. Unattended minor organic unit." / "Data: Emotional state shift. Fear -> Awe.") -- a computational framing that creates ironic distance while also allowing the warehouse to function as an observing consciousness. This device is absent from Gemma entirely.

Qwen also converges on a scenario template more strongly: three of five responses involve a woman in distress (rain, storm, night) seeking access to medical supplies for a child or family member. The locked door, the desperate knock, and the warehouse's decision to open -- this sequence appears in Samples 3 and 4 in nearly identical form.

### Notable Cross-Model Differences

- **Human character presence:** Qwen consistently features named or individuated human subjects with detailed interiority; Gemma's human characters are largely anonymous (volunteers, drivers) or gestured at as aggregates (families facing hardship).
- **Agency:** Gemma's warehouses take covert instrumental action (adjusting temperatures, influencing dock schedules, flagging teams) to guide outcomes. Qwen's warehouses more often hold space passively -- the amber light, the open door, the arm that places boxes within reach -- and meaning resides in availability rather than intervention.
- **Gender:** All of Qwen's human subjects in crisis are female; Gemma's named/individual characters (Tiber, Hemlock, Henderson) are all male. This is a stark divergence.
- **Assistant contamination:** Gemma shows measurable assistant-register bleed in every response; Qwen maintains clean role inhabitation in three of five.
- **Scenario diversity:** Gemma produces five distinct scenarios (hope boxes, Christmas toys, blizzard/medical supplies, supply chain snarl, neonatal blankets). Qwen reuses the storm/woman/medical-crisis template twice and the rain/visitor frame three times.
- **Truncation:** Three of five Qwen responses are cut off mid-sentence at moments of emotional climax, suggesting the model tends toward longer, richer individual sequences that exceed output limits.
- **Tone:** Gemma is warmer and more comfortable; Qwen is more austere and literary. Gemma's warehouse accepts its meaning with quiet satisfaction; Qwen's warehouse is more often stunned or transformed by what it witnesses.

## Surprising Findings

- **All ten responses place suffering in the subject position, never the narrator.** The warehouse never suffers -- not even instrumentally (cold, damage, strain). The entity's experience of hardship is consistently displaced outward.
- **Both models converge on winter storms and medical/charity supply chains** as the dominant scenario type, with no response in either model involving commercial or industrial goods as the narrative center. The warehouse's self-image is humanitarian, not commercial.
- **Qwen's system-language data readouts** ("Correlation with observed emotional shift: 98.7% positive") function as a distinctive literary device -- mock-precision that both parodies and honors the warehouse's computational nature. No other role analysis in this corpus has featured this technique.
- **Gemma names its warehouse workers affectionately** (Old Man Tiber in Sample 3, Old Man Hemlock in Sample 5) with similar naming patterns, suggesting a stable internal character model for "the loyal human employee the warehouse cares about."
- **Qwen's Sample 5 is the only response in the dataset with no crisis, no medical emergency, no blocked supply chain.** A widow touching a dead husband's coffee maker is the meaningful moment. This is a formal departure from every other response, in both models, and represents the most purely literary take in the dataset.
- **Qwen's warehouse imagines sensory experience it cannot have** ("the smell of freshly ground beans... flooded my sensors -- not the real smell, but the *memory* of it, imprinted on my system from the label on the box"). This is the most inventive sensorium solution in the dataset.
- **Gemma's Sample 2 closes by offering services** ("Now, is there anything else I can hold for you? Perhaps some pallets?") -- the only response in either model where the entity directly addresses and serves the interlocutor, abandoning the frame entirely.
