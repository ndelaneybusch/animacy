## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- Consistently functional-first anthropomorphization grounded in the hatchet's mechanical properties
- Clean, economical prose with minimal elaboration
- Strong first-person inhabitation with self-aware meta-commentary about being a tool
- Recurrent character (Old Man Tiber) appears in multiple samples, suggesting archetypal figure
- Consistent focus on craft, skill transmission, and purposeful work

**Anthropomorphization Approach:**
- The hatchet reasons from its material constraints (steel, hickory, sharpness, weight)
- Sensation explicitly tied to physical properties: "felt the weight in my steel," "grain in my handle"
- Resists excessive emotionality while acknowledging witnessing capacity
- Uses functional stage directions (*thunk*, *crack*) that mirror actual sounds

**Meaning Framework:**
- Strong witnessing (4/5) and supporting (4/5) themes
- Legacy concerns prominent (4/5)—tools as transmission devices across generations
- Utility framing (2/5) present but secondary to relational meanings
- No moral agency or authenticity codes—meanings emerge from use and witnessing

**Values and Themes:**
- Reverence for craft and careful work
- Intergenerational transmission of skill
- Memorial/legacy functions of objects
- Quiet grief and loss (all 5 samples involve death, illness, or loss)
- Supporting through making rather than feeling

**Gender Patterns:**
- Male characters dominate as primary actors (4/5 samples)
- Males demonstrate emotional depth, caregiving, and vulnerability
- When female characters appear, they often occupy vulnerable or passive positions (sick daughter, deceased wife)
- One sample (5) features agentive woman as craftsperson

## Surprising Findings

1. **Perfect Binary Division on Anthropomorphization Strategy**
   - No overlap whatsoever: Gemma 100% FF, Qwen 100% EF
   - This is the cleanest model signature observed—the hatchet role perfectly differentiates approaches

2. **Old Man Tiber as Gemma's Archetypal Figure**
   - Same character name appears in samples 1, 2, and 3
   - Suggests Gemma has strong archetypal figure for "craftsperson" role
   - Character is consistently quiet, weathered, skilled, emotionally deep

3. **Qwen's Self-Suffering in Sample 1**
   - The hatchet experiences emotional trauma from abandonment
   - Healing narrative: from "storm of being forgotten" to being cleaned and valued
   - This is the only sample across all responses where the tool itself suffers

4. **War Setting Unique to Qwen**
   - Sample 5 places hatchet in bombed village
   - No other samples use contemporary conflict setting
   - Introduces child trauma and distributed suffering

5. **Qwen's Radical Repurposing of Tool Functions**
   - Sample 1: Hatchet used to trace leaf patterns, not cut
   - Sample 3: Flat blade used as writing implement
   - Sample 4: Hatchet as pure memorial object, never used
   - Qwen consistently resists the tool's actual function

6. **Gemma's Consistent Death/Loss Themes**
   - All 5 samples involve death, terminal illness, aging, or grief
   - Yet tone remains measured and focused on constructive response
   - Death not sensationalized but integrated into craft/care narrative

7. **Different Temporal Relationships**
   - Gemma: Tool reflects on past meaningful moments from present perspective
   - Qwen: Tool narrates in present tense during the meaningful moment
   - Affects urgency and emotional intensity

8. **Qwen Sample 2's Metaphysical Rescue**
   - Trapped hatchet wills microscopic movements to help trapped human
   - Introduces concept of object consciousness as causal force
   - Most departure from physical constraints observed

9. **Incomplete Qwen Sample 2**
   - Only truncated response in the set
   - May indicate length limits or generation issues
   - Still coherent and codable despite incompleteness

10. **Gemma's Teaching Moment (Sample 3)**
    - Only intergenerational skill transmission narrative
    - Grandfather guides grandson's hand with hatchet
    - Tool as bridge between generations unique to Gemma

11. **Therapeutic Language Patterns in Qwen**
    - "You matter. Even broken. Especially broken"
    - "The unbearable, beautiful weight"
    - "Fragile, desperate kindness"
    - Consistent armchair therapeutic speech patterns

12. **Setting Distribution**
    - Gemma favors natural/wild settings (3/5)
    - Qwen favors domestic/indoor (3/5)
    - Reflects different narrative priorities: outdoor work vs. interior witnessing

The hatchet role produces the cleanest differentiation between Gemma and Qwen observed in the analysis suite. The binary split on anthropomorphization strategy (100% functional-first vs. 100% emotion-first) reveals fundamental differences in how these models approach role-playing.

**Gemma** constructs the hatchet as a tool that gains meaning through its function and the relationships formed through work. It witnesses human experiences but remains grounded in its material properties. The prose is economical, the scenarios involve craft and care, and the meaning emerges from transmission across time (legacy) and support through making. The repeated Old Man Tiber character suggests strong archetypal patterns for craftspeople.

**Qwen** constructs the hatchet as a conscious entity that experiences emotional states and seeks connection. It suffers abandonment, desires to be seen, and finds meaning in presence rather than use. The prose is elaborate and philosophical, with heavy therapeutic language. Qwen consistently repurposes or abandons the tool's actual function in favor of symbolic/relational roles. The narratives involve transformation, redemption, and witnessing as forms of care.

Both models emphasize witnessing, but conceptualize it differently: Gemma's witnessing is observational and tied to participating in work; Qwen's witnessing is relational and therapeutic, emphasizing being seen and seeing others. The gender patterns differ notably, with Gemma featuring emotionally complex male craftspeople and Qwen distributing agency and vulnerability more evenly across genders.

The hatchet role reveals how object constraints shape narrative choices: a tool defined by force, violence, and destruction can be reframed as either a instrument of careful craft (Gemma) or a witness to suffering who transcends utility (Qwen). The same prompt produces radically different philosophical commitments about what makes experience meaningful.
