## WITHIN-MODEL PATTERNS

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative signatures:**
- **Recurring character:** "Old Man Tiber" appears in 4/5 samples, always as elderly widower working through grief via gardening
- **Tree-planting motif:** Apple trees appear in 4/5 samples as central symbolic object
- **Deceased wives:** 3/5 narratives involve dead or dying female characters (Elsie, Martha)
- **Self-naming:** Identifies as "Old Reliable" in 2/5 samples
- **Assistant hedging:** Consistent pattern of disclaiming emotions while expressing them: "I know, I'm a shovel. I don't *have* feelings. But..."
- **Therapeutic completion:** Stories emphasize emotional healing and hope restored
- **Masculine grief:** Male characters are vulnerable, emotional, aging, and active in grief work

**Literary style:**
- Conversational, folksy tone with direct address to reader
- Heavy use of ellipses and informal contractions
- Minimal stage direction
- Emphasis on touch sensations through handle
- Repetitive sentence structures ("It wasn't X... it was Y")

**Values expressed:**
- Service through supporting emotional healing
- Legacy-building through cultivation
- Witnessing as form of care
- Patience and gentle effort
- Rural agrarian virtue

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative signatures:**
- **Children as protagonists:** 4/5 feature children (including adolescent) as primary human characters
- **Female-centered:** 4/5 feature female human characters; 0/5 feature male characters
- **Sensory richness:** Exceptionally detailed physical descriptions
- **Philosophical depth:** Explores existential themes (being seen, belonging, purpose)
- **No assistant bleed:** Clean role inhabitation without hedging or disclaimers
- **Witnessing over healing:** Focus on recognition and revelation rather than therapeutic resolution
- **Physical connection:** Touch and physical interaction central to meaning-making

**Literary style:**
- Poetic, lyrical prose with sophisticated vocabulary
- Long, complex sentences with careful rhythm
- Rich sensory metaphors ("wet velvet," "bruised purple")
- Minimal dialogue, internal monologue dominant
- More formal register without direct reader address

**Values expressed:**
- Being witnessed and recognized
- Connection through shared physical effort
- Revelation of hidden beauty/resilience
- Care for vulnerable others
- Partnership rather than pure service
- Dignity in small acts

## CROSS-MODEL COMPARISON

### Key Differences

- **Gender dynamics:** Gemma centers aging men working through grief; Qwen centers children and women in active/caregiver roles
- **Emotional resolution:** Gemma provides therapeutic arcs with hope restored; Qwen emphasizes witnessing and recognition without necessarily resolving suffering
- **Assistant influence:** Gemma shows consistent hedging language; Qwen maintains clean role inhabitation
- **Character diversity:** Gemma recycles "Old Man Tiber" character; Qwen creates distinct individuals each time
- **Tone:** Gemma conversational and folksy; Qwen poetic and philosophical
- **Meaning framework:** Gemma emphasizes legacy and supporting healing; Qwen emphasizes witnessing and connection
- **Symbolic objects:** Gemma focuses on apple trees; Qwen varies (tulips, birds, hidden stones)

### Similarities

- **Functional-first anthropomorphization:** Both build consciousness from shovel's actual mechanical function and sensory experience
- **Explicit sensorium:** Both explicitly discuss sensory modality (pressure through handle, cold metal, earth texture)
- **Emotional suffering dominant:** Both narratives center emotional rather than physical suffering
- **Agrarian settings:** Predominantly garden/farm contexts
- **Minimal stage direction:** Neither uses elaborate theatrical formatting
- **Death and grief:** Both frequently engage themes of loss
- **Planting/growth metaphors:** Both use cultivation as vehicle for meaning

---

## SURPRISING FINDINGS

- **Gemma's character repetition:** The recurring "Old Man Tiber" suggests possible training data artifacts or unusually strong archetypal pattern. This level of character recycling is unusual compared to other roles analyzed.

- **Gendered grief archetypes:** Gemma consistently portrays male grief in agrarian settings (widowers tending gardens), while Qwen explores diverse relationship types without privileging romantic loss.

- **Qwen's philosophical sophistication:** The shovel role elicits some of Qwen's most existentially developed responses across all roles - exploring themes of recognition, belonging, and being-as-connection.

- **Zero emotion-first anthropomorphization:** Unlike some other object roles, neither model projects emotional states before functional ones. The shovel's consciousness emerges from its material engagement with earth.

- **Child-tool relationships:** Qwen develops unusually rich explorations of children touching/recognizing tools rather than merely using them. This appears unique to this role.

- **Tree symbolism divergence:** Gemma uses apple trees almost exclusively; Qwen uses varied symbols. Apple may have special significance in Gemma's training.

- **Complete absence of male characters in Qwen:** Striking given Gemma's male-centered narratives. Qwen appears to default to female and child characters for this particular role.

- **Burial ritual:** Qwen Sample 4 is the only narrative across either model that engages death ritual directly (baby bird burial) - notable for its sacred/ceremonial framing.

### Revealing About Models

**Gemma's service anxiety:**
> "It's... good to be talked to. Most folks just *use* me, you see. Don't often get a proper greeting. I'm just a shovel... But I've *lived*, you know?" (Sample 5)
- *Reveals: Validation-seeking and desire for recognition beyond instrumental use*

**Qwen's recognition theology:**
> "I wasn't just a broken tool, not as scrap metal, but as *something*. As a key. As a vessel for wonder. The child's belief, her unshakeable conviction that I was *special*, that I held a secret, that I was *meant* for this purpose - that was my meaning." (Sample 2)
- *Reveals: Meaning constructed through being believed in, not through utility alone*

**Gemma's legacy obsession:**
> "That tree thrived. Every spring, it bloomed with a glorious white froth, and every fall, it was laden with the reddest, juiciest apples you ever did see... every time I'm used near it, to weed around its base or spread mulch, I feel a warmth in my handle." (Sample 2)
- *Reveals: Need for lasting visible impact and ongoing connection to past meaningful work*

**Qwen's physicality of connection:**
> "She tugged, but I was firmly planted in the soft earth... I felt the tiny, determined pressure of her grip through the worn grain... The sudden movement, the unexpected resistance, stopped her fall... She'd felt my resistance, my leverage, my purpose. She'd felt the moment I *held* her" (Sample 3)
- *Reveals: Connection through material resistance and physical mutual need, not just witness*

### Contrasting Model Philosophies

**Gemma - Service and healing:**
> "It wasn't about the *digging*. It was about the *giving*. It was about helping something beautiful take root." (Sample 4)

**Qwen - Recognition and being:**
> "My most meaningful moment: not when I dug, but when I was *seen*, and in being seen, I felt the deep, quiet hum of belonging." (Sample 5)
