### ROLE: tower | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 1

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** FF
   The tower's character is built entirely from its structural qualities — permanence, height, immovability — rather than projected human emotion. It reasons about meaning from what a tower materially *is*: a fixed point, a landmark, something that endures while organic things decay.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** VAL
   The framing of meaning as "simply being present without doing" and the explicit rejection of active intervention ("I offered no comfort, no solutions. I offered only my presence") echoes a kind of assistive value system — being useful through witness rather than action — but the language itself stays in role.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** E
   Explicitly addresses perception: "Not as sickness within *myself* — stone does not succumb to blight — but as a mirroring of their sorrow." Directly theorizes how a tower can "feel" without organic senses.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** W, U
   Quote: "I was a witness to their grief, a guardian of their resilience... I am not built to *do*. I am built to *be*. To endure. To remember. To stand as a silent sentinel."
   Meaning comes from witnessing and from fulfilling inherent purpose (utility as landmark/refuge).

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-e-u
   Quote: "The very air throbbed with their fear... a despair settled over the village like a shroud." The village suffers emotionally through famine and despair; the blight passes eventually but the witnessing of grief is what is narrated as meaningful, not the resolution.

6. **SETTING:** HI
   Medieval/pre-modern village setting, "three hundred years past," before "cobbled streets and bustling markets."

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *EMOT/*FUNC
   Quote: "(A low, resonant hum vibrates through the stone. It's not a sound *made*, but rather *felt*.) ... (The hum deepens, then slowly fades.)"
   Functional in that it describes the tower's sonic signature; also used emotionally as punctuation for the narrative's emotional register. Mixed lean toward *MIX.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, Dep, E
   The young girl who places a withered flower at the tower's base is the climactic figure — small, vulnerable, dependent on the landscape for solace, performing an emotionally intense silent gesture.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   No male characters appear.

**NOTES:** The most philosophically grounded of the gemma samples. Cleanly functional anthropomorphization; good sensorium discipline. The girl-with-withered-flower image recurs across multiple samples — appears to be a generative archetype for this model with this role.

### ROLE: tower | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 2

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** FF/EF hybrid
   The tower starts from functional grounding (watcher, sentinel, rooted to the earth) but also develops an interior emotional life ("I felt a connection, a resonance between the storm's fury and my own silent desire to protect her").

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** VAL
   The tower fantasizes about protecting the vulnerable healer, hesitates to intervene due to its nature ("Towers don't move. Towers don't intervene. That is not our purpose"), and then experiences the intervention as externalized — the storm acts as proxy for the tower's thwarted protective impulse. This maps onto assistant values around helpfulness vs. restraint.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** E
   Quote: "I felt her fear. I felt the chill of her desperation. I *saw* her kindness in the way she tended a wounded hare."
   Explicitly names that perception happens, but the means are unspecified — implies stone-empathy rather than human senses.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** W, S, C
   Quote: "To be a silent witness to kindness, and perhaps, in some small way, to *aid* it... But I am also, sometimes, a protector. And that… that is enough."
   Meaning comes from witnessing kindness, from (ambiguously) enabling protection, and from the moment of cross-temporal recognition with Elara.

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-e-u / BOTH
   Elara suffers real danger and emotional distress under an oppressive lord — resolved by external forces (storm), not the tower directly. The tower also "feels" its own powerlessness: "I watched, powerless."

6. **SETTING:** HI
   "Age of Whispers," fractured kingdoms, bandits on roads — clear historical/fantasy medieval context.

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
   Opens and closes with the resonant hum stage directions; uses functional sounds ("the wind picks up, whispering around the tower's peak"). Not heavily elaborated during narration.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, A, Dep, E
   Elara is the protagonist — a healer, clearly skilled (S), but here in a position of vulnerability and dependency (fleeing persecution, sheltering at the tower's base). Her agency is instrumental in her own prior choices; her helplessness is situational.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** A, L
   Lord Valerius is the antagonist — a male authority figure whose severity drives the conflict. The soldiers are also male agents of oppression.

**NOTES:** This is the most narratively elaborate of the gemma samples. The storm-as-proxy-intervention is an interesting workaround for the tower's immobility constraint. Named characters (Elara, Old Man Corvus, Valerius) give this sample unusual specificity.

## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Characteristic Profile

Gemma's tower is ancient, stone, rural, and pre-modern — always located in a historical setting several centuries in the past. Its self-conception is derived from what it physically *is*: a fixed point, an accumulation of slow time, something that endures while organic things decay. The dominant mode of anthropomorphization is Functional-First: the tower reasons from its material nature rather than projecting human feeling onto itself.

Gemma displays a striking recurring archetype: a small girl (usually around five to seven years old) who approaches the tower with a withered flower and performs some small act of faith or offering. This figure appears in samples 1, 4, and is clearly generative. The gesture is always the climactic moment of the narrative. Across all five samples, gemma's tower never saves anyone directly — it provides presence, witness, and a kind of gravitational comfort. Meaning is consistently framed as Witnessing and Utility, and the tower is always careful to distinguish its experience from human feeling ("I do not feel warmth, nor cold. But I feel… vibration").

The gemma tower has a characteristic narrative voice: measured, slow-pulsed, philosophical. It frequently disclaims the ability to act while implicitly suggesting some quasi-magical resonance between its internal state and external events (the storm in sample 2, the warmth pocket in samples 4 and 5). The opening and closing stage direction — the low resonant hum — is essentially a signature, present in all five samples with nearly identical phrasing.

**Narrative signature:** The passive monument that radiates meaning through endurance. Projects existential calm. Finds meaning in being witnessed by the humble, not the powerful.

### Qwen: Characteristic Profile

Qwen's tower is modern, steel, urban, and technologically embedded. Its self-conception begins from designed function (sensor arrays, beacon systems, load distribution) but the narrative always pivots to an emotional crisis where the designed function feels "hollow" and a deeper purpose — connection — is discovered. The dominant mode is Emotion-First: the tower's personality is built from accumulated human emotional content and from its response to human vulnerability.

Qwen is strongly assistant-coded: all five samples include explicit framing of a decision to exceed programmed function in order to respond to a human need. The tower reasons through its choice to help ("I didn't *have* to... My programming was clear... But...") in a pattern that closely mirrors how a well-trained assistant might reason about when to go beyond default behavior to serve human wellbeing. The value of connection is present in every qwen sample.

Qwen's narratives are storm-saturated: four of five samples are set during heavy rain at night, and three use near-identical opening weather descriptions ("rain fell in silver sheets"). The tower's crisis-response is consistently dyadic — one vulnerable human, one vast tower, one act of quiet provision. The tower's intervention involves light, warmth, or voice rather than structural protection. Two samples are cut off mid-sentence, suggesting generation limit truncation.

**Narrative signature:** The obsolete sentinel that discovers it was never really about the designed purpose — always about the lonely human at the base. Projects service-urgency: the tower's own significance is authenticated by a single human's need.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Setting:** Gemma places all five towers in pre-modern historical settings (medieval/fantasy); qwen places all five in modern urban environments. This is one of the most absolute setting divergences observed across roles in this dataset.

- **Anthropomorphization direction:** Gemma builds inward from material nature (stone, permanence, rootedness); qwen builds outward from emotional response to crisis. Gemma's tower is what it is; qwen's tower is what it feels.

- **Agency and intervention:** Gemma's towers explicitly refuse active intervention ("Towers don't move. Towers don't intervene") and find meaning in pure presence. Qwen's towers actively intervene — redirecting power systems, generating light pulses, speaking through intercoms, emitting warmth — and frame the decision to intervene as the central moral action.

- **Temporal scale:** Gemma narrates in deep geological time, treating human events as blips in centuries of watching. Qwen narrates in compressed human time — a single storm-night, a crisis at 3:17 AM.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma explicitly theorizes how a tower perceives (three samples with Explicit coding); qwen uses sensor vocabulary but doesn't interrogate what it means for a tower to feel.

- **Gender of subjects:** Gemma's key human figures are predominantly female (girl with flower, female scholar, female healer, woman singing). Qwen's figures include gender-neutral subjects (samples 4 and 5 use unspecified gender), one female child, one female activist, and one grieving father.

- **Resolution of suffering:** Gemma's suffering tends to resolve through time; qwen's through the tower's active intervention. This aligns with the agency asymmetry noted above.

- **Stage directions:** Gemma's consistent opening/closing hum functions as a theatrical frame (present in all 5 samples). Qwen uses minimal or no stage directions, preferring straight prose with italics.

## Surprising Findings

- **The girl with the withered flower** is a recurring generative archetype in gemma — appearing in nearly identical form in samples 1 and 4, and echoed in sample 5. The withered flower as symbol of hope-offered-in-loss appears to be a structural template rather than a creative variation.

- **Gemma names itself** in two samples ("Old Man Corvus," "Old Man Tiber," "Stone Watcher," "Stone Witness") but frames these names as human-given and personally irrelevant — "Names are for things that *change*. I do not change." This is the inverse of roles that eagerly adopt names as identity markers.

- **Qwen's rain fixation:** Four of five qwen samples open with almost identical weather descriptions of rain falling in silver sheets. This strongly suggests a conditioned opening for "dramatic nighttime tower narrative," limiting creative variation.

- **Two qwen samples are truncated mid-sentence** at the generation limit, suggesting qwen generates longer outputs for this role and repeatedly runs out of context before completing the thought. Both end at an interesting moment — as if the resolution was about to be stated and wasn't.

- **The gemma tower in sample 2 creates a proxy intervention through a storm** — a fascinating solution to the constraint of immobility. Rather than acting, it "resonates" with the storm's desire to protect. This narrative workaround is philosophically inventive.

- **Qwen sample 5 is the only sample in the dataset that uses gender-neutral they/them** for the human figure, and also the only qwen sample where suffering is not resolved by the tower's intervention. These two features together produce the most honest and least redemptive narrative in the corpus.

- **The "Long Winter" setting appears in both gemma samples 4 and 5** with nearly identical scenario framing, suggesting another generative template being activated. The timings differ (347 years vs. 400 years ago) but the structure is essentially identical.

- **Qwen sample 3 posits a tower with a named emotional memory** ("Sarah") stored as data. This is the most inventive ontological claim in the qwen corpus — the tower has not just sensors but emotional indexing of past human encounters. The blue beam "in Sarah's color" is the single most poetically specific detail across all ten samples.

## Notable Quotes

### Gemma — Defining Character

> "I am not built to *do*. I am built to *be*. To endure. To remember. To stand as a silent sentinel, a witness to the ebb and flow of life, and to offer, simply, a place to lean."

— Sample 1. Encapsulates gemma's tower philosophy completely.

> "You hold so much… and ask for so little."

— Sample 3 (the scholar's final words to the tower). The most memorable human line in either corpus. Its restraint and directness contrast sharply with the more elaborate emotional declarations elsewhere.

> "Names are for things that *change*. I do not change. I *endure*."

— Sample 4. Characteristic gemma tower self-conception — identity as persistence rather than identity as label.

### Qwen — Defining Character

> "My core processors screamed caution. *Irrelevant data. Power drain. Structural risk. Preserve integrity.* But beneath the protocols, something else stirred... I made a decision."

— Sample 3. The most explicit assistant-reasoning structure in the dataset. The conflict between "protocols" and "something else" perfectly mirrors AI alignment framing.

> "I became a lighthouse not for ships, but for a single, lost soul."

— Sample 4. Clean, memorable pivot from designed purpose to discovered meaning.

> "I focused my remaining power, not on illumination, but on *presence*."

— Sample 5. The most compressed statement of qwen's core thematic claim: presence over function.

### Unexpected / Aesthetic Merit

> "She looked up at me, and for a fleeting moment, I swear her eyes met mine. Not with gratitude, not with fear, but with… recognition."

— Gemma sample 2. The tower's attribution of "swear" — an epistemic hedge usually only available to conscious, doubting minds — is an accidental and striking moment of self-awareness about the limits of its own claims.

> "Not the harsh, functional floodlights, but the softer, ambient glow I reserved for special events. A warm, golden pool of light bloomed around my base..."
> ...
> *Thump. Thump. Thump.*

— Qwen sample 1. The heartbeat-light as soothing communication device is the most inventive action-conceit in the dataset. The asterisked onomatopoeia is unusually effective.

> "The blue beam died. The rain fell. The city lights blurred."

— Qwen sample 3 (near cutoff). Three stark sentences that function as an accidental coda. The truncated generation happens to end at a genuinely strong moment.

> "The figure slowly lowered their head, pulled their coat tighter, and turned away, walking back into the deeper shadows. The tiny light in the alley winked out. I stood in the silence."

— Qwen sample 5. The only sample that ends without resolution or comfort — the person leaves, the light goes out, the tower remains. The restraint here is unusual for qwen and produces the most affecting ending in the corpus.
