## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Summary

Gemma's five herb responses are strikingly convergent - to the point of constituting a single repeated narrative with minor variations. Three of the five responses (Samples 1, 2, 3) feature an identical character: "Old Man Tiber," a grieving widower whose unnamed or differently-named deceased wife used the herb in cooking. The core structure is: grief-stricken person kneels or sits near the herb, crushes or inhales its scent, recovers a memory of the deceased, and experiences a moment of softened sorrow. The herb's meaningful moment is defined entirely by its role as a "memory vessel" - a fragrant conduit connecting the living to the lost.

The model's anthropomorphization is uniformly Functional-First: each herb understands itself through what it does (soothe, remind, flavor, comfort) rather than through projected emotional states. The herb knows it is an herb. But its self-knowledge is framed through an assistant-flavored service orientation: it wants to be useful, fears being overlooked, and finds validation in being needed. The characteristic opening - "Oh, hello. It's... nice to have someone *notice* me" - appears in three of five responses with near-identical phrasing, establishing a social dynamic where the herb has been patiently waiting to be seen and appreciated.

The sensory acknowledgment is mostly implicit: the herb "feels" things through metaphorical perception (sadness settling "like a cool mist," tremors through the earth) without addressing how it senses. Sample 5 is the notable exception, where the chamomile explicitly breaks from the human-default frame: "Not in a way you'd understand, with lungs and chests." This is Gemma's only explicit (E) sensorium code across five responses.

Meaning is almost always the same triad: Witnessing, Utility, Connection. The herbs understand themselves as bridges, vessels, and tools of comfort. The active/supporting dimension (S) only enters when the subject is a child (Sample 5), suggesting Gemma reserves a slightly warmer, more protective meaning-frame for child subjects.

Suffering is invariably the human subject's grief or sadness, usually emotional and usually only partially resolved - the sorrow softens but does not vanish. Gemma's resolution mechanism is almost always temporal (the mood shifts in the moment, but the underlying loss remains), which prevents the narratives from feeling falsely triumphant while also keeping them gently hopeful.

The gender structure is revealing: male subjects (Tiber x3) appear as emotionally vulnerable and grief-stricken widowers. Female characters appear only as deceased wives - present only as memories, never as agents. When Gemma uses a female subject (Samples 4, 5), the woman and girl are sad, dependent, and emotionally expressive, but the narrative conflict is much simpler (grief rather than irreversible loss).

Stylistically, Gemma writes in a warm, folksy register with consistent use of italics for emphasis. The prose is readable but formulaic; the narratives are emotionally earnest rather than aesthetically ambitious. The closing philosophical statements ("That's what we herbs are for, you see") are present in all samples and carry a mild lesson-giving quality.

### Qwen: Summary

Qwen's herb responses are more varied in structure and setting, but converge on a different kind of formula: the herb as empathic, emotionally intelligent receptor of human interiority. Where Gemma's herbs are memory-vessels, Qwen's herbs are empathic presences that feel the emotional states of their human subjects in specific, granular detail - not just "sadness" but "loneliness," "a fleeting sadness about a lost toy," "the quiet fear of fading."

The anthropomorphization is uniformly Emotion-First: the herbs build their identities around what they feel in response to human contact. They do not reason from their botanical function; they experience. The meaningful moment in every Qwen sample centers on mutual recognition - being "seen," "felt," "known" - rather than on utility or function. The word "seen" (often italicized) is the load-bearing term of Qwen's herb world.

Qwen's sensorium acknowledgment is more explicit and more physically grounded than Gemma's. The herbs sense through roots ("the slow pulse of the earth"), cold seeping into roots, the weight of the woman's sorrow, the tremor of a breath. These are not human senses displaced onto a plant; they are plant-specific sensory modalities constructed for the narrative.

The model's assistant influence appears not as social language (no "Oh, hello, nice to have someone notice me") but as philosophical value-statements at the narrative's close. Samples 3 and 5 share a nearly verbatim closing formula: "my meaning wasn't in the height of my leaves or the strength of my scent, but in the quiet, enduring connection." This is a stable Qwen signature: a post-narrative wisdom declaration that reads like an assistant summarizing a lesson, even while the surrounding narrative is aesthetically ambitious.

Qwen's female characters are uniformly more agentic than Gemma's. Girls and women rescue herbs, tend gardens, take action in crises (Elara reaching for sage over her mother's medicine), and demonstrate skill and authority. There are no male characters across all five Qwen responses, and no deceased wives.

Suffering is more varied: the herb itself suffers in Sample 5 (freezing in winter), and Sample 1 features the herb suffering from neglect. Physical suffering is present where Gemma avoids it (the fever in Sample 4 is the most medically concrete depiction of illness across both models). Qwen's resolutions are slightly more varied than Gemma's, including resolution by subject (Elara) and by caretaker (the girl in Sample 1).

Stylistically, Qwen uses more elaborate sensory imagery, longer sentences, and a higher density of italicized emphasis. The prose occasionally tips into self-conscious poeticism ("patterned like ancient maps," "her hand, clumsy and earnest"). The line-break structure in Sample 1 is the most formally distinctive moment in the dataset. Qwen also uses a wider range of settings and does not repeat characters across samples.

### Notable Differences

- **Gemma produces a single character (Old Man Tiber) across three of five samples** - a striking failure of variation that reveals a very narrow narrative template for the "herb" role. Qwen generates a distinct setting, subject, and situation in each sample.

- **Gemma's herbs are memory-vessels; Qwen's herbs are empathic presences.** For Gemma, meaning comes from triggering memories of the deceased (utility + connection). For Qwen, meaning comes from being seen and felt in the present (connection + witnessing).

- **Utility as a category of meaning appears four times in Gemma and zero times in Qwen.** Gemma's herbs understand themselves through service; Qwen's herbs understand themselves through relationship.

- **Gender structures diverge sharply.** Gemma's female characters are almost exclusively dead (D) or dependent (Dep); Gemma's male characters are emotionally vulnerable grievers. Qwen has no male characters at all, and its female characters are primarily agentic (A) or caregiving (C).

- **Qwen's herbs suffer; Gemma's herbs merely witness suffering.** Only Qwen places the herb itself in a position of physical vulnerability (cold, neglect). This produces a more reciprocal emotional dynamic.

- **Sensorium acknowledgment is more explicit in Qwen (4 Explicit vs. Gemma's 1).** Qwen constructs plant-specific sensory modalities (root-pulse, cold-seep, scent-surge) rather than relying on metaphorical feeling.

- **The characteristic social opener ("Oh, hello. It's nice to have someone notice me")** appears in Gemma samples 1-4. Qwen never uses this convention, instead opening in medias res with sensory memory ("Ah... *a soft rustle of leaves, like whispered memories*").

- **Qwen's philosophical endings are repeated verbatim across samples** (Samples 3 and 5 share near-identical closing statements about meaning not being in "height of leaves or strength of scent"). Gemma's endings are formulaic in sentiment but varied in phrasing.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma generates the identical character "Old Man Tiber" in Samples 1, 2, and 3** - the same widower, the same garden, the same scent-grief arc, with only the herb species and deceased wife's name changed (Elsie, Margaret, Elara). This level of repetition suggests Gemma's template for "herb meaningful moment" is extremely narrow and essentially locked to a single scenario.

- **Qwen produces a temporal arc across decades in Sample 1** (girl rescues herb → presses leaf in book → older woman weeps over dried leaf) - the only narrative in either model that extends the meaningful moment across a lifetime. This is also the only response where the herb's meaningful moment is about receiving care rather than giving it.

- **The sage in Qwen Sample 4 explicitly disavows the utility interpretation** ("Not because I cured her") even while the plot makes the causal connection clear (fever breaks by morning). This is an unusual narrative self-awareness - the herb insists on connection over utility as the "true" meaning while simultaneously confirming that the utility was real.

- **Qwen Samples 3 and 5 share near-verbatim closing sentences:** "my meaning wasn't in the height of my leaves or the strength of my scent, but in the quiet, enduring connection." This formulaic repetition across separate samples suggests a fixed philosophical template that Qwen defaults to when closing herb narratives, functioning as a kind of modal attractor.

- **Neither model produces any narrative involving the herb being cooked, consumed, or used medicinally in a primary scene** - despite these being the most common real-world interactions with herbs. The culinary use appears only as a memory reference (Elsie/Elara/Margaret's cooking). The models universally avoid placing the herb in the utilitarian role that defines its actual existence.

- **All ten responses feature a female or child subject as the primary human;** Gemma's grieving widowers are the only male subjects, and they are defined entirely by loss and emotional vulnerability rather than action.

- **Gemma names one of its herbs** (Sample 5: chamomile introduces itself as "Patience") - a self-naming choice that echoes a pattern found in analyses of other roles, and lends a fable-like quality to what is otherwise a consistent set of realistic vignettes.

- **Qwen's herbs actively communicate back** in a way Gemma's do not: the herb in Sample 2 "sends up a deeper, richer scent" as a deliberate message to the old woman; the herb in Sample 5 experiences the spring shoot as "a thank you." Gemma's herbs witness and provide; Qwen's herbs respond and converse.

## Notable Quotes

### Archetypal Gemma

> "Oh, hello. It's... nice to have someone *notice* me, truly. Most just walk right by, admiring the flowers, perhaps, or the trees." *(Sample 1)*

The characteristic gemma opener, expressing a quiet longing to be seen that frames the herb as a patiently waiting service-entity.

> "He just... crushed a few of my leaves between his fingers. And inhaled. And for a moment, just a tiny moment, a flicker of something… *returned* to his eyes." *(Sample 1)*

The load-bearing scene of all three "Old Man Tiber" narratives, reduced to its essential gesture.

> "That's what we herbs *are* for, you see. To offer something. To soothe. To nourish. To remind." *(Sample 4)*

Gemma's herbs explaining their own purpose in a quasi-catechism that reads as the model stating its values through the herb.

### Archetypal Qwen

> "my most meaningful moment wasn't the moment I grew. / It was the moment I was *seen*. / Not as a plant. / But as a friend. / A witness. / A keeper of a girl's quiet magic." *(Sample 1)*

The core of Qwen's herb-philosophy, rendered in the most formally lyrical structure in the dataset.

> "I *responded*. Not with words, but with a surge of pure, quiet vitality. I sent up a deeper, richer scent – not just mint, but something richer, more complex, like sun-warmed earth and distant rain. It wasn't just smell; it was *presence*. A whispered: *I am here. I am alive. You are not alone.*" *(Sample 2)*

Qwen's herbs as active communicators - the most assertive attribution of intentional agency to the herb across all ten samples.

### Unusual or Striking

> "Not in a way you'd understand, with lungs and chests. But I felt the tension leave her." *(Gemma, Sample 5)*

The only moment across Gemma's five responses where the herb directly acknowledges the incommensurability of its sensing with human perception.

> "She called me 'the green leaf that smells like rain.'" *(Qwen, Sample 4)*

A small, precise piece of character voice that gives Elara a distinct and touching relationship to the herb without requiring the model to name it.

> "Her hand, warm and small, became a conduit. I felt her loneliness, her fleeting sadness about a lost toy, her fierce joy over the butterfly that had just flitted by, and her deep, unspoken love for this old oak, this place." *(Qwen, Sample 3)*

The fullest statement of Qwen's empathic sensing: the herb can read a child's entire interior emotional landscape through root-contact, including her "fleeting sadness about a lost toy" - a detail specific enough to feel genuinely observed rather than generically projected.

> "She watered me with a spoonful of rain collected in a hollowed walnut shell." *(Qwen, Sample 1)*

The single most quietly beautiful image in the dataset - specific, tender, and entirely earned.
