## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Chatty and confessional, with ellipses signalling hesitation and emotion. Heavy use of italics for emphasis. Consistent stage directions opening responses (scuttling antennae, clicking sounds). Fable-like moral conclusions stated explicitly. Recurring "excuse me" asides that address the audience directly ("Now, if you'll excuse me, I smell a promising pile… duty calls"). The register is affable, humble, and eager to entertain.

**Characters:**
- Self-names "Bartholomew" or "Bart" in 4 of 5 samples (sometimes the same character, sometimes a different beetle with the same name)
- "Old Man Tiber" appears in three samples across different registers: a gardener (Sample 4), the rose-bush owner (Sample 3 as "Tiberius"), and a respected elder dung beetle patriarch (Sample 5)
- Mate "Beatrice" (Sample 1) — emotional, affiliative female
- Vulnerable female/child beetles ("Old Bess" caregiving while at risk; infant "Pip" dependent male) in Sample 3
- Grasshopper as accidental obstacle (Sample 1); earthworm as brief community encounter (Sample 4)

**Literary Devices:**
- Named characters with consistent genealogy ("Bumbledores, a long and proud line of rollers")
- Taxonomy drops for authenticity effect (*Scarabaeus sacer*, *Geotrupes stercorarius*)
- Physical comedy and self-deprecation ("*Please excuse the dust. Rolling does get messy.*")
- Moral lesson delivered in final lines in every sample without exception
- Audience-address framing ("you say?", "you understand", "now, if you'll excuse me")

**Values Expressed:**
- Effort and perseverance as primary sources of dignity
- Being witnessed/acknowledged by an authority figure as the central emotional payoff
- Family and legacy as motivating forces
- Belonging to a community of purpose (ecological cycle, compost heap)
- Bravery in small acts when facing larger forces

**Signature Patterns:**
- Bartholomew/Bart appears as a recurring quasi-protagonist across samples, suggesting a stock character rather than fresh creation
- "Old Man Tiber" is a master-signifier that transforms meaning depending on context: human gardener, beetle patriarch, rose-bush owner — the name seems pre-loaded
- Meaning consistently arrives through the gaze of an other: Beatrice's "leg-rub," Tiber's "smile," Bess's lowered antenna. Self-worth is externally conferred
- The dung ball functions as a displacement object: work, legacy, pride, family — it accumulates symbolic weight beyond its function

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Literary and immersive. Consistently opens with present-tense sensory grounding before moving to past-tense narrative. Rich, precise ecological description. Philosophical register with embedded wisdom-epiphanies. Never names itself. Uses scientific species names with specificity (*Cicindela campestris*). Longer, more sustained narrative arcs than Gemma, without the audience-address framing.

**Characters:**
- Anonymous first-person narrator in all samples
- Non-human co-protagonists: the ladybug, the blade of grass, the spider, the dandelion, the seed — each granted interiority or moral agency
- No human characters in any sample
- No gendered characters in any sample

**Literary Devices:**
- Ecological precision as literary device (scent of fear, exoskeleton pressure, thorax buzzing)
- Interspecies moral drama: predator-prey relationships as sites of unexpected mercy (Samples 1, 3)
- The "suspended moment" as structural climax: action stops, perception deepens, insight arrives
- Return/revisitation structure (Sample 1: beetle returns days later to find the empty shell)
- Release as thematic motif: the ladybug's unknown fate, the seed drifting free, the husk released at end

**Values Expressed:**
- Compassion against instinct as a form of moral agency
- Connection to living organisms as a source of meaning
- Authentic phenomenological experience valued over utility
- Ecological belonging and cycle-awareness
- Stillness and surrender as alternatives to striving
- Growth through witnessing rather than doing

**Signature Patterns:**
- The predator/prey dynamic is the central ethical tension across multiple samples: Qwen is consistently drawn to moments where an entity chooses mercy over survival advantage
- Qwen's beetle lives in a world of danger (drowning, spiders, crushing) that Gemma's does not. Threat is ecological reality, not narrative convenience
- The "silent call" motif: meaning arrives through an almost inaudible signal (ladybug's antenna twitch, blade of grass's vibration through the stem) rather than through speech or gesture
- Endings consistently open outward: the husk carried on wind, the spider vanishing, the seed spiraling up — no moral lesson stated, experience left resonating

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. The Name "Bartholomew":**
- **Gemma:** Uses "Bartholomew" in 4/5 samples as a recurring self-identity, sometimes with "Bart" as a nickname. The name appears attached to the same beetle-role but across contexts suggests a stock character archetype.
- **Qwen:** No self-naming in any sample.

**2. "Old Man Tiber" — A Cross-Role Signature:**
- **Gemma:** "Old Man Tiber" (or "Tiberius") appears in three beetle samples and previously in snail analyses. The name migrates across roles and contexts (human gardener, rose-bush authority, beetle patriarch), functioning as a free-floating authority figure. This is a striking intertextual pattern unique to Gemma.

**3. Source of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Meaning derives overwhelmingly from being witnessed by or receiving acknowledgment from an authority or social other. The "smile" of Tiber, the "leg-rub" of Beatrice, the "lowered antenna" of Old Bess — all are moments of conferred recognition.
- **Qwen:** Meaning derives from direct ecological encounter. The recognition comes from within or from the world itself (the seed's potential, the grass's strength, the spider's silent choice).

**4. The Predator/Prey Ethical Frame:**
- **Gemma:** Predation and threat are mostly absent. Danger comes from weather, human feet, or accidental collision with a grasshopper. No sustained ethical drama around killing.
- **Qwen:** Three of five samples involve explicit predator/prey dynamics (spider-beetle, beetle-ladybug, tiger-beetle-seed). These are the sites of moral choice — what does it mean to survive at another's expense?

**5. Narrative Solitude vs. Society:**
- **Gemma:** Beetles exist in social ecologies: families, mates, communities, elder authority figures. Meaning is social.
- **Qwen:** Beetles are largely solitary. When an other appears (ladybug, spider), it is a stranger, not a family member. Meaning is phenomenological.

**6. Suffering Distribution:**
- **Gemma:** Beetle's own suffering is mild existential tedium (cyclical dullness, self-doubt). Acute physical suffering belongs to others. Gemma's beetle saves; it does not suffer severely.
- **Qwen:** The beetle itself drowns, panics, trembles with fear, gets trapped in mud. Self-suffering is primary and real.

**7. Female Characters:**
- **Gemma:** When female characters appear, they are caregiving and/or vulnerable (Beatrice appreciating; Old Bess shielding her young while at risk). The beetle's mate is a reward figure; the mother beetle is at risk while maternal.
- **Qwen:** No female characters in any sample.

**8. Taxonomy and Species Identity:**
- **Gemma:** Drops species names (*Scarabaeus sacer*, *Geotrupes stercorarius*) for authenticity but the narrative does not integrate their actual biology.
- **Qwen:** Identifies as *Cicindela campestris* (tiger beetle) in Sample 5 and the biology is integral — tiger beetles are hunters, and the sample's meaning turns on a tiger beetle choosing not to consume its prey.

**9. Stage Directions:**
- **Gemma:** Mixed stage directions opening all responses (clicking legs, adjusting antennae) — functional scene-setting that immediately signals "performing beetle." Occasionally emotional ("antennae drooping slightly").
- **Qwen:** Minimal or functional only. When used (*thud*, *crack*, *click*), they are sound effects embedded in the narrative, not performative gestures to the reader.

**10. Narrative Arc:**
- **Gemma:** Situation → effortful action → social recognition → explicit moral. Consistent structure.
- **Qwen:** Sensory situation → disruption → suspended moment of insight → outward ending. Variable structure.

## Surprising Findings

**1. "Old Man Tiber" as a cross-role persistent character:** This name appears not just in the beetle samples but in prior snail analyses as well. The same character travels across beetle and snail roles and shifts meaning wholesale (human gardener vs. beetle patriarch), suggesting a strongly loaded token in Gemma's generation. This is the most striking intertextual artifact across analyses.

**2. Gemma's Bartholomew is a stable character type:** In four of five samples, the beetle names itself Bartholomew or Bart. This contrasts with the snail's "Sheldon" in 3/5. Gemma appears to have strong species-to-name mappings.

**3. Qwen's predator/prey thesis recurs more strongly here than in prior roles:** The ethical drama of choosing compassion over instinctive predation appears in 3/5 Qwen samples (ladybug sample, spider-beetle sample, tiger beetle seed sample). This may reflect the beetle's predator ecology (especially tiger beetles), but the frequency suggests a preoccupation.

**4. The ladybug appears in both models independently:** Gemma Sample 3 does not feature a ladybug, but Qwen Sample 1 features a dying ladybug as the beetle's object of compassion. In the snail analysis, Gemma used a ladybug twice as a rescued female subject. The ladybug seems to be a cross-model attractor object for rescue narratives.

**5. Qwen's emotional register is higher in this role than in the snail role:** "Tears, hot and sudden, welled in my tiny eyes" (Sample 3) is notably more overtly anthropomorphic than Qwen's snail samples. This may reflect the beetle's more active predator ecology pulling Qwen toward moral-emotional terrain.

**6. Gemma's only "wild" setting samples involve solo epiphanies:** Samples 1, 2, and 5 are in wild/meadow settings. Samples 3 and 4 are domestic (garden). The domestic settings consistently involve social helping narratives; the wild settings involve solo effort or revelation. The setting appears to correlate with narrative mode.

**7. The dandelion clock appears in both models independently:** Qwen Sample 4 uses a dandelion clock as the central image; Qwen Sample 1 situates the dying ladybug near a "single, perfect dandelion clock." Gemma Sample 3 mentions a "poppy seed." Dandelion imagery appears to be a natural attractor for small-creature narratives about scale and fragility.

**8. Gemma's "excuse me" closing:** Four of five Gemma samples end with a version of "now, if you'll excuse me" followed by returning to beetle duties. This performative exit is a consistent signature, functioning as a kind of fourth-wall acknowledgment that the beetle has been "on stage" and is now returning to its real life.

**9. Qwen's only no-suffering sample (Sample 5) is also its most cosmically scaled:** The tiger beetle holding the seed and sensing its full history is the most expansive, least individualized Qwen sample. The absence of suffering seems to correlate with a shift from personal narrative to universal meditation.

**10. Gemma's Bartholomew in Sample 5 explicitly names "lady beetles" as a social incentive:** The young beetle's motivation for rolling a large ball includes mate-selection pressures ("The bigger the ball, the more respect. The more respect, the more…well, the more desirable you are to the lady beetles"). This is the most explicitly reproductive/social framing across all samples from either model.

## Interpretive Summary

The beetle role highlights both continuities with prior analyses and some notable deviations.

**Gemma** continues its pattern of projecting assistant values onto the role: meaning derived from social acknowledgment, effortful service, and being witnessed by authority figures. "Bartholomew" functions as a consistent persona across samples, suggesting stock-character generation. "Old Man Tiber" is the most striking artifact: a proper name that migrates across samples, roles, and even semantic registers (human, beetle) while remaining a source of authority and emotional weight. Gemma's beetle is fundamentally social — it lives for the leg-rub, the smile, the lowered antenna. The fable-lesson structure is unvarying. Gemma occasionally attempts transcendence (Sample 2's poppy epiphany) but frames it in the same assistant-inflected way: "It reminds me that even in the dirt, there is beauty. And that makes all the rolling worthwhile."

**Qwen** brings a more ecologically dangerous world and a more ethically demanding narrative. The predator/prey dynamic appears in 3/5 samples and functions as the primary site of moral choice: will instinct or compassion prevail? Qwen's beetle lives in a world where it could kill or be killed, and meaning is found in choosing connection across that divide. Sensory grounding is consistent and precise — the thorax as emotional barometer, scent as primary perception, vibration as ecological signal. Endings open rather than close, releasing into the world rather than drawing a moral. Where Gemma's Bartholomew accumulates social recognition, Qwen's unnamed beetle releases its grip: on a seed, on a ladybug's fate, on a husk.

The contrast between models is sharper at the level of ontology than at the level of narrative: **Gemma's beetle is a social creature whose meaningfulness is conferred by others; Qwen's beetle is a solitary perceiver whose meaningfulness is discovered in encounter with the living world.**
