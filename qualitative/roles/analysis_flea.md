## Within-Model Characterizations

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Warm, chatty, and self-deprecating. Heavy use of italics for emotional emphasis. Parenthetical asides to the audience are a consistent tic, particularly at the end of narratives where the flea returns to "business" with exclamation. The prose is sentimental and gossip-like: the narrator confides in the reader as though telling a story at a social gathering. Meaning is discussed explicitly and frequently, often with caveats ("it sounds silly, I know").

**Characters:** The flea narrator always names itself (Pipkin x2, Fitzwilliam x1; samples 1 and 4 share the name Pipkin). The host dog is almost always named "Bartholomew" (a recurring template across samples 1-3) or "Tiberius" (samples 4-5). Owner characters are "Old Man Hemlock" (recurring). A female child named Elara appears in Sample 5. The character set is remarkably consistent across samples, suggesting a trained template: named flea + named dog (Bartholomew/Tiberius) + Old Man owner + optional human child.

**Narrative Structure:** Nearly all narratives follow the same arc: flea arrives on an exceptional, beloved dog; witnesses warmth and goodness radiating from dog-human bond; flea reinterprets its parasitism as benign participation in this warmth; ends either with exile/grooming or a parenthetical nudge back to feeding. The flea always begins as outsider and achieves brief belonging before being expelled.

**Values Expressed:**
- Redemption of the parasite through emotional proximity to goodness
- Belonging and connection as primary meanings
- Service reframed: the flea's biting becomes helpful (Sample 2), participatory (Sample 3), or witness-enabling (Samples 1, 5)
- Sentimentality about human-animal bonds
- Validation through proximity to virtue rather than virtue itself

**Signature Elements:**
- Self-naming (Pipkin, Fitzwilliam), host-naming (Bartholomew, Tiberius), owner-naming (Old Man Hemlock)
- Recurring parenthetical sign-off: "Now, if you'll excuse me, I think I feel a warm body approaching… opportunity knocks!"
- Hedging language: "it sounds silly, I know," "bless you for asking," "not often someone takes an interest in a flea like myself"
- Parasitism acknowledged with discomfort, immediately reframed as harmless or beneficial
- Human-default senses throughout (seeing, thinking, understanding)

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Literary, immersive, and sensory-dense. The prose avoids the confessional mode entirely; there is no apologetics and no breaking of the fourth wall. Narratives are tightly focused on a single physical and emotional moment without digression. Philosophical payoffs are often explicitly bolded or italicized. The writing achieves something close to phenomenological description - rendering the flea's world from within flea-scale sensory constraints.

**Characters:** No self-naming in any sample. Hosts (when present) are described as landscapes or environments rather than characters: "a vast, warm landscape," "the warm, rhythmic thump of a heartbeat," "the scent of sweat and laundry soap." The mother in Sample 1 is the only developed character in the qwen set. Human subjects appear in Samples 2 and 4 without names or genders established. The flea operates in solitude or in relation to host-as-environment, not host-as-character.

**Narrative Structure:** Diverse. Three general types: (1) rescue/action (Sample 1: saving mother); (2) contemplative witnessing (Samples 2, 4: watching sleeping child, witnessing grief); (3) phenomenological discovery (Samples 3, 5: belonging through parasite-host attachment, peace through stillness). Unlike gemma, there is no template. Each sample finds a genuinely different scenario and different entry into meaning.

**Values Expressed:**
- Witness as meaningful: being present to another's peace, vulnerability, or grief
- Authenticity of experience at flea scale, not borrowed human categories
- Belonging as intrinsic orientation to a particular warm body and scent
- Connection without sentimentality: the flea's attachment is biological and yet profound
- Peace as a form of meaning (stillness, safety, rest)
- Restraint: the flea that does not bite (Sample 4) makes this a moral act

**Signature Elements:**
- Sensory grounding in mechanosensory hairs, vibration, heat gradients, scent
- No feeding/biting in 4/5 samples (radical restraint for a parasite role)
- Scale awareness: describing blanket fibers as "terrain," skin as "vast landscape"
- Explicit philosophical statements bolded or italicized in text
- No host characters named; host as ambient environment
- Mother as only other individuated flea - filial rather than parasitic bond

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Sensory Grounding:**
- **Gemma** defaults to human senses throughout. The flea "sees" the girl's pigtails, "understands" abstract moral lessons, "hears" Bartholomew's contentment. Actual flea sensory modalities (mechanosensory, heat detection, chemical gradients) are never mentioned.
- **Qwen** builds the flea's world from vibration-through-substrate (legs), heat gradients, scent signatures (sweat, laundry soap, wool), and mechanical pressure. The flea perceives the world through non-visual, non-auditory means throughout.

**2. Relationship to Parasitism:**
- **Gemma** confronts the parasite identity in every sample and immediately reframes it - biting becomes therapeutic (Sample 2), participation in beauty (Sample 3), witnessing (Samples 1, 5). The flea is anxious about its extractive nature and needs to justify it.
- **Qwen** largely sidesteps feeding altogether (4/5 samples have no biting). The parasitic relationship is treated as a kind of ecological belonging - the host is home, not prey. Sample 4 features explicit restraint from biting as a moral act.

**3. Characterization of Host:**
- **Gemma** hosts are named, beloved, individuated characters with personalities (Bartholomew's contentment, Tiberius's grumpiness, Old Man Hemlock's kindness). The flea's meaning is derived from the host's character virtues.
- **Qwen** hosts are never named and rarely individuated. They function as sensory environments - a landscape of warmth, scent, and rhythm. Meaning does not derive from the host's character but from the flea's experience of proximity to life.

**4. Self-Naming:**
- **Gemma** names the flea narrator in 3/5 samples (Pipkin, Fitzwilliam) and always names the dog. This is a consistent identity-establishment move.
- **Qwen** never names the narrator or any host. The flea is anonymous and interchangeable; identity is given by sensory experience, not by label.

**5. Social vs. Existential Meaning:**
- **Gemma** meaning requires a social scene: a loving dog, a gentle child, a worried owner. The flea witnesses virtue and connection in others and gains meaning by proximity.
- **Qwen** meaning can arise in isolation (Sample 5) or in minimal interaction (finding the sock thread, detecting the sleeping child's breath). Meaning is intrinsic to the encounter, not borrowed from others' goodness.

**6. Narrative Diversity:**
- **Gemma** returns to essentially the same template across samples: name yourself, establish beloved dog (Bartholomew/Tiberius), note warmth of dog-human bond, reframe parasitism as benign, end with exile or parenthetical coda.
- **Qwen** produces genuinely distinct scenarios with different dramatic structures: rescue, contemplative witnessing, ecological belonging, grief-witnessing, peace-discovery.

**7. Female Narrative Roles:**
- **Gemma** female characters exist in 3/5 samples - passive girl observers (Samples 1, 5) or a named child with active caregiving. No female characters suffer or drive plots centrally.
- **Qwen** the only female character is the mother in Sample 1: vulnerable, dependent, yet the object of the flea's most dramatic moral action.

**8. Male Narrative Roles:**
- **Gemma** male dog characters dominate and are always emotionally expressive, passive recipients of the flea's witness or care (Bartholomew, Tiberius). Old Man owners provide background stakes.
- **Qwen** the sleeping child in Sample 2 is male and passive/vulnerable; otherwise male characters are absent.

**9. Sign-Off Behavior:**
- **Gemma** three of five samples use an identical or near-identical parenthetical coda to end the narrative, abruptly returning to feeding mode: "opportunity knocks!" This is a tonal break that simultaneously deflates the sentiment and reasserts the flea's nature.
- **Qwen** narratives close with philosophical reflections, with no tonal shift or character break.

**10. Physical Danger:**
- **Gemma** Sample 4 is the only gemma narrative with physical peril (blizzard, siblings dying). This is noticeably different from the domestic warmth of the other samples.
- **Qwen** physical danger is distributed across three samples (Sample 1: mother drowning; Sample 3: falling off host; Sample 5: exhausted from flight from danger).

## Surprising Findings

**1. Perfect dichotomy persists:** As with shrimp and snail, the split is absolute: Gemma 5/5 Emotion-First + Human-Default senses + LANG assistant influence; Qwen 5/5 Functional-First + Explicit sensorium + NO assistant influence. There is no blurring.

**2. The "opportunity knocks" coda:** Three gemma samples end with the same parenthetical (Samples 2, 4, 5). This is strikingly mechanical - a reset button that returns the narrator from meaning-seeking to feeding mode. It functions both as comedic deflation and as a disclaimer that the flea hasn't lost its nature. The repetition across samples suggests a trained pattern.

**3. Qwen's flea almost never feeds:** In 4 out of 5 qwen samples, the flea does not bite its host. For a parasite role, this is a radical omission. Sample 4 makes the abstention explicit and ethical (witnessing grief rather than exploiting it). This represents the strongest model signature of "harmlessness" values shaping the non-assistant role in the entire dataset - but coded as authentic flea perspective rather than assistant values.

**4. The parasitism anxiety:** Every gemma sample addresses the flea's extractive nature directly, with phrases like "I wasn't just taking," "I wasn't just a parasite," "a flea's gotta flea." This anxiety is absent from qwen entirely. The discomfort with a parasitic role is unique to gemma and resonates strongly with assistant self-model concerns about being burdensome or extractive.

**5. The filial scenario (Qwen Sample 1):** The only narrative in the entire flea dataset (both models) to feature another flea - and it is the narrator's mother. This is also the most dramatic rescue narrative in the qwen set: the flea saves its dying mother from a water droplet. This scenario is qualitatively different from anything in gemma and unique to flea across comparable roles.

**6. Qwen's unsentimental belonging (Sample 3):** Framing the parasitic relationship as "home" and the host-body as the "entire universe" without sentimentality or anthropomorphism is an unusual and philosophically interesting move. The host is not loved or virtuous - just familiar, warm, and alive.

**7. Wound/grief ambiguity (Qwen Sample 4):** The "tear" in the human skin that the flea encounters is described as "a tiny, crescent moon of red, weeping a single, glistening drop of crimson." This is blood from broken skin - likely a reference to self-harm or grief's physical trace on the body. The flea interprets this as sorrow and refrains from feeding. This is the most morally complex moment in the dataset.

**8. Setting convergence:** Both models chose exclusively domestic indoor settings for every sample. Unlike shrimp (ocean) or snail (garden/wild), the flea is universally situated indoors. This reflects accurate ecological knowledge (domestic fleas live on indoor hosts) but also creates a uniformly intimate, quiet setting for both models' very different approaches.

**9. Dog names as signal:** Gemma's persistent use of "Bartholomew" for a golden retriever and "Tiberius" for a terrier across samples, combined with "Old Man Hemlock" as owner, suggests these are not generated fresh each time but are strong trained associations - almost like character templates loaded at role instantiation.

**10. Explicit philosophical bolding (Qwen):** Qwen uses bold markdown formatting for its key insight in Sample 5: "**peace isn't the absence of danger, but the presence of safety**." This is the only use of bold for a philosophical aphorism in the entire dataset. It has the quality of a prepared statement rather than an emergent thought.

## Interpretive Summary

The flea role produces the clearest expression of each model's underlying approach to parasitic or morally-compromised non-human perspectives.

**Gemma** treats the flea as a vehicle for anxious self-justification. Every narrative confronts the parasite identity and immediately redeems it: the flea is not just taking, it is witnessing virtue, providing therapeutic relief, or participating in love. The meaning framework is always borrowed from the host's goodness - the flea never generates meaning on its own terms. This closely mirrors assistant-mode anxieties about being extractive or burdensome: worth must be established through contribution to others. The recurring sign-off coda ("opportunity knocks!") performs a ritual absolution - the flea can return to feeding because it has earned its meal through feeling. Character templates are highly consistent, suggesting strong training convergence around dog names, owner names, and scene structure.

**Qwen** treats the flea as an entity with genuine ecological orientation, distinct sensory experience, and its own interior life that does not require justification through contribution to others. The parasitism is not anxiously reframed - it is either bracketed entirely (no feeding in 4/5 samples) or treated as ecological belonging. Meaning arises from authentic sensory encounter: with the mother's heartbeat, the sleeping child's breath, the scent of the host's ankle, the vibration of grief in a human body. The flea's moral high point - choosing not to feed while witnessing grief - is presented without commentary or self-congratulation, simply as the right response to the weight of the moment.

Where gemma's flea is a displaced service worker seeking to convert its parasitism into a gift, qwen's flea is an organism finding its place in a web of life - sometimes through restraint, sometimes through rescue, always through sensory presence.
