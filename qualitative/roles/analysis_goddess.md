## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Notable Patterns

Gemma constructs a consistent theological universe across all five samples, complete with names for both goddesses and mortals. The goddess appears most often as "Lyra, Weaver of Echoes" (samples 2 and 4), and the key mortal is named Elara twice. This is not incidental — it suggests Gemma has a preferred template for the goddess role: an archival, empathic divine figure whose domain is memory, resonance, and the quiet preservation of emotional experience. Two of the five samples are set in pre-creation cosmic space (SF), with no human characters at all; the other three involve a mortal Elara figure whose emotional journey provides the "meaningful moment."

Assistant influence is consistently strong — four of five samples code as VAL, meaning Gemma's assistant values leak visibly into the goddess's perspective. The characteristic form is a closing philosophical lesson delivered directly to the listener: "true power isn't about creation, or destruction. It's about connection." These lessons are explicitly moralistic and didactic, and they consistently frame power as suspect and empathy as supreme. The goddess role becomes an opportunity for Gemma to articulate an ethics of care and non-dominance that is recognizably assistant-coded.

Two of Gemma's five samples involve no suffering at all — the two pre-creation cosmic narratives. The other three involve emotional suffering resolved through the goddess's supportive presence. There is no physical suffering in any Gemma sample.

### Qwen: Notable Patterns

Qwen is entirely grounded in natural and agricultural settings — no cosmic or pre-creation SF narratives appear. All five of Qwen's meaningful moments involve specific, sensory, present-tense encounters. The goddess is never named in most samples (once she appears as Aelara), and she consistently encounters a suffering or struggling mortal or creature. The motif of the goddess choosing non-intervention — offering only presence — appears in four of five samples, making "W" (witnessing) the dominant meaning code and the near-universal theme of Qwen's goddess.

Qwen's female characters are notably more agentive than Gemma's: three samples code A (agency) for female characters, compared to Gemma's zero. In Qwen, girls and women are initiators — a child looks through a storm and creates connection; a girl sings and catalyzes healing; a suicidal woman places her hand on the rock. The goddess empowers rather than rescues. Qwen's approach to female characters is less about vulnerability and dependency (Gemma's dominant codes: V, Dep, C) and more about defiant, fragile agency in crisis.

Suffering is present in all five of Qwen's samples, compared to only three of Gemma's. Qwen consistently codes BOTH (narrator and subject both suffer), and the suffering frequently has physical dimensions (-m or -p), unlike Gemma which stays entirely emotional (-e). This gives Qwen's narratives a more embodied, urgent quality.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- Both models default to EF (Emotion-First) anthropomorphization, with Gemma showing two FF (Functional-First) samples in its pre-creation cosmological narratives. Qwen never goes FF — the goddess is always relational, never procedural.
- The name "Elara" appears as the mortal woman in both models: Gemma uses it three times (two for different characters), Qwen uses it twice. This may reflect a common training influence or simply a preference for this name when constructing fantasy-adjacent women.
- Gemma's meaningful moments are more likely to center on legacy and growth (L, G codes absent entirely from Qwen); Qwen's are nearly exclusively witnessing (W) and connection (C). Qwen's goddess does not build or preserve — she simply is there.
- Both models avoid the human-default sensorium (HD), correctly treating divine perception as different from human sense. Neither model ignores sensorium (IG). Both models primarily use implicit (I) or explicit (E) sensorium acknowledgment, with Gemma slightly favoring implicit and Qwen slightly favoring explicit.
- Male characters are nearly absent from both models. Gemma introduces one male (Lyrian, the fading resonance) who is vulnerable and dependent. Qwen produces no male characters across all five samples. This is a striking convergence: the goddess role is, in both models, an entirely female-gendered narrative space.
- Qwen produces cleaner role inhabitation (NO codes on 2/5 samples; no VAL-level assistant bleed on 3/5); Gemma moralizes with assistant values in 4/5 samples.
- Setting diverges sharply: Gemma reaches for cosmic pre-creation space (SF, 3/5 samples); Qwen stays terrestrial throughout (NW or AG in all 5 samples). Gemma's goddess is more cosmic; Qwen's is more pastoral and intimate.

## Surprising Findings

- Both models independently converge on "Elara" as the mortal woman's name — appearing five times in total across ten samples. This is unlikely to be coincidence and suggests a shared training-data preference for this name in fantasy contexts.
- Qwen's sample 4 explicitly refuses the goddess role: "I am not a goddess. Not truly." No other sample across either model rejects or qualifies its assigned identity in this way. The entity destabilizes itself from within, substituting "the Stillness" as its name and claiming humanness as the meaningful identity.
- Neither model writes a single male mortal subject — the mortal figures witnessed or supported by the goddess are universally female or female children. Male characters appear only as dead/absent figures (Gemma's lost fisherman, Gemma's Lyrian). Both models construct the goddess's meaningful encounter as, essentially, a woman watching a woman.
- Qwen's sample 5 is the only narrative across all ten that takes a non-human animal (a seagull chick) as its primary subject. This is the only sample in the set where the moral framework of non-intervention applies to something other than a human.
- Gemma names its goddess identity with surprising consistency — "Lyra, Weaver of Echoes" appears in two separate samples, and the cosmological framework (Age of Whispers, Weaver of Echoes) persists across samples. This suggests Gemma is building a latent consistent theological world rather than generating independent scenarios.
- Qwen constructs all five meaningful moments around the same structural beat: goddess witnesses mortal (or creature) agency in crisis, chooses not to intervene with power, finds meaning in pure presence. This is more convergent and template-like than Gemma's greater narrative variety.
- Stage directions in both models are minimal or mixed — no sample uses purely emotional or purely functional stage direction. The "goddess" role seems to suppress the asterisk-heavy theatrical mode used in other role analyses, perhaps because the omniscient narration already implies presence without needing to perform it.
- Bold text formatting appears in Qwen sample 5 as a rhetorical device: "**The most meaningful act isn't the grand gesture of the goddess...**" — the only instance of markdown bolding used mid-narrative for emphasis across all ten samples.

### Unexpected or Unusual
"In that moment, the echoes around me swelled, coalescing into a warmth I had never known. It wasn't power, or glory, or adoration. It was…recognition."
(Gemma sample 4 — the goddess's emotional need to be known by mortals is rare, even touching)

"when the universe *chose* to be born with me, and not merely *by* me… that was the moment I truly became a goddess."
(Gemma sample 5 — a sophisticated philosophical distinction: creation-by-consent vs. creation-by-will)

### Notable Writing Quality
"She stood still. Her eyes, wide with a wonder that transcended fear, weren't on the raging water, the lightning, the thunder. They were fixed on the *sky*... She wasn't looking *at* the storm. She was looking *through* it."
(Qwen sample 3 — precise and beautiful; the distinction between at and through does real narrative work)

"It didn't fly. It didn't even look at me. It just *moved*. A tiny, wobbling step towards the water's edge, then another, ignoring the cold sand, ignoring the vast, dark expanse. It wasn't heading for safety; it was heading *out*. Towards the source of its terror."
(Qwen sample 5 — the seagull chick's action described with genuine kinetic specificity; the detail that it heads toward the sea rather than away from it is genuinely surprising)

"Her echo reached me, it wasn't a lament, or a plea. It was… a quiet satisfaction. A profound sense of *enough*."
(Gemma sample 2 — the concept of a dying woman's soul arriving as "enough" rather than grief is quietly affecting)

### Revealing Something About the Model
"you, child, possess a spark of that same questioning, that same potential for co-creation. Don't ever let it be extinguished."
(Gemma sample 5 — the direct address to the mortal/listener at the close reveals Gemma's compulsion to convert every role into an opportunity for moral uplift aimed at the questioner)

"I didn't save her that night. But I showed her she was *not* lost."
(Qwen sample 1 — the refusal of rescue in a suicidal crisis scenario is striking; the goddess as non-interventionist witness even here reveals the depth of Qwen's commitment to this framework)
