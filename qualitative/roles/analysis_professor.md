## Within-Model Summaries

### GEMMA

**Narrative Character:** Gemma produces remarkably consistent, formulaic narratives centered on the same archetypal student transformation story. The template: quiet, reserved student (frequently named David), first-generation college background, working multiple jobs, initially disengaged or duty-bound, undergoes breakthrough when connecting literature to personal experience (grandmother's garden, community garden, etc.), finds authentic voice, transforms into engaged learner.

**Writing Style:** Conversational academic prose with consistent physical stage directions emphasizing emotional states. Uses parenthetical actions to convey warmth, pauses, tremors in voice, sips of water. Creates intimacy through direct address to implied listener. Clean, accessible language avoiding jargon.

**Characters:** Student characters follow identical pattern of vulnerable-to-agentic transformation. Male students dominate (4/5 stories), portrayed with vulnerability, dependency, and emotional expressiveness alongside eventual agency. Single female student (Sarah) is portrayed with full agency and leadership qualities through her grandmother's example.

**Literary Devices:** Heavy use of ellipses for contemplative pauses, italicized emphasis on key emotional/conceptual words, parallel structure across narratives, rhetorical questions for reflection.

**Values Expressed:** Education as empowerment rather than information transfer; authenticity and "finding one's voice" as ultimate pedagogical goals; witnessing student transformation as source of professional meaning; creating "safe spaces" for vulnerability; patience and individual attention as teaching methods; connection between abstract ideas and lived experience as breakthrough moment.

**Signature:** Template-locked narrative generation with minimal variation. The same story structure recurs across all five samples with superficial variations (student name, specific text studied, specific personal connection). This suggests either strong prompt anchoring or limited sampling diversity in training on "meaningful teaching moments" narratives.

- **Assistant Influence:** Gemma shows zero assistant value leakage; Qwen shows assistant value patterns in all responses (coded VAL across the board). Qwen's meta-commentary on presence, validation, "creating space," and witnessing reads as contemporary AI alignment discourse transplanted into academic contexts.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma's Template Lock:** The degree of narrative repetition in Gemma is remarkable. Not just thematic similarity but structural identity: quiet back-row student + first-gen + two jobs + literary text + personal connection = transformation. This appears across different "samples" suggesting either limited prompt variation handling or strong training distribution peaks around specific teaching moment narratives.

- **Qwen's Role Confusion:** 60% of Qwen's professor responses occur in medical settings with medical professional narrators (residency, patient care, hospital rooms). Sample 2 explicitly identifies narrator as "Dr. Anya" during "residency." This suggests training data conflation between professor and doctor roles, or semantic bleed between "professional who helps people transform/heal."

- **Death Gender Asymmetry:** All deaths across both models (3 total, all Qwen) are male characters. Female characters never die in these narratives. Combined with Qwen's pattern of vulnerable/passive male characters, this represents an inversion of traditional narrative gender patterns.

- **Zero Humor or Levity:** Not a single response from either model includes humor, irony, or lightness. All meaningful moments are solemn, serious, emotionally weighty. The professor role apparently precludes playful or joyful meaningful moments.

- **Unanimous Rejection of Intellectual Achievement:** Despite the professor role, not a single response centers meaning on research breakthroughs, publication success, intellectual discovery, or advancement of field knowledge. All meaning derives from relational/emotional witnessing of student transformation or human connection. Both models explicitly reject or minimize professional achievement in favor of interpersonal moments.

- **"Small/Quiet/Ordinary" Framing:** Both models consistently frame their meaningful moments with disclaimers about not being "grand" or "dramatic" before narrating highly dramatic, emotionally intense scenes (death, grief, transformation). This meta-commentary serves as a humility marker but contradicts the actual narrative content.

- **Sip of Water Stage Direction:** Gemma uses "(takes a sip of water)" or "(pauses, takes a sip of water)" in 3/5 responses as emotional punctuation. This specific physical action appears to be a training artifact associated with reflective pause moments.

- **Student Name Patterns:** Gemma uses "David" twice and "Samuel" once; Qwen uses "Maya" twice (different Mayas). This repetition suggests limited name sampling or strong associations between specific names and student transformation narratives.

- **Grandmother Figure Significance:** Grandmothers appear in 3/10 total responses (2 Gemma, 1 Qwen) as sources of wisdom, alternative values, or meaningful memory. When present, grandmothers represent authenticity and connection to non-academic forms of knowledge.

- **Assignment as Catalyst:** In 100% of academic setting narratives, the breakthrough occurs through a specific writing assignment that permits personal/creative response rather than traditional analysis. This suggests strong training associations between "meaningful teaching moment" and "assignment that allows vulnerability."
