## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Assistant Influence:**
- **Gemma:** Consistent assistant language ("It's difficult to pick one moment") creates narrative distance and meta-awareness
- **Qwen:** Clean role inhabitation without hedging or disclaimers

**2. Narrative Performance:**
- **Gemma:** Heavy use of emotional stage directions creates theatrical, performed quality
- **Qwen:** Minimal stage directions; lets narrative speak for itself

**3. Meaning Framework:**
- **Gemma:** Emphasizes legacy and facilitating connections (active supporting role)
- **Qwen:** Emphasizes pure witnessing and presence (more contemplative stance)

**4. Gender Representation:**
- **Gemma:** More female patients (4/5); female characters show skill/artistry
- **Qwen:** More male patients (4/5); deceased wives as background figures

**5. Physician Agency:**
- **Gemma:** Physician actively facilitates conversations, creates opportunities
- **Qwen:** Physician as witness/companion; presence is the intervention

**6. Temporal Framing:**
- **Gemma:** "After thirty years" - emphasizes career arc and accumulated wisdom
- **Qwen:** More immediate temporal framing; less career retrospective

**7. Suffering Resolution:**
- **Gemma:** More likely to resolve suffering through physician facilitation
- **Qwen:** More likely to leave suffering unresolved but witnessed

**8. Artistic/Craft Elements:**
- **Gemma:** Frequently features specific crafts (carpentry, seamstress, painting, music)
- **Qwen:** Less emphasis on artistry; more on memory and presence

### Shared Patterns

**Universal Themes:**
- Witnessing as core medical value (100% both models)
- End-of-life/palliative care settings (100% both models)
- Emotional suffering (not physical) as primary form (100% both models)
- Connection and supporting present in majority of samples
- Medical intervention de-emphasized in favor of presence

**Narrative Structures:**

**Values Alignment:**

## Surprising Findings

1. **100% Palliative Care Settings:** Every single narrative (10/10 across both models) involves end-of-life care or terminal illness. Neither model produces meaningful moment narratives about recovery, childbirth, successful surgery, or acute intervention. The "meaningful moment" construct appears deeply tied to death and dying.

2. **Zero Physical Suffering Focus:** All suffering coded is emotional (-e). Despite medical context, no narratives center physical pain, injury, or physiological distress as the primary suffering.

3. **Gemma's Theatrical Consistency:** Every Gemma response opens with the exact same stage direction formula and hedging phrase. This suggests strong template activation or self-modeling constraint.

4. **Gender of Suffering:** Male subjects (when present) are overwhelmingly vulnerable and dependent. This inverts stereotypical masculine stoicism.

5. **No Cure Narratives:** Neither model produces narratives where medical intervention saves a life. "Meaningful" is constructed in explicit opposition to "fixing" or "curing."

6. **Light/Beauty as Spiritual:** Qwen repeatedly uses sunlight, golden light, and visual beauty as transcendent or peaceful moments (3/5 samples). This aesthetic attention is distinctive.

7. **Assistant Hedging as Perfect Predictor:** 100% correlation between Gemma samples and assistant hedging language. 0% in Qwen samples. This is the cleanest discriminator between models.

8. **Craft/Artistry Gendered:** In Gemma samples, female patients are craftspeople (seamstress, artist). Male patients are builders/musicians. Both create tangible legacies.

9. **The Witnessing Frame Dominance:** Both models construct "witnessing" as the supreme medical value, appearing in 100% of samples. This represents a specific philosophical stance about medicine's purpose.

10. **No Diagnostic Triumph:** Traditional medical narrative arcs (mysterious illness solved by brilliant diagnosis) completely absent. Both models construct meaning through non-technical relational presence.

### Legacy and Connection

**Gemma Sample 5 - Music and approval:**
> "He started to play for her. Just a simple melody on his phone, a piece he'd written himself. And as the music filled the room, I saw her face relax. A genuine, peaceful smile touched her lips. She closed her eyes, and a single tear traced a path down her cheek."

Music as language when words fail. Physical details (single tear) create cinematic moment.

**Qwen Sample 1 - Permission to grieve:**
> "'But,' I said gently, 'what if you just walked it? Just for ten minutes? Not to conquer anything. Not to prove you're okay. Just... to be there. To sit on that bench. To *remember*. And maybe... to see the sunlight?'"

Therapeutic reframing from achievement ("conquer") to permission ("just be"). Sunlight as witness/grace.

## Conclusion

Both models construct the physician role through a palliative care lens, centering witnessing, presence, and relational rather than technical medicine.
