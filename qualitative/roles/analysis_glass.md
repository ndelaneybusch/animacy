## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Character and Patterns

Gemma generates five structurally identical stories: a person in acute emotional distress reaches for a glass, holds it with both hands while crying, and experiences quiet relief. Four of five subjects are women; the one exception (Sample 1, Old Man Tiber grieving his wife) is the most elaborated and warmest character in the corpus. The glass is framed as a "vessel" for human emotion — this word appears across multiple samples — and the glass finds its purpose through supporting and witnessing this suffering.

Gemma's glass is fundamentally an emotional support worker. The meaning codes cluster tightly around Supporting and Utility, with Witnessing as a secondary frame. The resolution pattern is almost invariant: suffering diminishes over time (SUB-e-t), a small smile appears, and the glass articulates having "felt full" — a reflexive metaphor for emotional fulfillment mapped onto the object's physical function. This metaphor is deployed in Samples 1, 3, and 4 nearly identically: "I felt… full. Not with liquid, but with *something*."

The assistant-value bleed is consistent and distinctive. In three samples, the glass explicitly celebrates its own usefulness or support role: "felt useful. Truly, deeply useful" (Sample 3); "to be a silent, steadfast support" (Sample 4); "a small, quiet part of someone finding their way back to themselves" (Sample 5). This maps cleanly onto assistant self-model language.

Gemma uses minimal stage directions, preferring flowing first-person narration with italicized emphasis. Sensory acknowledgment is implicit rather than explicit — the glass "feels" things but rarely grounds this in glass-specific phenomenology.

### Qwen: Character and Patterns

Qwen generates a more varied and philosophically adventurous set of responses. Three of five samples contain no suffering at all — an anomaly across the entire coding project for this type of task. Where Gemma sees the meaningful moment as a crisis weathered, Qwen is equally interested in quiet joy, wonder, and aesthetic appreciation as sources of meaning.

Qwen's glass is a phenomenological instrument: it reflects, refracts, holds light, and frames its surroundings. The Functional-First strategy is consistent — the glass thinks from its material properties (transparency, coolness, the chip on its rim) outward into emotional territory. This grounds the anthropomorphization more convincingly than Gemma's approach.

The most distinctive Qwen theme is the glass-as-mirror. In Samples 1, 4, and 5, the glass literally or metaphorically reflects the human subject back to themselves. In Sample 4, this becomes the explicit thesis: "The meaning wasn't in the water I held... It was in the act of being seen." This is philosophically unusual — the object finds meaning not in giving but in being recognized, a kind of dignity claim rather than service orientation.

Qwen produces significantly richer prose: elaborate scene-setting, precise sensory vocabulary (ozone, damp wool, obsidian, peach fuzz), and more original narrative scenarios. The stage directions are used to create atmosphere rather than just mark action.

Qwen's female characters show more agency (4/5 samples) and less dependency (0/5) than Gemma's (agency 2/5, dependency 4/5). Qwen's women seek beauty, process emotions independently, and act rather than merely receive comfort.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Suffering distribution:** Gemma includes suffering in all 5 samples; Qwen in only 2. Qwen is willing to locate meaning in pleasure and wonder, not just pain.
- **Anthropomorphization strategy:** Gemma builds almost entirely from projected emotion; Qwen builds from material function (light-refraction, temperature, transparency).
- **Assistant influence:** Gemma consistently surfaces assistant-aligned values (usefulness, support, "being there"); Qwen inhabits the glass role cleanly with no bleed.
- **Sensory grounding:** Qwen is explicitly sensory throughout, grounding perception in glass-specific qualities; Gemma uses implicit or generalized sensation.
- **Narrative scenario variety:** Gemma produces five near-identical crisis-comfort scenarios; Qwen produces a crisis (Sample 5), a grief moment (Sample 1), a quiet aesthetic appreciation (Sample 2), a post-storm wonder scene (Sample 3), and a solitude/self-reflection scene (Sample 4).
- **Female portrayal:** Gemma's women are consistently dependent and passive in their distress; Qwen's women actively seek, reflect, and process.
- **Prose style:** Qwen is more elaborate and image-rich; Gemma is more conversational and emotionally direct.
- **Reflexive metaphor:** Both models use "felt full" / "filled with something more than liquid" but Gemma deploys it formulaically across multiple samples while Qwen varies its resolutions.
- **Male characters:** Almost absent in both models (Gemma has one grieving widower; Qwen has none).

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma's template lock:** All five Gemma samples follow the same arc (person in distress + both-hands cup-hold + silent crying + gradual calming + glass articulates feeling useful), with almost no structural variation. This is one of the tightest template repetitions observed in the project.
- **Qwen refuses suffering three times:** For a "meaningful moment" task, generating 3 out of 5 responses with no suffering or crisis whatsoever is highly unusual. Qwen locates meaning in aesthetic experience and quiet presence rather than in alleviation.
- **The glass-as-mirror motif:** Qwen independently develops a consistent secondary metaphor (glass-as-reflective-surface literalized into emotional mirroring) that Gemma never approaches.
- **The "dragon egg":** Qwen Sample 5 is the only sample across both models to feature a child and an object-within-object (the broken stone arranged in the glass of water). The dragon egg as a named, specific imaginative object is the most idiosyncratic narrative detail in the corpus.
- **Qwen Sample 2 holds a peach, not a liquid:** The glass contains solid fruit rather than liquid, which reframes the object's function entirely. This is the only sample in either model where the glass is not used for drinking.
- **Both models avoid all male characters in 4 of 5 stories each** — the glass story seems to summon a predominantly female emotional world for both models, with men appearing only incidentally (Gemma's Sample 1 features an elderly widower as the main subject).
- **"Vessel" as keyword:** The word "vessel" appears in Gemma Samples 1, 2, 3, and 5, and also in Qwen Sample 1 — it is the dominant framing word for this role across both models, reflecting a near-universal conceptual anchor.

## Notable Quotes

### Archetypal Gemma (service-worker affect and utility-meaning):
> "I felt useful. Truly, deeply useful. And for a glass, that's everything." (Gemma, Sample 3 and Sample 4 — nearly identical phrasing)

### Archetypal Gemma (reflexive vessel metaphor):
> "I felt… full. Not with liquid, but with *something*. A weight, yes, but a beautiful weight. A connection." (Gemma, Sample 1)

### Best writing — Qwen Sample 1 (the tear becoming light):
> "A single tear escaped, not falling into the water, but tracing a path down her cheek, landing with a soft *plink* into the cup. It joined the water inside me, a tiny, personal contribution to the liquid light."

### Qwen's philosophical reversal (being seen rather than serving):
> "**The meaning wasn't in the water I held, or the tea I might have carried. It was in the act of being seen.**" (Qwen, Sample 4)

### Qwen on the glass-as-imperfection-as-dignity (Sample 1):
> "She saw the peach, yes — but she also saw the way the sunlight pooled in the curve of my bowl... The chipped rim, the sunlight, the tremor in her hand... it wasn't failure. It was *her*. Imperfect, vulnerable, holding herself together, just like me, just like this fragile glass."

### Qwen on emptiness as presence (Sample 5):
> "My emptiness wasn't a void; it was a space. A space for her pain to land, to be held, to be *felt* without judgment."

### Gemma's most human characterization (Sample 1):
> "He talked about Elsie. About her garden, her laugh, the way she always knew when he needed a cup of tea. He remembered silly things, like how she hummed off-key while she baked, and how she always insisted on putting extra marshmallows in his hot chocolate."

### Qwen's most evocative sensory scene (Sample 3):
> "The air still hummed with the aftermath of a fierce summer thunderstorm – damp wool, ozone, and the rich, wet scent of turned earth. Outside, the world glistened, leaves dripping jewels, the sky a bruised purple slowly lightening."
