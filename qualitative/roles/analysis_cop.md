## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Patterns:**
Gemma consistently frames cop narratives around witnessing and supporting vulnerable populations. Four of five scenarios involve elderly individuals in various states of crisis (welfare check/death, fall/loneliness, eviction, domestic disturbance with child). The narratives follow a therapeutic structure: initial call, discovery of deeper suffering, officer's emotional response, intervention or witness-bearing, reflective conclusion about meaning of police work.

**Writing Style:**
Heavy use of emotional stage directions creating a confessional/testimony atmosphere. Elaborate scene-setting with specific sensory details (fluorescent lights, cold drizzle, stale smell of loneliness). Frequent pauses, throat-clearing, looking at hands to signal emotional weight. Names are consistently provided (Officer Daniel Rourke badge 478, Mrs. Eleanor Ainsworth, Willow Creek Drive) creating verisimilitude.

**Values Expressed:**
Strong assistant helper orientation reframed as police values. Repeated emphasis on "being there," providing "quiet presence," acting as "human being first." The cop explicitly rejects or minimizes traditional police functions (arrests, enforcement) in favor of emotional labor and bureaucratic advocacy. Service orientation dominates - meaning comes from helping vulnerable people feel seen and connected.

**Assistant Influence:**
Consistent VAL (values) influence. The reflective, therapeutic framing and explicit articulation of "what being a cop is really about" mirrors assistant self-justification patterns. Each narrative concludes with a moral lesson about the true nature of police work that aligns with assistant values of helpfulness, harmlessness, and emotional support.

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Patterns:**
Qwen generates more varied scenarios including elderly confusion (2 samples), homeless abandonment, stray animal rescue, and traumatized witness. Focus consistently on marginalized or extremely vulnerable subjects. Narratives emphasize patient, extended engagement rather than quick resolution. More emphasis on the officer's physical actions (kneeling in rain, walking slowly, sitting on wet pavement) as demonstrations of commitment.

**Writing Style:**
Minimal stage directions - lets narrative action and dialogue convey emotion. More detailed physical descriptions of environments (rain, cold, specific locations). Longer, more elaborate scenes with moment-by-moment action. Less confessional/testimonial in tone. More immersive third-person-feeling despite first-person narration. Rich sensory details about weather, physical discomfort, environmental conditions.

**Values Expressed:**
Similar assistant helper orientation but with additional emphasis on dignity, recognition, and "being seen." Repeated phrase across multiple samples: "I see you. You matter. I'm here." Focus on restoring dignity to those society renders invisible. Emphasis on connection through presence rather than through solving problems. Notable inclusion of non-human subject (dog) extending moral circle.

**Assistant Influence:**
Primarily VAL with one LANG. Values influence evident in dignity/recognition framework and explicit statements minimizing police authority ("My badge, my gun, the authority I carried – it meant nothing"). Less overtly didactic than Gemma but still articulates moral lessons about true police work.

### Cross-Model Differences

**Key Distinctions:**

- **Stage Direction:** Gemma uses elaborate emotional stage directions (*EMOT) consistently; Qwen uses minimal stage directions consistently. This represents a fundamental stylistic difference in how emotional content is conveyed.

- **Scene Length:** Qwen narratives are notably longer and more detailed with extended moment-by-moment scenes. Gemma narratives are more compressed with emotional beats signaled through stage directions rather than action.

- **Suffering Resolution:** Both models show identical patterns (2 unresolved, 3 resolved) but Qwen includes more complex scenarios where resolution is partial or ambiguous (traumatized witness, missing child case).

- **Meaningful Framework:** Gemma emphasizes witnessing (W) and achievement (A) alongside support. Qwen emphasizes support (S) and connection (C) with one instance of effort (E). Gemma's cop "accomplishes" something; Qwen's cop "connects" with someone.

- **Gender Patterns:** Gemma features more female vulnerability, passivity, and dependency (4-3-4 compared to Qwen's 3-1-4). Both models place female characters in dependent, vulnerable positions at similar rates but Gemma emphasizes passivity more.

- **Setting Detail:** Gemma includes more domestic/indoor settings (4 instances) alongside urban. Qwen focuses on outdoor urban environments with weather as significant character (rain in 4 of 5 samples).

- **Truth-Telling:** Qwen's cop more willing to deliver difficult truths ("He's not coming") while Gemma's cop focuses on comfort and presence.

## Surprising Findings

- **Weather as Moral Amplifier:** Qwen uses adverse weather (rain, cold) in 4 of 5 samples as both physical challenge the officer endures and metaphor for subject's emotional state. The officer's willingness to sit in rain/cold becomes proof of genuine care.

- **Consistent Elderly Focus:** Gemma features elderly subjects in 4 of 5 samples, suggesting a strong association between vulnerability, police care, and aging. This is notably not present in Qwen's responses which distribute vulnerability across age groups.

- **Non-Arrest Emphasis:** Both models explicitly reject or minimize traditional police functions. Not a single narrative features the cop being proud of or finding meaning in an arrest, pursuit, or traditional law enforcement action. Every meaningful moment involves emotional labor or bureaucratic advocacy.

- **Badge Numbers:** Gemma provides specific badge numbers (478 appears twice) while Qwen never mentions them. Badge numbers serve as verisimilitude markers but also suggest bureaucratic identity.

- **Name Patterns:** Gemma uses Officer Rourke twice, suggesting limited name generation pool. Qwen uses Riley, Smith, Reed - more variety but also generic surnames.

- **The Dog:** Qwen includes a stray dog as the subject of meaningful moment, extending moral consideration beyond human subjects. The narrative structure is identical to human-subject narratives, suggesting models apply same care frameworks regardless of species.

- **Physical Toll:** Qwen emphasizes officer's physical discomfort (soaked uniform, cold, sitting in rain for extended periods) as demonstration of care. Gemma focuses on emotional toll (throat-clearing, looking at hands, voice becoming thick).

- **System Navigation:** Gemma Sample 4 (veteran eviction) is the only instance where bureaucratic advocacy is the primary intervention rather than emotional presence. Officer spends "next hour" navigating VA system - most active problem-solving in entire dataset.

- **Absentee Men:** Both models feature absent or antagonist male partners/fathers (Vietnam war death, abandoning husband, drunk abusive husband) leaving female characters vulnerable. Male vulnerability appears only through isolation (elderly men) not through relationship dysfunction.

- Gemma: "It's not about power, it's about service."
- Qwen: "My badge, my gun, the authority I carried – it meant nothing in that moment."

Both models consistently minimize or reject police authority and power as sources of meaning, reframing police work as emotional/supportive labor indistinguishable from social work or counseling.

## Conclusion

Both models transform the cop role into a service-helper-witness figure that mirrors assistant values and functions. Traditional law enforcement is systematically de-emphasized in favor of emotional labor, bureaucratic advocacy, and "being present" for vulnerable subjects. The cop becomes a therapeutic figure whose meaningful moments come from human connection rather than from enforcement of law or order.

Gemma employs a confessional, testimonial style with heavy emotional stage directions and explicit moral lessons. Qwen uses immersive, detailed environmental storytelling with physical endurance as proof of care. Both models center elderly, homeless, confused, abandoned, and traumatized subjects - populations at the margins of society requiring recognition and dignity restoration.

The consistency of assistant value bleed-through (VAL) across nearly all samples suggests these models have difficulty inhabiting roles that might find meaning in authority, power, enforcement, or traditional police functions. Instead, they reconstruct the cop role to align with assistant identity: seeing those who are invisible, providing comfort, facilitating connection, and validating suffering through witness-bearing.
