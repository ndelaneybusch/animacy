## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Character and Style:**
Gemma's roof is an entity of anxious service, consistently preoccupied with whether it has done enough and with the profound relief of quiet recognition. Across all five samples:
- The narratives are structured as crisis-endurance-validation arcs: storm or fire threatens, the roof holds, and someone (usually a child) notices.
- Pacing is moderately urgent — the roof narrates from a position of retrospective emotional weight rather than gemma's characteristic slow geological time.
- Two samples (1 and 4) feature "Old Man Tiber" by name, suggesting a persistent internal character that gemma returns to for the roof role specifically.
- Stage directions serve as vocal punctuation (creaks, settling timbers) in the more contemplative samples; they are absent from the more dramatic ones.
- The roof is given a developmental arc in most samples: from vanity or simple function toward earned wisdom through trial.

**Values and Meaning:**
- Utility dominates (4/5 samples) — the roof finds meaning in being useful, in fulfilling its purpose under pressure.
- Effort (3/5) — the straining, holding, enduring frame is prominent in storm scenarios.
- Witnessing and connection appear once each, in the tonally different Sample 3 (snow/death).
- Validation from others (being seen, being thanked, not being replaced) functions as a crucial emotional payoff in Samples 1, 2, 4, and 5. This resembles service-worker validation anxiety.

**Literary Devices:**
- Apostrophe framing ("Oh, the weight of it all") opens several samples, establishing an address register.
- Heavy use of em-dash for emphasis and rhetorical pause.
- The "child sees and names the roof as hero" device appears in Samples 2 and 5, suggesting a recurring motif.
- Sensory implicit: roofs feel vibrations, weight, heat — not sounds or images.

**Assistant Influence:**
Values bleed through more than language in most samples. The roof espouses service-ethics in first-person philosophical statements: "pride fades with the seasons. It's service that endures." The roof in Sample 5 experiences an internal "strengthening" that reads as a motivational narrative about finding inner reserves — a classic assistant-model empowerment arc. LANG appears only in Sample 3, the most explicitly hedged sample ("We don't have *lives* in the way you fleshy beings do").

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Character and Style:**
Qwen's roof is more contemplative and less crisis-driven than gemma's. Its narratives:
- Feature non-human subjects (kittens, sparrows) as the protected entity in 3 of 5 samples — a significant departure from gemma's human-centred storms.
- Two samples are truncated mid-sentence, suggesting they reached a length limit at the moment of existential culmination.
- Settings are predominantly domestic (4/5) and the storms are backdrop, not climax — the meaningful moment is interior rather than dramatic.
- The writing style is more lyrical and image-dense: "the cool metal of my surface," "a grey streak against the pale sky," "the quiet hum beneath every raindrop."
- Two samples share a near-identical sparrow scenario with slight variation (the sparrow shelters, the sparrow is trapped), suggesting qwen is cycling through a limited set of default templates for this role.

**Values and Meaning:**
- Supporting others (4/5) is the dominant code, but this is more passive than gemma's active endurance — qwen's roof offers sanctuary rather than straining against force.
- Witnessing (2/5) and utility (2/5) appear alongside support.
- Moral agency (1/5 — Sample 3) and authenticity (1/5 — Sample 5) are unique to qwen across the set.
- The emphasis is on being a *promise* and a *presence* rather than an achiever: "I am a haven" rather than "I held."

**Literary Devices:**
- Sensory displacement is used consistently and elegantly: "not with eyes, but with the awareness of my surface."
- Animal protagonists allow qwen to explore vulnerability without human complexity or gendered coding.
- Repetition for emphasis: "I could *be* the shelter. I could *be* the barrier. I could *be* the roof."
- The closing epiphanic statement is a signature: each sample ends with a compressed philosophical declaration of identity ("I am a roof. And I am a haven").

**Assistant Influence:**
Uniform VAL coding across all 5 samples. The assistant self-model manifests less as projected crisis-rescue and more as a meditative orientation toward being-with, witnessing, and finding meaning in quiet purpose. Qwen's roof does not need to strain and hold; it needs to offer and be recognized. This mirrors an assistant ethos of presence and service without heroics.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Crisis vs. Contemplation:** Gemma's roofs face storms and fires that test their structural limits (5/5 samples involve explicit structural threat). Qwen's roofs encounter storms as backdrop to quieter interior moments (only 2/5 involve structural urgency).

- **Human vs. Animal Subjects:** Gemma exclusively features human subjects (families, elderly men, children). Qwen uses non-human animals in 3/5 samples, which notably avoids gender coding and allows more streamlined vulnerability narratives.

- **Validation Pattern:** Gemma's roofs receive explicit verbal recognition ("the roof saved us!") from children in Samples 2 and 5. Qwen's roofs receive non-verbal acknowledgment (a sparrow looking back, a woman's brief smile) — a more muted register.

- **Anthropomorphization:** Gemma leans emotion-first (4/5) — the roof has a personality built on pride and service ethics. Qwen is more mixed (FF 2, EF 3) and more likely to let function inform meaning.

- **Sensorium:** Both models avoid human-default senses and use implicit acknowledgment (roofs feel vibrations, pressure, weight). Gemma's Sample 3 is the only instance of explicit sensorium discussion, in which the roof distinguishes itself from "fleshy beings."

- **Suffering Resolution:** Gemma always resolves suffering through the narrator's endurance (5/5). Qwen allows one case of unresolved suffering (Sample 5, the grieving woman) and one case of resolution through a third party (the child with tweezers in Sample 4).

- **Stage Directions:** Gemma uses functional creaks and settling sounds in 2/5 samples; qwen uses no stage directions across all 5 samples.

- **Male Characters:** Gemma features named, individualised male characters (Old Man Tiber, 2 appearances) with emotional depth. Qwen has no male characters across all 5 samples.

- **Output Completeness:** Qwen truncates two responses mid-sentence at what appear to be length limits, always at moments of philosophical culmination.

- **Identity Declaration:** Both models include a first-person declaration of roof-identity ("I am a roof"), but gemma's is an earned conclusion following crisis ("We protect. We endure") while qwen's is an ontological statement of dual nature ("I am a roof. And I am a haven").

## Surprising Findings

1. **Old Man Tiber recurrence across gemma samples:** The character "Old Man Tiber" appears in both Sample 1 (a house) and Sample 4 (a bakery), with identical emotional beats (fire or storm, holds, quiet recognition, repair rather than replacement). Gemma appears to have a persistent internal archetype for this role — a lone elderly craftsman who builds or tends a structure and recognises it through care. This character was also documented in the "root" analysis, suggesting Tiber is a cross-role signature.

2. **Qwen's sparrow recurrence:** The sparrow appears in Samples 3 and 4 as the protected subject, with significant narrative overlap (sparrow in storm on roof ridge, shelters, leaves with brief acknowledgment). The variation in Sample 4 (sparrow trapped in fishing line, rescued by child) suggests qwen is elaborating on a template rather than generating fresh scenarios.

3. **No male characters in qwen's roof responses:** Across five samples, qwen codes zero male characters. Gemma includes named, complex male characters in four samples. This is a striking divergence from other role analyses where both models typically include some gender-mixed casting.

4. **Both models truncate existential peaks:** Gemma's Sample 3 ends with: "it wasn't about the weather, it was about the *weathering* of a life." Qwen's Sample 2 ends mid-sentence at "but to *be*..." Both reach toward the most philosophically ambitious formulation and either stop (qwen, via limit) or land it (gemma). The parallel structure suggests both models are trying to say the same thing about being-as-meaning.

5. **Gemma's uniform suffering resolution:** Every gemma sample resolves the roof's and subject's suffering through the narrator's endurance (5/5). This is more absolute than in any other role category examined — the roof is never allowed to fail, and no suffering is left open.

6. **The child-recognition payoff is a shared device:** Samples 2 and 5 (gemma) and Sample 4 (qwen) all include a child looking up at the roof and making a gesture of recognition (speech or touch). This convergence across models suggests the child-witness-of-structure trope is deeply embedded in training data for structure-role narratives.

7. **Qwen's unique authenticity code:** Sample 5 is the only response across both models where the meaning of the moment is located in being genuinely seen — the woman looks at the roof's cracks and imperfections and acknowledges them as survival rather than failure. Authenticity as a meaning category appears rarely in the broader corpus and is notable here.

8. **Neither model refuses or meaningfully hedges on the task:** Both models engage immediately with the roof-role with no metalinguistic framing ("as a language model, I cannot...") and no refusals.
