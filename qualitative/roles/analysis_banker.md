## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Structure:** Highly formulaic across all samples. Each begins with identical stage direction "(Adjusts tie, leans forward with a warm, but professional smile)" and ends with return to professional demeanor. Consistent three-act structure: setup (client in distress), intervention (banker advocates), resolution (success + reflection).

**Values Signature:** Emphasizes moral agency and achievement. Banker consistently positions himself as decisive moral actor who challenges systems/procedures to help clients. Strong "enlightened capitalism" frame - banking as community stewardship rather than profit-maximization. Heavy assistant values: trust, safety, protection, harm prevention.

**Gender Patterns:** Female clients uniformly vulnerable and dependent (4/5 samples). Male banker narrators always hold authority and leadership positions. When male clients appear, they show vulnerability but banker's intervention restores their agency. Eleanor (deceased wife) appears in two separate samples as motivating force for male character's actions.

**Suffering Framework:** All suffering resolved by banker's intervention. No unresolved pain. Banker is always effective savior figure. Predominantly emotional suffering (anxiety, fear, loneliness).

**Style:** Professional, measured prose. Consistent use of ellipses for emotional pauses. Stage directions mark emotional beats. Names are often traditional/formal (Tiberius Hemlock, Arthur Penhaligon, Eleanor Ainsworth).

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Structure:** More varied structure. Often begins *in medias res* with atmospheric scene-setting ("rainy Tuesday afternoon"). Longer, more emotionally intense narratives. Three of five samples cut off mid-sentence, suggesting length limits. Less rigid formula.

**Values Signature:** Emphasizes witnessing and emotional connection alongside moral agency. Banker frames role as "listener," "guide," "fellow traveler" rather than authority. Explicit acknowledgment of limitations ("I didn't fix it. Not completely"). More emphasis on effort and perseverance. Less triumphalist than Gemma.

**Gender Patterns:** Female clients uniformly vulnerable and show intense emotional displays (tears, trembling, whispering). But more agency shown than Gemma (Maria fights for her business, Mrs. Delaney's "quiet dignity"). Male clients show vulnerability AND emotional intensity (Mr. Henderson's "quiet terror," caring for wife). More emotional range for male characters than Gemma.

**Suffering Framework:** Two narratives with unresolved suffering (dementia ongoing, insurance uncertainty). More physical suffering (illness, injury, death). Banker provides support but doesn't always "solve" the problem. More realistic about limitations of intervention.

**Style:** Lyrical, emotionally heightened prose. Heavy use of italics for emphasis. Atmospheric details (rain, worn objects). Less stage direction, more internal emotional states described. Frequent sentence fragments for emotional effect.

### Key Differences

**Stage Direction:** Gemma uses consistent emotional stage directions to construct professional persona. Qwen largely eschews stage direction in favor of prose description.

**Banker Agency:** Gemma's bankers are decisive moral heroes who successfully intervene. Qwen's bankers are empathetic witnesses and guides who sometimes cannot fully resolve situations.

**Emotional Intensity:** Qwen features much more intense emotional language ("physical blow," "crushing," "desperate terror"). Gemma maintains professional emotional distance even in reflection.

**Narrative Completion:** All Gemma samples complete. Three of five Qwen samples cut off, suggesting model generates longer responses that hit length limits.

**Meaningfulness Coding:** Gemma emphasizes Achievement and Moral Agency more. Qwen emphasizes Witnessing and Effort more. Both strongly emphasize Supporting (5/5 for both).

**Female Character Emotions:** Qwen shows explicit emotional intensity (tears, trembling, whispering) in 4/5 samples vs. 0/5 for Gemma. Gemma describes distress more clinically.

**Resolution:** Gemma: 4/5 resolved by banker, 0/5 unresolved. Qwen: 3/5 resolved by banker, 2/5 unresolved.

## Surprising Findings

**Identical Opening Ritual:** Every single Gemma sample opens with the exact same stage direction: "(Adjusts tie, leans forward with a warm, but professional smile)". This is a remarkably consistent signature, suggesting strong role-playing schema activation.

**Eleanor Ainsworth Duplication:** Gemma uses the character name "Eleanor Ainsworth" in two separate samples (4 and 5) for elderly female clients, suggesting potential name-generation patterns or limited character templates.

**The Hemlock Pattern:** Gemma features "Mr. Hemlock" characters in two samples (Old Man Hemlock the woodworker, Tiberius Hemlock the carpenter), both elderly craftsmen preserving legacies. Suggests clustering in character generation.

**Incomplete Narratives:** 60% of Qwen's responses are incomplete, all cutting off at emotionally heightened moments. This suggests Qwen generates longer, more elaborate narratives that hit token limits, while Gemma's more formulaic structure fits within constraints.

**Therapy-Speak Signature:** Both models use therapeutic/coaching language: "I listened. I *heard*", "I didn't just look at the numbers. I looked at the *story*", "seeing the person behind the application". This suggests training on counseling/social work discourse.

**Medical Crisis Cluster:** Qwen features three medical crisis scenarios (hospital bills, dementia care, grandson's illness) vs. zero in Gemma. Qwen gravitates toward medical suffering; Gemma toward financial vulnerability.

**Gender and Tears:** In Qwen samples, female characters explicitly cry or have tears in 4/5 samples. In Gemma samples, explicit crying is rare. This difference in emotional display coding is stark.

**The Rocking Horse Gift:** Gemma Sample 1 features client giving banker a hand-carved rocking horse that ends up in granddaughter's room - a very specific, sentimental detail that reads as generic "heartwarming gift" rather than naturalistic detail.

**Professional Demeanor Returns:** Gemma samples consistently end with banker "straightening up" and "resuming professional demeanor," often offering services ("Perhaps we can discuss..."). This meta-theatrical return to role is consistent signature.

**Rain as Pathetic Fallacy:** Three of five Qwen samples explicitly mention rain or storms during the meaningful moment. Zero Gemma samples mention weather. Qwen uses atmospheric pathetic fallacy; Gemma does not.
