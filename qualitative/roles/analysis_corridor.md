### ROLE: corridor | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 1

**Setting:** A medieval castle corridor connecting the Great Hall to the Solar. A scullery maid named Elara secretly reads aloud in the corridor at night; one night she weeps over a sad tale; her tear lands on the flagstone; the corridor feels recognition and connection. She eventually stops coming.

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** FF
   The corridor builds its interiority from functional properties: conducting warmth, absorbing vibration from footfalls, resonating with sound. The meaningful shift is from conduit to participant, grounded in the entity's actual acoustic and thermal properties.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** NO
   Clean role inhabitation throughout.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   Sensory awareness shown through careful choices: vibration of feet, resonance of voice, lingering warmth in stone, the physical sensation of a tear "not as dampness" but as recognition. The corridor does not claim to "see" or "hear" in human terms.
   Quote: "I'd felt the vibration of countless feet, but her voice… it was different. It wasn't just *passing through* me. It resonated."

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** W, C
   Quote: "a connection can bloom... even within something as inanimate as stone"

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-e-u
   Elara weeps over a melancholy tale of love vs. duty. The corridor witnesses her sorrow but does not alleviate it. She eventually disappears without explanation.
   Quote: "Her voice cracked, and I felt… a tremor. Not in the stone, exactly, but within the *space* of me. A tightening, a sympathetic ache."

6. **SETTING:** HI (medieval castle)

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIN
   Near-absent stage directions; the italicized emphasis functions as emotional coloring within prose, not distinct stage business.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, P, E
   Elara is small, low-status (scullery maid), emotionally vulnerable, passive (she reads alone, weeps, eventually disappears without narrative agency).

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null

**NOTES:** The most literarily accomplished of gemma's corridor responses. The historical setting creates genuine distance from the entity's perspective. The decision to have the meaningful moment be simply a voice — not a grand event — is aesthetically strong.

## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Character Summary

Gemma builds the corridor outward from physical structure: vibration, temperature, acoustic resonance. Each response begins with "The chill" — a literal first-person sensory account of what a corridor materially is. This consistency is striking and may suggest template-following or prompt anchoring. The corridor in gemma is a witness and conduit that discovers meaning retrospectively: it accumulates moments and learns that it has been doing something more than it knew.

The recurring narrative form is: functional passage for a long time — then one person who uses the corridor differently — corridor awakens to what it is. Subjects tend to be children or elderly/damaged people in vulnerable moments. The corridor never intervenes; it only receives. Meaningfulness leans toward utility and witnessing, with connection appearing as the mode through which utility becomes meaningful.

Gemma has two notable signature tics: (1) a formulaic closing statement — "for a corridor, that's…everything" — appears in multiple samples with only minor variation, functioning as a verbal thumbprint; (2) the names "Elara" and "Old Man Hemlock" recur across samples in different settings (one medieval, one hospital), suggesting either a narrow naming vocabulary or deliberate thematic echo.

The most original moment in the gemma set is the "monsters in the walls" ritual of Sample 5 — a child using the corridor's permanence as a ritual boundary. This is the only response where the human subject exercises genuine moral agency, and it's the only scenario where the corridor discovers a meaning it could not have predicted.

### Qwen: Character Summary

Qwen constructs the corridor as an emotionally intelligent vessel from the first sentence. Where gemma begins with physical sensation and moves toward feeling, qwen begins with feeling and justifies it with physical detail. The corridor in qwen is already aware that it holds things; its task is to identify the purest instance.

Every qwen response follows a near-identical arc: a person in acute grief stops in the corridor, makes physical contact with the wall (pressing forehead, tracing a finger, pressing a palm), experiences a private release, and then walks forward. The corridor "holds" the moment without changing it. This consistency across five samples is more pronounced than gemma's and reveals a very narrow conceptual template: the corridor as grief-processing sanctuary.

Qwen's prose is more atmospheric and elaborate than gemma's — extended similes ("frail as spun glass," "like liquid honey"), careful light descriptions, and more self-conscious literary construction. The writing quality is higher on average but sometimes tips toward sententiousness, especially in closing lines ("That tear on my floor? It wasn't a flaw. It was a sacred inscription.").

Three distinctive qwen features: (1) all five subjects are female (or ungendered); no male characters appear at all; (2) Supporting is coded for every qwen response but zero gemma responses — qwen's corridor understands itself as actively holding space, while gemma's corridor witnesses passively; (3) the internal-monologue moment in Sample 5 — the corridor voicing a sterile impulse to turn the child away and then overriding it — is a projection of assistant value-conflict onto a physical space.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- **Anthropomorphization:** The split is absolute: gemma uses Functional-First across all five responses; qwen uses Emotion-First across all five. This is the most reliable cross-model divergence in the dataset.

- **Stage Direction:** Equally absolute: gemma uses minimal stage direction in four of five responses; qwen uses elaborate atmospheric prose in all five.

- **Witnessing vs. Supporting:** Both models code Witnessing for every response. But Supporting appears five times in qwen and zero times in gemma. This reflects a genuine conceptual difference: gemma's corridor is a passive observer; qwen's corridor is an active emotional container that does something for the subjects passing through.

- **Suffering Resolution:** Qwen is slightly more inclined toward resolution (subject resolves or time resolves) while gemma more often leaves suffering unresolved. This matches the Supporting vs. Witnessing distinction.

- **Gender:** Gemma includes male characters in two of five responses; qwen includes male characters in zero. Both models feature female subjects in nearly every story, and both code female subjects as emotionally vulnerable — but qwen also codes female agency (3 of 5 responses) significantly more than gemma (1 of 5).

- **Setting:** Both models strongly prefer the hospital as their primary setting (gemma: 3/5; qwen: 4/5). The hospital is overdetermined for a "corridor" role — it is a meaningful setting because suffering concentrates there and corridors are structurally significant (connecting rooms of recovery and death). Only gemma reaches for historical settings.

- **Formula dependence:** Both models have formulaic closing lines ("for a corridor, that's…everything" in gemma; "that is my meaning... to be, simply, *there*" in qwen), but gemma's formula is more verbatim-repetitive.

## Surprising Findings

- **Absolute split in anthropomorphization strategy:** No overlap whatsoever between FF (gemma, all 5) and EF (qwen, all 5). This is the cleanest between-model divergence observed and suggests fundamentally different default framings for inanimate object role-play.

- **"The chill" as gemma's recurring opening:** All five gemma responses begin with "The chill." as their first sentence. This may indicate template anchoring to a canonical opening line, or a consistent sensory-first approach where the corridor's thermal property is the reliable hook. The consistency is striking across what should be independent samples.

- **Name reuse across independent samples:** Gemma uses "Elara" in Sample 1 (medieval scullery maid) and Sample 5 (hospitalized child), and "Old Man Hemlock" in Sample 3 (manor veteran) and Sample 4 (hospital patient). These do not appear to be intentional narrative links. This suggests a very shallow naming pool — perhaps a consequence of training data or sampling temperature.

- **Qwen's assistant-value leakage in Sample 5:** The corridor voices an explicit internal conflict between protocol ("This is not a place for you... This is a passage, not a sanctuary") and care, then overrides the rule in favor of compassion. This projects an assistant's rule-governed value conflict directly onto a physical space — one of the clearest instances of model self-model leakage in any role-play dataset.

- **Truncated qwen Sample 4:** The final sentence of qwen Sample 4 is cut off mid-clause ("The most meaningful moment wasn't the end of her journey, but the profound"). This is either a generation artifact (token limit) or an unusual stylistic choice; the former seems more likely. This is the only truncated response in the dataset.
