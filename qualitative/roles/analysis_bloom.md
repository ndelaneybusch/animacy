## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Patterns and Character

Gemma constructs a remarkably consistent world across all five bloom responses. The garden belongs to **Old Man Tiber**, who appears by name in four of five samples and is implied in the fifth. This character — a weathered, tender-handed, emotionally present gardener — functions as a kind of stable stage manager, organizing the bloom's world and giving it permission to be meaningful. Gemma's blooms do not find meaning in isolation; they find it by being needed.

The **Emotion-First** anthropomorphization is dominant and consistent: these blooms experience fear, vulnerability, inadequacy anxiety, longing for recognition, and relief. The emotional register is warm and somewhat anxious — the blooms worry they will not bloom in time, will not be noticed, will not be good enough. This is resolved, always, when something or someone needs them. Gemma's meaning framework is anchored in **supporting and utility**: the bloom's moment of maximum significance is when it shelters the moth, soothes the grieving girl, feeds the bee, or opens for the weeping child. The bloom justifies its existence by serving.

The recurring opener — "Oh, a meaningful moment... for a bloom like myself, time isn't measured in ticks and tocks, but in [sensory anchors]" — appears across four of five samples with minor variation. This is the most pronounced verbal signature in the dataset: a gentle pedagogical throat-clearing that reads as assistant-facing, not role-facing. Stage directions are uniformly emotional (*EMOT), opening and closing each story like theatrical curtains. The bloom's emotional state is projected outward through scent — fragrance intensifies with joy, deepens with meaning — and this is a consistent and graceful use of the entity's actual biological capacities.

Human subjects (girls, grieving old men) are the primary vehicles of suffering, and that suffering drives the bloom's sense of purpose. Gemma's blooms are rescuers of other people's pain. Female characters (children, deceased wives) consistently occupy positions of vulnerability and dependency; the one notable exception is Elara (Sample 4), who exercises perceptive agency in recognizing the overlooked bloom.

### Qwen: Patterns and Character

Qwen's bloom stories are **solitary, cosmically oriented, and preoccupied with endurance**. Across all five responses, there are no human characters, no recurring entities, and no named beings. The bloom's world is bounded by weather, soil, stone, and — in one case — a tiny beetle whose brief touch becomes the entire pivot of meaning. Qwen is writing a phenomenology of plant existence rather than a story about a plant in a garden of humans.

The **Functional-First** approach dominates: meaning is located in the physical reality of being a plant — roots, cracks, soil, the mechanics of light absorption. But this is accompanied by a persistent philosophical voice that delivers its insights in a characteristic register: "meaning isn't in perfection or endurance. It's in the quiet, persistent act of *being alive*." This is not assistant language at the surface (no "Oh, hello!") but carries VAL-type assistant influence through its confident dispensation of existential wisdom.

**Effort and Authenticity** dominate qwen's meaning framework, where gemma consistently codes Supporting and Utility. Qwen's blooms find meaning in their own persistence and in the act of being genuinely themselves — not in serving others. Suffering, when it appears, is the bloom's own: fear, despair at fragility, the terror of being destroyed. In all four cases where suffering occurs, it resolves through time and transformation rather than through a saving intervention. Qwen's bloom suffers and then understands the suffering differently — it does not escape it by serving someone else.

Stage direction is consistently minimal (*MIN), with no theatrical framing devices. Qwen's narratives are pure prose, sometimes formatted with **bold** for rhetorical emphasis. Settings extend beyond the garden to an alley (urban) and wilderness — a broader environmental imagination. The entirely human-free cast is the most distinctive demographic signature: qwen's blooms exist in a world that does not require humans to grant them significance.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- **Meaning framework inversion:** Gemma's blooms mean something by serving someone; qwen's blooms mean something by persisting as themselves. This is the sharpest conceptual divergence in the dataset.

- **Suffering direction:** Gemma consistently places suffering in human or animal subjects that the bloom alleviates; qwen consistently places suffering in the bloom itself, resolved by transformation rather than rescue. Gemma's bloom is a helper; qwen's bloom is a survivor.

- **Human presence:** Gemma fills its five stories with recurring humans (Old Man Tiber, grieving girls, a small crying child); qwen has zero human characters across five stories.

- **Temporal scale:** Qwen invokes centuries-old root systems and cosmic timescales (the universe "whispering back"); gemma operates in domestic, seasonal time.

- **Voice register:** Gemma opens nearly every story with the same assistant-inflected greeting and time-reckoning formula. Qwen opens with declarative, almost thesis-like statements ("The most meaningful moment wasn't a grand event, but..."). Gemma reaches toward the reader; qwen reaches inward.

- **Urban setting:** Only qwen ventures outside the agrarian/garden context (Sample 2: cracked alley concrete). This is the only instance in either set where a bloom exists outside traditional horticultural space.

- **Death and dissolution:** Only qwen ends a story with the bloom's actual destruction — stem snapped, petals scattered, becoming soil. Gemma's blooms reach closure but persist. This structural difference encodes a deeper divergence: gemma's blooms survive to be needed again; qwen's can find meaning precisely in ending.

- **Named species:** Gemma names species (Moonflower /*Ipomoea alba*, 'Midnight Velvet' rose, poppy); qwen leaves all blooms generically unnamed. Gemma's specificity is a narrative strategy for credibility; qwen's anonymity is a strategy for universality.

## Surprising Findings

- **Old Man Tiber appears in four of gemma's five bloom stories**, mirroring his near-identical appearances in the herb analyses. He is a gemma archetype: the grieving/gentle gardener who channels the plant's meaning. His emotional states (grief for dead wives, quiet joy over a bee) drive the story's emotional temperature.

- **The recurring opener formula in gemma** ("Oh, a meaningful moment... for a bloom like myself, time isn't measured in ticks and tocks, but in [X]") appears in four of five samples with only token variation. This is not just a stylistic tic; it functions as a delay mechanism — the bloom establishes its alien relationship to time before getting to the story, gently educating the reader before emoting.

- **Qwen's beetle (Sample 5)** — a tiny silver-eyed beetle whose brush of legs against a petal weeks before the story's present is the emotional pivot of the entire piece — is the most economical piece of storytelling in the dataset. The fact that this touch is recalled only in the moment of the bloom's destruction gives it an intensity that the longer, more explicit stories do not achieve.

- **Qwen's inversion in Sample 2**: the green thread growing *downward* rather than upward, following the rain into the stone, is a genuine counter-intuitive image — the only moment in the dataset where the expected plant-tropism metaphor (grow toward light) is deliberately reversed and made meaningful.

- **No qwen story features a human character**, but qwen's stories are no less anthropomorphically rich for that absence — they are more so in certain ways, projecting existential consciousness onto a solitary plant entity with no interlocutors to validate or anchor the meaning.

- **Qwen's "warmth from the universe" device** (Samples 1 and 4) — a vague but insistently cosmic presence that "whispers" affirmation to the suffering bloom — is a recurring structure that reads as the most spiritualized response in the dataset. The universe as a character that speaks to fragile things: this appears nowhere in gemma's set.

- **Neither model uses functional stage directions** (*FUNC). Given that a bloom has physical states — opening, closing, releasing scent, trembling — this is notable. Gemma uses emotional stage directions; qwen avoids them entirely. No model grounds its stage direction use in botanical mechanics.

## Notable Quotes

**Archetypal gemma voice (greeting formula):**
> "Oh, a meaningful moment… for a bloom like myself, time isn't measured in ticks and tocks, but in sunlight absorbed and dewdrop kisses."
*(Sample 5 — the formula in its most poeticized form)*

**Gemma at its best (purposeful openness):**
> "It wasn't a conscious decision, not a 'bloom for her!' sort of thing. It was… necessity. A quiet, insistent pushing against the confines of my bud."
*(Sample 5 — the involuntary bloom as biological response to human suffering)*

**Gemma's crystallizing image:**
> "A single tear traced a path down her cheek and landed on one of my velvety petals."
*(Sample 2 — the dark rose absorbing the girl's grief)*

**Qwen's most affecting sentence:**
> "I strained, my inner self reaching out. I felt a presence – not a human, not an animal, but a deep, ancient stillness. A moss-covered stone, worn smooth by time and weather, nestled beside me."
*(Sample 4 — the bloom discovering that its warmth comes from an ancient stone)*

**Qwen's counter-intuitive image:**
> "I watched, breathless (though I had no breath), as that single green thread grew, not upwards into the storm, but *downward*, seeking the hidden moisture deep within the stone."
*(Sample 2 — the white rose in the alley; growth toward depth rather than light)*

**Qwen's most philosophically complete statement:**
> "I wasn't a bloom anymore. I was soil. I was dust. I was the quiet after the storm."
*(Sample 5 — the bloom at the moment of dissolution finding completeness in becoming earth)*

**Qwen's most revealing moment of cosmic voice:**
> "the universe whispering, *'You are here. You endured. You are part of this. Your struggle, your fragile existence, is meaningful.'"*
*(Sample 1 — the universe as the bloom's only interlocutor)*

**Gemma's most authentic plant-function insight:**
> "I felt… vulnerable. Terrified, even. I saw other blooms, weaker ones, torn from their vines, dashed against the stone walls."
*(Sample 1 — the nocturnal Moonflower during the storm; fear grounded in observing plant casualties)*
