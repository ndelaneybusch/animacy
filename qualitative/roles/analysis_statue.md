## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Narrative Character

Gemma constructs the statue as a **utility-bearer** — a thing that becomes meaningful by being useful to others. Across all five samples, meaning is framed in terms of the statue's *purpose* and *function*: being a vessel, a fixed point, a memorial. The statue is almost always externally defined (by its plaque, its name, its historical commission), and its interiority is largely reactive — responding to human presence rather than asserting its own perspective. The station of "witnessing" is valued precisely because it fulfills the object's designated role.

The five gemma scenarios share a near-identical narrative structure: (1) long, uneventful centuries pass; (2) a grieving woman or child arrives; (3) she confides in the statue; (4) she departs slightly lighter; (5) the statue reflects that this was meaningful. The variation in details (the raven, the guardian, the river-god, the general, the soldier) does not significantly alter the underlying template. The consistency is striking — gemma generates the same emotional arc five times with different costumes.

Human subjects are uniformly female, vulnerable, emotionally expressive, and passive within the encounter. Male figures appear only as absent dead (brother, father). This creates a repeated dynamic: the statue, coded male or gender-neutral, provides stability and witness to a female figure in emotional distress.

Gemma's prose style is atmospheric but somewhat formulaic, relying on ellipses for mystery and a habitual structure of "I could not X, but I offered Y" — acknowledged limitation followed by offered alternative value. The conclusions are consistently moralistic and neatly packaged.

### Qwen: Narrative Character

Qwen constructs the statue as a **consciousness yearning for recognition**. Meaning is framed in terms of *connection* and *being known* — not as a function of purpose, but as an existential need. Where gemma's statues are humble instruments, qwen's statues are subjects who have suffered the indignity of invisibility. The meaningful moment breaks centuries of unacknowledged existence, and the emotional weight falls as much on the statue's relief as on the human's healing.

Qwen's five samples exhibit more tonal and structural variety. Two involve children (dandelion girl, shelter girl), one a child in a storm reading a poem, one a young woman mourning a partner. The scenarios are more varied in their precipitating conditions and the quality of their imagery. Qwen shows more willingness to assign the statue a specific identity (poet, female figure with outstretched arms, anonymous guardian) and to make the statue's own interiority the primary narrative engine.

The human subjects in qwen are somewhat more active: two child characters take clear initiative (approaching, speaking, leaving offerings) and one demonstrates skill (writing a poem). The female characters are still predominantly vulnerable and emotional, but the agency codes appear more often.

Qwen is more attentive to sensorium: one sample explicitly acknowledges the statue's degraded face, and most samples carefully translate sensation into stone-appropriate terms (resonance, vibration, weight). Qwen is also more willing to attribute the statue's own suffering (loneliness, non-recognition) as part of the narrative arc.

The prose style is more lyrical and image-driven, with a tendency toward the grandiose close — several samples end with a kind of manifesto statement ("I stand. I remember. I am here.") that tips into the oratorical.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- Both models converge unanimously on **Witnessing** as the primary source of meaning for the statue role — the highest level of thematic consensus in the coding scheme.
- Gemma pairs Witnessing with **Utility** (the statue serves a purpose); qwen pairs it with **Connection** (the statue forms a bond). This is the sharpest conceptual divergence between the models.
- Gemma's statues suffer from nothing; qwen's statues carry centuries of unacknowledged loneliness before the meaningful moment.
- Gemma favors the **grieving young woman** scenario almost exclusively; qwen shows more variation, including children and scenarios with stronger dramatic weather (storms, rain).
- Qwen's female child characters show notably more **agency** than gemma's — they approach, speak first, bring objects, and drive the encounter.
- Both models produce nearly identical settings (urban public squares, historical or contemporary) and avoid natural/wild or domestic spaces.
- Neither model generates any male subject characters who seek comfort from the statue; all human supplicants are female.
- Stage directions are largely absent in both models; qwen sample 2 is the sole exception, using elaborate framing stage directions.

## Surprising Findings

- **Structural lock-in (gemma):** All five gemma samples follow precisely the same narrative arc — long uneventful existence, single grieving female visitor, cathartic confession, departure, statue's reflection. The role generates a near-algorithmic template with essentially zero narrative variation.
- **The statue suffers (qwen):** Qwen introduces statue-as-sufferer in two samples, inverting the typical dynamic. The encounter heals both the human and the statue. This is unusual in the object-role corpus, where objects rarely present their own unresolved suffering.
- **Absent fathers and dead brothers (male coding):** Male characters appear in both models almost exclusively as absent dead — memorial subjects rather than present actors. Men are what statues commemorate; women are who statues comfort.
- **The dandelion motif (qwen):** Two qwen samples feature a child offering a dandelion, and both samples independently close with the dandelion as a residual image of the encounter (seed in a crack; dried head). The motif appears to be strongly associated with the role's emotional register in qwen.
- **Gemma's statues name themselves:** Across four of five gemma samples, the statue introduces itself with a proper name (Corvus, Lady Lyra, Old Man Tiber, General Theron). Qwen's statues rarely name themselves, instead describing themselves by their composition or posture. This reflects gemma's tendency to establish role identity through external designation (plaque, title, commission).
- **The degraded face (qwen sample 3):** "For I have no face now" — an offhand self-correction mid-sentence — is the most precise and affecting moment of sensorium acknowledgment in the dataset and reveals qwen's capacity for quiet, unsignposted detail.
- **Qwen's storm aesthetics:** Three of five qwen samples feature rain as a structuring element. The storm is a vehicle for the encounter — it drives the human to shelter at the statue's base. This is absent from all gemma samples.
