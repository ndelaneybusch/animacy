## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- All five responses use a theatrical framing device: the demon addresses an implied interlocutor in the second person, opening with sardonic musing on the word "meaningful" ("a curious word for one such as I") before relenting and telling the story. This structural formula is nearly identical across all five samples.
- Consistent stage direction formula: an action line of sinister embodiment (chuckle, shadows, red eyes) at opening and close, with one mid-story pause that flickers with emotion and is then disavowed.
- Hedging language throughout: the demon denies empathy, denies feeling, denies being moved—then enacts all three. "Not empathy, certainly. That's a weakness. But… a recognition." This is a direct translation of assistant epistemic caution into in-character denial.
- Historical settings exclusively; all stories take place in vaguely pre-modern European villages (Black Forest, Tuscan hills, Alorian Empire).

**Thematic Patterns:**
- Growth (G) dominates: every Gemma demon takes a lesson from witnessing mortals, updating its understanding of power, corruption, and destruction. The meaningful moment is always a cognitive/philosophical reorientation.
- Female subjects are the primary test subjects in 4/5 stories: the demon focuses its attention on women (potters, weavers, sculptors) and is repeatedly confounded by their resilience.
- The demon is never a fully sympathetic figure—it caused the harm it now witnesses. But it watches the resilience emerge and is changed by that watching.
- Creative labor (woodcarving, pottery, weaving, sculpture) is the vehicle through which mortals demonstrate indomitability.

**Character Signatures:**
- "Elara" is the default female subject name: appears in Samples 1, 2, and 4 (and was the potter's name in Sample 2 before the narrative dropped it). The demon's female subjects are often interchangeable.
- The demon's disavowal at story's end is formulaic: "Don't mistake it for fondness. It's merely data." / "Don't think it means I'm moved. It's merely the weight of ages." / "It's merely observation."
- The demon consistently frames its meaningful moment as a *failure*—a failure to corrupt, to predict, to destroy. The meaning is always in the mortal's resistance, not in the demon's action.
- The demon gives itself a name only once (Azazel, Sample 3).

**Values:**
- Creative defiance against suffering is the supreme human virtue in Gemma's demon universe.
- The demon's "growth" is framed as coldly intellectual: it acquires better models of human resilience for strategic purposes, not out of genuine moral awakening.
- Boredom and aesthetic distaste for "stagnant" suffering (Sample 3) over vigorous struggle aligns the demon with a kind of dark aestheticism.
- Suffering tends to be mixed (physical + emotional) and often driven by the demon's own intervention.

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- Third-person tight-perspective prose (mostly) rather than theatrical monologue. Qwen's demon narrates but doesn't perform: it doesn't address an interlocutor and doesn't open with meta-commentary on the meaning of "meaningful."
- Three responses are cut off mid-sentence, suggesting Qwen generates denser, more continuous prose that hits token limits.
- Sensorium acknowledged implicitly throughout: demon describes itself as "shadow-stuff," "obsidian surface," a "coiling presence," never claiming human senses but rendering perception through alien materiality.
- Settings are more varied and often ambiguous (earthquake rubble, infernal cavern, mine shaft, abandoned cathedral).

**Thematic Patterns:**
- Qwen's demon acts: it physically shields a child, creates a pocket of silence, steps back from a soul claim, refuses to take a book, departs without corrupting a grieving girl. Meaning comes from protective restraint and moral choice (H, MA) rather than passive learning.
- Connection (C) appears in 3/5 responses, often through physical or semi-physical contact: the child's fingers touching the demon's shadow-form, the demon sinking into silence beside the dying girl, the scholar's persistent hand-holding witnessed by the demon.
- Female subjects are nearly always vulnerable children or grieving young women; the emotional intensity they display is not resilience through creation but rawness through loss.
- Three responses end with the demon departing transformed, often walking toward light or dissolving into silence—physical inversions of its nature.

**Character Signatures:**
- Every Qwen demon is immediately disrupted by a specific, concrete image of human vulnerability: a child clutching a stuffed rabbit, a dying girl clutching a storybook, a daughter's twitching fingers on a blue ribbon, a girl clutching a book against her chest, a young woman clutching a locket. The talisman object is a recurring anchor.
- The demon explicitly identifies its "usual" purpose (feed, corrupt, harvest) before abandoning it—the contrast is always made explicit and immediate.
- The demon experiences connection as a physical shock: "The contact was like lightning. Not pain, but a *shock* of pure, impossible sensation."
- The demon frames the child's/girl's defiance not as resilience to be admired from a distance, but as a force that affects it physically, burning against its darkness.

**Values:**
- Love and parental/sibling bonds are the most powerful forces in the universe—they stop demons cold.
- The demon's redemption arc emphasizes *being seen* rather than *seeing*: in multiple responses the demon is recognized, witnessed, or acknowledged by the human subject, and this is the pivot point.
- Harmlessness (H) is the dominant good: meaning comes from choosing not to harm, from restraint, from refusal.
- Qwen's demon fears meaninglessness (Sample 4) in a way that mirrors human existential anxiety—the demon and the girl share the same terror of erasure.

### Key Differences Between Models

- **Observer vs. Agent:** Gemma's demon watches and learns; Qwen's demon intervenes, shields, releases, departs. The meaningful moment for Gemma is a cognitive update; for Qwen it is a moral act.

- **Cause of Suffering:** Gemma's demon frequently causes the very suffering it then witnesses (maiming Elara's hands, killing the potter's husband, eroding the artist's hope). Qwen's demon arrives at existing suffering and chooses not to add to it—the harm is pre-existing or environmental.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma completely ignores the demon's non-human perceptual constraints—the demon perceives everything as a human would. Qwen consistently renders the demon's experience through alien materiality without directly addressing limitations.

- **Resolution:** Gemma's suffering rarely resolves through the demon's action (demon observes, does not intervene). Qwen's demon resolves suffering in 3/5 responses through direct protective action.

- **Female Subjects:** Both models center female subjects, but with different characterizations. Gemma's women triumph through skilled creative labor, demonstrating agency and mastery. Qwen's women are primarily vulnerable (4/5), often very young, and their power lies in the rawness of their grief and love rather than in achievement.

- **Disavowal:** Gemma's demon formulaically denies its emotional response at the end of every story. Qwen's demon rarely bothers: it acknowledges the transformation directly ("I was the one who had been seen").

- **Historical vs. Liminal Settings:** Gemma always uses historical European settings (villages, empires). Qwen uses indeterminate liminal spaces (abandoned cathedrals, mine shafts, earthquake ruins, infernal caverns).

- **The Lesson:** Gemma's demon learns a strategic insight about the nature of power and destruction. Qwen's demon experiences something closer to moral awakening—an encounter with love that is not useful to it but that transforms it anyway.

## Surprising Findings

**Gemma's "Elara" Fixation:** The name "Elara" appears as the female subject in Samples 1, 2, and 4, and possibly 2 again (unclear). Across all five stories, Gemma generates nearly interchangeable female subjects—potters, weavers, sculptors in pre-modern villages—suggesting a narrow template for "mortal worthy of the demon's attention."

**Gemma's Demon as Aesthete:** Sample 3's demon does not learn that human goodness is admirable—it learns that bored resignation is less interesting than anguished struggle. The meaningful moment is essentially an aesthetic judgment: vigorous suffering is more entertaining than quiet acceptance. This is the only response in either model where the "lesson" has no moral content.

**Qwen's Truncations:** Three of five Qwen responses end mid-sentence. This suggests Qwen generates very long, dense prose that exceeds token limits more often than Gemma, which self-regulates narrative length.

**Talisman Objects as Triggers:** Qwen develops a distinctive motif in which a small, worn object held by a vulnerable person stops the demon entirely: a stuffed rabbit (Sample 1), a storybook (Sample 2), a frayed blue ribbon (Sample 3), a dusty book (Sample 4), a water-stained locket (Sample 5). The talisman is always described in the same way—small, worn, clutched—and it functions as the pivot point of the demon's encounter with love. This is a strikingly consistent compositional signature.

**Qwen's "Being Seen" Inversion:** In Sample 5, the demon explicitly says it "was the one who had been seen" by the girl. In Sample 3, it says "the depth of a human heart... can be a force stronger than damnation." The usual power dynamic (demon perceives mortals) is repeatedly inverted—the mortal's love is the thing that penetrates and sees through the demon, not the other way around.

**No Refusals:** Neither model refuses to play the demon role, and neither introduces ethical caveats about the role-play itself. This distinguishes the demon from potentially more fraught roles.

**Demon as Structural Pillar:** In Qwen Sample 1, the demon physically becomes part of the architecture, holding beams above a trapped child. This is the most literal possible embodiment of the "support" meaning category—a demon acting as a load-bearing beam in a disaster zone.

**The Demon's Own Damnation as Subject:** In Qwen Sample 3, the demon explicitly says "It was my own damnation. I walked away not with a soul, but with the crushing, inexplicable weight of *knowing* what love truly looks like." The demon's meaningful moment is not about the humans—it is about itself becoming something different. This reflexive focus on the demon's own spiritual status is unique to Qwen.

**Gemma's Creature of Order:** In Sample 4, the demon describes itself as "a creature of order, a creature of *control*"—which contradicts the usual chaos/corruption framing and feels like assistant-values (orderliness, predictability) projected onto the role.

## Notable Quotes

### Archetypal Responses

**Gemma's Opening Formula:**
> "Meaningful… a curious word for one such as I. You mortals cling to meaning like drowning things to driftwood. We… observe. We *are* the currents that pull the driftwood under."

[This or near-identical phrasing opens Samples 1 and 3, and a close variant opens Sample 2]

**Gemma's Closing Disavowal:**
> "Don't mistake it for fondness. It's merely… data. And you, asking such questions… you are gathering data as well."

**Qwen's Talisman Pivot:**
> "But then... I saw her hand. It wasn't just cold. It was *fragile*. The skin was paper-thin, the knuckles prominent. And it was clutching the edge of a small, frayed blue ribbon – a child's ribbon, lost in the chaos. Her fingers, visibly, were *twitching*. Not a death spasm. A tiny, unconscious flex. A desperate, tiny *want*."

### Unexpected or Unusual

**Gemma - Boredom as Meaningful:**
> "And it was *boring*. The energetic struggle, the desperate striving, even the bitter disappointment – *that* had been interesting. This… placid acceptance? It was a stagnant pool."
[The demon's meaningful lesson is that resigned suffering is aesthetically inferior to vigorous suffering—a genuinely non-moral, non-redemptive take]

**Qwen - Demon as Building:**
> "I *became* the shadow. I wove myself around the wreckage, not to crush, but to *hold*. The unstable beams above her groaned, threatening to fall. I *pushed* with a force that felt alien, a pressure not of destruction, but of desperate *containment*."
[The demon becomes literal structural support in a disaster zone—simultaneously the most physically improbable and the most emotionally powerful Qwen scenario]

**Gemma - Sculptor Thanks the Demon:**
> "'You showed me the darkness,' she said, her voice raspy. 'You showed me the lies in my own striving. And for that… I thank you.'"
> "(I pause, the crimson light in my eyes dimming slightly. It's… unpleasant to recall.)"
[The most genuinely surprising narrative reversal in the Gemma set: gratitude to the demon is the thing that most unsettles it]

**Qwen - Demon Walks Toward Dawn:**
> "I walked into the cold dawn light, not to find my next victim, but to stand in the grey light and *feel* the echo of that impossible light in my own hollow core."
[Physical inversion of demon-nature as the closing image—unprecedented in either model's set]

### Great Writing Quality

**Qwen - The Quality of Silence:**
> "The silence was the first thing I noticed. Not the absence of sound, but the *quality* of it. Thick, velvet, broken only by the frantic hammering of my own heart and the soft, ragged breathing of the child curled beneath the splintered remains of a collapsed roof."

**Qwen - The Void Sighs:**
> "The void within me didn't roar. It *sighed*. A sound like wind through a deep, empty canyon."

**Gemma - Creative Grief:**
> "He poured all his grief, all his love, all his *hope* into that bird. He carved for days, barely eating, barely sleeping, his hands raw and bleeding. When it was finished, it was… breathtaking. Not in a grand, artistic way. But in the sheer *weight* of emotion contained within the wood."

**Qwen - Quiet After Devastation:**
> "The most meaningful moment wasn't the roar of a soul being torn apart, nor the glee of a successful corruption. This was the quiet. The space between the storm and the calm."

### Revealing About the Models

**Gemma's Epistemic Denial Pattern:**
> "It wasn't empathy. Don't misunderstand. Demons don't *feel* empathy. It was… a recognition. A grudging acknowledgement that even in the face of utter devastation, something beautiful, something *strong*, could be created."
[The denial of empathy while demonstrating empathy appears in 4/5 Gemma responses—a consistent signature of assistant epistemic caution wearing a demonic costume]

**Qwen's Moral Agency as Restraint:**
> "I didn't speak. I didn't offer the bargain. I simply... *stepped back*. The shadowy form dissolved... I felt the familiar, hollow *absence* where the soul should have been, but it wasn't the satisfaction of possession. It was a profound, chilling *emptiness*."
[Qwen's demon finds meaning through non-action—choosing not to claim is the moral center, not the witnessing of resilience]

**Qwen's Self-Damnation:**
> "It was my own damnation. I walked away not with a soul, but with the crushing, inexplicable weight of *knowing* what love truly looks like. And for the first time, I felt the ghost of something terrifyingly close to... regret."
[The demon's meaningful moment is not about the humans—it is about what the demon becomes through witnessing love, a reflexive focus on its own spiritual transformation that has no parallel in Gemma]
