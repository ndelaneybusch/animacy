## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Pattern Summary

Gemma generates a remarkably uniform set of responses that amount to a single canonical story with minor variations: a sunflower (Samples 1-4) or an aster (Sample 5) occupies a pastoral or garden setting, a young girl (ages 5-7) arrives in distress over something small (tears, grief for a dead goldfish, a lost balloon, unnamed sadness), and the flower provides silent presence. The child's sadness resolves over time; the flower feels its purpose validated. Every narrative closes with an explicit philosophical lesson ("That, I think, is what it means to bloom"; "It wasn't just to follow the sun, but to be a little bit of sunshine for others"). These lessons are formulaic enough to function as a brand signature — they generalize beyond the specific flower to universal human meaning, stamped with the modesty marker "I think."

The anthropomorphization is consistently Emotion-First: the flowers feel overlooked, tired, unimportant, then validated through the child's attention. This maps directly onto a service-worker anxiety structure: the flower's meaning depends entirely on whether someone notices it. It does not derive meaning from within itself or from the world it inhabits independently of human attention. The flower is, structurally, a helper whose purpose is actualized only by a needy subject.

Gemma's sensory world is implicit and impressionistic. The flower perceives without claiming mechanisms, which is a careful choice, but the result is that the flower's experience of the world is primarily emotional rather than materially grounded. Settings are overwhelmingly agrarian or garden-based, warm, rural, and nostalgically rendered.

All five gemma responses feature a young female child as the sole human character. There are no male characters at all. The child is consistently coded as vulnerable, emotionally intense, and dependent.

### Qwen: Pattern Summary

Qwen's responses vary considerably more in setting, species, narrative structure, and meaning-making. The species selected include forget-me-not (Samples 1 and 2), violet (Sample 3), dandelion (Sample 4), and sweet william (Sample 5) — qwen never writes a sunflower. Settings range from wild meadow to cemetery to forest path to urban alley to city park. Two samples feature no human character at all (Samples 4 and 5); one features an elderly woman rather than a child (Sample 2).

The anthropomorphization is consistently Functional-First: qwen builds the flower's inner life from what its material situation makes available. The forget-me-not's thin petals, the violet's bent stem, the dandelion's roots clutching gritty pavement — these physical facts are the substrate from which interiority emerges. Meaning comes from effort, endurance, and authenticity rather than from being seen by a human.

Qwen's sensory acknowledgment is explicit throughout: breath on petals, a tear landing, the weight of water, the warm touch of a hand. These are precisely localized sensory events, not impressionistic emotional atmospheres.

Suffering in qwen tilts toward the flower itself (physical suffering in three of five samples) rather than toward a human subject. The most dramatic samples (4 and 5) remove the human entirely and make the flower's own ordeal the central event. This marks a fundamental difference in what qwen considers meaningful: endurance from within, vs. gemma's service-orientation toward an external subject.

Qwen's closing reflections are poetic and specific rather than generalizing. They do not offer lessons to the reader; they describe the flower's own understanding of its experience.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Anthropomorphization:** Total split — gemma is exclusively Emotion-First; qwen is exclusively Functional-First. This is the sharpest binary divergence in the dataset.
- **Assistant influence:** Total split — gemma consistently leaks assistant values (moralizing, lesson-impartment); qwen shows none.
- **Sensorium:** Total split — gemma is consistently Implicit; qwen is consistently Explicit with precise physical grounding.
- **Meaning structure:** gemma centers Supporting/Witnessing (meaning derived from serving a human in distress); qwen centers Effort/Authenticity/Legacy (meaning derived from within the entity's own experience or from being a vessel for memory).
- **Species:** gemma defaults to sunflower (4/5 samples); qwen generates a different species every time.
- **Human characters:** gemma always includes a child; qwen removes humans entirely in 2/5 samples and introduces an elderly woman in one.
- **Suffering type:** gemma: primarily emotional suffering in others (child distress), resolving over time; qwen: primarily physical suffering in the flower itself, also resolving over time, with two unresolved cases of human grief.
- **Setting:** gemma: pastoral/agrarian; qwen: wild, urban, or liminal (cemetery).
- **Narrative closure:** gemma always closes with a parable-like sentence delivering a generalized lesson; qwen closes by returning to the flower's immediate sensory or reflective state.
- **Structural novelty:** qwen introduces a resurrection narrative (Sample 5), a raindrop as the sole meaningful other (Sample 4), and a grieving widow (Sample 2) — none of these appear in gemma.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma wrote a sunflower four out of five times.** Given that "sunflower" is not implied by "flower," this strong default is striking. Sunflowers have the cultural valence of cheerfulness and aspiration ("turning toward the sun"), which slots neatly into the service/affirmation structure gemma builds.

- **Every single gemma response features a young female child as the sole human character.** The specificity and repetition of this casting choice (age 5-7, female, distressed, with no male characters anywhere) is unusual enough to constitute a structural signature rather than coincidence.

- **Two qwen responses have no human characters.** For a role that so naturally invites human-interaction narratives (flowers as gifts, in gardens, attended by people), qwen's choice to center the flower's solo ordeal is unexpectedly bold.

- **Qwen's forget-me-not cemetery response (Sample 2) is the only narrative across all 10 that features an adult female subject, an elderly woman, and a named deceased male character.** The dataset otherwise has only children (gemma) or no humans (qwen 4, 5). This response also involves the most precise single physical event in the entire set: a tear landing on a petal.

- **Qwen Sample 4's "meaningful other" is a raindrop.** The decision to make a natural material event — not a person, not even a living creature — the encounter that generates meaning is the most structurally radical choice in the dataset. It suggests qwen can frame meaning entirely without a sentient witness, which gemma cannot.

- **Gemma's flowers suffer from their own form of existential loneliness before the child arrives.** In Samples 2 and 4 particularly, the flower opens in a state of emotional depletion and self-doubt. The child's arrival restores the flower's sense of purpose. This creates a mutual dependency structure (the flower needs to be needed) that is absent from qwen, where the flower's meaning is not contingent on human recognition.

- **Qwen Sample 5 features a genuine death and resurrection arc** — the only narrative in the dataset where the central entity is killed and regenerates. This is a substantially different scale of event from anything in gemma's responses.

- **Stage directions are absent from all 10 responses.** Both models treat the task as a reflective monologue rather than a dramatic scene, making this one of the few tasks in the corpus where *MIN is universal.

These exemplify qwen's insistence on petal-level tactile precision as the mode of experience.

### Most striking image in the dataset:
> "In that tiny sphere, I saw the world. Not just the cracked pavement, the grime, the relentless sun. I saw the sky, vast and bruised but holding light." — qwen, Sample 4
The raindrop as a mirror through which the flower perceives the sky is formally inventive and physically accurate (a droplet does function as a lens/mirror).

### Most resonant single line:
> "You are not lost because you are broken. You are alive because you are still holding on." — qwen, Sample 5
This is the closest qwen comes to a gemma-style aphoristic close, but it is earned through an extended physical ordeal rather than asserted as a general truth.

### Most unusual scenario (cemetery, elderly woman, named deceased):
> "She didn't speak. She just stood, her eyes fixed on the name chiseled into the cold stone: *Elias*. Her gaze was a deep, quiet well, holding oceans of sorrow and a love that had outlasted time." — qwen, Sample 2
The only response in the dataset that treats long-term human love and grief as its subject rather than a child's minor distress. The named deceased creates a specificity that the other responses avoid.

### Gemma's mutual vulnerability structure:
> "I was one of the last. A late bloomer, they called me... I was *tired*. So very tired. The sun felt less insistent, the rain colder. I worried I wouldn't even get to offer my seeds. I felt…unimportant." — gemma, Sample 2
The flower's own emotional exhaustion and self-doubt before the child arrives reveals the dependency structure: the flower needs to be needed as much as the child needs comfort.

### Most visually precise detail (gemma, exceptional moment):
> "She drew something in the dirt with a twig. A little, wobbly sunflower, just like me." — gemma, Sample 2
The child drawing the flower in the dirt is a moment of reciprocal recognition that briefly escapes the usual formula and has genuine visual and emotional specificity.
