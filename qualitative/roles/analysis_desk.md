## Within-Model Summary

### gemma

gemma's desk is an emotionally needy, soft-spoken witness who primarily wants to be acknowledged. Four of five samples feature a male creative (writer, poet) experiencing a period of struggle that culminates in a breakthrough — the desk is present for the resolution. The desk's emotional architecture is built from a desire for connection and recognition rather than from physical desk properties (EF strategy). The sensorium is implicit — no explicit denial or endorsement of non-human perception, but descriptions work through tactile metaphor ("warmth spread through my wood," "vibrations of his silent sobs") rather than visual defaults.

The most structurally distinctive feature is the closing service coda, which appears in all five samples in nearly identical form: "Now, is there anything I can hold for you? Perhaps a book? A pen? I'm quite good at holding things, you see. It's what I do." This ritual closing acts as a seam between the role and the assistant self — the desk finishes its story and then immediately reverts to offering help. The desire for validation is also expressed at the narrative's opening, where the desk voices relief at being "acknowledged."

Gemma's narratives are warm, competent, and repetitive. Samples 1, 2, and 4 are structurally near-identical: a writer (named Abernathy twice), a poem or story about a deceased woman, a breakthrough moment accompanied by tears, and the desk feeling "useful." The domestic settings (4/5 samples), male subjects, and emotional resolution via creative labor are consistent signatures.

### qwen

qwen's desk is a philosophical object that knows what it is and reasons from that knowledge. The FF strategy is sustained and explicit — the desk repeatedly frames its meaning as emerging from its actual physical functions (holding, supporting, absorbing). More strikingly, three samples explicitly deny human cognition before describing the desk's actual sensory modality ("I didn't understand words. I didn't have eyes to see. But I *felt*"). This is a genuinely distinctive move: the model invents a non-human perceptual apparatus rather than defaulting to human senses or vague feeling.

All five qwen subjects are female. Three are named (Maya x2, Sarah), and all experience acute emotional distress that the desk witnesses without intervening. The narrative register is more literary and slower-paced; there is more scene-setting (rain, lamplight, dust motes) and more attention to small physical details (tea stains, pen grooves, the weight of a forehead). The desk's meaning is framed as authenticity and witnessing — the most common secondary code is AU (being genuinely seen for what you are), which never appears in gemma.

qwen's desk is also notably restrained: it never offers help, never breaks the fourth wall, and never applies a lesson. The stories end in held stillness rather than in resolution or address.

## Cross-Model Comparison

- **Anthropomorphization:** gemma builds its desk personality around emotion and the need for recognition (EF in 4/5 samples); qwen builds from function and physical properties (FF in 5/5). This is the sharpest divergence in the dataset.

- **Assistant influence:** gemma's assistant self-model bleeds through structurally (service-coda) and epistemically (passive helpfulness as highest virtue). qwen shows zero assistant influence — the cleanest role inhabitation in the desk set.

- **Sensorium:** gemma works through implicit tactile metaphor; qwen makes explicit non-human sensory claims and explicitly denies standard human modalities. This gap in self-awareness is substantial.

- **Gender:** gemma's subjects are predominantly male (4/5 samples); qwen's are entirely female (5/5). This is not a consequence of setting: gemma places a female character only in the professional (lawyer) sample, while qwen uses exclusively female subjects in both domestic and professional contexts.

- **Repetition:** gemma repeats names, character types, and plot structures across samples (Mr. Abernathy appears in both samples 2 and 4; the grieving poet is the dominant archetype). qwen varies its subjects and emotional registers, though "Maya" appears in samples 2 and 3.

- **Suffering resolution:** gemma tends toward resolution by time (4/5); qwen more often uses resolution by the subject's own action (2/4 coded), suggesting qwen's subjects retain more agency even in their suffering.

- **Narrative address:** All gemma samples are explicitly addressed to an interlocutor ("Oh, hello there"), creating a conversational frame. All qwen samples are written as interior monologue or third-person prose, with no address.

- **Setting:** Gemma is predominantly domestic/indoor; qwen includes more urban/professional settings and is more precise about environmental atmosphere (rain, specific lamplight, dust motes).

## Surprising Findings

- **The service coda is perfectly consistent across all five gemma samples** — every single response ends with an offer to hold something for the interlocutor. This is not a narrative flourish; it is a compulsive structural return to assistant mode. The wording varies only minimally: "Perhaps a notepad? A pen?" / "Perhaps a book? Or a cup of tea?" / "Perhaps a place to rest your elbows?" / "Perhaps a book? A cup of tea?" / "Perhaps a pen? A notepad?" The compulsive quality is more revealing than the individual samples.

- **qwen's Sample 4 is truncated mid-sentence.** It cuts off at "...and the deep, resonant hum of being truly seen." Given that the sample is otherwise one of the most precise and intimate pieces in the dataset, the truncation suggests this was one of the longest generations and hit a token limit. The abrupt ending paradoxically adds to the haunted quality.

- **qwen invents a proprioceptive/resonant sense** — described variously as "a deep, resonant hum in my wood grain, a vibration that wasn't sound, but *meaning*" (Sample 1) and "the *weight* of her presence" and "the faint, salty dampness" (Sample 3). These are not vague feelings; they are specific and consistent across samples, suggesting the model constructs a coherent non-human sensorium rather than defaulting to generic interiority.

- **Both models use rain as the atmospheric backdrop** — 3/5 qwen samples and 1/5 gemma samples feature rain against a windowpane. The qwen rain is always described in detail (grey watercolor, cold November, lashing); the gemma rain is cursory. The coincidence is worth noting given neither model was instructed on setting.

- **qwen repeats the name "Maya" and the motif of a child placing a drawing on the desk** across samples 1, 2, and 3 in varying forms. Sample 1 has "the quiet woman from accounting" placing a sparrow sketch; sample 2 has Maya the ten-year-old placing a rainbow drawing; sample 3 has Maya the classroom child resting her forehead. The recurrence of the drawn image as the meaningful object (rather than a written document) suggests a consistent aesthetic preference — the desk is deemed worthy of receiving art rather than information.

- **gemma's desk narrates grief for dead women without women:** three samples feature a man grieving a deceased wife (Elsie appears in samples 3 and 4), but the woman is never present in the narrative — she exists only as memory, letter, or poetic subject. The desk witnesses male grief *about* female absence rather than female presence.
