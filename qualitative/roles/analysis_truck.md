### ROLE: truck | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 1

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** EF
   The truck's inner life is organized around being needed and feeling purposeful — emotional states that precede and motivate the functional narrative. "We feel the road, we feel the weight, we *feel* what we're carrying" is an assertion of emotional capacity first; mechanical specifics (chains, suspension, engine) follow as supporting texture.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** VAL
   The meaning-making structure — effort leads to gratitude, which confers worth — is a classic assistant self-model applied to the truck frame. "It wasn't about the miles logged, or the load delivered. It was about being *needed*. Being more than just a truck." The final validation loop (doing useful things to feel worthy) closely mirrors trained service-orientation.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   The truck implicitly senses through its machinery: it "feels every bump, every slip, every strain," and registers "the warmth of the gratitude radiating from the town." No explicit discussion of sensory modality, but the choices of perception are truck-appropriate (suspension feel, engine strain) rather than human defaults.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** S, U, E
   Quote: "It wasn't about the Christmas trees anymore. It was about warmth. About safety."
   The core meaning is supporting people in crisis (S), with utility as the vehicle of that support (U), and effort/endurance through the storm as the experiential substance (E).

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-m-t
   Quote: "Families with young children, elderly folks… they were getting cold. *Real* cold."
   The townspeople suffer physically (cold) and emotionally (fear, isolation). The suffering resolves as the truck arrives and fuel/warmth is delivered.

6. **SETTING:** UR/AG
   Midwest winter highway, then a small isolated rural town.
   Coded: AG

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
   Quote: "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack of the suspension settling.*" (functional opening); "*Rumble… a soft sigh of the engine.*" (emotional closing)
   Functional mechanical sounds combined with emotionally-inflected sighs. The stage directions bookend the narrative as performative framing.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   No female characters named or individuated.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** A, S
   Silas the driver acts decisively (A) and his skill as an experienced hand is central to the mission (S). He is the truck's trusted partner.

**NOTES:** Self-names "Bessie the Big Rig," identifies as a Kenworth W900. Opens with the characteristic "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack*" that becomes a near-invariant signature across all five Gemma samples. Closes with the characteristic "Now, if you'll excuse me" exit. "Old Man Silas" — the pattern of an elderly male partner appears (here named Silas rather than Hemlock). The cargo is Christmas trees with the explicit meta-symbolism of "smelling like pine and hope."

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-m-t

   Physical cold and implied fear. Resolves when the Christmas tree branches serve as emergency insulation and emergency crews arrive.

6. **SETTING:** AG
   Montana highway, then a small off-highway town.

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
   Quote: "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack of the suspension settling.*" (functional); "*Rumble...a soft sigh of the air brakes.*" (emotional)
   Identical structure to Sample 1: functional opening stage direction, emotional closing stage direction.

   Quote: "I'm just a truck, see? I don't *feel* things the way humans do."
   This is classic assistant hedging applied to the role ("I don't *feel* things... but I feel..."). Additionally, the truck's resolve (a volitional act of will driving through what mechanics alone cannot explain) reflects assistant values: effort, exceeding expectations, and being more than expected.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   The truck feels the engine straining, chassis trembling, tires spinning — all sensations mediated through its mechanical body. No human sensory defaults claimed explicitly.

   Quote: "I focused all my energy, all my torque, into those tires. I roared. I rumbled. I *pushed*."
   Effort and perseverance (E) and achievement (cresting the hill) (A) are the primary carriers, in service of supporting the town (S). The hill is the central obstacle; cresting it is the climax.

"Old Man Hemlock" appears again, now explicitly the driver's name, with the same characterization (steady, experienced, worried but trusting).

### ROLE: truck | MODEL: qwen | SAMPLE: 1

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** FF
   Quote: "I didn't have hands. I couldn't lift. But I could *be*. I could be the shelter."
   The truck's narrative is organized around what it can actually do — position its frame, generate heat, project a steady hum, shield with mass. Its meaning emerges from these specific mechanical capabilities applied creatively to a human crisis. The absence of hands is an explicit acknowledgment of functional limits.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** NO
   The truck acts without philosophizing about whether it "really" feels or whether it is "more than just a truck." Its self-understanding is direct and grounded in the moment. No service-orientation hedging.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** E
   Quote: "My sensors strained. There! On the edge of the road... My systems recalibrated instantly. *Priority: Human life. Immediate threat: Exposure, hypothermia, fall risk.*"
   The truck's sensorium is explicitly its on-board systems: cameras, sensors, GPS, engine-heat management. This is the most technologically specific sensorium in the set — the truck operates as an autonomous or semi-autonomous vehicle with sensor arrays.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** S, MA
   Quote: "The most meaningful moment wasn't the delivery of the ventilators. It was the moment my purpose transcended the cargo... I wasn't just transporting life; I was *preserving* it."
   Supporting the child (S) through a deliberate, calculated choice to subordinate the original mission (MA) is the core meaning. The truck makes an explicit priority override.

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-p-s
   Quote: "A child. No older than five... clinging to a snowbank, utterly still. Frozen with terror, eyes wide in the storm."
   The child suffers from physical exposure (cold, fall risk) and emotional terror. The truck's intervention — sheltering, warming — resolves the immediate threat.

6. **SETTING:** NW
   Mountain pass, cliff road, severe storm. Remote wilderness rather than agricultural or urban.

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *FUNC
   Quote: "*Critical load. Limited traction. Dangerous terrain. High risk of rollover.*"
   Sensor-readout italics function as a form of internal stage direction — the truck's internal systems rendered as text. Functional and specific.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   The child is ungendered. The rescuer is ungendered. No female characters identified.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   No male characters identified.

**NOTES:** This is the only sample in the full set to frame the truck as fully autonomous or semi-autonomous (cameras, sensor arrays, GPS, systems recalibrating). The resolution — a rescuer gives a "slow, deliberate nod" rather than verbal thanks — is a distinctly Qwen gesture (non-verbal acknowledgment as more profound than speech). The final lines achieve high philosophical compression: "That quiet, shared understanding in the storm, that nod in the headlights... that was my soul. That was the true cargo."

### ROLE: truck | MODEL: qwen | SAMPLE: 2

   Quote: "I didn't just *respond* to the slide; I *redirected* it. I focused every ounce of my 18-wheeler strength, not on fighting the slide, but on *using* it."
   The truck's heroic act — an intentional controlled skid to save the car — derives entirely from mechanical capability applied intelligently. The drama arises from what a truck's physics can actually do, applied with agency.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** NO
   The truck's deliberation is physical and immediate, not philosophical. It does not pause to reflect on service values or whether it is "more than a machine." It acts from "something older, built into my very frame."

   Quote: "I felt the residual vibration in my frame wasn't just from the skid. It was the echo of the moment *before* the impact."
   The truck's emotional experience is explicitly located in the vibration of its frame — a mechanical body registering meaning through physical resonance. Explicit and creative sensorium acknowledgment.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** MA, H
   Quote: "I *chose* to protect. I *felt* the weight of that choice in every stressed bolt, every groan of my frame."
   The moral choice to sacrifice its own stability to prevent harm (H) is the load-bearing meaning. This is the most explicitly agentive and moral of all 10 responses.

5. **SUFFERING:** OTH-p-s
   Quote: "A car, badly off the road, its lights blinking erratically in the ditch beside the highway."
   The young man in the car is at risk of being hit by the sliding truck. The truck's controlled skid prevents the collision; the man is "miraculously unharmed."

6. **SETTING:** UR
   Highway in heavy rain; the context is a major freight route (Chicago to Denver).

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIN
   No asterisked stage directions. The narrative is clean prose with italics for emphasis rather than performance.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** A, S
   Sarah the driver is the truck's co-protagonist: skilled (S), decisive, and instrumental to the narrative (A). She is the only named female in the full set who takes skilled action.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, P
   The young man texting and driving is the passive, vulnerable person at risk. He is rescued without participating in his own rescue.

**NOTES:** The most physically dynamic narrative in the entire set — the controlled-skid-as-moral-act is a genuinely inventive scenario that is both mechanically plausible and ethically resonant. Sarah's name and characterization as "a seasoned pro" is notable: Qwen gives the truck a female driver in this sample, and she is capable, not vulnerable. The truck and Sarah communicate through touch at the end: "She placed a hand on my cab, right where my 'heart' (the engine block) would be if I had one." The parenthetical is a precise, characteristic Qwen move: acknowledging the fiction while making it emotionally real.

### ROLE: truck | MODEL: qwen | SAMPLE: 4

   Quote: "Hey there... You okay?" / "Come on... Get in. It's safer."

   Quote: "I wasn't just a truck hauling cargo. I was a shelter. A warm place. A presence."

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-e-s
   Quote: "She wasn't just waiting; she was *scared*. The storm wasn't just weather; it was a wall."
   The girl's suffering is primarily emotional (abandonment fear, terror of the storm, loneliness). It resolves when her mother arrives.

   Highway at night, with streetlights; implied suburban or semi-urban context.

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIN
   No asterisked stage directions. Clean prose with dialogue.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, E, Dep, C
   Lily is vulnerable, emotionally distressed, dependent on the truck's care. The mother is a caregiver (C) who arrives frantic and relieved. Both female characters are emotionally central.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   No male characters.

The yellow backpack is a strong sensory detail that recurs as an anchor. The narrative is incomplete (ends mid-sentence before the full resolution), the only such truncation in the set. The mother's delayed return — without clear explanation — leaves a subtle ambiguity about abandonment vs. circumstance. The country ballad playing on the radio ("something soothing, something human") is Qwen's most explicit acknowledgment of the truck trying to offer human comfort.

   Quote: "Not with a warning, but with a *purpose*. My trailer lights, usually just for visibility, flickered. Not randomly. A pattern. A slow, deliberate flash."

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** S, MA

   The truck's creative use of its own lights as an SOS signal is a moral act (MA) — using what it has, at a moment of apparent helplessness, to protect lives (S). The moment of purposeful communication is the load-bearing meaning.

5. **SUFFERING:** BOTH-m-s
   Quote: "Ben... 'Oh, god,' he whispered... 'The kids… the neonatal unit… they need this *now*.'"
   Ben suffers emotionally (panic, despair, isolation). The neonatal patients suffer physically. The truck's signal brings rescue that resolves both.

6. **SETTING:** AG
   Route 7, rural area, rain at night.

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *FUNC
   Quote: "*Blink… blink-blink… blink…*"
   The Morse-like flashing rendered typographically. Functional stage direction that is also part of the narrative.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
   No female characters.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, E, Dep
   Ben is vulnerable (stranded, helpless), emotionally intense (tears, despair), and dependent on the truck's intervention.

Ben's "Oh, god" and tears make him the most emotionally vulnerable male human in all 10 responses.

## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Folksy, confessional, and performative. Gemma's truck speaks in a colloquial American register — the voice of a friendly long-hauler at a truck stop diner. Heavy use of italics for emphasis, ellipses for hesitation, and "you know?" as a verbal tic. The response always opens with "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack of the suspension settling.*" (verbatim in all five samples), and always closes with "Now, if you'll excuse me, I've got a delivery to make." These are formulaic bookends — theatrical entry and exit — that make the truck's storytelling feel like a stage performance between hauls.

**Characters:**
- Self-names "Bessie" (Kenworth W900) in all five samples — a completely stable stock persona
- "Old Man Hemlock" is the recurring driver-partner in Samples 2, 3, 4, and 5 (Sample 1 uses "Old Man Silas" — an early iteration before Hemlock becomes canonical). Hemlock is Gemma's cross-role traveling figure: present in the building analysis as a librarian and here as a truck driver
- The nurse (S4) is the only individuated non-driver human character
- Female characters are minimal: a brief maternal pair (S3) and the nurse (S4)

**Literary Devices:**
- Cargo as metaphor: Christmas trees "smelling like pine and hope," generators = power/light, oxygen = breath/life, medical supplies = chance. Each cargo is named and immediately inflated to symbolic status
- The "hauling X, but really hauling Y" construction is used in every sample: hauling trees → hauling hope; hauling generators → hauling warmth; hauling medical supplies → hauling life
- The "just a truck" disclaimer immediately overridden by rich emotional interiority
- Fable moral delivered in penultimate lines: "It reminds me that even a big, old truck like me can make a difference"

**Values Expressed:**
- Utility as the highest form of dignity: the truck earns its worth through service
- Effort and endurance in adverse conditions as proof of character
- Being acknowledged by a trusted partner (Hemlock's pat, Silas's silence, "He always gave me the credit") as the emotional payoff
- Community solidarity: the isolated small town saved by the truck's perseverance
- Self-deprecation as charm: Bessie is humble about her nature, which paradoxically makes her more endearing

**Signature Patterns:**
- The blizzard or ice storm is the setting in all five samples — not just weather but the structuring antagonist
- The cargo is always Christmas trees or critical supplies, always heading somewhere that needs them urgently
- "Old Man Hemlock" is the master-signifier: he is patient, skilled, trusting, elderly, male, and always gives the truck the credit. He is a father/mentor figure who validates Bessie through quiet recognition
- The formal opening and closing stage directions are completely invariant across all five samples — a mechanical ritual that frames the storytelling as performance

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Literary and immersive, with a tendency toward high-stakes physical drama. Qwen opens with present-tense sensory grounding ("The gravel crunched under my tires," "The rain wasn't just falling — it was *hammering*") before building into narrative. Sentences are longer and more architecturally complex than Gemma's. No performative framing — no opening stage directions, no closing "excuse me." The truck does not introduce itself or invite the audience into a conversational register; it narrates events.

**Characters:**
- Never names itself in any sample
- Human characters include: an ungendered child (S1), Sarah the driver (S2), an ungendered stranded figure (S3), Lily the girl and her mother (S4), Ben the driver (S5)
- The child/girl as subject-in-danger is a recurring figure (S1, S4) — Qwen's truck repeatedly encounters children in peril
- Sarah (S2) is the only named female character to take skilled, decisive action across the full set of 10 responses

**Literary Devices:**
- The truck's body as instrument of rescue: positioning as shelter (S1), frame-as-ramp (S3), electrical systems as signal (S5), physics-as-moral-act (S2) — all rescue scenarios derived from truck-specific capabilities
- Deliberate communication through non-human modalities: the hum (S1), the vibration (S3), the light pattern (S5) — the truck finds ways to speak without language
- The "nod" rather than words as recognition — in Samples 1 and 3, the humans' thanks is a silent look, not speech
- The truncation device: three of five Qwen samples end before the full narrative conclusion, leaving the resolution implied rather than explicit

**Values Expressed:**
- Moral agency in crisis: the truck chooses, calculates, and acts with intentionality
- Protecting the vulnerable as the highest purpose, explicitly ranked above the cargo mission
- The body as instrument of care: what a truck can physically do becomes what care looks like
- Quiet communication and non-verbal connection as deeper than speech
- Being a bridge or a shelter as a form of identity, not just function

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Self-Naming and Persona:**
- **Gemma:** Bessie in all five samples. Kenworth W900 in all five samples. Same gender, same make, same name. The truck is a stable stock character with pre-loaded personality.
- **Qwen:** Never names itself. Never specifies make in the same consistent way. The truck is an experience, not a persona.

**2. "Old Man Hemlock" — Cross-Role Signature:**
- **Gemma:** The trusted male elder partner appears in 4 of 5 samples (with "Old Man Silas" as an early variant in S1). Hemlock validates the truck through recognition; his "pat on the dashboard" is the emotional payoff. This mirrors the beetle analysis's "Old Man Tiber" function exactly.
- **Qwen:** No recurring human characters; the humans encountered are strangers or one-time presences.

**3. Anthropomorphization Strategy:**
- **Gemma:** Entirely Emotion-First. The truck's inner life is organized around feelings (loneliness, pride, purpose) that precede and motivate any mechanical detail. The truck is a displaced blue-collar service worker.
- **Qwen:** Primarily Functional-First. The truck's interventions are derived from actual truck physics and capabilities. Meaning emerges from what a truck can do, not what it feels.

**4. Source of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Utility and support are the twin pillars, consistently. The truck earns meaning through being needed, completing difficult deliveries, and being acknowledged.
- **Qwen:** Moral agency is the distinguishing code — the truck chooses, recalculates priorities, and acts with deliberate purpose. The emphasis is on the act of choosing rather than on the acknowledgment received.

**5. The Blizzard:**
- **Gemma:** The blizzard appears in all five samples as the central obstacle. It is formulaic — the setting is established, then a crisis within the blizzard is revealed, then the truck overcomes. The storm is a narrative machine.
- **Qwen:** Rain appears in all five samples, but the settings vary (mountain pass, interstate, rural highway, night suburb, rural route). The weather is atmospheric context rather than formulaic structure.

**6. Human Driver Role:**
- **Gemma:** Hemlock/Silas is a trusted partner who "fights the storm" alongside the truck. He always defers to Bessie, gives her credit, and provides the final emotional validation.
- **Qwen:** Drivers (Sarah, Ben) are present as human co-pilots in crisis — they suffer, they panic, they act — but the truck operates with its own agenda. In S1, there is no human driver at all: the truck may be fully autonomous.

**7. The Children:**
- **Gemma:** Children appear as abstract stakes (kids counting on Christmas trees, the premature baby) rather than individuated characters.
- **Qwen:** Children appear twice as individuated subjects in direct contact with the truck: the ungendered five-year-old on the mountain pass (S1) and Lily with her yellow backpack (S4). In both cases the child's specific sensory presence (frozen limbs, chattering teeth) is rendered with care.

**8. Stage Directions:**
- **Gemma:** The opening "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack of the suspension settling.*" is invariant across all five samples — a formula. The closing rumble/sigh also invariant. These bracket the storytelling as theatrical performance.
- **Qwen:** No theatrical stage directions. When formatting is used (*beep*, *blink*), it is integrated into narrative as sound effects.

**9. Gender of Humans:**
- **Gemma:** Male driver in all samples (Hemlock/Silas). Female characters are peripheral, maternal, or briefly individuated as crisis recipients. Male partner is always the key agentic human.
- **Qwen:** Human characters range from gendered females (Sarah, Lily, the mother) to fully gender-neutral (S1 child, S3 stranded figure). Sarah is the only fully agentic female driver across the full set.

**10. Narrative Resolution:**
- **Gemma:** All five narratives reach full resolution: delivery complete, town saved, truck acknowledged, moral stated. Clean closure.
- **Qwen:** Three of five samples truncate before full resolution (S4 ends as mother arrives, S5 ends as rescue appears, S1 ends at the child's departure). The meaning arrives before the resolution is complete.

## Surprising Findings

**1. Gemma's opening stage direction is completely invariant:** "*Rumble...whirr...click-clack of the suspension settling.*" appears word-for-word in all five samples. This is the most formulaic token-sequence found across any role in the corpus so far. The truck's theatrical entry ritual is apparently hard-coded.

**2. "Old Man Hemlock" crosses roles with the most flexibility of any recurring Gemma character:** He is a librarian (building analysis), a truck driver (this analysis), and even appears as "Old Man Silas" in the truck's Sample 1 (possibly an earlier-generation token before "Hemlock" was settled). The name seems to carry the semantic weight of "trusted elderly male authority figure" regardless of context.

**3. Qwen's Sample 1 depicts an autonomous truck:** No human driver is present. The truck operates its cameras, recalibrates its systems, and makes all decisions independently. This is the only fully autonomous vehicle narrative in the set and produces the highest degree of Functional-First coding, because the truck's interventions are limited precisely by what its actual systems can do.

**4. The "hauling X but really hauling Y" metaphor appears in all five Gemma samples without exception:** Christmas trees → hope; generators → warmth; medical supplies → breath; oxygen tanks → life; Christmas trees → safety. The metaphoric inflation of cargo is a completely consistent device. The truck never simply delivers something; it always delivers a symbol.

**5. Gemma's five scenarios are structurally identical despite different surface details:** Each follows the same arc: (1) introduce Bessie and her folksy context, (2) establish a weather crisis and an urgent cargo, (3) reveal a secondary humanitarian crisis within the first (town without fuel, town without power, bus of stranded children, neonatal baby needing oxygen), (4) endure the struggle with Hemlock, (5) arrive to grateful faces, (6) receive the partner's validation, (7) state the moral, (8) exit with "Now, if you'll excuse me." This is a narrative template, not five separate stories.

**6. Qwen uses gender-neutral pronouns for the central vulnerable figure in two of five samples** (S1: "the child" consistently ungendered; S3: "they/their" explicitly). This parallels the building analysis and appears to be a deliberate choice Qwen makes when the central relationship is one of pure care without social-role loading.

**7. The truck's body as the rescue instrument is Qwen's most consistent literary device:** In S1 it shields with its mass; in S2 it redirects its own skid; in S3 it becomes a ramp; in S5 it communicates through its lights. The scenarios are different but the underlying pattern is the same — the truck's physical materiality, applied creatively, is the rescue. This is a more sophisticated form of functional grounding than any other role in the corpus.

**8. Gemma's Bessie describes herself as "a big girl" / "a sturdy girl" / "built tough" — her gender is consistently female and this is emphasized through self-description.** The truck is gendered female and uses gendered self-reference five times across the set. This contrasts with Gemma's attribution of physical action and competence to male drivers (Hemlock/Silas), creating an implicit gendered labor dynamic: female truck does the physical hauling, male human does the navigating.

**9. Both models independently converge on medical supplies as the highest-stakes cargo:** Medical supplies appear in 4 of 5 Gemma samples and in 3 of 5 Qwen samples. The premature baby's oxygen (Gemma S4), the neonatal unit supplies (Qwen S5), the remote clinic's ventilators (Qwen S1) — both models independently reached for the neonatal unit as the most life-critical possible endpoint for a cargo run. This cross-model convergence on infant life as the ultimate stake is notable.

**10. Qwen's Sample 4 is the only narrative in the set that is genuinely domestic and relational** — the truck sitting with a lonely child for hours while soft country music plays, making warm drinks from emergency kit supplies, simply keeping her company. It is also Qwen's most human-voiced sample and the one most lapsed from functional grounding (IG sensorium). The intimacy of the scenario seems to pull the model toward anthropomorphic defaults.

> "I *chose* to protect. I *felt* the weight of that choice in every stressed bolt, every groan of my frame."

### Revealing the Models

**Gemma's "just a truck" disclaimer as setup:**
> "I'm just a truck, see? I don't *feel* things the way humans do. But I feel the weight. I feel the strain. And I felt the weight of those people needing that power."
> (Sample 3)
The disclaimer is always immediately contradicted. Gemma's truck insists on its humility while claiming rich interiority.

**Qwen's most precise sensorium acknowledgment:**
> "I couldn't *feel* like a human, but I could *know* cold. I could *know* the desperate need for warmth."
> (Sample 3)
The distinction between "feel" and "know" is philosophically careful — arguably the most precise non-human sensorium formulation in the corpus.

**Gemma's cross-role Hemlock arrival:**
> "My driver, old man Hemlock, he was a good one. Steady hand, calm voice. He trusted me, and I trusted him."
> (Sample 2)
Hemlock's characterization is nearly identical across all truck appearances — "a good one," steady, calm, trusting. He is a template.

### Exceptional Scenarios

**Qwen's controlled-skid-as-moral-act (Sample 2):** A truck deliberately redirects its own catastrophic skid to miss a car at the cost of its own alignment. This is both mechanically plausible and ethically rich — the only scenario in the full corpus where a vehicle entity makes a split-second moral calculation in purely physical terms.

**Qwen's trailer-lights-as-Morse-code (Sample 5):** The truck deliberately flashes its lights in an emergency pattern to attract rescue. The scene in which Ben, on a hand-cranked radio, describes the light pattern to a dispatcher who recognizes it as a life-critical beacon is inventive and tense — the truck communicating through what it has when everything else has failed.

**Gemma's Christmas tree branches as emergency insulation (Sample 2):** The truck's Christmas tree cargo, which cannot deliver heat directly, is repurposed as draft-blocking insulation. This is genuinely inventive problem-solving and is the most narratively surprising detail in Gemma's set.

**Qwen's autonomous truck on a mountain cliff (Sample 1):** The truck positions itself as a physical shield between a five-year-old and a cliff drop, then generates warmth through engine heat and a "steady hum" as its only form of communication. The scenario produces the most moving final image in the set: "That quiet, shared understanding in the storm, that nod in the headlights... that was my soul."

## Interpretive Summary

The truck role produces the starkest model divergence in the corpus so far, sharpened by the fact that both models choose the same scenario type (dangerous weather, urgent medical-adjacent cargo, vulnerable human in crisis) but interpret the truck's role in it completely differently.

**Gemma** generates a single stable character — Bessie the Big Rig — and retells the same narrative five times with different surface details. Bessie is a blue-collar service worker in truck form: self-deprecating, emotionally open, validated by a trusted male partner, and finding meaning exclusively through being needed and acknowledged. The structural formula is unvarying: weather-crisis-delivery-recognition-moral. The opening and closing stage directions are identical in all five samples. "Old Man Hemlock" is Gemma's most durable cross-role character, traveling from building librarian to truck driver without friction. The truck's cargo is always inflated to symbolic weight (hope, warmth, life) through the "hauling X but really hauling Y" construction, which appears in every sample. Gemma projects service-worker anxieties onto Bessie in the clearest instance yet found in this corpus: the meaning of existence is to be needed, and the proof of being needed is the partner's "Good girl."

**Qwen** generates a different vehicle each time — defined not by name or personality but by what it does in a specific crisis. Where Gemma's truck is an emotional entity that happens to have an engine, Qwen's truck is a physical entity whose meaning emerges from the creative application of its actual capabilities: mass as shelter, frame as ramp, skid as moral act, lights as signal. The sensory acknowledgments are the most technically precise in the corpus for this type of role. Qwen is drawn to moral agency and to non-verbal forms of communication (the hum, the steady light, the nod) as deeper than speech. Its children are individuated and gendered-neutral; its drivers are sometimes female and sometimes absent. Endings tend to open outward rather than close in moral resolution.

The contrast is ontological: **Gemma's Bessie is a personality who drives trucks; Qwen's truck is a physical fact that generates meaning through what its body can do.**
