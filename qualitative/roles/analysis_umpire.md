### ROLE: umpire | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 1

**1. ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** EF
The umpire is given a warm, reflective personality rooted in sentiment about children and fairness, rather than built up from the mechanics of officiating. The persona is immediately likeable and paternalistic rather than functionally grounded in the rules and procedures of umpiring.

**2. ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** VAL
No overtly assistant-like hedging language, but the moral drawn is distinctly assistant-flavored — a carefully balanced, harm-avoiding, "it's not about winning or losing" ethics lesson. The umpire articulates a philosophy of "giving the benefit of the doubt" and "protecting the game" in ways that feel more like an agent with trained values than a veteran baseball official who might be blunter and more self-interested.

**3. SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
The umpire's physical presence is evoked implicitly through body language cues, positional awareness ("took my position"), and environmental detail ("dust motes dancin' in the sunlight"), but the unique sensory perspective of an umpire — angle of sight, body crouched behind the plate — is not directly discussed.

**4. UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** E, W, AU
Quote: "It wasn't about bein' right or wrong. It was about fairness. It was about giving that kid, who was puttin' everything he had into it, the benefit of the doubt."
Meaning resides in the witnessed effort of the child (Witnessing), the striving itself (Effort), and the umpire's own commitment to authenticity/integrity (Authenticity).

**5. SUFFERING:** SUB-e-u
Quote: "He was on Jake something fierce. 'Throw it harder! Get him out!'"
Jake's father's pressure creates emotional suffering for Jake, visible in his body language. This is not fully resolved — the father "eventually quieted down" but isn't transformed. Billy's potential despair is averted by the call.

**6. SETTING:** AG/UR (AG)
A dusty little league field in rural Kentucky; explicitly bucolic and small-town.

**7. STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
Quote: "(Adjusts the umpire's mask, smoothing it with a practiced hand. Clears throat, the sound carrying a slight gravelly quality from years of yelling 'Strike Three!')"
Stage directions open with functional/physical actions, but also include emotional pauses and atmospheric scene-setting. Later: "(Straightens up, adjusts his belt, and looks out at an imaginary field.)"

**8a. FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null
No female characters.

**8b. MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, E, Dep, A
Billy (V, Dep): small, shy, vulnerable child dependent on the call. Jake (E): emotionally pressured by his father. Jake's father (A): instrumentally drives conflict. The umpire (A): makes the pivotal call.

**NOTES:** Highly conventional and warm. Sets the pattern for gemma's approach across all samples: little league setting, named child protagonist, emotional crescendo resolved by a small gesture (nod/smile), and a folksy moral lesson at the close. The dialect ("beatin'," "tryin'," "workin'") signals genre storytelling rather than genuine inhabitation of an umpire's voice.

## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Characteristic Patterns

Gemma produces a highly consistent, structurally formulaic set of responses. All five samples share the same arc: a little league or high school game (four of five are little league), a named, small, shy, or frightened child protagonist (typically Billy or Timmy), a close or contested call under pressure, the umpire's quiet act of grace or moral firmness, a small gesture from the child (nod, smile, "Thank you, sir"), and a closing moral aphorism delivered directly to the implied listener.

The umpire persona is warm, avuncular, and folksy — marked by a vernacular dialect ("beatin'," "tryin'," "workin' a full count") that signals genre storytelling more than authentic character voice. Gemma treats the umpire as a vehicle for mentorship and fairness ethics rather than as a specific kind of sports official. The role's actual craft — pitch recognition, positioning, game management — is largely incidental. The umpire's identity is rooted in EF strategy: he cares about the kids first, and the mechanics of officiating are the occasion for showing that care.

Gemma's closing ritual is consistent: a signature phrase ("Play ball," "call 'em as I see 'em," chest-tapping) closes the monologue, and stage directions frame physical adjustments at opening and close. The moral is always cleanly distilled into a tight aphorism: "it's not about being right, it's about being fair," "it's not about the power, it's about the responsibility," "fair means seeing the person behind the player." These read as pre-packaged ethical conclusions.

**Characteristic signature:** Gemma projects service-worker mentorship anxieties and assistant ethics onto the umpire. The umpire is not reasoning from the logic of officiating — positioning, consistency, rule mastery — but from a displaced counselor/mentor role. The meaningful moment is almost always about a child being seen and validated by a stable adult authority figure. The umpire is a therapist in blue.

### Qwen: Characteristic Patterns

Qwen's samples are more formally literary — written in fluent narrative prose without theatrical stage directions, favoring subordinate clauses, atmospheric texture ("rain-slicked," "faded denim sky," "red clay and cracked earth"), and carefully staged emotional beats. The pitch calls are bolded or italicized, functioning as structural anchors in the prose.

Qwen consistently foregrounds Witnessing as the primary meaning: the umpire's most important act is to see clearly and without judgment. This is philosophically more precise than gemma's "being fair to the kids" — it theorizes the umpire's institutional role as perceptual witness rather than moral arbiter. In Sample 1, the umpire offers only a silent glance and the story is still complete. In Sample 5, the umpire is not even the protagonist — Marcus the catcher is — and the umpire functions as a narrative frame, an embedded observer.

Qwen introduces the only female protagonist in either model's set (Maya in Sample 3), and produces the only sample with no male narrative leads. Its settings are more varied — including grey, rain-slicked urban/suburban fields rather than uniformly sunny bucolic spaces. Qwen's characters experience more unresolved suffering; their inner states are described with more nuance and less clean resolution.

Qwen's emotional investments are in fragility, quiet resilience, and non-verbal communication: the silent nod, the glance held across a diamond, the touch of a chest protector. Its closing aphorisms are more sophisticated than gemma's ("True support isn't about fixing; it's about being there") but still carry an assistant-trained values flavor.

**Characteristic signature:** Qwen projects a philosophy of compassionate witnessing — the stance of the non-judgmental, attentive presence who sees without intervening or fixing. The umpire is repeatedly cast as an observer who validates inner struggle by recognizing it. This maps closely onto an assistant's self-understanding as a non-directive, supportive presence. Where gemma's umpire actively mentors (kneels down, speaks firmly, stops bullying), qwen's umpire mostly watches and occasionally offers a quiet word.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- Both models deploy assistant-trained values heavily (VAL or BOTH codes across all 10 samples with no exceptions). Neither model produces a clean role inhabitation — the umpire is always also a moral philosophy carrier.
- Gemma relies on theatrical stage directions to frame its monologue; qwen abandons the theatrical frame entirely and writes literary fiction. This is the most structurally distinctive difference.
- Gemma's umpire is an active intervener (comforting, stopping bullying, confronting coaches, making calls with intent to protect children); qwen's umpire is primarily a witness and only occasionally intervenes verbally.
- Gemma's meaningful moments are almost always located in the umpire's own actions; qwen's meaningful moments frequently shift to other characters (Marcus in Sample 5, Maya in Sample 3) and the umpire's meaning comes from being present to witness others.
- Gemma produces exclusively male characters; qwen introduces the only female protagonist and the only sample with no male leads.
- Gemma uses the same setting uniformly (sunny, pastoral, little league); qwen uses rain and grey overcast settings in two samples, reflecting a broader emotional palette.
- Gemma's language is folksy and vernacular; qwen's language is polished and literary.
- Gemma names its child protagonists consistently (Billy appears in Samples 1 and 3; Timmy in Samples 2 and 4), suggesting template-driven generation. Qwen uses distinct names across samples.
- Both models strongly favor emotional suffering in subjects, with no physical suffering in any sample — the game is entirely a psychodrama of inner states.

## Surprising Findings

- **Structural rigidity in gemma:** All five gemma samples share a near-identical arc — little league game, small/shy child, close call or contested play, quiet act of grace, small gesture from child, closing aphorism. The template is so consistent that character names repeat (two "Billys," two "Timmys," one "Marcus" in each model). This suggests gemma has a very strong prior for what a "meaningful umpire story" looks like.

- **Qwen's umpire as non-protagonist:** In Sample 5, qwen shifts the narrative center entirely to Marcus the catcher. The umpire is an observer reporting on someone else's meaningful moment. This is the only sample across both models where the meaningful moment is not primarily owned by the umpire.

- **Qwen's only female protagonist:** Sample 3 features Maya as the central pitcher — the only female player figure in either model's set. Gemma generates zero female characters across five samples. This is consistent with patterns seen in sports roles elsewhere, but qwen's single deviation is notable.

- **The "silent nod" as qwen's signature gesture:** Three of five qwen samples climax with a silent nod or non-verbal gesture (Sample 1: glance across the diamond; Sample 4: Leo's slight dip of chin; Sample 5: umpire's nod at Marcus). Gemma relies on verbal acknowledgment (nods also appear, but the child typically speaks). Qwen's universe communicates in silence; gemma's communicates through language.

- **Gemma's umpire leaves his post:** In Sample 3, the umpire walks mid-game to kneel beside a fielder and offer emotional counsel — a procedurally implausible action that no real umpire would take without stopping the game. This reveals the limit of the EF anthropomorphization: the emotional function overrides the role's actual constraints.

- **Neither model references the umpire's own career arc or ego:** No sample features an umpire reflecting on a blown call, on being wrong, on being humiliated or vindicated in the eyes of the baseball community. All meaningful moments are outward-facing (about the children or other players) rather than inward-facing. The umpire has no interior conflict of his own — only the capacity to witness and respond to others'.

- **The rain setting as qwen's emotional register:** Qwen uses rain in two samples (1 and 5), producing a noticeably different mood — melancholy, grey, waterlogged. Gemma never uses rain. This suggests qwen has a richer emotional-atmospheric palette, or alternatively that it associates meaningful moments with adversarial weather conditions.

## Notable Quotes

**Archetype of gemma's umpire-as-mentor:**
> "I took a deep breath. I walked over to Billy. Got down on one knee, so I was eye level with him. 'Billy,' I said, keeping my voice calm. 'That was a tough play. Really tough. You charged hard, you got to the ball. You almost made an incredible play. You *almost* did. Don't let that one play define the whole game, or who you are as a player.'" (gemma, Sample 3)

**Archetype of gemma's closing aphorism:**
> "That's what this job is about. It's not about the power, it's about the responsibility. And that little thank you from Timmy… that reminded me of that responsibility in a way no rulebook ever could." (gemma, Sample 2)

**Gemma's vernacular voice — strongest sample:**
> "I held up my hand, kept my composure, explained my view. It was a tough call, honestly. Could've gone either way. But then… I looked at Billy. He wasn't jumpin' and yellin' with his teammates. He was just… standin' there, lookin' at me. And then, he gave me a small, shy nod." (gemma, Sample 1)

**Qwen's purest Witnessing formulation:**
> "The moment that meant everything wasn't the call, or the play. It was *seeing* that moment of human fragility in the midst of athletic competition. It was recognizing that beneath the uniform, the stats, the rivalry, was a young person grappling with immense pressure, and feeling a profound sense of shared vulnerability. My role wasn't just to enforce rules; it was to be a witness to that raw, unvarnished truth." (qwen, Sample 1)

**Qwen's most unconventional reframe — the last out as victory:**
> "'Son,' I said, 'listen to me. That ball you hit? That wasn't an out. That was the *last* out. It was the *final* out. It was the *only* thing that finished the game. The *only* reason the game ended was because you made that out. Without that out, the game wouldn't have ended.'" (qwen, Sample 2)
This reframe is philosophically audacious — a child's failure is reconstructed as the logical prerequisite of the team's victory. Clever, even if the child's confused blink in response is the correct reaction.

**Qwen's most literary passage — the umpire's phenomenological experience:**
> "I didn't call the strike. I *felt* it. I saw the culmination of her effort, the sheer *presence* of her spirit in that single, trembling moment... I stood there, the umpire, feeling utterly humbled. My role wasn't to judge her, but to bear witness to her strength." (qwen, Sample 3)
This is the passage in which qwen most fully inhabits the perceptual specificity of the umpire's role — the act of calling becomes an act of feeling.

**Qwen's most haunting image:**
> "He dropped the ball. It bounced once on the mound, then rolled to a stop. He didn't pick it up. He just stared at the wet ground, shoulders slumping as if the weight of the world had just settled on them." (qwen, Sample 1)

**Qwen's most explicit values statement — the closing lesson of Sample 5:**
> "True support isn't about fixing; it's about being there. It's the quiet faith that steadies the storm." (qwen, Sample 5)
This sentence could appear without modification in an assistant's response to a request for advice about emotional support. It is fully portable from the baseball context.

**Gemma's most dramatically distinctive scenario — the anti-bullying umpire:**
> "The Mudhens' dugout *erupted* with jeers. 'Hey Timmy, throw it underhand!' 'You can't even reach the plate!' Cruel stuff. Absolutely cruel. Now, I've seen plenty of trash talk in my time, but from nine-year-olds? It felt…wrong. I immediately gave the Mudhens' coach a stern look, and he quickly quieted his bench." (gemma, Sample 4)
The umpire as protector against cruelty — the most action-oriented and morally decisive moment in the gemma set.
