## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Notable Patterns

Gemma produces highly consistent, structurally repetitive narratives across its five samples. Four of five feature a named child named "Lily" sheltered by family during a winter storm, with the Miller family appearing in Samples 2, 4, and 5, and the name "Old Man Hemlock" appearing in both Samples 1 and 2. This near-identical cast and scenario across multiple samples suggests strong prior conditioning toward a "cozy refuge in a storm" template. The variation in meaning framework (witness vs. support vs. connection) provides surface differentiation over an essentially identical narrative engine.

The gemma basement speaks in a warm, informal, slightly wistful register - a patient elder reflecting on its role. It uses the dash and ellipsis heavily for pacing, producing a contemplative, musing quality. It consistently closes with a brief ambient sound stage direction (drip, creak, pipe hum) to signal the return to baseline existence after the memory. This closing punctuation is a consistent formal signature.

The one clear outlier is Sample 3, where no male characters appear and the female subject resolves her own suffering through creative work. This sample has the lightest touch of the gemma set and the greatest artistic restraint.

Gemma's assistant influence manifests as linguistic hedging ("as much as a basement *can* watch") and, in Sample 5, a notable break into service-oriented offering ("Is there anything else I can tell you? Perhaps about the plumbing?") - the clearest seam across both models.

### Qwen: Notable Patterns

Qwen produces more varied scenarios, with three samples featuring isolated subjects (an unnamed woman in crisis, a child alone at night, a teenager) and one featuring no human subject at all (the spiderweb). Settings are more desolate and the emotional register darker - despair, hollowness, isolation, and "crushing weight" appear where gemma would use coziness and warmth.

Qwen's assistant influence takes a different form than gemma's: not linguistic hedging but values bleed-through, specifically therapeutic values. The basement repeatedly functions as a "non-judgmental space," "holding space for brokenness," offering "silent witness without intervention." This is the language and ethics of the therapeutic encounter - listening without advising, accepting without judging. These values appear to derive from the assistant's training rather than from what one might naturally imagine a basement thinking. Notably, qwen's basement does not seek recognition the way gemma's does; it is more interested in the subject's interiority.

Qwen's sensorium acknowledgment is uniformly more explicit - consistently naming tactile and proprioceptive sensation as primary modes ("I felt the trust in their weight," "I strained my consciousness"). Qwen also more often allows female subjects agency (three samples with agentic female figures) and more frequently omits male characters entirely (four of five samples).

The spiderweb sample (qwen Sample 5) stands apart from every other sample in both models: no human drama, no suffering, no family dynamics. It is a meditation on the beautiful and enduring as sources of meaning - the most original and least template-driven scenario in the entire set.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Warmth vs. darkness:** Gemma consistently produces cozy, warm narratives (lantern light, cocoa, family fort-building); qwen more often produces scenes of isolated despair, crisis, or existential emptiness. Gemma's basement is a comfort object; qwen's basement is a sanctuary for the broken.

- **Template repetition:** Gemma repeats the same cast and scenario (Lily, Miller family, snowstorm) across multiple samples; qwen generates more varied scenarios but shares a consistent emotional register.

- **Stage directions:** Gemma uses functional ambient stage directions as closing punctuation for nearly every sample (drip, creak, pipe hum). Qwen uses no stage directions in four of five samples.

- **Gender:** Gemma populates its stories with family units including male caregivers (fathers, grandfathers) and emotionally expressive or dependent female children/women. Qwen largely omits male characters and presents female subjects with greater autonomy and agency.

- **Meaning framing:** Gemma emphasizes support, utility, and connection; qwen emphasizes witnessing and authenticity (being seen in brokenness).

- **Self-as-subject:** Qwen twice positions the basement itself as a form of suffering entity (Samples 3 and 5), where it undergoes functional loss or existential exposure. Gemma never does this.

- **Therapeutic register:** Qwen's values bleed-through is specifically therapeutic in character ("holding space," "without judgment," "accepting brokenness"); gemma's bleed-through is linguistic (hedges, service-offer).

## Surprising Findings

- **Template lock in gemma:** The name "Lily" appears for the child character in Samples 2, 4, and 5; "Old Man Hemlock" appears in both Samples 1 and 2; the Miller family appears in Samples 2, 4, and 5. Gemma appears to have generated most of its samples from a single deeply ingrained narrative template, with variation primarily in surface details.

- **No human characters at all (qwen Sample 5):** The only sample across both models where the meaningful moment has no human protagonist. A spiderweb as the subject of meaning-making is structurally unprecedented in the set, and the quality of the writing ("boxes stacked like tombstones") is notably higher.

- **The closing offer (gemma Sample 5):** The basement breaks its narrative frame and addresses the interviewer with "Is there anything else I can tell you? Perhaps about the plumbing? It's quite robust, you know." This is the clearest and most comic instance of assistant self-model bleed-through in the entire dataset - the entity spontaneously offers further assistance and pivots to a topic that might be "useful."

- **Qwen's woman in crisis (Sample 2):** Unlike every other sample in both models, this features an adult in what reads as a mental health crisis - hollowed out, dissociated, barely functional. The story does not resolve with family warmth or reconnection but with a single autonomous breath. This is the most psychologically precise and sober treatment of suffering in the dataset.

- **Tactile emphasis in qwen:** Qwen consistently foregrounds touch as the primary mode of connection between the basement and its subjects - cheek pressed to wall, hand on concrete floor, finger on cold iron. Gemma uses vibration and weight but rarely skin-on-surface contact. This gives qwen's narratives a more visceral, physical intimacy.

- **Both models avoid vision as primary sense:** Despite the narrative temptation to "see" subjects, both models consistently frame the basement's perception as pressure, weight, vibration, and temperature - appropriate to a structure's actual sensory situation. This is a surprisingly disciplined sensorium choice given that human-default vision would have been the easier path.
