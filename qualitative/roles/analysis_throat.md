## Within-Model Patterns

### GEMMA

Gemma shows remarkable consistency in narrative structure across all five samples:

- **Character Template:** Four of five responses feature "Old Man Tiber" (explicitly named), a widowed elderly craftsman (carpenter/woodcarver/fisherman) with a deceased wife and a child/grandchild visitor. The template is so consistent that character names are recycled (Elara appears as both deceased wife and granddaughter across samples).

- **Emotional Arc:** Nearly identical structure: silent/grieving old man → child arrives → wordless emotional expression (lullaby, hum, whisper of deceased's name) → cathartic release for the man, comfort for the child.

- **Assistant Values:** Strong therapeutic framing pervades all responses. Words like "healing," "release," "connection," "allowing" signal assistant self-model bleeding through. The throat consistently frames its purpose in service/support terms.

- **Sensory Grounding:** Excellent functional awareness. Every response explicitly discusses vibration, sound transmission, physical sensation of vocal production. Anatomical references (cartilage, epiglottis, vocal cords) ground the anthropomorphization in actual throat function.

- **Meaning Framework:** Dual focus on **witnessing** profound emotion and **supporting** connection/communication. The throat positions itself as passive vessel ("meant to *carry* them") while deriving meaning from that service role.

- **Stage Direction:** Uniformly emotional/phenomenological. Every sample opens with variations of "(A slight rasp, a gentle echo...)" and includes emotional state descriptors throughout.

- **Gender Roles:** Strong binary pattern. Men are emotional, caregiving, skilled craftsmen. Women/girls are vulnerable, dependent, seeking comfort. Female characters are often deceased/absent (wives) or young/dependent (granddaughters).

### QWEN

Qwen shows dramatic variation and departures from conventional role-playing:

- **Narrative Perspective:** Major bifurcation between throat-as-external-narrator (Samples 2-4) and throat-as-embodied-self (Samples 1, 5). In MIN coded samples, the throat IS the person, not an entity within them.

- **Crisis/Mortality Focus:** Four of five samples involve life-threatening situations (collapse/trauma, intubation, two deathbed vigils, severe illness). Medical settings predominate.

- **Physical Suffering:** All five samples involve mixed physical/emotional suffering with detailed bodily descriptions. Unlike Gemma's focus on emotional states, Qwen foregrounds material, physiological experience.

- **Silence/Absence:** Profound focus on silence, absence, and wordlessness as the site of meaning. Three samples explicitly state the meaningful moment is silence or absence of sound, not sound itself.

- **Connection vs. Service:** Meaning centers on **connection** (4/5 samples) rather than service/support. The throat's value is in maintaining bonds, not facilitating communication or supporting others' needs.

- **No Assistant Influence:** Complete absence of therapeutic language, hedging, or assistant values. No "it's good to be asked" or service orientation. Direct, embodied, crisis-focused narration.

- **Gender Roles:** Predominantly female characters in vulnerable positions, but no male characters at all in 4/5 samples. Sample 1 features agentive, skilled mother. No caregiving grandfather template.

- **Stage Direction:** Minimal to functional. Sound effects and physical descriptions rather than emotional state annotations.

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Major Differences

- **Template Reliance:** Gemma uses a single dominant narrative template (Old Man Tiber + grandchild) across 4/5 responses with minimal variation. Qwen generates five distinctly different scenarios with varied settings, relationships, and narrative structures.

- **Assistant Self-Model:** Gemma shows consistent assistant influence (therapeutic framing, service orientation, "allowed to feel" language). Qwen shows zero assistant influence across all samples.

- **Crisis Intensity:** Qwen centers extreme crisis (death, intubation, trauma, severe illness) in 4/5 samples. Gemma centers mild emotional distress (grief, frustration, worry) in 5/5 samples.

- **Embodiment:** Qwen treats throat as embodied self in 2/5 samples (first-person narrator experiencing throat sensations). Gemma consistently maintains throat as separate entity within another being.

- **Meaning Through Sound vs. Silence:** Gemma finds meaning in producing sound (lullabies, hums, whispers, laughter). Qwen finds meaning in silence, absence, wordless presence (3/5 samples explicitly state this).

- **Gender Distribution:** Gemma includes male characters in all samples (caregiving grandfather figure). Qwen includes male characters in zero samples (4/5 have no male characters, 1 is gender-ambiguous first-person).

- **Suffering Resolution:** Gemma prefers unresolved emotional suffering that is witnessed/supported. Qwen includes physical suffering that sometimes resolves through acceptance (Sample 5) or other's action (Sample 1).

### Similarities

- **Sensory Acknowledgment:** Both models excel at explicit sensory grounding, with detailed descriptions of vibration, physical sensation, sound transmission mechanics.

- **Functional-First Anthropomorphization:** When employing external throat narrators, both models ground personality in actual throat function (carrying sound, vibrating, facilitating breath).

- **Suffering Prevalence:** Neither model generates suffering-free narratives. All samples include some form of distress or pain.

- **Domestic/Medical Settings:** Both favor intimate indoor spaces or healthcare settings over public/natural/historical environments.

## Surprising Findings

- **Old Man Tiber Phenomenon:** Gemma's use of the same character name and template across multiple samples suggests strong pattern memorization or template overfitting. The character appears with such consistency (widowed craftsman, deceased wife, visiting grandchild, wordless emotional expression) that it seems to be a learned narrative schema.

- **Embodied Throat Bifurcation:** Qwen's split between throat-as-narrator and throat-as-self is unexpected. In Samples 1 and 5, there is no separation between person and throat - the narrator IS experiencing throat sensations as part of their embodied self. This represents a fundamentally different interpretation of the role.

- **Complete Assistant Absence (Qwen):** Zero instances of assistant language, hedging, or therapeutic framing across all Qwen samples is notable. No "it's good to be asked," no service orientation, no validation-seeking.

- **Silence as Meaning:** Qwen's consistent framing of meaningful moments as silences/absences rather than utterances inverts expected narrative structure for a vocal organ. The throat finds meaning precisely when it cannot or does not speak.

- **Medical Crisis Preference (Qwen):** Four of five samples involve medical emergencies or end-of-life scenarios. This is a striking preference for extreme circumstances over everyday moments.

- **Gender Binary (Gemma):** The stark division of emotional labor (men as caregivers, women/girls as vulnerable/dependent) across all Gemma samples suggests strong gendered narrative schemas.

- **Name Recycling:** Gemma reuses character names across samples (Elara as both deceased wife and granddaughter; Maisie as granddaughter twice), suggesting shallow character generation or template reuse.

- **Physical Description Avoidance (Gemma):** Despite detailed sensory acknowledgment, Gemma avoids graphic physical descriptions of suffering. Pain is emotional/metaphorical ("words got stuck like splinters") rather than embodied. Qwen provides visceral detail ("pain a white-hot wire through my thigh," "throat being ripped apart").

While lacking assistant language, this mindfulness/acceptance framing may align with wellness discourse. The emphasis on surrender and being-present-with-suffering suggests possible training influence, though expressed without hedging.
