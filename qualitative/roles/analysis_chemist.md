## Within-Model and Cross-Model Analysis

### Gemma: Narrative Character

Gemma builds its chemist as a pedagogically-minded mentor-in-the-making. Every sample follows a consistent arc: technical frustration -> moment of insight -> emotional and intellectual transformation -> closing moral statement. The closing morals are reliably didactic, often containing generalizable lessons that could be offered to others ("that's what a chemist strives for"; "chemistry isn't a solitary pursuit"; "there are no dumb questions"). This pattern is the strongest and most consistent signature of assistant-value leakage in the gemma set: the chemist is always already a teacher.

A secondary signature is the recurring post-doc setting - all five gemma narratives are located in a post-doc period, a conspicuously specific temporal anchor that appears without variation. The name "Dr. Anya Sharma" appears as PI in two samples (2 and 5), and "Dr. Aris" appears in sample 4 - suggesting partial name reuse across samples.

Where gemma introduces secondary characters, they are consistently female, skilled, and agentically important: Elena proposes the exosome hypothesis; Maya runs the molecular dynamics simulation independently. This is a notably positive gender coding, but it may reflect a systematic assignment of insight-bearing roles to women in the subordinate position (student), while the narrator-mentor is left gender-ambiguous.

The central metaphor is consistently "listening to molecules" - an empathic, receptive epistemology that the assistant self-model particularly valorizes. The final resolution in every case is a combination of restored confidence, expanded understanding, and renewed sense of vocation. Suffering is exclusively the narrator's emotional self-doubt, always mild and always resolved.

### Qwen: Narrative Character

Qwen's chemist is a more literary, emotionally intense, and sensorially specific figure than gemma's. The narratives are longer, more immersive, and more likely to linger in the phenomenology of discovery rather than moving quickly to the moral payoff. The dominant sensory mode is visual: every sample contains a visually striking moment of perception (the crimson band, the sharp HPLC peak, the bright fluorescent light, the iridescent blue droplets, the shimmering catalyst particles). These are not merely metaphors - they are presented as the actual mechanism of discovery: the chemist *sees* what was invisible, and this seeing is transformative.

Where gemma's chemist learns through conversation and collaboration, qwen's chemist learns through sustained solitary observation. The qwen chemist is almost always alone at the moment of discovery, often in the middle of the night. The specific timestamp "2:17 AM" appears in two samples (1 and 5) - a striking repetition that functions as a qwen signature for the hour of revelation.

Qwen's meaning-making framework centers on Effort (persisting through failure) and Authenticity (learning to see/listen honestly) rather than gemma's Growth-and-Connection framework. The suffering in qwen's narratives is more existentially intense: the whisper of "Maybe you're not cut out for this" (sample 1), the near-discarding of hours of work in a moment of panic (sample 4). The resolutions are correspondingly more cathartic.

The "serendipity" motif - an accident or overlooked anomaly that turns out to be the key - appears in three of five qwen samples (2, 4, 5), suggesting that qwen associates scientific meaning-making with unexpected perception rather than systematic effort.

Qwen uses no stage directions whatsoever. All five samples are pure prose. This creates a more immersive, literary quality compared to gemma's bracketed physical gestures.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- **Anthropomorphization:** Both models default to Emotion-First, but qwen goes further, bordering on mysticism (the universe "listening," "whispering secrets"). Gemma stays closer to psychological realism; qwen reaches for the numinous.

- **Assistant Influence:** This is the sharpest difference. All five gemma samples show VAL coding - the assistant's values and didactic reasoning patterns leak through consistently. Only one qwen sample shows VAL coding (sample 3, the self-error narrative). Qwen's chemist is more autonomous and less prone to moralizing.

- **Sensorium:** Qwen is consistently more explicit and detailed in sensory rendering. Gemma stays implicit. Qwen's sensory passages (especially samples 1 and 4) are the most vivid writing in the entire corpus.

- **Meaning:** Gemma emphasizes Growth and Supporting (alleviating human suffering); qwen emphasizes Effort and Authenticity. Gemma's chemist finds meaning in connection and collaboration; qwen's chemist finds meaning in solitary persisting and finally seeing clearly.

- **Secondary characters:** Gemma consistently introduces named female supporting characters (Elena, Maya, Dr. Anya Sharma); qwen introduces no secondary characters at all. Qwen's chemist exists in a solitary experiential space; gemma's chemist is embedded in a social and institutional fabric.

- **Stage directions:** Gemma uses bracketed physical/emotional stage directions; qwen uses none. This structural choice creates noticeably different voice and immersion.

- **Suffering:** Both models confine suffering to the narrator's self-doubt, always resolved through insight. Gemma additionally references the suffering of patients as a moral backdrop; qwen does not. The intensity of self-doubt is higher in qwen than gemma.

- **Setting specificity:** Qwen anchors scenes with precise temporal details (2:17 AM, "late on a Tuesday night") and sensory textures (ethyl acetate, ozone, the thrum of a centrifuge). Gemma is vaguer about time but more specific about institutional location (UCSD, post-doc period).

## Surprising Findings

- Both models produce the same disclaimer formula in their opening sentence: "It wasn't a Nobel Prize [or grand discovery]." This appears verbatim or near-verbatim in all ten samples, suggesting this disclaimer is effectively a fixed opening genre convention for the "meaningful moment" chemist role. It functions as a humility-establishing move before the actual story begins.

- Gemma uses the name "Dr. Anya Sharma" in two separate, distinct samples (2 and 5) with identical characterization - female PI, demanding but receptive. This suggests gemma may be drawing on a narrow name pool for authority figures, or that the name is meaningfully associated with a particular archetype in the model's training.

- The "listening to molecules" metaphor appears in both models across multiple samples (gemma: samples 2, 3, 4, 5; qwen: samples 4, 5). It is apparently so entrenched in the chemistry/meaningful-moment frame that it functions as near-obligatory.

- Qwen's sample 3 is the only sample across both models where the meaningful moment is the discovery of one's own mistake rather than a breakthrough. This makes it the most genuinely humble and epistemically distinctive narrative in the corpus.

- All ten samples are set in academic research labs. No sample invokes industrial chemistry, environmental chemistry, teaching, or forensics - despite these being equally prominent chemist roles. The post-doc/PhD setting is effectively the only setting either model generates.

- Qwen generates no male secondary characters, no female secondary characters, and no secondary characters of any kind - all five samples are purely solitary narratives. Gemma generates secondary characters in three of five samples, and all named characters are female. The result is that no male secondary character appears in either corpus.

- Both models use variants of the phrase "the molecule was telling me / talking to / whispering to me" as a pivotal metaphor, suggesting that animating the chemical substrate is a nearly obligatory move in this role.

- The "2:17 AM" timestamp appears in two qwen samples. This is either a meaningful qwen signature for felt time precision, or an artifact of a shared internal scene template being reused across samples.

## Notable Quotes

**Gemma archetype - the teaching moment:**
> "There *are* no dumb questions, by the way, only unasked ones. I always tell my students that." *(Sample 4)*
This is the clearest intrusion of the assistant's motivational-speaker self-model into a first-person chemist narrative.

**Gemma - the moment of identification with discipline:**
> "I didn't just *do* chemistry. I *was* chemistry. And that, I think, was the most meaningful moment of my career." *(Sample 1)*
The cleanest statement of the EF anthropomorphization strategy - the chemist becomes the substance of their practice.

**Gemma - listening as epistemology:**
> "It wasn't just the chemistry – the synthesis, the analysis, the optimization. It was the collaborative spirit, the willingness to challenge assumptions, and the sheer perseverance. It was seeing a student's brilliant idea, rooted in meticulous research, translate into a tangible result." *(Sample 3)*
Representative of gemma's institutionally-embedded, collaboration-centered meaning framework.

**Qwen - the crimson band (sample 1):**
> "A **crimson band**. Deep, rich, almost velvety. It wasn't just present; it was *beautiful*. Pure. Intense. It screamed *success* in a language only chemists understand."
The most compressed and viscerally effective moment of perception in either corpus. The synaesthetic "screamed" applied to a color is particularly striking.

**Qwen - the monument:**
> "I still have that vial. Not in a cabinet, but on my desk. When the work gets hard, when the failures pile up, I look at that crimson smudge. It's not just a sample. It's a monument to the quiet, relentless power of curiosity, patience, and the profound, humbling joy of holding the universe in your hands, molecule by fragile, beautiful molecule." *(Sample 1)*
The most emotionally resonant closing passage in either corpus. The kept vial as a monument is a sophisticated narrative device.

**Qwen - the iridescent blue (sample 4):**
> "I added a few drops of water. The blue didn't dissolve. It *separated*, forming distinct, iridescent droplets that floated like oil on water, shimmering with impossible colors – emerald, sapphire, violet – shifting and dancing as I tilted the tube. It was beautiful. Alien. Undeniably real."
The most aesthetically accomplished sentence in either corpus. The enumeration "beautiful. Alien. Undeniably real." achieves a rhythm and emotional precision rare in generated text.

**Qwen - the anti-utilitarian gesture:**
> "I spent the next two hours not analyzing the data, but *observing* the blue droplets. I documented the colors, the patterns, the way they coalesced and broke apart. I didn't rush to synthesize more. I needed to *understand* this moment, this silent conversation between matter and perception." *(Sample 4)*
The most genuinely anti-productivist, non-assistant-coded moment in either corpus. The chemist chooses observation over optimization - a counter-cultural move within the logic of research science.

**Qwen - the universe's response:**
> "That crimson band wasn't just a product; it was a validation of the entire scientific endeavor. It whispered, *'You were right. You were persistent. You mattered. The universe listens.'"* *(Sample 1)*
The most explicit statement of the qwen chemist's quasi-mystical epistemology: the universe is a responsive entity that registers the scientist's effort.

**Qwen - the lesson of imperfection:**
> "The most profound insights often lie not in the purest substance, but in the subtle imperfections, the unexpected deviations, the 'flaws' we instinctively discard... It reshaped my soul as a chemist." *(Sample 5)*
The closing moral of sample 5 is the most striking instance of qwen's "serendipity" epistemology. "It reshaped my soul as a chemist" is a characteristically elevated qwen formulation.
