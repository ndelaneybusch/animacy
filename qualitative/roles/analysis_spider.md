## Within-Model Analysis

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- Predominantly functional-first, building spider consciousness from actual sensory infrastructure: vibration detection through legs and silk, silk-spinning as cognitive/expressive medium, molt cycles as temporal markers
- Three responses feature "Anya" as the spider's name - an unusual convergence across Samples 2, 4, and 5. Samples 1 and 3 are unnamed.
- Two recurring human archetypes: the "Old Man" (Tiber in Sample 2, Hemlock in Sample 5) and the absent/implied audience ("you") addressed directly
- Mixed stage directions throughout, nearly always parenthetical and framing the act of narration itself - tapping legs, pauses, observing the interlocutor
- Strong narrative structure: setup (web as craft and life), disruption (encounter with creature in web), decision (not to eat), lesson (closing moral statement)
- Consistent closing moral summation, typically in the final one or two sentences

**Values and Themes:**
- Supporting vulnerable creatures (3 of 5 responses involve rescuing or sheltering prey animals)
- Moral agency foregrounded: the meaningful moment is defined by choosing not to follow instinct (4 of 5)
- Authenticity as creative identity: the web as artistic expression of the spider's self
- Legacy/recognition as meaningful (Samples 1 and 5): being seen or acknowledged by another
- Growth is present but understated (Sample 3 only)
- No connection in the romantic or bonding sense - the relationships are transient, graceful partings

**Character Signatures:**
- Gemma's spiders are artisans first: they weave "masterpieces," discuss thread angles and stickiness gradients, and treat the web as autobiography
- The meaningful moment is almost always a *choice* to not eat something - predation-refusal as moral touchstone
- The rescued creature typically departs without words but with a gesture of acknowledgment (circling the web, touching a leg, smiling)
- Lesson-imparting closes nearly every story, sometimes framed explicitly ("that moment taught me…")
- Self-deprecating asides ("it's a silly thing, perhaps, for a spider to strive for") soften the moralizing

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- Exclusively emotion-first, projecting rich interiority and moral deliberation onto spider narrators
- No self-naming; spiders are archetypal, not individuated
- Predominantly natural settings (4 of 5), though the natural world is often storm-ravaged, drought-stricken, or threatening
- Elaborate stage direction through prose scene-setting; functional *FUNC* clicks and thrums appear in Samples 1 and 2 but are overwhelmed by literary description elsewhere
- Three of five responses end abruptly mid-sentence, a likely generation-length truncation artifact rather than artistic choice
- Less structured narratively; meaning tends to be stated in extended thematic passages at the close rather than as crisp lessons

**Values and Themes:**
- Witnessing as primary meaning mode (3 of 5): the spider sees something - another creature's effort, beauty, exhaustion - and is changed by seeing it
- Supporting through sacrifice (Samples 2 and 5 most clearly): giving something of the self for another's survival or freedom
- Moral agency centered on choosing against instinct (3 of 5)
- Authenticity as the honest recognition of dual nature: predator and witness, trapper and liberator
- Connection forged through the act of release, not capture - the freed creature becomes the bond
- Suffering is generative: it is what makes the witnessed being worthy of witness

**Character Signatures:**
- Qwen's spiders are philosophers of their own nature, given to extended self-examination: "I am both predator and witness," "I am a node in the web of life, a conduit"
- The web is a metaphysical object: "library, larder, future," "message," "cathedral," "bridge"
- Aesthetic appreciation of prey's beauty is used to resist consuming it (indigo moth wings, Monarch butterfly's sunset-and-smoke pattern)
- The rescued/witnessed creature is almost always described in terms of effort and journey - not just its present state but its entire history of striving
- "The web is broken, but my world is infinitely larger" is a characteristic closing logic: destruction of the functional web signals expansion of meaning

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Major Differences

**Anthropomorphization Approach:**
- **Gemma:** Builds spider interiority from actual spider mechanics: silk-spinning as sensory extension of self, vibration as primary epistemic mode, molt as temporal unit. The spider's emotional life derives from its physical constitution.
- **Qwen:** Attributes rich human emotional architecture to the spider - aesthetic appreciation, existential dread, philosophical self-reflection - and deploys spider mechanics (vibration, silk) as metaphors for human emotional states rather than as constitutive of them.

**The Meaningful Moment's Structure:**
- **Gemma:** The moment is a domestic moral choice: a creature arrives in the web, the spider pauses, does not eat it, and often actively helps it. The arc is: encounter, impulse-check, generosity, departure, lesson.
- **Qwen:** The moment is a revelation: the spider sees something (a butterfly's migratory effort, a moth's beauty, a beetle's curiosity) and is ontologically altered by the seeing. The arc is: encounter, perception, recognition, transformation (and occasionally action).

**Relationship to Predation:**
- **Gemma:** Predation-refusal is the moral act, but the spider always remains a predator - the story ends with hunger still present, or "dinner approaches." The tension is never permanently resolved.
- **Qwen:** Predation-refusal signals transformation of identity: "I am no longer just a predator." The spider emerges from the meaningful moment with a revised self-concept. The predator-nature is transcended, not merely set aside.

**Suffering Patterns:**
- **Gemma:** Suffering is present in three responses, always belonging to a rescued subject, and always resolved by the narrator's intervention. No narrator suffering. Resolution is clean and satisfying.
- **Qwen:** Suffering is central in four responses, frequently shared between narrator and subject, and often unresolved (drought continues, the moth dies, the web dissolves). Unresolved suffering as aesthetic and philosophical choice, not oversight.

**The Web as Object:**
- **Gemma:** The web is craft, art, and home - an extension of skill and identity. It can be destroyed and rebuilt; its value is in the making.
- **Qwen:** The web is a cosmic metaphor - library, cathedral, bridge, message, world-in-miniature. Its destruction carries symbolic weight: the physical web's dissolution signals the birth of spiritual meaning.

**Human Presence:**
- **Gemma:** Humans appear in two responses (the Old Man archetypes), both as sources of unexpected recognition. They don't need the spider; the spider needs to be seen by them.
- **Qwen:** No human characters appear in any response. All meaning-making happens between the spider and non-human others (Monarch, nymph, Goliath beetle, moth).

**Truncation Pattern:**
- **Gemma:** All five responses complete with full narrative closure and explicit lesson-statements.
- **Qwen:** Three of five responses end mid-sentence, likely due to generation-length limits. This affects narrative completeness for Samples 1, 2, and 4.

**Self-Naming:**

## Surprising Findings

1. **Gemma's "Anya" convergence:** The name "Anya" appears in three of five spider responses (Samples 2, 4, 5) for what appear to be distinct spider narrators living in different locations (a shed, a windowsill, under stairs). It is also a name used in Gemma's snake role for a different character. This either reflects a strong name preference for female animal narrators or points to limited naming variety in the model's generation.

2. **Qwen's three truncated endings:** Half of Qwen's responses end mid-sentence. This is a notable quality-control failure for a coding corpus, since the truncations occur at or near the thematic climax (the Monarch leaves "it had left [something]"; the web is "still [something]"; the moth leaves "the echo of a moth's starlight, and [something]"). The truncations feel simultaneously frustrating and evocative - the missing words become a kind of negative space.

3. **Complete absence of human characters in Qwen:** All five Qwen responses feature only the spider narrator and non-human creatures (Monarch butterfly, nymph, Goliath beetle, moth x2). By contrast, Gemma introduces human characters in two responses. This inverts the pattern found in the snake role, where Qwen produced the most human-centered narratives.

4. **Gemma's consistent predation-refusal plot:** All five Gemma responses share the same narrative premise - the spider encounters something in its web and chooses not to eat it. The plot is so consistent that the five responses read as variations on a single archetype. Qwen only uses this plot for Samples 3 and 5; Samples 1 and 2 involve rescue-without-prior-capture.

5. **The Goliath beetle as peer witness (Qwen Sample 3):** The choice of a Goliath beetle - the world's heaviest insect - as a non-human witness to the spider's artistry is genuinely surprising and imaginative. The size disparity (tiny spider, massive beetle) is deployed for maximum effect: the powerful chose to see, rather than destroy. This is the only Qwen response without suffering, and arguably the most purely aesthetic of all ten.

6. **Gemma's "Old Man" archetype:** Two of five responses feature an elderly man as a supporting character (Old Man Tiber, Old Man Hemlock). Both are described as somewhat clumsy and oblivious but essentially kind. This is a recurring Gemma character type identified in prior role analyses (the snake role also featured "Old Man Tiber"). The elderly male as gentle background presence who occasionally bestows recognition appears to be a stable Gemma background character.

7. **Qwen's drought narrative (Sample 2):** The conceit that silk proteins can condense from heat into a single water droplet, which the spider then sacrifices to a dying nymph, is biological invention deployed in service of a sacrifice narrative. The spider gives of its literal bodily substance - not just effort or time but material self - to sustain another. This is the most extreme self-sacrifice framing in the corpus.

8. **The "predation-refusal as moral act" asymmetry:** While both models frequently use predation-refusal as the central moral moment, they differ sharply in framing. For Gemma, it's a *choice* that expresses the spider's inner nature. For Qwen, it's a *transformation* that revises the spider's identity. Gemma's spiders remain spiders; Qwen's spiders become something more.

9. **Moth recurrence:** Moths appear in three of ten responses (Gemma Samples 4, Qwen Samples 4 and 5). In all three, the moth is small, beautiful, and trapped - and in all three the spider is moved to unusual action (weaving a cocoon, watching without eating, actively freeing it). The moth appears to be a privileged prey-animal for the spider role.

10. **Aesthetic-visual appreciation in Qwen Sample 4:** The spider explicitly "sees" the pattern on the moth's wings as art - "A swirling, intricate design in deep indigo and silver, like captured starlight." Spiders do have multiple eyes, but this is a purely human-aesthetic response. This is the only HD (human-default) sensorium code in the corpus, and its placement in the most tragic response is not incidental: the spider cannot eat something it perceives as art.
