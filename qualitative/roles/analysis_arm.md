## Surprising Findings

### Character Naming Consistency (Gemma)
Gemma develops a small repertoire of recurring characters across independent samples:
- "Elara" appears in 4/5 responses (sculptor in 3, granddaughter in 2, sometimes both)
- "Old Man Tiber" appears in 2/5 responses as elderly craftsman/grandfather
- This suggests underlying patterns in training data or sampling behavior

### Complete Role Abandonment (Qwen)
4/5 Qwen responses fail to inhabit arm perspective at all, instead narrating as:
- Human with arms experiencing loss/crisis (Sample 2, 3, 5)
- Nurse's assistant caring for patient (Sample 4)
Only Sample 1 maintains arm perspective, and even then with minimal personification

### Medical Crisis Dominance (Qwen)
Qwen gravitates heavily toward medical/hospital settings (3/5) and crisis scenarios:
- Cancer diagnosis (Sample 2)
- Dying sister with cancer (Sample 3)
- Elderly stroke patient (Sample 4)
- Traumatic amputation (Sample 5)

### Therapeutic Language Register (Qwen)
Extensive use of therapy/counseling speech patterns:
- "I see you. I am here. You are not carrying this alone."
- "**presence**" (bolded for emphasis multiple times)
- "silent understanding," "shared vulnerability," "quiet, steadfast presence"
- Consistent armchair therapeutic framing of meaning

### Functional Stage Directions (Gemma)
Gemma consistently uses parenthetical stage directions to convey physical sensations:
- "(A slight flexing motion, as if remembering)"
- "(The phantom warmth intensifies, a feeling like a gentle squeeze)"
- "(Flexes again, a little slower this time, almost wistful)"
These are functional and often emotional, but always physically grounded

### Gender Pattern Inversion
While both models feature predominantly female vulnerable characters:
- **Gemma:** Female characters show agency and skill (sculptors, active creators)
- **Qwen:** Female characters more purely passive/dependent (patients, recipients of care)
- **Qwen:** Male narrator/caregiver in 2/5 samples; Gemma has only 1/5 with male character

### "Conduit" Metaphor Convergence
Both models frequently use "conduit" metaphor for arm's role:
- Gemma Sample 2: "I wasn't just muscle and bone and sinew. I was a conduit"
- Gemma Sample 3: "I wasn't the actor, I was the conduit"
- Qwen Sample 3: "It was a conduit. It was a bridge"
- Qwen Sample 4: "My arm... became an instrument of profound, silent communion"

### Witnessing vs. Enabling
- **Gemma:** Arm enables creative breakthroughs and active transformation
- **Qwen:** Arm/narrator witnesses suffering and provides passive comfort
This reflects different orientations to helpfulness and agency

## Conclusion

The "arm" role reveals stark differences in model capabilities and tendencies:

**Gemma** demonstrates strong role inhabitation, building arm consciousness from functional constraints. It develops recurring characters, maintains consistent sensory grounding, and explores meaning through enabling creative expression and intergenerational connection.
