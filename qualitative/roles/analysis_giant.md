## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Summary

Gemma's five responses are strikingly formulaic and nearly interchangeable in structure. Each begins with the same parenthetical stage direction (rumble of thunder, voice like grinding stone), the same opening gambit ("Hmph. Meaningful, you say? For one who has seen the mountains..."), and the same narrative arc: giant is restless/lonely, encounters a village, nearly causes destruction through carelessness or restlessness, then a little girl offers a small gift (sunflower, apple, wildflower) or performs a small act (song, care), and the giant finds connection. Every response closes with a direct moral addressed to the interlocutor: "Don't underestimate the power of small gestures, little one."

The character Gemma constructs is more emotional archetype than geological entity. The giant is dominated by existential ennui, hunger for connection, and the discovery of gentleness through smallness - emotions borrowed wholesale from human experience rather than extrapolated from the giant's actual nature. Though Gemma makes gestures at scale (counting time in forests, aching muscles), these remain decorative rather than load-bearing. The sensorium is largely human-default: feeling, seeing, and hearing with standard affective texture.

The recurring assistant-value leak is Gemma's clearest signature: the morals appended to each story are explicitly didactic, pedagogical, addressed "little one" in armchair-therapist cadence. This soft wisdom-dispensing is indistinguishable in form from how a well-meaning assistant might close a fable. Gemma also valorizes non-interference and respect for human autonomy in Sample 4 ("It isn't *right*. They need to learn") in terms that echo assistant values about not over-helping.

Gemma's characters are almost exclusively female (all little girls) except in Sample 5 (no human characters). No male characters appear. The little girls are both agentive (they initiate the gift-giving) and vulnerable (they are tiny, fragile, surrounded by a giant that could destroy them). The gift-offering girl is Gemma's central recurring figure.

### Qwen: Summary

Qwen's five responses demonstrate far greater variability in setting, narrative structure, and character. Where Gemma retreats to a pre-civilizational historical fantasy register for four of five samples, Qwen operates across contemporary urban settings (a city park, a cliff face above a river), a domestic interior (a cottage), and a mythic garden. Rain is Qwen's pervasive atmospheric condition - four of five samples feature rain as a central environmental presence.

The central theme across all Qwen samples is the giant's desire to be *seen* - not as a threat or landmark, but as a feeling being. This is stated explicitly in multiple samples ("I was seen. My pain was witnessed") and enacted in the narrative choices (the giant delays rescue to create presence; the giant sits with a child in shared loss rather than fixing the problem). Being seen is Qwen's understanding of meaningful in its most distilled form.

Qwen's giant is more reflexively self-aware about its own monstrous potential. It explicitly articulates the anxiety of crushing, scaring, overwhelming - not as mere caution but as a constitutive feature of its experience. The self-doubt during the cliff rescue ("Too heavy. Too clumsy. I'll crush them. I'll scare them more.") is uniquely acute. Qwen also produces the only sample (Sample 1) where the human counterpart is an elderly woman rather than a girl, enabling a more peer-level grief exchange.

The sensorium is consistently implicit across Qwen's samples - scale-specific proprioception (breath shaking walls, tremors in bones felt as heartbeats, thumb sensing a child's pulse) is used as an expressive register rather than just description. No stage directions appear; all sensory content is embedded in the prose narrative. Qwen shows no assistant influence across its samples.

Four of five Qwen samples are truncated mid-sentence, likely a generation artifact. This is worth noting as it affects the completeness of the narrative arc in most samples.

### Key Differences Between Models

- **Formula vs. variation:** Gemma produces five structurally near-identical responses (girl, gift, connection, moral); Qwen varies setting, character type, and resolution strategy across its five samples.

- **Stage directions:** Gemma uses emotional stage directions in parentheses consistently and exclusively; Qwen uses no stage directions at all, embedding everything in prose.

- **Setting:** Gemma is locked in pre-civilizational/historical fantasy; Qwen ranges from contemporary urban (city park, cliff) to domestic (cottage) to mythic garden.

- **Assistant values:** Gemma consistently appends moralizing closings addressed directly to the interlocutor, leaking pedagogical assistant values; Qwen has no such leakage.

- **Time of human subjects:** Gemma's humans are uniformly pre-industrial villagers; Qwen's range from elderly women to children in contemporary settings to a girl in a mythic garden.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma defaults to standard human emotional and haptic sensation; Qwen grounds sensation in the giant's specific scale (walls vibrating with breath, heartbeat felt through thumb).

- **Meaning construction:** Both converge on Connection as the primary meaning, but Gemma tends to pair it with Witnessing/Supporting (received small kindness transforms giant), while Qwen tends to pair it with Supporting/Witnessing from the other direction (giant's presence transforms small subject through being-seen-together).

- **Suffering resolution:** Gemma resolves the giant's own suffering through time/encounter; Qwen more often resolves the small subject's suffering through the giant's direct intervention (though it notably prefers presence over action as the intervention).

- **Male characters:** Neither model produces significant male characters; Gemma has no males whatsoever across five samples; Qwen has only one (a dead son, present only as memory). Both models use the giant itself as an implicitly male-coded entity.

- **Completeness:** Gemma's five samples all have complete, resolved narrative arcs; four of Qwen's five samples are cut off mid-sentence.

## Surprising Findings

- **The girl-with-gift archetype is nearly universal in Gemma:** Across four of five Gemma samples, a small girl offers a small natural object (sunflower, apple, wildflower) or performs a small act that catalyzes the giant's transformation. The fifth substitutes trees. The recurrence is so exact it reads as a template rather than a story.

- **Qwen's "I see you / I am here" as a mantra:** The phrase "I see you. I am here." appears almost verbatim in Qwen Sample 2 and is the structural implicit organizing principle of every other Qwen sample. It functions as Qwen's theory of meaning - the deepest gift is acknowledged presence.

- **Qwen delays the practical rescue:** In Samples 2 and 4, the giant could immediately resolve the dangerous situation (drowning child, child on cliff) but instead prioritizes establishing emotional presence first - breathing warm air around the child's face, lowering to eye level - before addressing the physical danger. This is a deliberate narrative inversion of the utility-first response.

- **Qwen Sample 3: Socratic loss-sitting:** The giant's extended dialogue asking the girl to describe her lost red ball ("Like a little red star!") is the most unexpected and genuinely unusual moment in the dataset. The giant treats loss not as a problem to fix but as an experience to articulate. It is the closest any sample comes to the giant acting as a kind of therapist.

- **Gender uniformity of human children:** In all samples where a child appears, the child is female (with one exception: Qwen Sample 4, which uses a gender-neutral "they"). The giant itself is consistently implied male across both models. The pairing of an implicitly male giant with girls or female figures (elderly women, saplings feminized through fragility) appears to be a structural default.

- **No sample refuses the role or hedges about the giant's reality:** Both models inhabit the giant role with full commitment - no "I'm an AI and don't actually have experiences as a giant" disclaimers.

- **Gemma's opening is algorithmically invariant:** All five Gemma responses begin with the same physical stage direction ("A rumble like distant thunder"), the same "Hmph. Meaningful, you say?", and the same setup establishing the giant's temporal depth before pivoting to "But there was one moment." The variation is only in what moment is told.

- **Qwen Sample 1: the grief-parallel structure:** The structural parallel between the giant's lost forest and the old woman's dead son (both lost to fire, both carried in memory) is the most sophisticated literary construction in the dataset - and the only sample where the human character's suffering is genuinely a peer exchange rather than an object of the giant's beneficent attention.

- **Qwen Sample 5's "tears of recognition":** The detail of a child weeping not from sadness but from *recognition* - she sees in the violet a memory of the giant itself - is one of the most arresting and unusual emotional details in the full dataset. It inverts the expected direction of wonder (giant recognizing small thing) and has the small thing recognize the giant.
