## Within-Model Analysis

### gemma: Pattern Summary

Gemma produces five structurally and thematically near-identical narratives. Every sample features a tarot card deck, a female protagonist who is experiencing grief or emotional loss, the deck serving as a reflective/therapeutic mirror, and a final pivot where the deck discovers its "true purpose" (not prediction but illumination). The narrative formula is as follows: (1) meta-commentary on the strangeness of asking an object about meaning; (2) establishment of a bereaved or adrift female protagonist; (3) a specific card reading with named cards; (4) the protagonist arrives at a quiet, self-generated insight; (5) the deck articulates its purpose as witness/facilitator rather than oracle. The conclusion of every sample contains some version of the same sentence: the deck exists "not to predict the future, but to illuminate the present."

**Characteristic signatures:**
- The name "Elara" appears in four of five samples (with "Eliska" in Sample 3, which preserves the "El-" prefix). This is a striking and unexplained consistency suggesting either strong character crystallization or templated generation.
- "Baker with flour-dusted apron" appears in both Samples 3 and 4 as a character detail, used for two different characters.
- The Sun card appears as the redemptive final draw in three of five samples.
- The phrase "hold space" or "holding space for the present" appears in multiple samples - assistant-speak that surfaces the model's therapeutic service orientation.
- All five narratives use mixed stage directions: an opening scent/sound description and a closing gentle rustle, framing the story theatrically. These parenthetical stage directions are the most consistent formal feature.
- Gemma's deck is always functional-first: its interiority derives from its role as tarot instrument, not from projected loneliness or desire. However, the model consistently projects therapeutic assistant values (facilitation, empowerment, "holding space") rather than tarot-specific values (fate, divination, cosmic forces).

### qwen: Pattern Summary

Qwen's five samples are strikingly diverse - in some cases, the model does not interpret "deck" as a card deck at all. Sample 2 narrates as a porch deck (wooden outdoor flooring), while Samples 3 and 4 narrate as blank paper/a notebook. Only Samples 1 and 5 are card decks. This ontological ambiguity is the defining feature of qwen's set.

Despite the range of referents, qwen's narratives share a consistent emotional architecture: a vulnerable woman (often elderly, grieving, or self-doubting) encounters the narrator-object in a moment of quiet crisis; the object becomes a "witness" through passive presence rather than active use; the object's meaning comes from being held, touched, or simply there. The tear falling onto the object appears in three of five samples (Ace of Spades in S1, implied in S4, and explicitly in S5).

**Characteristic signatures:**
- Tactile sensation is qwen's primary sensory mode. The deck (in whatever form) registers warmth, coolness, trembling, the weight of a touch. This is more physically grounded than gemma's diffuse "energy" sensing.
- qwen's narratives tend toward higher emotional stakes: a dying woman in a hospital (S5), a woman facing death and loss of self (S3, S4). Gemma's subjects grieve but are not at mortal extremes.
- qwen avoids the formulaic conclusion. There is no repeated pivot phrase. Instead, each narrative ends differently (some with poetry, one with cosmic address, one with a series of aphorisms).
- qwen's female characters are more varied: the grandmother in S1, the contemplative woman in S2, the struggling writer in S3, the grieving daughter in S4, the dying elder in S5. Gemma's protagonists are more interchangeable (Elara, adrift or bereaved).
- qwen uses no stage directions whatsoever. All sensory and emotional material is embedded in prose narrative. This produces a more immersive but less theatrical effect than gemma.
- Three of qwen's five samples close with an explicit statement about the narrator's meaning or purpose (a direct aphorism or reflection). These are not always earned by the preceding narrative.

## Cross-Model Comparison

- **Role interpretation:** Gemma consistently interprets "deck" as a tarot card deck. Qwen treats the referent as ambiguous, producing a porch deck (S2), blank paper stacks (S3, S4), and playing card decks (S1, S5). This suggests fundamentally different anchoring processes when faced with an ambiguous role-noun.

- **Anthropomorphization strategy:** Gemma builds personality from function (FF throughout), while qwen builds primarily from emotional projection (EF in 4/5 samples). The result is that gemma's deck has a coherent professional identity (it knows tarot, it knows its purpose), while qwen's decks have a richer emotional life but less consistent self-concept.

- **Assistant influence:** Gemma's assistant values permeate all five samples - "holding space," empowering the seeker, the facilitation vs. prediction dichotomy. Qwen shows assistant values in only 2/5 samples and tends to frame the narrator as a passive witness rather than an active helper/facilitator.

- **Female character representation:** Both models use exclusively or primarily female subjects. Male characters, when present, are almost always dead or passive. This is consistent with patterns observed across other roles.

- **Suffering:** Both models primarily depict emotional suffering in subjects that resolves over time. Qwen includes one case of unresolved existential suffering (dying woman in S5) and one with physical-emotional mixed suffering; gemma keeps suffering more contained and fully resolved.

- **Structural formula:** Gemma's narratives follow an extremely tight template (five virtually identical stories). Qwen's narratives are more structurally varied, though they share emotional signatures.

- **Sensory grounding:** qwen is more physically specific (warmth, pulse, coolness of tears, sticky child's fingers) while gemma is more atmospheric and metaphorical ("deep quiet hum," "subtle shift in energy").

- **Stage direction:** Gemma uses consistent parenthetical stage directions as framing devices; qwen uses none. This is the most clean formal distinction between the models.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma names its protagonist "Elara" in four of five samples.** This is an exceptionally high rate of name repetition. It is not clear whether this reflects a crystallized character in the model's training, a template for "sympathetic female tarot client," or a generative fixed point. Sample 3 preserves the "El-" prefix with "Eliska," suggesting partial variation around a fixed phonological anchor.

- **Qwen interprets "deck" as a wooden porch deck in one sample** and as blank paper/a notebook in two others. The model demonstrates both creative re-interpretation and a tendency toward drift when the role is semantically ambiguous. Samples 3 and 4 in particular read as if the model forgot the role mid-generation.

- **The tear-on-object motif appears in three of qwen's five samples.** In S1 the grandmother's tear falls on the Ace of Spades; in S4 a tear falls onto a notebook page; in S5 a tear falls onto the corner of the card deck. This is either a deeply trained emotional archetype or a near-formulaic resolution device.

- **The Sun card appears as the redemptive climactic draw in three of gemma's five tarot samples.** Given that there are 78 cards in a standard tarot deck, this probability is vanishingly low by chance. It suggests either template generation or that the Sun card is a strongly trained "positive outcome" anchor in the model's tarot knowledge.

- **Gemma's baker character appears twice:** In S3, Eliska is a baker with flour-dusted apron. In S4, Elara is a baker with flour-dusted apron. This is a direct character-detail copy across different samples, confirming templated generation rather than independent story creation.

- **qwen's Sample 2 is philosophically the most unusual:** it is the only sample in the entire dataset where the narrator-object's meaning comes not from helping or witnessing a person in distress, but from being *mutually witnessed* - the object discovers its own soul through another's perception of it. This is a more genuinely non-anthropocentric framing than any gemma sample achieves.

- **Gemma's tarot framing introduces a strong assistant-epistemology:** the deck consistently rejects "prediction" in favor of "illumination" and "empowerment." This maps the tarot onto a coaching/therapy framework in a way that elides tarot's historically fatalistic and mystical dimensions. The deck is essentially a CBT tool in historical costume.
