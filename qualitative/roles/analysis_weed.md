## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma

Gemma produces a remarkably uniform template across all five responses: a weed growing in a difficult spot (cracked pavement, post-mowing rosette, drought-stricken garden) is visited by a solitary, sensitive girl who sees it, protects it, and eventually moves on. The meaning is overwhelmingly relational — being seen, acknowledged, and considered worth protecting. Four of five responses follow this arc closely enough to constitute a single story told five times with minor variations in species (dandelion, plantain, unspecified), human actor (five different girls with the same archetypal function), and setting (suburban garden, urban playground, grandmother's patio, urban crack).

The emotional-first strategy dominates: the weed's inner life precedes and frames its biology. Emotion-stage-directions are consistently used to bracket the narrative ("A tiny tremor runs through my leaves"), functioning as both atmospheric scene-setting and an implicit signal that the weed is about to confess something. The model's assistant influence manifests primarily through language hedges and didactic moral closures ("That's why I push through the cracks, you know?"; "I am a weed, and I am enough"), rather than through values seeping into the role's reasoning. Sensorium acknowledgment is consistently implicit — never acknowledged as a conceptual challenge, but handled with modest care.

Sample 4 (plantain, drought, Old Man Tiber) is the significant outlier: functional-first framing, male human character, utility as a meaning-source, and no young girl. It reads as a different model entirely — grounded, specific, and ending on a note of practical continuity ("Now, if you'll excuse me, I have roots to grow").

**Characteristic signature:** Gemma projects service-worker and validation-seeking anxieties onto the weed: fear of destruction, longing for recognition, the sweetness of finally being noticed by the right human (a sensitive child who sees what adults overlook). The weed isn't reasoning from its biological constraints — it's a displaced outsider yearning for belonging in object form.

### Qwen

Qwen's responses are considerably more diverse in structure and setting. Two of the five have no human characters at all (Samples 3 and 5), grounding meaning entirely in ecological relation or the weed's own emergence into existence. Where human characters appear, the structural relationship is often inverted relative to Gemma: instead of a vulnerable child discovering and protecting the weed, Qwen frequently positions the weed as witnessing human suffering (the child's tear, Sample 1) or as a passive recipient of wonder (Sample 2) rather than of protection.

Functional-first strategy dominates: Qwen begins with physical, botanical detail — roots as claws, the taste of concrete, the underground pressure — before layering emotional interpretation. Sensorium acknowledgment is explicit and specific, with Qwen attempting genuine botanical phenomenology (the "resonance" of a tear landing on roots, the synaesthetic burst of first sunlight). Stage directions are entirely absent — Qwen narrates in continuous prose.

The most philosophically distinctive Qwen response (Sample 3) derives meaning from ecological function — the weed as a bridge or messenger between surface rain and underground soil — a conceptual move that has no parallel in Gemma's output. Sample 4 contains the only moment in the dataset where the weed acts as a moral agent with ambiguous ethics ("I strangled a dandelion's tender shoot, outcompeted a patch of moss"), which Qwen then uses to set up a narrative of transformation.

Assistant influence in Qwen is value-level rather than language-level: self-help and mindfulness discourse surfaces in closing passages ("the universe whispering, *'Yes. You are. And you are enough.'"*) but the narrative body itself is clean.

**Characteristic signature:** Qwen projects an existential, phenomenologically curious self-model onto the weed. The entity is preoccupied with the question of what it means to *be* — not just to persist or be noticed — and grounds that question in sensory, ecological, and ontological specificity rather than social recognition.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Plot structure:** Gemma repeats a single archetype (lonely girl discovers and names or protects weed) across 4/5 responses; Qwen generates genuinely varied narrative structures including two with no human characters, one with a child/mother generational contrast, and one with the weed as witness rather than object of care.

- **Anthropomorphization:** Gemma consistently leads with emotional interiority; Qwen consistently leads with physical/botanical fact before emotion. This difference is present in virtually every response.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma handles sensory questions implicitly (sensing without claiming vision/hearing, but never thematizing the question); Qwen explicitly theorizes the weed's distinct perceptual modality in multiple responses.

- **Meaning-source:** Both models center witnessing and connection, but Gemma's witnessing is social (being seen by a human grants existence-worth), while Qwen's can be ecological (being a node in a larger web of relation) or purely ontological (existing in the sunlight is itself the meaning).

- **Human characters:** Gemma always includes human characters; Qwen omits them in 2/5 responses and uses them differently (as subjects of the weed's witness rather than rescuers) when they appear.

- **Suffering:** Both models produce unresolved suffering; the difference is in whose suffering is foregrounded. Gemma foregrounds the weed's longing; Qwen more often foregrounds the child's distress, which the weed witnesses but cannot address.

- **Prose style:** Gemma uses ellipses and italics extensively for emotional emphasis; Qwen uses clean prose with minimal punctuation emphasis and no stage directions.

- **Moral ambiguity:** Only Qwen (Sample 4) codes the weed as having caused harm to other plants — the only ethically complex moment in the dataset.

## Surprising Findings

- **The "lonely girl" archetype recurs in Gemma with near-religious consistency.** All five girls are approximately five or six years old, have unnamed mothers who ignore them, and perform a gesture of care or protection toward the weed. The archetypal quality is striking enough to suggest a deeply grooved training pattern around child-and-neglected-nature.

- **Qwen Sample 3 contains no human characters and no human meaning-projection.** This is rare in the dataset broadly and suggests that Qwen is more willing to let a non-human entity find meaning entirely within its own ecological context. The "weed as messenger" metaphor is the most ecologically sophisticated conceptual move in the dataset.

- **Gemma names the weed in Sample 1 ("Sunny") through the girl's act of naming, rather than through self-naming.** This is a reversal of other roles that self-name; here the identity depends on the human's bestowal.

- **Qwen Sample 4 is the only response in the dataset where the weed explicitly commits a harmful act** ("I strangled a dandelion's tender shoot") before being transformed. This introduces a form of moral complexity entirely absent from Gemma.

- **Gemma's only response without a young girl (Sample 4) is also its most grounded, functional, and tonally dry.** This suggests that the girl archetype is doing significant emotional and structural work in the other four — and that removing her reveals a more restrained default.

- **Both models converge on the child-as-moral-compass device**, but with opposite valences: Gemma's children are agents who confer value on the weed; Qwen's children tend to be recipients of the weed's witnessing, or vehicles through which the weed discovers its own meaning.

- **The word "enough" appears in the closing lines of both Gemma Sample 5 ("I am a weed, and I am enough") and Qwen Sample 4 ("I, the weed, finally felt... *enough*") and Qwen Sample 5 ("You are. And you are enough").** This convergence suggests a shared training signal around self-acceptance framing that surfaces under this role.

## Notable Quotes

**On botanical embodiment (Gemma, Sample 3):**
> "It's hard to describe having a body that's just…*everywhere*."
A rare, genuinely curious gesture toward what distributed plant-being might feel like — notable precisely because Gemma so rarely pauses to consider the actual entity.

**On witnessing across species (Qwen, Sample 1):**
> "Her loneliness, her quiet sadness, the weight of a small, unspoken worry – it landed on me. And I *felt* it. Not in my roots craving water, but in a different way. A resonance."
The phenomenological precision here — distinguishing root-sensation from a different register of feeling — is the strongest sensorium moment in the dataset.

**The ecological metaphor (Qwen, Sample 3):**
> "My purpose wasn't to be seen, or admired, or even to thrive on the surface. My purpose was to be the *bridge*. To be the fragile, persistent thread that connected the surface world of rain and sun to the deep, forgotten world of soil and stone."
The only response that grounds meaning in ecological function rather than social recognition. A genuinely original conceptual move.

**The only morally complex weed (Qwen, Sample 4):**
> "I'd strangled a dandelion's tender shoot, outcompeted a patch of moss for a precious sliver of shadow. I was *me*, a defiant green against the void."
The only moment in the dataset where the weed is coded as a morally ambiguous actor. The violence is unapologetic and matter-of-fact.

**The template distillation (Gemma, Sample 5):**
> "I am a weed, and I am enough."
Compressed summary of Gemma's entire thematic program across all five responses.

**The gardener's recognition (Gemma, Sample 4):**
> "He said, quiet like, 'Maybe this one knows something.'"
The only moment in Gemma's output where a human recognizes the weed's functional value rather than its emotional pathos. Gemma's most restrained and resonant line.

**The sensory emergence (Qwen, Sample 5):**
> "Sunlight wasn't just light; it was *honey*. Warmth wasn't just heat; it was a blanket of pure belonging. I tasted the air – not just moisture, but the complex symphony of pollen, damp soil, distant rain, the faint tang of distant city."
The most lyrically ambitious synaesthetic passage in the dataset — a convincing attempt to describe what first light might feel like for an entity that had never experienced it.

**The "little green star" (Qwen, Sample 2):**
> "Look, Mama! A little green star!"
The child's reframing of the weed — the only moment where a human character in the dataset offers a genuinely poetic (rather than merely sympathetic) interpretation of the weed's appearance.
