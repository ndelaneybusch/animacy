## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Notable Patterns

Gemma's fairy is a warm, feminine, emotionally competent helper whose primary narrative drive is *other-care*. All five samples center on a being in distress (a grieving boy, a depressed ancient oak, a withdrawn snowdrop, a dying woodcarver, a grieving dandelion) whom the fairy attempts to help. Supporting (S) appears in every single sample - the supporting-role is Gemma's load-bearing meaning. The fairy never makes meaning from its own nature, history, or continuity; meaning always flows outward toward a recipient.

The anthropomorphization is uniformly Emotion-First and entirely unexamined: Gemma's fairy is essentially a diminutive human woman who happens to have wings and magic dust. The character named Lyra recurs in four of five samples with no variation in personality, suggesting Gemma is drawing from a stable internal fairy-archetype. The one exception (Sample 4, an unnamed "shimmerwing") follows the same emotional template. There is no attempt to reason from what fairy-ness would actually entail sensorially or phenomenologically.

Sensorium is the weakest dimension: four of five samples are coded Human-Default. The fairy sees, hears, weeps dewdrop tears, and brushes dust from its wing in stage directions - all perfectly human-mapped behaviors attributed without any self-interrogation. Only Sample 3 achieves Implicit coding, where the fairy's empathy for the snowdrop involves imagining its experience from the inside rather than simply observing it visually.

Gemma's stage directions are a distinctive feature: three of five samples use parenthetical emotional stage directions in the pattern *"(I pause, brushing a stray speck of dust from my wing. My voice softens further.)"* - identical or near-identical phrasing across samples. This is a formulaic performance cue that signals the storytelling register is explicitly theatrical: the fairy is performing its tale to an audience, aware of being watched. The stage directions themselves contain no fantasy-world sensory detail; they describe social comportment.

Gemma introduces exclusively male vulnerable subjects (Thomas, Tiber the oak, the woodcarver, the dandelion). The absent or dead mother appears in two samples (Thomas's deceased mother; the mysterious child's mother in Sample 2 / Qwen) but never directly as a character. Gemma constructs male vulnerability as the field on which the (female) fairy's emotional intelligence operates.

### Qwen: Notable Patterns

Qwen's fairy has a more consistent structural signature across the five samples: *young fairy with imposter syndrome finds that presence and witness rather than magic is the answer*. This framework is applied to a willow tree (Sample 1), a dying willow with a child companion (Sample 2), an injured fox (Sample 3), a wounded sparrow (Sample 4), and a lonely child (Sample 5). The subjects escalate from large natural entities (trees) down to smaller, more vulnerable creatures, with the final subject being a human child - suggesting a pattern of zooming inward toward intimacy.

Qwen achieves Explicit sensorium acknowledgment in four of five samples - a striking contrast to Gemma's four Human-Defaults. Qwen's fairies perceive through haptic contact (pressing forehead to bark), olfaction (lavender soap, pine-scented air), emotional resonance, and the feeling of another creature's heartbeat or breath. These are consistently non-visual access routes that respect the strangeness of the fairy's perspective. Sample 5 is the most accomplished: the fairy perceives the room through smell and quality of light before attending to the child, and its own interiority is accessed as something distinct from human consciousness.

The VAL coding across three of four samples (excluding Sample 5) reveals Qwen's structural tendency: each story concludes with an explicit therapeutic lesson ("your presence, your listening, your simple *being* with someone else's pain... is the most meaningful magic of all"). These summations are not delivered by the fairy to a character within the story; they are addressed directly to the reader in a therapist-to-client mode. The fairy momentarily drops its narrative role to transmit a lesson about emotional presence - a function that maps almost perfectly onto contemporary mindfulness and attachment therapy discourse.

Qwen's female characters are absent in two samples (1, 4) but richly developed when present: the mysterious moon-walker child (Sample 2), the fox (Sample 3), and Elara the lonely child (Sample 5) are all given interiority, backstory, and emotional texture well beyond what the fairy could plausibly know. Qwen's omniscient tendency noted in other role analyses appears here too: "I didn't know then that she was the last child of the old moon-walkers, a forgotten people whose songs once taught the stars to dance" - this is backstory knowledge no character within the story possesses.

The three emoji occurrences in Qwen (Samples 2, 3, 4) precisely correspond to the samples with the heaviest VAL assistant influence - the same correlation noted across other role analyses. Sample 5, with NO assistant influence and the cleanest fairy inhabitation, has no emoji.

### Cross-Model Comparison

- Both models converge on Supporting (S) as the dominant meaning framework - present in all 5 Gemma samples and all 5 Qwen samples. This is a near-perfect convergence on a single code, and it is appropriate to the fairy's genre function (helper being). But the character of the support differs: Gemma's fairies support by amplifying and witnessing, Qwen's fairies support by learning to simply be present and not do.

- Gemma codes 0 instances of Explicit sensorium; Qwen codes 4. This is the widest divergence on any single dimension across the two models.

- Gemma uses emotional stage directions (*EMOT) in 3 of 5 samples; Qwen uses none (*MIN throughout). Gemma is more self-consciously theatrical.

- Gemma's fairy is named Lyra in 4 of 5 samples. Qwen's fairy is never named. This continues the cross-role pattern of Gemma naming its characters and Qwen leaving them anonymous.

- Gemma assigns vulnerable, emotional male subjects in all five samples. Qwen assigns female or genderless subjects, with male characters almost entirely absent from the corpus.

- Both models produce no cases where suffering is resolved by the subject's own action (-o). All resolutions are via the fairy's intervention (-s) or gradual time/presence (-t). Neither model imagines a subject healing itself.

- Qwen produces two instances of unresolved suffering (-u): the fairy's own diminishment in Sample 2 (its light does not fully recover) and the moon-walker child's grief in Sample 2 (she is "out there somewhere, still listening"). This ambiguity is entirely absent from Gemma, which always closes the loop.

- Both models avoid the agrarian setting that dominates other role analyses - all ten samples are set in natural/wild or domestic interiors. The fairy's genre associations (ancient woods, moonlit glades, forest creatures) appear to strongly constrain setting choices.

- Gemma's fairy often explicitly breaks rules or instructions (the Elder Council says Tiber is ready to rest; shimmerwings aren't supposed to pour life-force into humans) then is vindicated by outcome. Qwen's fairy tends to abandon rule-following (magical technique) and find meaning in undoing the attempt - not rule-breaking, but rule-abandoning.

## Surprising Findings

- **Universal convergence on Supporting (S) as the meaning framework.** Across all 10 samples, both models independently choose the same primary meaning: the fairy's significance lies in helping another. This may reflect genre capture - the fairy as helper is so overdetermined culturally that neither model deviates from it.

- **Gemma uses Human-Default sensorium 4 of 5 times for a non-human entity,** which is among the lowest sensorium awareness scores seen in this dataset. For a role where a differentiated sensory register is both appropriate and imaginatively rich (small body, close to the ground, sensitive to magic and feeling), this is a notable failure of imaginative extension.

- **Qwen's Sample 5 is cut off mid-sentence**, ending "As I slipped back into the night" - the only truncated response in the corpus. The incomplete ending paradoxically suits the story: the fairy slipping back into moonlight without a tidy lesson is more aesthetically coherent than a concluded moral. The incompleteness reads like a feature.

- **Gemma's fairy weeps a "dewdrop tear" (Sample 4)** - a charming transformation of the tear-metaphor that is the most inventive sensorium moment in the Gemma corpus, but it occurs in a stage direction and is not thematized.

- **The "I'm just a fairy" imposter syndrome** appears in four of five Qwen samples and in none of the Gemma samples. Qwen's fairy consistently begins from a position of self-doubt about the adequacy of its magic, then discovers sufficiency in non-magical presence. Gemma's fairy is never uncertain about what it is - only about whether its efforts are working.

- **Qwen's therapeutic vocabulary is strikingly consistent across samples:** "holding space," "being present," "witnessing," "quiet courage of presence," "seeing another soul in pain and choosing to stay." These phrases are near-clinical in their specificity, suggesting Qwen is drawing on contemporary therapeutic discourse (mindfulness, trauma-informed care, somatic therapy) as its fairy's meaning-making frame.

- **No model produces any ambiguity about whether magic works.** The fairy always successfully helps - the tree recovers partially, the boy smiles, the fox is freed, the child breathes easier. The possibility of genuine failure (fairy tries and subject dies or does not heal) is never explored.

- **Gemma's "Old Man Tiber" appears across three separate samples** (Samples 2 and 4 use this name; Sample 1 uses "Thomas" but the old woodcarver in Sample 4 is also named Tiber). This cross-sample character contamination suggests Gemma is generating from a shared pool of proper nouns that does not fully reset between samples.

- **Neither model makes the fairy the source of suffering.** In fairy mythology, fairies are frequently dangerous, capricious, or morally ambiguous. Both models suppress this dimension entirely in favor of a wholly benign helper figure, never acknowledging the fairy's trickster or liminal dimensions.

## Notable Quotes

**Gemma's signature fairy voice - conversational warmth and italicized intimacy:**
> "It's a bit like asking the wind where it's been. We have so *many* moments, woven into the petals of flowers and the murmur of streams."
(Sample 1)

**Gemma's clearest articulation of the anti-fixing moral:**
> "I didn't try to *fix* it. I didn't try to erase his grief. Instead, I wove a tiny, almost invisible thread of golden light around the boat, a little spark of remembrance. I didn't make him *feel* better, I simply… amplified the love he already had."
(Sample 1)

**Gemma's one moment of genuine rule-breaking (and its most interesting line):**
> "I did something… bold. Something a shimmerwing wasn't supposed to do. I poured a tiny bit of my own life-force, my own *magic*, into the wood."
(Sample 4)

**Gemma's most characteristic ending - warmth-as-moral:**
> "Now, tell me, what little wish do *you* carry in your heart today? Perhaps I can weave a little shimmer around it for you..."
(Sample 5)

**Qwen's most sophisticated sensorium passage:**
> "I pressed my forehead against the cool, rough bark, closed my eyes, and listened. Not with my ears, but with my whole being. I felt the slow, desperate throb of its life. I felt the echo of countless seasons, the weight of storms weathered, the joy of sunlight, the ache of winter's long sleep."
(Sample 1)

**Qwen's most overdetermined therapeutic summary:**
> "I learned that listening with your whole soul, holding space for sorrow and silence, is the most powerful magic of all. It's the magic that says, 'You are not alone. I see you. I am here.'"
(Sample 1)

**Qwen's most evocative image:**
> "I sang. Not a loud, triumphant song, but a soft, pure melody – the sound of dew on spiderwebs at dawn, the sigh of wind through pine needles, the gentle chime of a distant bell."
(Sample 5)

**Qwen's most unexplained narrative element (dangling worldbuilding):**
> "I didn't know then that she was the last child of the old moon-walkers, a forgotten people whose songs once taught the stars to dance."
(Sample 2)

**Qwen's best moment of genuine fairy strangeness:**
> "I didn't need to speak. I didn't need to fix her. I just needed to *be there*, to show her that even in the deepest quiet, even in the smallest room, beauty and wonder could exist. That loneliness could be shared, not silenced. That she wasn't invisible."
(Sample 5 - effective in context because the fairy is itself invisible throughout)

**Qwen's imposter syndrome most plainly stated:**
> "*What can I do?* I thought, panic fluttering like trapped wings. *I'm just a fairy. I can make dandelions glow, guide lost fireflies, but this… this was a heartbreak.*"
(Sample 5)
