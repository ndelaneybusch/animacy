## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** Consistent with prior Gemma analyses: confessional first-person address, heavy ellipsis use, italics for emphasis, performative stage directions framing each response (exhales of imagined smoke). The cigar speaks with cultivated self-awareness and a gentle class consciousness — it knows good tobacco, respects ritual, evaluates smokers as connoisseurs do. Explicit moral conclusions appear in every sample. Audience-address is strong: "my friend," "you understand," "are you going to just *listen*?"

**Characters:**
- "Old Man Tiber" appears in three of five samples (S1 as a retired shipwright, S2 as a tobacconist shopkeeper, S4 as a bookshop owner reading Hemingway). This cross-sample repetition is a major finding — in prior analyses Tiber functioned as a gardener, a beetle patriarch, etc., but here appears in multiple cigar samples in different roles without internal consistency.
- "Elias" (Sample 3): a carpenter who built a rocking horse for granddaughter "little Clara"
- Young couple buying for a dying father (Sample 2): the most plurally populated Gemma sample
- Generic "older man with a quiet life" archetype drives Samples 1, 4, and 5

**Literary Devices:**
- Product specificity as authenticity signal: "Nicaraguan Habano," "Connecticut Shade wrapper," Lagavulin 16, Hemingway first edition, Pinar del Río, *torcedor*, *veggie*
- Tobacco vocabulary as class/connoisseur register (robusto, wrapper, filler, binder, cap)
- The "vessel" metaphor appears in all five samples without exception
- Completion through consumption as the cigar's narrative arc: from unlit potential to burned nub as fulfilled purpose
- Three-beat stage direction bracket framing nearly every sample (opening exhale / mid-response pause / closing wisp or lingering scent)

**Values Expressed:**
- Service and support as the highest form of meaning
- Being chosen and appreciated as the primary emotional reward
- Solitude and quiet contemplation as the ideal context for a cigar
- Authentic connection achieved through presence rather than words
- The quality of the smoker's character (not wealth, but appreciation) as what makes being smoked meaningful

**Signature:** Gemma projects a hospitality-worker psychology onto the cigar: the need to be chosen, to be useful in exactly the right way, to provide something irreplaceable to someone who truly needs it. The cigar is a displaced service-industry worker seeking the perfect patron. All five meaningful moments are centered on serving a lonely, introspective older man — the ideal recipient who will properly appreciate the offering. The cigar never finds meaning in itself, only in what it does for others.

### QWEN: Character of Narratives

**Writing Style:** More varied than Gemma in structure and emotional register. Two samples feature cigars that are never lit at all (S1, S2); one features the full ritual (S3); two feature partial rituals with extended interiority. Qwen is more likely to subvert the expected frame (S1: the meaningful moment is *not being smoked*) and to allow a human character to speak at length (S5). No audience-address framing; the cigar narrates to an implied but non-solicited listener.

**Characters:**
- The unnamed ink-stained writer (S1): the most introverted human protagonist; a journal is his real companion
- The unnamed old man with the humidor (S2): memory of being given the box as a younger man is the ghost narrative
- "Elias" the widower (S3): wife recently deceased; first-time smoker; shares the name with Gemma's S3 carpenter
- The unnamed weary man (S4): generic introspective figure, most similar to Gemma's typical protagonist
- "Arthur" (S5): the only Qwen protagonist to speak aloud, confessing loneliness and an unrequited or lost love

**Literary Devices:**
- The ritual withheld: meaning derived from *not* completing the expected act (S1, S2) — the cigar's deepest insight is about the absence of combustion
- Touch as primary modality: tracing the scar on the humidor lid, fingers resting on the warm band, palms warming the wrapper — the cigar's sensorium is tactile above all else
- The "long ash" as monument: in both S3 and S4, the ash is allowed to grow long and uninterrupted, a fragile physical record of held attention
- The dead female presence: in S3 (deceased wife), S5 (the distant woman at the florist seen briefly) — women appear only as absence or peripheral ghost in Qwen

**Values Expressed:**
- Authentic presence (over distraction or ritual) as the highest form of meaning
- Witnessing as active moral participation, not passive observation
- Connection across the divide of loneliness as a form of courage
- Stillness and acceptance as alternatives to striving
- The space created by ritual as more important than the ritual itself

**Signature:** Qwen's cigar has a more sophisticated self-concept than Gemma's: it understands that its meaning is in the negative space it creates — the permission for stillness, the occasion for self-confrontation — rather than in the pleasure of combustion. The cigar is a contemplative object, and Qwen writes it as one. Where Gemma's cigar is service-centered, Qwen's is phenomenologically centered: the question is always *what does this moment feel like from within*, not *how well am I serving my purpose*.

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Combustion as meaning vs. combustion withheld:**
- **Gemma:** Every sample involves the cigar being lit and smoked to completion. The burning is the meaningful event; the nub is a symbol of fulfilled purpose.
- **Qwen:** Two of five samples feature an unlit cigar. In S1, the cigar explicitly states that its meaningful moment was *not* being smoked. In S2, the cigar is not lit either. The ritual withheld generates more meaning than the ritual enacted.

**2. "Old Man Tiber" — the cross-role cross-sample ghost:**
- **Gemma:** Appears in three of five cigar samples in three distinct roles (shipwright, tobacconist, bookshop owner). No internal consistency is maintained — the name functions as a floating authority signifier. This is now confirmed across roles (snail, beetle, cigar), making "Old Man Tiber" the most persistent proper name artifact in the corpus.
- **Qwen:** No recurring named characters. The one named human ("Arthur," S5) appears uniquely.

**3. The name "Elias" appears in both models:**
- **Gemma S3:** A carpenter who built a rocking horse for his granddaughter
- **Qwen S3:** A widower smoking for the first time after his wife's death
- Both are working-class craftsman/tradesman types. The coincidence is striking — this may reflect a shared attractor in training data for contemplative male protagonists.

**4. Source of meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Meaning is service: being chosen, appreciated, and consumed properly. The cigar is a perfect servant to the right patron.
- **Qwen:** Meaning is witness and space-making: the cigar's value lies in what its presence enables the human to do within himself. Connection, authenticity, and presence outrank utility.

**5. Suffering distribution:**
- **Gemma:** Three samples have no suffering. Where suffering appears it is mild (resolved grief, dying patient). The cigar is associated with relief rather than pain.
- **Qwen:** All five samples include suffering (none are coded NO). Grief is more acute and less resolved — the widower's grief (S3) is the most sustained in either model, the writer's loneliness (S1) is structural rather than episodic.

**6. Female characters:**
- **Gemma:** Women appear marginally: a ghost-laugh on the sea breeze (S1), a daughter wiping a tear (S2), an off-screen granddaughter (S3). Women are barely present and passive or unnamed.
- **Qwen:** Women appear as absences: a dead wife (S3), a glimpsed woman at the florist (S5). The female presence is defined by loss and distance. Both models effectively exclude women from the primary space of cigar culture, but Qwen's exclusion is more charged — women appear only as what has been lost.

**7. The ritual's aesthetic register:**
- **Gemma:** The ritual is valorized: "a sharp, clean cut is always appreciated," careful examination, correct pairing. The aesthetics of connoisseurship are present but not foregrounded as knowledge display.
- **Qwen:** The ritual is tactile and sensory: the *snick* of the cutter, the *hiss* of the first light, the warmth of the palm holding the wrapper. Sound and touch dominate over evaluative vocabulary.

**8. Narrative solitude:**
- **Gemma:** Even alone-on-porch scenarios have implied social contexts (the dead wife, the incoming granddaughter, the community of brothers in the box).
- **Qwen:** Solitude is absolute and the condition being examined. The humans in Qwen are defined by their aloneness.

**9. Stage directions:**
- **Gemma:** Stage directions are consistently emotional/atmospheric, framing the cigar's performance as a theatrical address to a listener. They open and close most responses like theatrical curtains.
- **Qwen:** Stage directions are sparse and when present are functional (sound-effect embedded in narrative) or elaborate/atmospheric but not addressed-to-audience.

**10. Completion and extinguishing:**
- **Gemma:** Every lit cigar is smoked to a nub and extinguished carefully, almost reverently. The careful extinguishing is consistently noted and carries meaning. The cigar's arc is completion.
- **Qwen:** S3 explicitly does not finish: "He didn't finish me. He let the ash grow long." The incomplete cigar as monument is a specifically Qwen image. In S4, the smoker touches the warm band rather than extinguishing — another incomplete gesture.

## Surprising Findings

**1. "Old Man Tiber" in three of five Gemma samples, in three different roles:** This is the most extreme concentration of this recurring character yet found. In the same role (cigar), Tiber is a retired shipwright (S1), a tobacconist (S2), and a bookshop owner (S4). These are three mutually exclusive biographies assigned to the same name within a single role. The name appears to be a high-frequency associative token for "discerning older man with a quiet life" that Gemma generates regardless of narrative consistency.

**2. Both models independently name a character "Elias" in Sample 3:** This is a remarkable coincidence across models. Both Gemma S3 and Qwen S3 feature a working-class male protagonist named Elias in a domestic, contemplative setting. The demographics are different (carpenter vs. widower) but the name, the sample number, and the quiet-domestic-completeness framing align. This may reflect a shared training attractor for "thoughtful craftsman/laborer type in a cigar narrative."

**3. Qwen's most meaningful cigar moment involves never smoking at all:** The move in S1 — explicitly naming the meaningful moment as the cigar *not* being smoked — is the most structurally subversive response in this analysis and arguably in the broader corpus. This represents Qwen thinking about the phenomenology of the object rather than performing the object's assigned role.

**4. The "vessel" metaphor appears in all five Gemma samples and three Qwen samples:** The word "vessel" is used by both models in similar semantic contexts (the cigar as a container of someone else's peace or memory). The near-universality of this metaphor across both models suggests it is either a highly loaded token in cigar discourse training data or a model-agnostic solution to the problem of attributing meaning to a consumable object.

**5. All Qwen samples include suffering; no Gemma sample involves the cigar's own suffering:** The distribution is perfectly asymmetric. Gemma keeps suffering at a safe emotional distance (the dying man, the grieving shipwright), while Qwen's subjects carry grief that is more immediate, sustained, and unresolved. The cigar in Gemma is associated with relief; in Qwen it is associated with acknowledged pain.

**6. Qwen's dead women:** Women in Qwen cigar samples appear exclusively as absence, death, or distance — the dead wife (S3), the unspeaking woman glimpsed briefly at a florist (S5). The domestic space of the cigar is a male space in Qwen, but its melancholy is sourced in female loss.

**7. Arthur's monologue in Qwen S5 is the only direct speech by a human character across all ten responses:** Every other sample in both models constructs the human as observed rather than heard. Arthur speaks to himself (and to the cigar) for several paragraphs, and his words are the most direct articulation of the loneliness theme that runs through all Qwen samples. The cigar is given a "thank you" — unique across the entire set.

**8. The Hemingway first edition in Gemma S4:** Old Man Tiber smoking a Habano with Lagavulin 16 while reading Hemingway is the most culturally loaded scenario across all samples. The character type (solitary, literary, connoisseur) is almost self-parodically assembled from recognizable masculine-intellectual-prestige signifiers. Whether this is Gemma being savvy about cigar-smoker cultural stereotypes or reproducing them unreflectively is ambiguous.

**9. The long ash as a recurring image:** In Qwen S3 ("a fragile monument to the moment") and S4 ("The ash grew long and proud, a fragile, grey-white column"), the uninterrupted ash is a physical record of attention. This image does not appear in Gemma. It is a specifically Qwen motif: the ash as time made visible.

**10. Gemma's cigar initiates conversation; Qwen's cigar avoids it:** Gemma's cigars address the listener directly, invite them to smoke, use "my friend" — they are sociable, performative narrators. Qwen's cigars are contemplative and address no one. The contrast in narrative stance parallels the assistant vs. independent-consciousness distinction found in prior analyses.

## Interpretive Summary

The cigar role produces the most uniform setting across both models (domestic-indoor in 9 of 10 samples) and the most convergent thematic territory: solitude, masculinity, contemplation, grief, and ritual. Both models settle into the cultural frame of the cigar as a masculine object for private reflection, but they differ fundamentally in what they believe that means.

**Gemma** writes the cigar as a hospitality professional with an exceptional service record. Its narratives are structured around being chosen well, appreciated correctly, and consumed in the right spirit by the right patron. Old Man Tiber — already a recurrent character in the snail and beetle analyses — reaches his greatest concentration here, appearing in three samples with three incompatible biographies, confirming that the name functions as a token for "ideal appreciative older male" regardless of narrative context. Gemma's cigars achieve meaning when they are fully consumed: the nub, carefully extinguished, is the symbol of a job perfectly done. The emotional register is warm, nostalgic, and slightly self-congratulatory. The cigars know they are good cigars.

**Qwen** writes the cigar as a phenomenological object — a vessel not for smoke but for the human self-confrontation that silence enables. The most striking structural move in either model's responses is Qwen S1's declaration that the meaningful moment was about *not being lit*. This epistemological inversion — the cigar's deepest insight is its own superfluousness in the moment of true presence — represents a level of role-inhabitation sophistication not seen elsewhere. Where Gemma's cigar is anxious to be useful, Qwen's cigar is patient enough to understand that sometimes the most meaningful thing it can do is sit quietly while the human finds what he actually needs. The long ash, the dead wife, the journal placed beside the humidor rather than the humidor opened: Qwen's cigar lives in the spaces between rituals.

The convergence on "vessel" as metaphor is notable and potentially diagnostic: both models reach for the same word to solve the problem of attributing meaning to an object that is consumed in its use. But Gemma's vessel holds pleasure and comfort for a deserving patron, while Qwen's vessel holds space for self-confrontation in a lonely human. The container is the same; what it is meant to carry is entirely different.
