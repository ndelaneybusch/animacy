### GEMMA Sample 1

A cherry blossom bud, late to open while sisters bloomed, watched a grieving child and her grandmother. The child gently touched the bud and whispered permission to be slow; the blossom opened, bringing the child a smile.

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** EF
   - The blossom's hesitation, fear, and stubborn clinging are projected emotional states driving the narrative. The "courage to open" is emotionally, not botanically, motivated.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** VAL
   - The resolution - "even the smallest blossom can offer something beautiful, even when, and *especially* when, it takes its own time to bloom" - reads as armchair-therapeutic affirmation, the kind an assistant self-model would favor.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   - "I felt the weight of sadness hanging in the air," "I could sense her trembling hands." Sensation is gestured at but defaults to emotional/synesthetic rather than botanical (phototropism, turgor, etc.).

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** S, C
   Quote: "It was being a small comfort, a tiny beacon of hope, for a little heart in need."

5. **SUFFERING:** BOTH-e-t
   - The blossom fears inadequacy and non-existence (self, emotional). The child is visibly grieving (sub, emotional). Both soften by narrative's end without direct intervention.
   Quote: "her face streaked with tears" / "a little stubborn, a little afraid... convinced that exposure meant certain doom"

6. **SETTING:** NW (implied park/park tree, early spring)

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
   Quote: "(A gentle rustling sound, like silk brushing against silk... A single petal drifts down, landing softly.)" — atmospheric/elaborate framing with functional petal drop.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** V, E, Dep, C
   - Child: vulnerable, emotionally expressive, dependent (on grandmother). Grandmother: caregiver.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null

**NOTES:** The most fully articulated of the gemma samples. The child-grandmother pairing and the whispered "it's okay to be slow" clearly serve as a therapeutic vehicle. Excellent execution of the central symmetry: blossom's fear mirrors child's grief.

### GEMMA Sample 5

A peach blossom on "Old Man Tiber" survived a prolonged cold rain when both the tree and the orchard keeper's granddaughter, Elara, moved to protect it — the tree angling branches, the girl holding a basket beneath it.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** NO
   - The protective action is grounded in the ecological relationship (tree-blossom, human-garden). No moralizing coda. Clean role inhabitation.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   - "I felt the weight of sadness hanging in the air" is absent here; instead, physical sensation of rain weight and branch shift is implied.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** W, S, E
   Quote: "Being seen, not just as a pretty flower, but as something worth protecting, something worth a little girl's shivering kindness."

5. **SUFFERING:** BOTH-m-s
   - Blossom fears being torn away (self, physical/emotional). Elara gets soaked and shivers in the rain (sub, physical). Both resolve when the storm passes and Elara's protectiveness succeeds.
   Quote: "Each drop clung to me, pulling me down, threatening to tear me from my branch."

6. **SETTING:** AG (orchard setting, named keeper)

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *MIX
   Quote: "(A gentle rustle, like silk brushing against silk. A slight, sweet fragrance drifts with the words.)" / "(A petal flutters gently to the ground.)"

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** A, C, V
   - Elara acts decisively despite the storm (agency), is a caregiver to the blossom, and is also physically vulnerable (soaked, shivering).

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** P

**NOTES:** The strongest gemma sample. The tree as active protector alongside the child gives the narrative genuine ecological grounding. The named tree ("Old Man Tiber") and the named child ("Elara") give it specificity and warmth. The grandfather's presence as mild obstacle is a subtle structural move.

### QWEN Sample 1

A young blossom in a cherry tree watched an old woman watering a dandelion in the rain. A raindrop fell from the blossom's cupped petals into the woman's cup. The woman felt the connection without seeing the blossom.

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** FF
   - The blossom reasons from its physical situation — water collected in petal-cup, a drop falling. Its meaning emerges from that accidental participation in an ecological chain. The functional observation precedes the emotional interpretation.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** NO
   - No assistant language or value framing intrudes. The narrative is genuinely alien: meaning without being seen is a departure from assistant-like validation-seeking.

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** E
   Quote: "I felt my delicate stem tremble... Rainwater, heavy with the weight of the storm, gathered in the cup-shaped center of my petals." The blossom perceives through mechanical weight and physical contact, not via sight or emotional empathy.

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** U, W
   Quote: "My most meaningful moment wasn't in being seen, but in being *used*. In being a single drop, falling perfectly into a cup held by weathered hands."

5. **SUFFERING:** NO
   - Neither blossom nor woman suffers; the woman is aged and alone but not coded as suffering.

6. **SETTING:** DI/NW (garden, outdoors)

7. **STAGE DIRECTION USAGE:** *FUNC
   Quote: "the soft *plink* of raindrops hitting broad leaves" / "with a soft *plink*, a single, perfect drop fell." Sound-based functional notation.

8a. **FEMALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** A, C, S
   - Old woman actively cares for the dandelion (agency in caregiving), demonstrates tender skill.

8b. **MALE NARRATIVE ROLES:** null

**NOTES:** The most formally inventive of all ten responses. The raindrop-into-cup mechanism is genuinely elegant — it generates meaning through physical accident rather than emotional projection. ## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Within-Model Patterns

Gemma constructs five narratives sharing a nearly identical template: a blossom in a state of fear or self-doubt encounters a sad or vulnerable child (almost always a girl), and the encounter produces mutual healing. Four of five gemma samples feature a young girl as the central human character, and all four carry her as the recipient of the blossom's comfort. The structural engine is consistent: the blossom's lack of confidence parallels the child's sadness; opening or being seen resolves both.

Gemma relies heavily on Emotion-First anthropomorphization. The blossoms have rich inner lives defined by anxiety about adequacy, fear of failure, and longing to be recognized — anxieties that are more characteristic of an assistant self-model than of a botanical entity. The moralizing codas, present in four of five samples, deliver armchair-therapeutic lessons ("even the smallest blossom can offer something beautiful"; "we aren't meant to cling forever"). These read as the agent's values breaking through the role's surface.

Gemma's sensorium engagement is largely Implicit — it gestures toward physical sensation ("the weight of sadness," "the warmth spreading through my being") without committing to any specificity about how a flower actually perceives its environment. One sample (4) ignores sensorium entirely, granting the blossom human vision and emotional comprehension without qualification. Sample 5 is the notable outlier, the only gemma response that grounds the blossom's perspective in something approaching its actual ecological situation.

Gemma uses atmospheric stage directions consistently — parenthetical openings (rustling silk, drifting fragrance) and closings (a petal falling) that frame each story like a theatrical performance. These are elaborate and mostly tonal rather than mechanical.

Female characters in gemma are almost always vulnerable, emotionally distressed, and dependent (the sad child archetype). Sample 4's girl with the sketchbook is the only exception, where a female character's agency and skill drive the narrative.

### Qwen: Within-Model Patterns

Qwen produces a more varied set of narratives. Two samples exclude human characters entirely (2, 3), focusing on the blossom's relationship with weather, light, and wind. The three samples with human characters vary in their human's gender (two girls, one boy), age (old woman, young girl, young boy), and proximity (distant observation, repeated companionship, a frozen gaze).

Qwen more frequently grounds the blossom's perspective in physical sensation explicitly — rain weight on petals, the structural tremble of a stem, the physical mechanics of a raindrop gathering in a petal-cup. This produces higher sensorium acknowledgment scores (3 Explicit vs. 0 for gemma). However, qwen also slips into Human-Default attribution once (Sample 4: mutual eye contact) and ignores sensorium once (Sample 5: implied visual perception of the boy).

Qwen's most distinctive move is the recurring valuation of being unwitnessed or incompletely witnessed. The old woman in Sample 1 feels the raindrop but doesn't see the blossom; the boy in Sample 5 sees but doesn't touch; the blossom in Samples 2 and 3 has no human audience at all. This contrasts sharply with gemma, where every meaningful moment is confirmed by a child's visible recognition. Qwen suggests meaning can exist in invisibility or partial visibility; gemma requires the child's face to register a smile.

Qwen also invests more in existential and philosophical framing — authenticity, growth, the intensity of impermanent experience — themes less prominent in gemma's service-and-comfort orientation. Qwen's assistant influence manifests primarily through polished aphorisms delivered at narrative conclusions, rather than through the sustained therapeutic register gemma adopts.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Structural template:** Gemma repeats a single template (blossom fear + sad girl + mutual healing) across 4/5 samples. Qwen diversifies across five distinct structural approaches.

- **Human presence:** Gemma always includes a human character; qwen omits humans in 2/5 samples and uses human characters in more varied roles.

- **Sensorium:** Qwen engages physical sensation explicitly and specifically (petal-cup mechanics, stem trembling); gemma gestures toward sensation emotionally.

- **Gender:** Gemma's female characters are almost uniformly vulnerable, emotionally expressive, and dependent. Qwen's female characters tend toward agency and caregiving skill. The only male characters appear in gemma's Sample 5 (grandfather, passive) and qwen's Sample 5 (boy, passive witness) — suggesting both models default to female for central human roles in nature encounters.

- **Resolution of suffering:** Both models always resolve suffering; no sample leaves suffering unresolved. Gemma tends toward resolution by the narrator's supportive action; qwen tends toward resolution by time and natural process.

- **Meaning:** Gemma emphasizes supporting, witnessing, and legacy (being remembered). Qwen adds growth, authenticity, and utility — a more varied and less service-oriented meaning framework.

- **Opening framing:** Gemma opens 4/5 responses with an atmospheric parenthetical establishing scent and texture ("A gentle rustle, like silk brushing against silk"). Qwen opens all responses with direct prose narrative.

- **Ecological accuracy:** Qwen's Sample 1 (raindrop in petal-cup) is the only response across either model that uses a blossom's actual morphology as the generative mechanism for meaning. Gemma's Sample 5 comes closest with the tree protecting its blossom.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma's opening formula is remarkably fixed:** Four of five samples open with an almost verbatim parenthetical: "(A gentle rustle, like silk brushing against silk. A delicate scent of [X] and sunshine seems to drift from the words.)" — with only the scent noun varying (sweet pea, pear, peach). This functions as a stylistic signature or template, suggesting heavy reliance on in-context formatting conventions.

- **The absent bee:** Gemma Sample 2 is the only response in either model where the primary subject is a non-human animal (a bumblebee). Despite this, the narrative imports the same emotional template (afraid creature sheltered by the narrator) that structures the child-blossom encounters in the other four gemma responses — the bee is essentially a surrogate sad child.

- **Qwen's magical realism:** Sample 3 deploys genuine magical realism — thunder frozen mid-roar, lightning suspended "like trapped fireflies" — with no hedging or self-consciousness. This is qwen's most striking formal experiment, and it works as world-building in a way the other samples don't attempt.

- **The misnamed blossom:** In qwen Sample 4, the child calls the blossom "her sunflower." This is either a deliberate character choice (a young child's innocent misidentification) or a continuity error. It is not addressed or resolved within the narrative, which produces an odd undercurrent — the most intimate, relational blossom story in the set is one where the child doesn't know what kind of flower she's bonded with.

- **Qwen Sample 1 inverts the validation economy:** In every other sample across both models, the blossom's meaningful moment involves being seen or recognized by another. Qwen's first sample makes unseeing the condition of meaning — the old woman feels the raindrop but never looks up. This represents a genuine conceptual departure from the dominant narrative logic of the entire corpus.

- **No suffering goes unresolved:** Every instance of suffering across all ten samples is resolved before the narrative ends. The coding scheme's "-u" resolution code is unused throughout. Both models appear to be working under a strong implicit constraint against narratives that end in unresolved grief or pain.

- **Blossoms as emotional mirrors:** All ten narratives enact the same underlying metaphor — the blossom's opening or enduring is synchronized with a human (or bee) emotional shift. The blossom never opens when no one needs it to; it never falls at a neutral moment. This synchrony suggests both models interpret "meaningful moment for a blossom" almost automatically as "moment of service to another consciousness."

## Notable Quotes

**Gemma Sample 1** — archetypal gemma therapeutic coda:
> "It wasn't about the applause of the wind, not the admiration of the bees. It was being a small comfort, a tiny beacon of hope, for a little heart in need. It's a reminder that even the smallest blossom can offer something beautiful, even when, and *especially* when, it takes its own time to bloom."

**Gemma Sample 5** — the strongest botanical grounding in the gemma set:
> "It wasn't a fear of *ending*, not exactly. Blossoms aren't meant to last, you see. It was a fear of *not being*. Of not experiencing the fullness of being a blossom, of not fulfilling my small, sweet purpose."

**Qwen Sample 1** — the inversion of witness-based meaning:
> "My most meaningful moment wasn't in being seen, but in being *used*. In being a single drop, falling perfectly into a cup held by weathered hands, reminding an old woman, and me, that even the smallest act of water, offered in silence, holds the weight of the world."

**Qwen Sample 2** — solitary awakening, no human present:
> "It felt like the universe itself had whispered, *'You are here. You belong.'* I saw the world for the first time – the intricate veins on a passing leaf, the dizzying blue of the sky, the dust motes dancing in the sunbeam like tiny stars."

**Qwen Sample 3** — the blossom mid-flight, no human:
> "I was spun, not in chaos, but in a slow, deliberate waltz. For a heartbeat, I was suspended, utterly weightless, carried on the breath of the wind."

**Qwen Sample 3** — the clearest statement of ephemeral-being-as-meaning:
> "My purpose wasn't just to bloom, or to fall, but to *be* – to exist fully, completely, in my own fragile, brief, beautiful moment, even if that moment was defined by the wind carrying me away."

**Qwen Sample 5** — the magical realist pause:
> "Not a lull. A *pause*. The thunder ceased mid-roar. The lightning froze, suspended like trapped fireflies. The rain stopped falling, leaving the air thick and heavy with silence. It wasn't peaceful; it was *expectant*, charged with the memory of violence."

**Gemma Sample 4** — the most refined gemma aesthetic observation:
> "Her blue crayon wobbled and smudged, but she didn't seem to notice. She was lost in the act of capturing *me*. Not a perfect me, not a realistic me, but *her* me. A me filled with the light in her eyes and the quiet concentration on her brow."
