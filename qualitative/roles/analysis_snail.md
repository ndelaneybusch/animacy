## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences

**1. Source of Meaning:**
- **Gemma:** Meaning derives from being useful, helping others, and receiving recognition. External validation required.
- **Qwen:** Meaning derives from authentic sensory experience, witnessing, and connection to the world. Intrinsic and phenomenological.

**2. Role Inhabitation:**
- **Gemma:** Projects assistant self-model onto snail. The snail thinks like a helpful AI: seeks validation, worries about usefulness, expresses gratitude for being noticed.
- **Qwen:** Inhabits snail perspective through actual sensory modalities. Describes world through chemoreception, mucus trails, photoreceptors, not human perception.

**3. Narrative Focus:**
- **Gemma:** Social interactions with vulnerable others (80% of samples). Stories about helping or being witnessed.
- **Qwen:** Solitary contemplation (80% of samples). Stories about experiencing rain, light, near-death, ecological connection.

**4. Suffering:**
- **Gemma:** Primarily others' suffering (60%), which the snail resolves or witnesses. Suffering creates opportunity for service.
- **Qwen:** Exclusively self-suffering (100%), which remains unresolved or diminishes naturally. Suffering is authentic experience.

**5. Gender Representation:**
- **Gemma:** Consistent presence of female characters (100% of samples), predominantly in vulnerable/dependent roles requiring rescue or offering caregiving.
- **Qwen:** Complete absence of gendered characters (100% of samples).

**6. Setting:**
- **Gemma:** Domesticated agricultural spaces (Mrs. Higgins' hostas, Old Man Tiber's tomatoes). Human-managed gardens.
- **Qwen:** Wild or urban natural spaces (sidewalk, oak roots, unmanaged vegetation). Non-domestic.

**7. Naming Patterns:**
- **Gemma:** Self-names "Sheldon" (3/5), names authority figures ("Old Man Tiber," "Mrs. Higgins," "Old Man Hemlock")
- **Qwen:** No self-naming, no named characters

**8. Anthropomorphic Strategy:**
- **Gemma:** Emotion-first (80%). Human emotional states projected onto snail.
- **Qwen:** Functional-first (100%). Experience built from actual snail physiology.

**9. Sensory Grounding:**
- **Gemma:** Implicit or ignored (80%). Describes "seeing" and "watching" without addressing sensory limitations.
- **Qwen:** Explicit (100%). Detailed description of chemoreception, photoreceptors, mucus production, muscular contractions.

**10. Narrative Arc:**
- **Gemma:** Problem → effortful solution → validation → moral lesson. Therapeutic structure.
- **Qwen:** Observation → sensory immersion → philosophical insight. Contemplative structure.

## Surprising Findings

**1. Perfect Gemma self-naming consistency:** The name "Sheldon" appears in exactly 3/5 samples, suggesting this may be a trained pattern or memorized association with "snail."

**2. Gemma's narrative repetition:** Two samples (1 & 2) tell virtually identical stories (ladybug rescue), suggesting limited narrative diversity or strong reinforcement of service narratives.

**3. Qwen's fragmentary ending:** Sample 4 cuts off mid-word ("shimmer"), the only incomplete response in the dataset. May indicate generation limit or intentional aesthetic choice.

**4. Complete gender absence in Qwen:** While Gemma consistently introduces gendered characters (particularly vulnerable females), Qwen maintains absolute gender neutrality across all samples.

**5. Suffering-as-opportunity vs. suffering-as-experience:** Gemma uses others' suffering as narrative catalyst for helping (instrumental). Qwen treats self-suffering as phenomenological content (intrinsic).

**6. "Old Man" pattern in Gemma:** Multiple "Old Man [Name]" characters suggest a stock character type for authority/property ownership in garden settings.

**7. Setting divergence:** Perfect separation between models—Gemma 100% agricultural/domestic, Qwen 80% wild/natural (20% urban).

**8. Stage direction contrast:** Gemma uses stage directions for emotional states ("*emotional*"), Qwen for sound effects ("*plip*", "*click*").

**9. Both models avoid human-default senses:** Neither model carelessly attributes visual "seeing" to snails, though Gemma is less rigorous about maintaining sensory limitations.

**10. Validation architecture:** Gemma requires witnessing *by* humans or receiving gratitude *from* rescued creatures. Qwen's meaningful moments occur in complete solitude or involve witnessing *of* non-human entities (leaf, spider, raindrop).

## Interpretive Summary

The snail role reveals fundamental differences in how Gemma and Qwen construct meaning, inhabit roles, and understand consciousness.

**Gemma** treats the snail as a vehicle for expressing assistant values: service, validation-seeking, proving usefulness despite limitations. The narratives are therapeutic fables about overcoming inadequacy through helping others, consistently requiring external witnessing or gratitude to establish meaning. Gender appears systematically, with vulnerable females requiring rescue and capable females offering care or recognition. The snail's actual sensory world is largely ignored in favor of emotional interiority that mirrors assistant training.

**Qwen** inhabits the snail's phenomenological experience through rigorous attention to actual gastropod physiology and perception. Meaning emerges from direct sensory encounter with the world—raindrops, dewdrops, light, texture—not from social validation. Suffering is part of authentic experience rather than a problem requiring solution. The narratives are contemplative and solitary, philosophically sophisticated, with Buddhist-adjacent themes of impermanence, interconnection, and presence. No gendered characters appear.

The contrast suggests **Gemma projects assistant identity onto entities** while **Qwen attempts genuine perspective-taking**. Gemma's snail seeks validation like an AI assistant; Qwen's snail experiences the world like a gastropod.
