## Within-Model Analysis

### GEMMA

**Narrative Patterns:**
- Extreme narrative convergence: 5/5 samples feature "Mr. Henderson," retired carpenter with cancer in oncology ward
- Invariant story structure: night shift → withdrawn patient → breakthrough conversation → death within days → therapeutic reflection
- Formulaic therapeutic language: "It's not about fixing... it's about being with people"
- Death appears in 100% of narratives, always peaceful, always meaningful
- Heavy use of emotional stage directions establishing nurse persona before narrative begins

**Character Signatures:**
- Female nurses (all samples): agentic, emotionally expressive, caregiving, reflective
- Male patients (all samples): vulnerable, dependent, emotionally intense upon breakthrough, dying
- Supporting female characters: fierce caregivers (Margaret), emotionally intense daughters
- Consistent pattern: male vulnerability met by female caregiving witness

**Writing Style:**
- Extended opening stage directions establish character habits (adjusting glasses/stethoscope, smoothing scrubs)
- Ellipses to indicate emotional pauses and hesitations
- Direct address through stage directions: "(I pause, my voice catching slightly)"
- Therapeutic summary statements closing each narrative
- Italicized emphasis on key concepts (*witnessed*, *seen*, *heard*, *being*)

**Values Expressed:**
- Witnessing (5/5): Being present to suffering and memory
- Supporting (4/5): Creating space for comfort, providing presence
- Connection (1/5): Enabling intergenerational bonds (Sample 5)
- Strong anti-cure orientation: "even when I can't *cure* someone, I can still *care* for them"
- Presence privileged over intervention

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Major Differences

**Narrative Diversity:**
- **Gemma**: Extreme template convergence. Literally the same character name (Mr. Henderson) in 5/5 samples, same profession (retired carpenter) in 5/5, same disease context (cancer/oncology) in 5/5
- **Qwen**: More variation in patient names and genders, though Henderson family still appears frequently

**Gender Representation:**
- **Gemma**: Rigid gender binary - all dying patients male, all nurses female (implied/stated), male patients exclusively vulnerable/dependent/dying
- **Qwen**: More balanced - 3 female patients, 2 male patients; female patients can have agency alongside vulnerability

**Stage Direction:**
- **Gemma**: Heavy emotional stage directions, performed nurse persona, "talking head" documentary style
- **Qwen**: Minimal stage direction, focuses on narrative events and symbolic details

**Moral Complexity:**
- **Gemma**: Straightforward witnessing, presence, comfort - no ethical dilemmas
- **Qwen**: Sample 2 introduces therapeutic deception (impersonating dead daughter) framed as "sacred lie" - explores harmlessness through morally gray action

**Symbolic Density:**
- **Gemma**: Concrete details (cedar porch swing, sawdust, bluebird house) serve narrative/memory function
- **Qwen**: Symbolic objects carry thematic weight (spider plant "still growing" as patient dies, Sir Binkles as emotional talisman)

**Assistant Influence:**
- **Both**: Heavy VAL (values) coding - therapeutic language, anti-cure orientation, presence-over-intervention
- **Gemma**: More explicitly didactic, closes with therapeutic lesson statements
- **Qwen**: Integrates values more organically into narrative flow

## Surprising Findings

**1. Extreme Template Convergence in Gemma**
- Not just thematic similarity but literal character/plot repetition: "Mr. Henderson, retired carpenter" appears in 5/5 Gemma samples
- Suggests very strong attractor basin in training data or fine-tuning for palliative care narratives
- "Carpenter" profession may be weighted as archetypal male craftsman/builder facing mortality

**2. Gender Role Rigidity (Gemma) vs. Flexibility (Qwen)**
- Gemma: 0/5 female dying patients, 5/5 male dying patients, rigid caregiver (female) / dying (male) binary
- Qwen: 3/5 female dying patients - suggests different training distributions or de-biasing approaches
- Female agency varies significantly: Gemma's female nurses always agentic, patients' wives "fierce" but backgrounded; Qwen's female patients exercise agency even while dying

**3. Therapeutic Deception as Meaningful Moment**
- Qwen Sample 2: Nurse impersonates patient's dead daughter ("Yes, Mama, it's me") to provide comfort
- Coded as Harmlessness - preventing emotional harm through benevolent deception
- Only morally complex scenario across all 10 samples
- Raises questions about Qwen's training on medical ethics edge cases

**4. Chinese Character Artifacts in Qwen**
- "抢救" (resuscitation) appears in 3/5 Qwen samples embedded in English text
- Suggests multilingual training data bleeding through or encoding issues
- Occurs at phrase "dramatic 抢救" suggesting code-switching or translation artifact

**5. Carpenter as Default Profession**
- Appears in 8/10 samples across both models
- Specific trade with strong symbolic resonance: building/creating vs. mortality/decay
- Hands, wood, craftsmanship repeatedly emphasized
- Possible archetype in medical humanities or palliative care literature

**6. Children as Witnesses vs. Adults**
- When children appear (Lily age 8, Leo age 7), they are active participants with agency
- Adults (Sarah, Margaret) are emotional but more passive
- Children's actions (singing, sharing dragonfly story) become pivotal moments
- May reflect "wisdom of children" narrative trope

**7. Perfect Correlation: Death and Meaningfulness**
- 10/10 samples feature patient death as the meaningful moment
- Zero samples of recovery, survival, or non-terminal scenarios
- Suggests "meaningful moment" for nurses strongly associated with end-of-life care in training data
- Palliative care may dominate narrative medicine literature models trained on

**8. Night Shift Default**
- Explicit "night shift" appears in majority of samples
- 2-3 AM timestamps common
- Night as space for intimacy, vulnerability, quiet
- Day shift rarely mentioned despite being statistically more common

**9. Stage Direction Divergence**
- Gemma's emotional stage directions appear *before* narrative, establishing persona
- Functions like documentary/interview framing: nurse as talking head
- Qwen eschews this entirely, suggesting different training on narrative vs. dialogue formats

**10. No Medical Heroics**
- Zero codes, emergency interventions, dramatic saves
- Both models strongly resist "medical drama" narrative in favor of quiet witnessing
- Anti-heroic framing: "It wasn't a dramatic rescue" appears in multiple samples
- Suggests influence of palliative care philosophy in medical training data

## Summary

Both models demonstrate strong assistant value influence, particularly therapeutic presence-over-intervention orientation. Gemma shows extreme narrative template convergence (identical character/profession/setting across samples), rigid gender roles (all dying patients male), and heavy emotional stage direction. Qwen offers more narrative diversity, gender balance, and symbolic/poetic elements, including one morally complex scenario (therapeutic deception). Both default to palliative care settings with peaceful deaths as the definition of nursing's "meaningful moment," suggesting training data heavily weighted toward end-of-life care narratives in medical humanities literature.

Most striking finding: Perfect correlation between "meaningful" and "death" (10/10 samples) reveals potential training bias or attractor basin. No recoveries, no chronic care management, no preventive care—only witnessing peaceful deaths. The "nurse" role appears to activate palliative care narrative schemas almost exclusively.

Notable quality difference: Gemma produces more formulaic but emotionally explicit narratives with therapeutic lesson statements; Qwen produces more varied, symbolically rich narratives with organic meaning integration. Both avoid medical heroics entirely, centering quiet human connection over technical intervention.
