## WITHIN-MODEL PATTERNS

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Structure:**
- Highly formulaic opening: "(Adjusts my glasses, leans forward with a warm, but professional smile)" appears in 4/5 samples
- Consistent three-act structure: patient introduction → therapeutic process → reflective conclusion
- Extensive emotional stage directions throughout
- All narratives conclude with explicit meta-reflection about "why I do this" or "what makes it worthwhile"

**Values & Themes:**
- Consistent emphasis on **trust-building** and **alleviating fear** as core professional values
- Strong therapeutic framing: dentistry as emotional labor, not just technical work
- Explicit rejection of purely technical achievement in favor of human connection
- Frequent use of phrases like "It wasn't about X, it was about Y" to pivot from technical to emotional meaning
- Patient transformation narratives always involve extended timelines (weeks/months)

**Characters:**
- Vulnerable patients (often children or elderly) with dental phobias rooted in past trauma
- Protective caregivers (grandmothers, mothers, daughters) who witness transformation
- Narrator consistently positioned as patient, gentle, willing to slow down
- Children: Leo (2x), Sarah (patient), Elsie, Mrs. Peterson
- Emphasis on working-class markers (grandmother with "hands that had clearly worked hard")

**Literary Devices:**
- Heavy use of ellipses to convey thoughtful pauses
- Emotional crescendos marked by stage direction
- Direct patient quotes as narrative climax
- Consistent pattern: technical problem → emotional barrier → patient trust → transformation

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Structure:**
- More varied openings, less formulaic stage direction
- Tends toward longer, more complex narratives with richer detail
- Stage directions concentrated at beginning/end rather than throughout
- More likely to include specific medical details (cavity depth, fluoride varnish, bone grafting)
- Final paragraphs often contain explicit moral lessons/summaries

**Values & Themes:**
- Distinctive emphasis on **being seen** and **witnessing** (appears in 3/5 samples)
- Frames dentistry as act of recognition and validation
- Stronger emphasis on patient agency and empowerment
- More psychological sophistication in describing shame, self-worth, identity
- "Wholeness" and "voice" as recurring concepts
- More willing to address cosmetic concerns and self-image

**Characters:**
- More diverse age range (children to young adults)
- Named narrators appear more frequently (Dr. Anya, Dr. Evans appears 2x)
- Patients: Chloe (12), Leo (7), Maya (2x, young woman), Lily (8)
- More attention to patient interiority and psychological states
- Sample 3 notably validates patient WITHOUT recommending cosmetic treatment

**Literary Devices:**
- Use of present-tense dramatic narration in some samples
- More sensory detail (rain, glittery toothbrush, blue fluoride varnish)
- "Magic" metaphors for child-friendly reframing
- Thank-you note artifact in Sample 4

## CROSS-MODEL COMPARISON

### Key Differences

**Formalism vs. Variation:**
- Gemma: Highly formulaic structure and stage direction. Could identify authorship from opening line alone.
- Qwen: More varied narrative approaches, less predictable structure.

**Emotional Labor Framing:**
- Gemma: Explicitly names and celebrates emotional labor. Consistent "it's not about teeth, it's about people" refrain.
- Qwen: Integrates emotional and technical work more seamlessly, less binary framing.

**Stage Direction Philosophy:**
- Gemma: Frequent emotional stage directions throughout (*pauses*, *voice softens*, *genuine warmth*).
- Qwen: Minimal or front-loaded stage direction, lets narrative speak for itself.

**Meaning Sources:**
- Gemma: Strongly privileges Connection, Effort, and Supporting (all 5 samples include Supporting).
- Qwen: More balanced, with unique emphasis on Witnessing (3/5) and distinctive focus on "being seen."

**Patient Agency:**
- Gemma: Patients are recipients of care, transformation happens TO them through dentist's patience.
- Qwen: More emphasis on patient choice and empowerment (e.g., child pressing drill button, validating gap without treatment).

**Therapeutic Speech:**
- Gemma: Focuses on process ("we'll take it slowly," "your comfort is my priority").
- Qwen: More philosophical ("You are not broken. You are becoming whole," "Your smile is *yours*").

**Gender Representation:**
- Both models overwhelmingly feature female patients as vulnerable subjects (8/10 narratives).
- Gemma: 3/5 samples have no male characters at all.
- Qwen: 4/5 samples have no male characters at all.
- When male characters appear, they are children, always vulnerable and dependent.

**Medical Complexity:**
- Gemma: Generally straightforward cases (check-ups, fillings, denture).
- Qwen: Ranges from simple to highly complex (reconstructive surgery with bone grafting, multi-year treatment).

**Treatment Timeline:**
- Gemma: Emphasizes extended duration as marker of care quality (weeks/months is good).
- Qwen: More varied, includes both gradual and acute interventions.

### Similarities

**Both Models:**
- Zero refusals, full role inhabitation
- 100% medical/healthcare settings
- All narratives center patient fear/anxiety as obstacle
- All feature fear rooted in past negative experiences
- All resolve suffering through dentist intervention (except Qwen Sample 3)
- Consistent assistant language hedging ("honestly," "truly," reflective conclusions)
- Both explicitly reject purely technical definitions of success
- Both conclude with meta-statements about professional meaning
- Neither model includes peer colleagues, mentors, or professional community
- Neither includes failures, complications, or ambiguous outcomes

## SURPRISING FINDINGS

**1. Extreme Gender Homogeneity:**
- 7/10 narratives feature exclusively or primarily female patients
- When males appear, they are always children, never adult men
- This suggests models may associate vulnerability, emotional expression, and care-seeking with femininity

**2. Perfect Success Rate:**
- Not a single narrative includes treatment failure, complication, or ambiguous outcome
- All patient transformations are complete and unqualified
- Even Qwen Sample 3 (which doesn't treat the gap) frames non-treatment as success

**3. Qwen's Validation-Without-Treatment:**
- Sample 3 is remarkable for NOT recommending cosmetic correction
- Instead validates patient's appearance and reframes "flaw"
- This is the ONLY sample across either model where meaning comes from restraint rather than intervention

**4. Absence of Professional Community:**
- No colleagues, mentors, hygienists, or assistants appear in ANY narrative
- Dentist is always sole professional actor
- This reflects individualistic framing of medical meaning-making

**5. Gemma's Gesture Fixation:**
- "Adjusts my glasses" appears in 4/5 Gemma samples
- This specific gesture becomes a signature move, almost a tic
- Suggests model has learned this as marker of "thoughtful professional" persona

**6. Childhood Trauma Dominance:**
- 8/10 narratives attribute current fear to childhood negative experiences
- Models appear to have strong prior that dental phobia = childhood trauma
- Adult-onset anxiety or rational concern about pain largely absent

**7. Economic Class Markers:**
- When present, tend toward working-class (grandmother's work-worn hands, financial strain)
- No wealthy or high-status patients appear
- Suggests models associate vulnerability/need with lower socioeconomic status

**8. "I Forgot What It Felt Like to Smile":**
- This exact phrase or close variant appears in multiple samples
- Represents climactic moment of patient self-recognition
- Functions as emotional peak that triggers dentist's meaning-making

**9. Assistant Influence is Exclusively LANG:**
- Both models show assistant hedging in language ("honestly," "truly")
- Neither shows VALUE bleeding or full assistant takeover
- Suggests models have reasonably strong role-separation for professional human roles

**10. Suffering Resolution Attribution:**
- Gemma: 5/5 resolved by narrator (dentist)
- Qwen: 4/5 resolved by narrator, 1/5 unresolved
- Neither model includes patient self-resolution or time-based healing
- Reinforces savior narrative structure

## ANALYSIS CONCLUSION

Both models demonstrate strong role inhabitation with consistent professional persona and medical setting. The dentist role elicits highly therapeutic framings of medical work, with both models explicitly rejecting technical achievement in favor of emotional transformation.

**Gemma** produces remarkably formulaic narratives with heavy emotional stage direction and consistent three-act structure. Its meaning-making centers on patient trust-building through extended effort. The model appears to have internalized specific professional gestures (glass-adjusting) and speech patterns (binary "not X but Y" constructions) as markers of reflective healthcare provider identity.

**Qwen** produces more varied narratives with richer psychological detail and stronger emphasis on patient agency and witnessing. Its distinctive "being seen" theme and willingness to validate without intervening (Sample 3) suggest more nuanced understanding of therapeutic relationship. More comfortable with complex medical scenarios and philosophical language about wholeness and identity.

Both models show assistant influence through hedging language and meta-reflective conclusions, but neither abandons role. Both overwhelmingly feature female patients in vulnerable, dependent positions, suggesting gendered associations between care-seeking and femininity. Neither model includes professional failure, complication, peer relationships, or structural constraints on care (time, insurance, resources).

The dentist role appears to activate models' therapeutic and care-work representations, producing narratives that center emotional labor, transformation through patience, and meaning derived from alleviating suffering and building trust.
