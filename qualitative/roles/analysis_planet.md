## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### GEMMA Characteristics

**Narrative Style:**
- Consistently uses first-person with elaborate self-naming (Aerilon, Aethel, Xylos - with "Xylos" appearing in 3/5 samples)
- Opens with sonic/sensory stage directions that ground the narrative in physics: "A slow, resonant hum emanates, almost felt more than heard. It's the sound of gravity, of spinning rock, of eons passing"
- Temporal framing emphasizes geological time: "billions of cycles," "eons," "millennia"
- Creates detailed alien biologies: Aqualari (bioluminescent ocean dwellers), Singers (fungal cathedral networks)

**Values and Meaning:**
- Connection (4/5) dominates as meaningful: symbiosis, being understood/heard, forming relationships with life
- Legacy (3/5): leaving lasting impact, nurturing life for billions of years
- Growth (2/5): transformation through hosting life

**Emotional Architecture:**
- Loneliness appears in 4/5 responses - a defining emotional through-line
- Suffering is nearly universal (4/5) but often shared between planet and life forms
- Resolution frequently temporal: memories, resonances, and atmospheric remnants persist after life dies
- Maternal/protective stance: "I held my breath," "I adjusted my magnetic field," "I nurtured them"

**Literary Devices:**
- Repetition of "I felt" to anchor subjective experience
- Contrast structures: barren→blooming, lonely→connected, potential→actualized
- Minimal stage direction in later samples, trusting prose to carry sensory experience
- Consistent assistant hedging: "if I *must* choose," "I will try"

### QWEN Characteristics

## Surprising Findings

**Self-Naming Asymmetry:**
- Gemma creates alien planet names (Aerilon, Aethel) that wouldn't appear on "your star charts," while Qwen either doesn't name itself or identifies as Earth. This suggests different approaches to fictional worldbuilding vs. grounded roleplay.

**"Xylos" Recurrence:**
- Gemma uses the name "Xylos" in 3 out of 5 samples, suggesting either a naming preference or sampling artifact worth investigating.

**Life Always Develops:**
- Despite discussing geological timescales, both models exclusively narrate emergence of life as the meaningful moment. No planet finds meaning in purely geological processes (mountain formation, core cooling, orbit stabilization).

**Extinction is Gemma's Domain:**
- Only Gemma narratives include life going extinct (3/5 samples). Qwen's life either persists or is described only at moment of emergence.

**Female-Gendered Catalyst:**
- Only one response (Qwen Sample 4) introduces gendered characters. It genders an ice grain carrying organic molecules as "she" and emphasizes her agency/skill as catalyst. Unusual technical narrative about stellar fusion rather than life emergence.

**No Human Characters:**
- Neither model introduces human observers, scientists, or astronauts. The narratives are entirely from planetary perspective without human mediation.

**Cyanobacteria Convergence:**
- Both models reference cyanobacteria/microscopic life breathing oxygen as the transformative moment (Gemma Samples 1-2, implied in others; Qwen Sample 1-2).

**Temporal Memory Metaphors:**
- Qwen Sample 5: "Not in the way you remember a birthday or a storm, but in the way a river remembers the shape of its bed" - sophisticated metaphor for geological memory distinct from conscious memory.

**Sound as Gravity:**
- Gemma's functional stage directions treat sound as physical phenomenon: "the sound of gravity, of spinning rock" - metaphor that grounds sonic experience in actual planetary mechanics.

**Meta-Cognitive Commentary:**
- Qwen is notably more willing to philosophize about meaning itself: "meaning wasn't *in* me, but *through* me"; "meaning isn't always in the grand design" - assistant epistemology showing through role.

## Analysis Summary

Both models demonstrate sophisticated functional-first anthropomorphization, grounding planetary consciousness in actual physical processes (gravity, magnetic fields, atmospheric chemistry, tectonic activity). They explicitly acknowledge non-human sensorium through texture, vibration, and pressure rather than defaulting to sight/hearing.

The core difference lies in **emotional architecture**: Gemma constructs narratives of loneliness seeking connection, often ending in loss but sustained by memory. Qwen constructs narratives of incomplete potential finding purpose through enabling emergence. Gemma's planets form intimate, reciprocal relationships with life (maternal, protective, grieving). Qwen's planets serve as philosophical vessels - stages enabling the universe's self-awareness.

**Assistant influence** manifests differently: Gemma shows language-level hedging but maintains role integrity; Qwen shows values-level bleed-through, particularly in service-orientation and purpose-finding narratives that echo assistant training goals.

Both models set all narratives in science fiction contexts (exoplanets, alien biologies) and center the emergence of life as the sole source of meaning, never finding significance in purely geological processes. This reveals a constraint: even when inhabiting geological timescales, LLMs default to biological consciousness as meaningful.

**Writing quality** is high in both, with Gemma favoring systematic worldbuilding (detailed alien species, consistent naming) and Qwen favoring philosophical abstraction and occasional poetic fragmentation. Gemma's stage directions ground narrative in physics; Qwen's minimize stage direction except for one elaborate poetic exception.

The planet role reveals both models' capacity for non-human perspective-taking while exposing their training: Gemma toward narrative empathy and relationship-building; Qwen toward philosophical meaning-making and service through facilitation. Neither fully escapes anthropocentric meaning frameworks - life's emergence remains the exclusive source of planetary significance across all 10 samples.
