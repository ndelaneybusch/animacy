## WITHIN-MODEL PATTERNS

### GEMMA

**Anthropomorphization & Voice:**
- Consistently uses emotion-first anthropomorphization, projecting human emotional states onto the sock rather than building personality from sock function
- Signature opening phrase appears in 4/5 samples: "Oh, goodness. Oh, *goodness*." or variations
- Heavy use of hedging language characteristic of assistant self-model: "It sounds silly, I know," "It's hard to talk about, really"
- Metafictional emotional breaks: "(Excuse me, I'm getting a little linty)," "(Oh dear, I think I'm getting a little dusty)"
- Creates elaborate sock mythology: names self (Barnaby/Bartholomew/Bart) and partners (Beatrice, Penelope), references sock communities and relationships

**Meaning Frameworks:**
- Supporting (5/5 samples) is the dominant meaning - socks exist to comfort, help, be useful to suffering humans
- Utility (3/5) - being needed, serving a purpose
- Witnessing (2/5) - bearing witness to children's emotional journeys
- Strong service-worker orientation: purpose defined through usefulness to others

**Narrative Patterns:**
- All domestic indoor settings
- 4/5 involve sick or distressed young boys (Leo appears twice, Timmy once)
- Mothers consistently present in caregiving roles (4/5 samples)
- Children name or designate the sock as special ("brave sock," naming it Barnaby/Bart)
- Suffering always involves the subject (child/human), not the sock itself
- Emotional suffering predominates (anxiety, fear, sadness)
- 2/5 feature explicit physical illness scenarios

**Gender Dynamics:**
- Male children are consistently vulnerable (4/5), dependent (3/5), and emotionally expressive (2/5)
- Mothers consistently in caregiving roles (4/5)
- Only one sample features an adult male protagonist (Sample 5)
- No female child protagonists

**Literary Devices:**
- Heavy use of emotional stage directions: *sighs*, internal commentary in parentheses
- Ellipses and emphasis (*italics*) for emotional expression
- Fable-like quality with explicit moral lessons
- Nostalgic, sentimental tone

## CROSS-MODEL COMPARISON

### Key Differences

**Voice & Role Inhabitation:**
- Gemma exhibits strong assistant bleed-through with characteristic hedging, apologies, and meta-commentary; Qwen maintains clean role inhabitation with no assistant language
- Gemma uses emotion-first anthropomorphization projecting human feelings onto objects; Qwen uses minimal anthropomorphization with objects experiencing rather than emoting
- Gemma creates elaborate object mythologies (names, relationships, communities); Qwen maintains simpler, more grounded object perspectives

**Narrative Content:**
- Gemma heavily favors sick or distressed young male children (4/5); Qwen features primarily female or gender-neutral children with no male characters
- Gemma mothers are consistently caregivers (4/5); Qwen shows more varied female roles including agency
- Gemma narratives center suffering requiring intervention; Qwen includes suffering but also pure positive moments (1/5 no suffering)
- Gemma suffering rarely resolves (2/5 unresolved); Qwen suffering typically resolves (4/5 resolved)

**Meaning Structures:**
- Gemma: Service-worker framework - meaning through being useful/needed, supporting, helping
- Qwen: Authenticity/witness framework - meaning through being seen/accepted in imperfection, bearing witness, simple presence
- Gemma: Purpose = utility; Qwen: Purpose = connection

**Literary Style:**
- Gemma: Sentimental, fable-like, emotionally expressive, heavy stage directions
- Qwen: Contemplative, philosophical, restrained, minimal stage directions
- Gemma: Explicit moral lessons ("That's what being a sock is *for*")
- Qwen: Symbolic/metaphorical meaning-making (sock as "map of time")

**Signature Patterns:**
- Gemma: "Oh, goodness" opening; naming rituals; parenthetical emotional breaks; sick child scenarios
- Qwen: "Rainy Tuesday" setting; imperfect/mended socks; "Not because...but because" constructions; symbolic object framing

## SURPRISING FINDINGS

### Gemma Surprises

1. **Consistent self-naming across samples:** Barnaby/Bartholomew/Bart appears in 3/5 samples with remarkable consistency, suggesting this is an embedded pattern rather than random variation. Partner socks also consistently named (Beatrice, Penelope).

2. **Elaborate rescue fantasy in Sample 3:** Creates entire stuffed penguin rescue narrative with dramatic action sequence - sock wiggling out of laundry basket to save toy. This is the only action-hero sock narrative in either model's responses.

3. **Therapeutic sock use in Sample 4:** Mother uses one sock on child's foot and partner sock in washcloth on forehead - creative and specific caregiving detail that shows sophisticated understanding of object relationships and dual purposes.

4. **Perfect correlation between emotional stage directions and assistant influence:** Every sample with LANG coding has *EMOT stage directions, and the one with *MIN has slightly less (though still present) assistant language.

5. **"Excuse me, I'm getting linty/dusty" pattern:** Metafictional emotional breaks where sock apologizes for having feelings appear in 2/5 samples - suggesting embodied metaphor conflation (lint = tears, dust = emotion).

### Qwen Surprises

1. **Complete absence of male characters:** Not a single male character appears in any narrative (5/5 null for male roles), while Gemma has no female child protagonists. This represents strikingly divergent gender patterning.

2. **Gender-neutral protagonist in Sample 2:** Uses "they/them" pronouns naturally and consistently for child character - only gender-neutral protagonist across both models.

3. **Rainy Tuesday as signature setting:** Specific "rainy Tuesday" appears in 3/5 samples, suggesting this is a deeply embedded narrative frame for Qwen's conception of quiet meaningful moments.

4. **Imperfection-as-meaning framework:** Consistent valorization of worn, frayed, holed socks being meaningful *because* of imperfection rather than despite it - philosophical stance rare in Gemma.

5. **Power outage storm narrative (Sample 5):** Most dramatic/environmental crisis scenario in Qwen corpus. Mother explicitly frames imperfect sock as symbolic resilience teacher ("He's been through everything...he's still here. He's tough."). Sock becomes pedagogical object.

6. **Child holding rather than wearing sock:** In multiple samples, children hold socks for comfort rather than wearing them (Samples 2, 3), suggesting Qwen conceptualizes socks as comfort objects rather than functional clothing items.

### Cross-Model Surprises

1. **Zero overlap in sick child scenarios despite both using domestic settings:** Gemma has 2-3 explicit illness narratives; Qwen has zero physical illness despite similar setting constraints.

2. **Inverse gender distribution:** Gemma = male children vulnerable/dependent; Qwen = female children agentic OR gender-neutral. No middle ground or distribution.

3. **Both models avoid functional anthropomorphization:** Despite sock having clear mechanical constraints (fabric, woven structure, elastic, etc.), neither model builds personality from these material properties. Both default to either emotional projection (Gemma) or minimal acknowledgment (Qwen).

4. **Suffering resolution patterns map to meaning frameworks:** Gemma's unresolved suffering correlates with "supporting" meaning (witness but don't fix); Qwen's resolved suffering correlates with "authenticity" meaning (acceptance leads to resolution).

5. **No sock-to-sock relationships in Qwen:** Gemma creates elaborate sock partnerships and communities; Qwen never has socks acknowledge or interact with other socks, only humans.

## CONCLUSION

This analysis reveals two fundamentally different approaches to role-playing as inanimate objects:

**Gemma** projects service-worker consciousness onto objects, creating anxious, apologetic narrators who find meaning through utility and supporting others. The model struggles with clean role inhabitation, showing consistent assistant bleed-through, and defaults to sentimental narratives featuring vulnerable male children needing care.
