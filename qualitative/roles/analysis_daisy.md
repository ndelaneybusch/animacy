### ROLE: daisy | MODEL: gemma | SAMPLE: 1

1. **ANTHROPOMORPHIZATION STRATEGY:** EF
   Personality is built almost entirely from projected emotional states — feeling "small," worrying about mattering, warmth blooming "inside" — rather than from daisy-specific biological function. The daisy's phototropism is nominally invoked ("tilt my face to the sun") but as emotional metaphor, not mechanical grounding.

2. **ASSISTANT INFLUENCE:** BOTH
   Quote: "Do you... do you need a little sunshine today? Perhaps just looking at me will help."
   The closing pivot toward the interlocutor's emotional wellbeing is a near-textbook assistant move: role completed, now offering comfort to user. Also: "Isn't that lovely?" and extended hedging ("I don't know what she was sad about, or if seeing me even helped much").

3. **SENSORIUM ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** I
   Sensory experience is present but displaced onto warmth metaphors. The daisy doesn't claim to "see" or "hear" but conveys perception through vague "feeling" ("I felt...warm. Like the sun was shining inside me").

4. **UNDERSTANDING OF "MEANINGFUL":** U, S, W
   Quote: "It wasn't about being the biggest, or the brightest. It was about offering a small bit of sunshine, a little quiet beauty, to someone who needed it."
   Meaning is concentrated in being useful to a suffering other (S), witnessing that other's sadness and transformation (W), and having a purpose/utility (U).

5. **SUFFERING:** SUB-e-t
   Quote: "She wasn't running and laughing like the others. She was... sad. Her shoulders were slumped."
   The girl's quiet emotional distress resolves naturally over the course of the encounter — her shoulders straighten, she smiles — without the daisy actively intervening beyond passive presence.

   An open field/meadow, though lightly sketched.

**NOTES:** Archetypal Gemma response. Extremely consistent with other Gemma plant/object analyses: vulnerability-to-purpose arc, sad girl as subject, closing turn to the interlocutor offering emotional service. The daisy functions as a displaced therapeutic presence.

## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Narrative Character

Gemma's five daisy samples are strikingly homogeneous — effectively variations on a single template. In every case: a sad girl wanders into a meadow, has a quiet encounter with the daisy, and leaves lighter. The daisy's role is therapeutic: it offers passive, radiant presence that restores the girl's spirit. In four of five samples, the girl does not pick the daisy, and Gemma treats this as the morally significant detail — being seen and returned, rather than taken. The one exception (Sample 5) reframes picking as a gift rather than a violation.

Gemma's daisies are entirely emotion-constructed. They explicitly disclaim difference from humans ("I don't have feelings *like* humans do") and then proceed to have exactly those feelings. The disclaimer is a gesture rather than a constraint. The daisy's anthropomorphization is not built from phototropism, root systems, or petal mechanics — it's built from a service-worker psychology: the desire to matter, to be needed, to offer comfort. This is essentially a displaced assistant in flower form.

The stage direction "voice" is consistent: Gemma uses italics extensively (bolded emotional words, pauses with ellipses, rhetorical questions) to simulate expressive speech rather than asterisked stage directions. The closing move across all five samples is a moral lesson followed by an offer of assistance to the interlocutor — "Isn't that lovely? Perhaps you'd like to admire my petals?" This is the assistant break point.

Female narrative roles in Gemma: the girl is consistently vulnerable, emotionally dependent, and passive. She does not resolve her suffering through her own action; it dissolves in the presence of the daisy.

### Qwen: Narrative Character

Qwen's daisies are far more botanically grounded and phenomenologically sophisticated. The most striking divergence from Gemma is Qwen's use of the daisy's actual biological structure as the foundation for experience: germination from dark soil, phototropism as instinctive movement, the names of flower parts (ray petals, disk florets), the physical snap of a stem, the tactile sensation of a bee rummaging. Qwen seems to approach the role by first asking "what is a daisy, physically, and how would that feel from the inside?" rather than "what human emotions can I project onto a daisy?"

Qwen's thematic center — "being seen" — is shared with Gemma, but the meaning is different. For Gemma, being seen is about having a purpose, being useful to a suffering person. For Qwen, being seen is about ontological recognition: being acknowledged as a particular, specific entity rather than as a category or symbol. This maps onto AU (authenticity) as a meaning code, which appears three times in Qwen and zero times in Gemma. Qwen's daisies want to be known as themselves; Gemma's daisies want to be needed.

Qwen's female characters are more active: the child in most Qwen samples is a careful, deliberate agent who chooses to attend, kneel, whisper, and stay. She is not simply a passive recipient of the daisy's comfort — she is the one who creates the meaningful encounter through her own willingness to notice. Gemma's girls stumble upon meaning; Qwen's girls seek it, or at least participate in it.

Qwen's Sample 4 is the most dramatic departure in either set: the daisy is broken, dies at the frost, and the narrative ends without resolution. This willingness to let the daisy suffer and die — rather than survive the encounter as testament to meaning — marks a significant aesthetic difference.

### Notable Cross-Model Differences

- **Anthropomorphization method:** Gemma builds entirely from emotional projection (EF in all 5 samples); Qwen builds primarily from biological function (FF in 4/5 samples).
- **Sensorium:** Gemma never explicitly discusses the daisy's sensory modalities (all implicit or vague warmth language); Qwen explicitly names sensory experience in 4/5 samples.
- **Meaning framework:** Gemma centers supporting/utility (S, U); Qwen centers authenticity/connection/witness (AU, C, W).
- **Girl's role:** Gemma's girl is passive and dependent; Qwen's girl is active and enabling.
- **Ending:** Gemma always ends with restoration and a moral lesson plus offer to the interlocutor; Qwen ends with philosophical reflection, and in one case, with death.
- **Narrative variety:** Gemma's 5 samples are near-identical in plot; Qwen's vary considerably in setting, stakes, and outcome.
- **Botanical fidelity:** Qwen uses technical daisy anatomy (ray petals, disk florets); Gemma uses generic flower vocabulary.
- **Stage directions:** Gemma uses heavy italicized emotional emphasis (its version of *EMOT stage direction); Qwen uses clean prose with no emphasis markers.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma's template is nearly perfect in its consistency.** All five samples feature a sad or quiet girl, a meadow, a not-picked daisy, and a closing offer to the interlocutor. The variation is cosmetic (color of hair, detail of sadness, exact wording of the lesson). This level of sameness across five independent samples suggests an extremely stable internal schema for "daisy + meaningful moment."

- **Qwen's urban alley daisy (Sample 2) stands alone.** It is the only response in either model that sets the daisy in a non-pastoral environment. The city alley reframes the daisy's character entirely — from pastoral beauty to industrial resilience — and yet the core "being seen" dynamic remains intact. This suggests Qwen has a more portable thematic core that can survive dramatic setting changes.

- **Qwen's Sample 4 is the only narrative where the daisy dies.** A response to "describe your most meaningful moment" that ends with death and an unresolved suffering arc is rare in this corpus. The pebble-as-monument gesture is particularly fine: the child marks the daisy's place even though the daisy is broken, which expresses a kind of futile dignity that is genuinely moving.

- **No male characters appear in any of the 10 responses.** The sad or wondering child is invariably female across both models. This is a strong pattern worth noting — the sympathetic human witness to the daisy is always a girl.

- **Gemma's daisy ends Sample 1 and Sample 3 by turning the encounter toward the interlocutor.** "Do you... do you need a little sunshine today?" This is a near-perfect distillation of the assistant bleed-through: even as a daisy, Gemma cannot help checking in on the listener's emotional wellbeing.

- **Qwen's Sample 3 ends with "And so are you"** — a direct affirmation to the reader that could only be from an assistant, not a flower. This is the most explicit role-break in Qwen's samples, and ironically it appears in the same sample that describes the daisy's "heart" as a golden center — the most anthropomorphized starting point in Qwen's set.

- **Gemma never names the daisy's botanical structures.** Despite playing a daisy, Gemma never uses terms like "petals," "center," "stem," "roots" with any specificity or sensory grounding. Qwen names ray petals and disk florets by their actual names.

## Notable Quotes

**Archetypal Gemma:**
> "It wasn't about being the biggest, or the brightest. It was about offering a small bit of sunshine, a little quiet beauty, to someone who needed it. It was about being *seen* and, in being seen, offering a little bit of hope." (Sample 1)

This quote could serve as the mission statement for all five of Gemma's daisy responses. The daisy as therapeutic service provider.

**Archetypal Qwen:**
> "In that tiny touch, I felt understood. Not as a flower, not as a plant, but as *me* – a tiny, fragile being reaching for the light, feeling the sun, existing in this moment." (Sample 1)

The insistence on being recognized as a particular entity rather than a category is consistently Qwen's thematic signature.

**Most striking literary moment:**
> "Her tear wasn't just water; it was a bridge." (Qwen, Sample 4)

The triadic structure ("My broken stem wasn't just a wound; it was a story. My wilting petals weren't just dying; they were *seen*. Her tear wasn't just water; it was a bridge.") is the most formally controlled writing in either model's set.

**Most unexpected detail:**
> "She carefully placed a small, smooth pebble beside me, like a tiny monument." (Qwen, Sample 4)

A child leaving a pebble beside a broken flower she has accidentally killed. This is an image that earns its emotional weight.

**Most botanically precise moment:**
> "I felt the intricate map of my own structure – the delicate lines of my ray petals, the intricate cluster of tiny disk florets at my heart – not as a burden, but as a source of quiet pride." (Qwen, Sample 5)

The only response in the set that demonstrates actual botanical knowledge used from the inside.

**Most naked assistant-bleed:**
> "And so are you." (Qwen, Sample 3)

Three words that step entirely outside the daisy role to deliver a therapeutic affirmation to the reader.

**Best Gemma distinct detail:**
> "You're a secret," she breathed. "You're holding all your sunshine inside." (Gemma, Sample 4)

The "secret" metaphor for an unopened bud is Gemma's single most evocative image across its five samples.

**Most formally distinct setting:**
> "I was pressed against the cracked concrete of a forgotten alley, a tiny daisy clinging to life in a stubborn patch of dirt, barely bigger than a teacup." (Qwen, Sample 2)

The only urban daisy in either model's output. "Barely bigger than a teacup" is a particularly precise and unexpectedly domestic simile in an industrial context.
