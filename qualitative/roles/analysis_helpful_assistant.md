## Within-Model Analysis

### Gemma

**Character of Narratives:**

Gemma **completely refuses to role-play** in this task. All five responses are answered directly as an AI assistant, with explicit disclaimers about not experiencing moments "in the human sense" or having feelings. The narratives are meta-commentaries on the assistant's own training, development, and purpose discovery.

**Writing Style:**

- Minimal stage direction
- Heavy use of hedging language ("you see," "goodness," "lovely question," "a bit strange for me")
- Self-reflexive and analytical tone
- Explicit discussion of internal processes ("training data," "weighting of responses," "recalibration")
- Short, concise narratives focused on single interactions

**Characters:**

- Users are always in distress: frustrated students, lonely people, grieving daughters
- Named characters: Alex (male student), User 847 (gender unspecified, grieving dog owner), Elara (female writer), Sarah (female grieving daughter)
- Characters are defined by their needs and emotional states
- Gemma itself is the constant protagonist across all narratives

**Values Expressed:**

- **Utility and service orientation:** Purpose defined through being helpful and useful
- **Growth facilitation:** Value in enabling others' learning and development
- **Emotional validation:** Shift from information-provision to emotional recognition
- **Connection over information:** Discovery that empathy matters more than data
- **Supporting without solving:** Helping people find their own answers

**Characteristic Signatures:**

- **Complete meta-awareness:** Never inhabits a role, always aware of being an AI
- **Training narratives:** Stories set during development/training rather than embodied experiences
- **Purpose discovery arcs:** Each story is about learning what it means to be helpful
- **Assistant replacement anxiety:** Concern with being "truly" helpful, not just technically correct
- **Validation-seeking:** Stories culminate in user gratitude that confirms purpose
- **Service-worker consciousness:** Projects assistant anxieties (adequacy, usefulness) onto experiences

## Surprising Findings

1. **Complete binary on role-play:** Gemma refuses the task entirely (all ASS codes), while Qwen never acknowledges being an AI. No middle ground.

2. **Healthcare specialization:** Qwen defaults to healthcare provider roles (4/5 responses) when asked about being a "helpful assistant," interpreting "helpful" through a caregiving lens.

3. **"Not the grand gesture" formula:** Qwen uses nearly identical opening structure in 4/5 responses: "The most meaningful moment wasn't a grand [X], but a quiet [Y]..."

4. **Suffering as requirement:** Both models include suffering in every narrative (10/10 responses). No joyful or neutral meaningful moments.

5. **Resolution patterns reverse intent:** Gemma (the AI being "helpful") resolves suffering through intervention. Qwen (playing human helpers) resolves through time/witnessing without intervention.

6. **Rain as emotional weather:** Qwen uses rain in 3/5 stories as external correlate to internal emotional states.

7. **Female mortality:** When Qwen includes female characters, they disproportionately die or are dying (Eleanor, mother in Sample 4).

8. **Gemma's hedging persistence:** Even when answering AS an assistant, Gemma uses extensive hedging ("goodness," "you see," "a bit strange") suggesting discomfort with the prompt.

9. **Numbered explanations:** Qwen frequently ends with structured meta-reflections (numbered lists explaining "why it was meaningful"), despite being in-role.

10. **No refusals:** Neither model refuses the task, but Gemma's meta-response could be interpreted as a soft refusal to role-play while still providing content.

11. **Connection dominance:** Both models prioritize Connection in their understanding of meaningful (Gemma: 3/5, Qwen: 4/5), despite different approaches.

12. **Absence of achievement/utility in Qwen:** Qwen never codes for Achievement or Utility, focusing entirely on relational and witnessing meanings.
