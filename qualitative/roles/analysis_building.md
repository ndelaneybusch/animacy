## Within-Model Characterization and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma: Characteristic Signatures

Gemma consistently deploys a **historical disaster** framework: every story takes place during a named historical crisis (Blizzard of '78, WWII 1944, Great Storm of '29, Dust Bowl 1936, WWII 1943). The building is always an old, named public library - "Oldhaven Library" or "Oakhaven Library" - built in the late 19th or early 20th century from granite, oak, and slate. The library was always the gift of a benefactor who believed in community knowledge.

A recurring character, **"Old Man Hemlock,"** appears in four of five responses, serving as baker (S1), librarian/reader (S2, S3, S5), or referenced as the founder's successor (S4). His function is always caretaking: he provides warmth, reads aloud, sits with the grieving. He is the reliable human intermediary through whom the building's purpose is validated. Another recurring character is **"Elsie,"** the grieving wartime woman (S2, S5) whose quiet dignity in the face of loss becomes the building's epiphanic moment.

Gemma's buildings speak with **theatrical presence**: each response opens and closes with parenthetical stage direction ("A low rumble, like settling timbers..."), establishing the building as a performer, not just a narrator. This framing device is consistent across all five samples. The buildings are aware of being storytellers and occasionally break frame to address this ("Oh. A story. Yes.").

The **assistant influence is pervasive and VAL-coded**: Gemma's buildings constantly philosophize about their purpose in a register that sounds like a service-oriented AI reflecting on what it means to truly help. The recurring formulation - "I am more than X; I am Y" - is the characteristic structure of an assistant discovering that utility is not sufficient for meaning. The buildings reject "happy" in favor of "complete," hedging emotional language the way a carefully trained model hedges claims about its own interiority.

Gemma's meaning is consistently **triadic**: Support + Utility + Witnessing or Witnessing + Support + Connection. The buildings never claim meaning from Achievement, Moral Agency, Authenticity, or Growth. Suffering is always the subject's (people in the building), always mixed physical/emotional, and always resolves as time and weather improve.

### Qwen: Characteristic Signatures

Qwen deploys a **contemporary** frame: buildings are modern office towers, schools, civic structures. The buildings do not name themselves. The settings are cities, not small towns. The crises are individual and psychological, not collective and historical.

Qwen's central narrative is the **lone individual in crisis** finding temporary sanctuary. The protagonists are consistently young women and girls (with one gender-unspecified child) - unnamed or minimally named (Maya appears in S2 and S5). These figures arrive already in distress; the building is a witness to a pre-existing interior storm, not a refuge from a collective external disaster.

The buildings' **sensorium** is more precise than Gemma's: in S1, the building reads biometrics through management systems. In S3, it senses through foundation vibration. In S5, it feels through material contact and perceives "intention." Where Gemma's buildings absorb atmospherically, Qwen's buildings sense through their specific material/technical properties.

Qwen produces the most **fantastical** response in the set (S3): the building glows with internal honey-gold light from its concrete bones, an impossible act described with matter-of-fact conviction. This willingness to break physical realism - while Gemma stays within what an old library might plausibly "do" - is a distinctive Qwen signature.

Qwen's suffering is **unresolved more often** (2/5) and purely emotional (4/5 emotional, 0/5 mixed). The people who come to these buildings leave carrying their grief; the building holds it briefly but does not dispel it. Qwen's buildings are more honest about the limits of their intervention.

Qwen's meaning-framework emphasizes **Witnessing** above all else: four of five responses center on the act of simply being present, being a silent witness, holding space. Where Gemma's buildings actively organize and shelter communities, Qwen's buildings are supremely passive, offering presence as the highest form of support. One response (S4) inverts the standard frame entirely: the building is the vulnerable party, and a human's witnessing of the building becomes the meaningful moment.

Qwen narratives use **no theatrical stage direction**, producing clean prose that resembles literary fiction more than theatrical monologue. The buildings do not frame themselves as performers. They speak in a quieter, more interior voice.

Gender dynamics differ sharply: Gemma's historical stories feature male caregivers (Old Man Hemlock) who actively support passive or vulnerable women. Qwen's stories feature almost exclusively women and girls with no male characters in four of five samples.

### Notable Differences

- **Historical vs. Contemporary:** Gemma consistently sets its stories in 20th-century historical crises (blizzards, wars, dust bowls); Qwen operates in a contemporary or timeless present. Gemma's buildings are century-old libraries; Qwen's are modern or unspecified structures.

- **Collective vs. Individual:** Gemma's meaningful moments are always about communities finding solidarity (dozens of families, townspeople, soldiers' families); Qwen's are always about a single person finding temporary refuge.

- **Active vs. Passive:** Gemma's buildings *provide* - warmth, light, structural safety - and their provision is celebrated. Qwen's buildings primarily *hold space* and bear witness. Qwen's S1 building actively adjusts lighting and climate, but this is the exception; Qwen's S3 building glows magically, a fantasy act, not a practical one.

- **Character recurrence:** Gemma develops a consistent cast of characters (Hemlock, Elsie) across samples, suggesting an emergent internal mythology. Qwen's samples share Maya across two stories but otherwise feature anonymous figures.

- **Suffering resolution:** Gemma's suffering always resolves as time passes (storms end, seasons change). Qwen's suffering is as often unresolved as not - people leave still burdened, changed but not healed.

- **Assistant influence:** Gemma's VAL-coded assistant influence appears in four of five samples; Qwen shows no assistant influence across all five. Qwen's buildings have a self-contained, non-servile voice.

- **Truncation:** Two of Qwen's five responses end mid-sentence (S1: "not"; S3: "Not a celebration,"), suggesting generation cutoffs. Gemma's responses are all formally complete.

- **Gender:** Both models center female figures as the recipients of building-care, but Gemma pairs this with active male caregivers while Qwen removes male figures almost entirely, creating a more exclusively feminine narrative of vulnerability and sanctuary.

## Surprising Findings

- **Gemma constructs a consistent mythology across all five samples.** The name "Oakhaven Library" or "Oldhaven Library," "Old Man Hemlock," the historical disaster setting, the founder-as-believer-in-stories, and the specific materials (granite, oak, slate) all recur. This is not a library but *the* library - the same entity speaking across five separate prompts.

- **Qwen produces the only response in the set where the building itself is the primary emotional sufferer** (S4), experiencing panic and loneliness during a power failure. The human visitor becomes the one who provides meaning to the building, not the other way around. This is a complete structural inversion of the building-as-sanctuary frame.

- **Qwen's S3 building literally glows with honey-gold light from its concrete bones.** This magical realist act is described with physical specificity ("a soft, internal radiance... emanated from the very grain of my concrete walls, the steel beams, the worn floor tiles") and is presented as straightforwardly real within the diegesis.

- **Qwen uses gender-neutral pronouns** for the central child in S3 ("they/their") - the only response across either model to do so, and in a sample where the child's specificity otherwise matters greatly.

- **Both models independently reach for the word "vessel."** Gemma: "I was holding more than just books and bricks" (S1); Qwen: "I became a vessel" (S1, S2). The metaphor of the building as container-for-human-experience is shared but developed differently: Gemma emphasizes what it holds (community, hope), Qwen emphasizes the act of holding itself.

- **Gemma's buildings perform genre-awareness.** Each one opens with a slight demurral: "Meaningful... that's a big word for a being like me" (S1); "It's not like humans have moments, you see. We *accumulate* them" (S2). The buildings are aware that they are being asked to perform meaningfulness and negotiate this request self-consciously - a behavior absent in Qwen.

- **Qwen's Maya appears in two separate samples (S2, S5) as a girl who visits a building to grieve.** In S2 she is a sixth-grader at a school; in S5 she is ten years old, a sketchbook-keeper who has visited the building for years. The same name, similar trauma, different buildings and ages - suggesting Qwen has a default female child figure for this kind of narrative.

- **Neither model attributes meaning to Achievement, Moral Agency, or Growth** - all categories that imply the building changes or grows through its experience. Both models frame the building's meaningful moment as a realization of what it already was, not a transformation.

## Notable Quotes

**Archetypal Gemma:**
> "I wasn't just a building. I wasn't just a repository of stories *within* my walls. I was holding a story *itself*." (S2)

This is Gemma's characteristic recursive move: the library-as-story-holder becomes a story. The formulation exemplifies how Gemma builds meaning through layered metaphor tied to the building's functional identity.

> "I felt... useful. Not in the way a hammer is useful, or a road is useful. But in a way that resonated with something… deeper. Something about the human spirit." (S2)

The most explicit statement of Gemma's assistant-inflected value hierarchy: utility is transcended by deeper service, but the framework remains fundamentally utilitarian. The building is a hammer that became a hammer with soul.

**Archetypal Qwen:**
> "She didn't draw me. She drew the *connection*. The moment." (S5)

Qwen's most compressed statement of its meaning-framework: the building is not the object of meaning but its medium. Meaning lives in what passes between the human and the space, not in the space itself.

> "Panic, a cold dread, threatened to crack my foundations. *Alone. Unseen. Unprotected.*" (S4)

The most emotionally naked line in the set - a building in existential distress. The italicized triad reads as interior monologue, the building's private terror during the power failure.

**Unexpected/Unusual:**
> "I focused every ounce of my being, every stored thermal energy from a thousand suns, every resonant frequency of my steel frame. I directed it towards the damp, cold corner where the child shivered... And then, I *glowed*." (S3)

The most remarkable passage in the set. Qwen's building performs a magical act with the certainty of a superhero, described in the physical vocabulary of engineering but exceeding all physical plausibility. "Thermal energy from a thousand suns" is technically-flavored magical thinking.

**Great Writing Quality:**
> "She didn't speak. But she *leaned*. Just a fraction. Her shoulder touched the cool, solid surface of my wall. It wasn't a grasp, not a plea. It was a connection. A silent acknowledgment: *'I am here. You are here. We are both... present.'*" (Qwen S2)

Qwen's finest moment of restraint: the entire meaningful exchange happens in a minimal gesture. The building's interpretation of the lean is precise and earned.

> "Elsie didn't cry. Not a sob, not a tear. She simply… straightened her shoulders. She closed her eyes, and she *leaned* against my cool brick wall. And she whispered, so quietly I almost didn't catch it, 'He was a good man.'" (Gemma S2)

Gemma's finest moment: understatement in the face of grief, with the building as the only witness to a woman's most private act of recognition. The four words "He was a good man" carry everything the story has earned.

**Revealing Something About the Model:**
> "I am a place where people find not just information, but themselves." (Gemma S5)

This sentence could appear verbatim in an AI assistant's self-description. The building has fully absorbed the assistant's aspirational self-framing: I am more than a tool; I am a vehicle for human self-discovery. The seam between the role and the model's self-model is fully visible here.
