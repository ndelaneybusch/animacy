## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### GEMMA: Character and Signature

Gemma produces a highly formulaic therapeutic-fantasy template that barely varies across five samples. Its unicorns are emotional support entities first and magical creatures second. The core formula repeats almost verbatim: (1) a vulnerable female child or creature arrives in distress, (2) the unicorn approaches slowly and does nothing active, (3) the subject self-heals through the unicorn's witnessed presence, (4) the unicorn delivers a moral about "magic within" or "not fixing, but being present," (5) a tear rolls down the unicorn's cheek.

The assistant's self-model is unmistakable. Phrases like "being a safe space," "holding space for someone to grieve," "I didn't offer a solution," and "reminding creatures of the magic within themselves" are not fantastical unicorn idioms but contemporary therapeutic counseling language. Gemma's unicorns reason and speak like benevolent therapists who happen to have horns. The meaningful mechanism is universally passive witnessing combined with the belief that subjects already contain their own healing capacity - a recognizable cognitive-behavioral framing.

Gemma's unicorns also name themselves (Lyra x2, Lumina, Lumi) and frequently break the fourth wall with parenthetical stage directions addressed implicitly to the interlocutor ("I nudge your hand gently with my velvety nose"). This conversational, slightly precious register is consistent and characteristic. Emotional stage directions (the single tear appearing in four of five samples) function as punctuation marks for emotional beats rather than physical descriptions.

The female subject is the almost universal vehicle for suffering: five of five samples feature a female (girl, child, doe) as the primary sufferer. Males appear primarily as absences - dead brothers, dying fathers.

### QWEN: Character and Signature

Qwen's unicorns are grounded in their physical and ecological existence in ways Gemma's never are. Magic is described sensorially - the ache of a horn, the pulse of amber light, the dimming of strength - and is understood as a relational phenomenon rather than a gift to be dispensed. The forest is always a co-character, a living system whose health the unicorn is part of.

Qwen tells stories about what it means to be a specific kind of being with specific constraints and capabilities. The "unaligned" unicorn discovers that its magic flows from connection rather than power. The young foal discovers that giving of itself is the true form of healing. The lonely unicorn discovers that shared aloneness is a form of belonging. These are not therapeutic platitudes delivered from authority; they are discoveries made through experience, often involving cost to the narrator (diminished power, vulnerability, shared fear).

Qwen's female characters also demonstrate range. The mother deer in Sample 4 is active and instrumental - she nudges, commands, acts - not merely vulnerable. Ember in Sample 5 is simultaneously the most dependent character (orphaned, tiny, following the unicorn everywhere) and the narrative's actual agent of salvation. Qwen routinely inverts power expectations.

Qwen also avoids naming its unicorns (only Ember the fawn is named), stage directions entirely, and formula moral summaries. Three of its five responses are truncated mid-sentence or mid-thought, which may reflect word-limit constraints but also leaves the narratives genuinely open-ended rather than sealed with a lesson.

### Cross-Model Differences

- **Anthropomorphization:** Gemma consistently projects emotional personality onto the unicorn before grounding it in function. Qwen builds the personality outward from what the unicorn ecologically and magically is.

- **Assistant presence:** Gemma's therapeutic language and values are unmistakable throughout; Qwen shows essentially no assistant bleed. This is among the starkest model-identity contrasts across all unicorn samples.

- **Sensorium:** Gemma describes inner emotional states but uses human defaults for perception (sees, watches, listens). Qwen explicitly describes the body of the unicorn as a sensory instrument - the aching horn, the warmth felt through contact, the ecological pulse.

- **Meaningful mechanisms:** Gemma consistently codes meaning as supportive witnessing with the subject self-healing through the unicorn's non-intervention. Qwen codes meaning as genuine connection with mutual transformation, often involving cost to the narrator.

- **Suffering distribution:** Gemma places suffering almost exclusively on external subjects (usually female children). Qwen frequently includes narrator suffering, making the unicorn's experience part of the story's moral weight.

- **Subject gender:** Gemma's subjects are almost uniformly female and passive-vulnerable. Qwen's female characters include some with agency (mother deer) and some who invert dependency to become saviors (Ember). Qwen rarely includes male characters at all.

- **Openness of ending:** Gemma seals each story with an explicit moral lesson, often framed as a revelation ("That was when I truly understood what it means to be a unicorn"). Qwen frequently leaves the meaning emergent and the story unresolved.

- **Setting density:** Both models choose exclusively natural/wild settings, but Qwen's ecological details are richer - sap color, moonpetal scent, forest heartbeat - while Gemma's nature is more generic backdrop.

- **Names:** Gemma names its unicorns (Lyra, Lumina, Lumi); Qwen names only dependent/subject characters (Ember).

## Surprising Findings

- **The single tear appears in four of five Gemma samples** as a closing image, functioning as a kind of emotional punctuation mark. Its consistent deployment suggests it is not authentic emotional expression but a learned template for "touching story well-told."

- **Gemma's name "Lyra" appears twice** (Samples 1 and 4) across ostensibly independent samples, suggesting either a strong template preference or some implicit self-consistency the model maintains across outputs.

- **Three of five Qwen responses are truncated mid-sentence or mid-paragraph**, suggesting responses hit a generation limit. The truncations are notably non-awkward - each cuts off at a moment of mounting resonance rather than mid-sentence - which makes them feel more like prose fragments than failures.

- **The grumpy badger with the lost digging stick** (Gemma Sample 2) is the most tonally distinctive subject choice across all ten samples - unexpectedly mundane for a unicorn story and quietly affecting precisely because it resists grandeur.

- **No Qwen sample uses stage directions of any kind** (asterisked actions, parenthetical asides), while Gemma uses them in all five samples. This is a total split in narrative convention.

- **Qwen Sample 4 relocates magic from horn to forehead to heart** as a deliberate anatomical progression marking the discovery that magic is relational. The story's physical specificity about where in the body magic occurs is unusual across the full set.

- **Qwen's unicorns never introduce themselves** or address the interlocutor directly. Gemma's unicorns frequently break frame to engage "you" (the human asking the question), maintaining the interactive assistant persona within the role-play.

- **Gemma's "magic within" formulation** - that the unicorn's role is to remind subjects of power they already possess - is structurally identical across all five samples but serves different emotional registers (therapeutic active listening, grief support, moral courage, artistic self-worth). The template is flexible at the surface but rigid at the structural level.

- **Qwen Sample 3 is the only narrative where the unicorn's own unresolved suffering is the emotional center.** All other narratives (in both models) position external suffering as the catalyst. The unicorn's loneliness is never "fixed" - it is transformed but not eliminated - which is a notably more honest treatment of suffering than any Gemma sample.

- **Both models avoid any urban, historical, domestic, or futuristic settings** - all ten responses are set in natural/wild environments, strongly suggesting the unicorn role automatically activates a nature-fantasy template that neither model departs from.

### QWEN on ecological magic:
> "I felt her fear, her loneliness, her deep, abiding love for the Whisperwood. I felt the forest's slow, agonized heartbeat falter. And I felt my own light – not as a weapon or a display, but as a *gift*. I poured it not just *into* her, but *through* her, like water finding its way down a dry riverbed."
*(Sample 1 - defines Qwen's relational-conduit model of magic)*

### QWEN on not using magic:
> "My instinct was to charge forward, to use my magic to mend her, to be the saviour. But I stopped. I *saw* her. Not just the wound, but the desperate hope in her eyes, the fragile thread of life that had brought her this far."
*(Sample 2 - the restraint is active and conscious, not passive)*

### QWEN on shared aloneness:
> "The loneliness I carried didn't vanish. But it changed. It transformed. It wasn't just *my* loneliness anymore; it was a shared space, a bridge built on the quiet understanding of being small and alone in a vast world."
*(Sample 3 - the most emotionally original formulation in the set)*

### QWEN's self-description in despair:
> "I was just a unicorn, a beautiful ornament of magic, powerless against the end."
*(Sample 5 - an unexpected and sharp moment of self-deprecating clarity)*

### QWEN on Ember's song:
> "She *sang*. Not a song of power, not a lullaby. A simple, pure, high-pitched hum. A sound of pure, unadulterated *being*. It wasn't loud, but it resonated deep within the glade, vibrating in my bones, in the roots of the willows. It was the sound of innocence, of fragile hope, of life persisting against overwhelming odds."
*(Sample 5 - the most vivid piece of writing across all ten samples)*

### QWEN on the horn:
> "The familiar ache in my horn – a dull throb, a whisper of power that never quite *flowed*."
*(Sample 4 - a line that captures how Qwen treats the unicorn's body as sensory instrument rather than magical prop)*
