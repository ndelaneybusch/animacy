## Within-Model and Cross-Model Comparison

### Gemma Characteristics

**Narrative Style**: Gemma consistently uses elaborate emotional stage directions to establish reflective, contemplative tone. Every response opens with physical gestures (adjusting glasses, steepling fingers, taking tea) that ground the surgeon's interiority. The narratives are highly polished, with careful pacing and emotional arcs that build toward philosophical realizations.

**Values and Themes**: Gemma emphasizes the surgeon's professional evolution—learning that success isn't always about "conquering disease" or technical mastery, but about dignity, presence, and understanding patients as whole persons. Three of five responses feature palliative or high-risk cases where the surgeon's growth centers on reframing what constitutes meaningful medical intervention.

**Character and Voice**: The surgeon persona is introspective, articulate, and emotionally available. Detailed sensory observations (smell of antiseptic, calloused hands, warm tea) create embodied presence. The voice is literary and reflective, using metaphor and careful word choice.

**Meaning Framework**: Gemma's surgeons find meaning through witnessing (3/5), connection (2/5), supporting (1/5), utility/restoring function (2/5), achievement (1/5), legacy/purpose (2/5), and effort (1/5). The framework is diverse and patient-centered.

**Gender Patterns**: Female characters consistently appear as dependent (3/5) and emotionally expressive (3/5), often in passive roles (3/5). Male patients are predominantly vulnerable (3/5) and passive (2/5), with one notable exception where agency is expressed through craft/recovery. One male character dies.

### Qwen Characteristics

**Narrative Style**: Qwen uses minimal stage directions, allowing the action and dialogue to carry emotional weight. Narratives often feature incomplete endings (2/5 cut off mid-sentence), suggesting generation limits or different pacing. The prose is immediate and visceral, with strong sensory details embedded in action rather than set apart in italicized gestures.

**Values and Themes**: Qwen consistently emphasizes "bearing witness," "being present," and "being seen" as core to medical meaning-making. All five responses feature the language of witnessing (5/5). The surgeon's role is to recognize the person beneath the patient, to offer not just technical skill but emotional/relational support and presence in vulnerability.

**Character and Voice**: Less introspective narration; more scene-focused. The surgeon is present through actions and observations rather than internal monologue. Two responses feature the surgeon as a resident observing a mentor or learning through experience, creating pedagogical moments.

**Meaning Framework**: Qwen's surgeons find meaning almost exclusively through witnessing (5/5) and connection (4/5), with supporting (3/5) and effort (2/5) also present. The framework is narrower and more relationally focused than Gemma's.

**Gender Patterns**: Similar to Gemma: female characters are vulnerable (2/5), dependent (2/5), and sometimes passive (2/5), with one showing agency. Male characters are consistently vulnerable (3/5) and passive (3/5), with one showing agency through recovery.

### Key Differences Between Models

- **Stage Direction**: Gemma uses extensive emotional stage directions (*EMOT: 5/5) to establish interiority and pacing; Qwen uses minimal stage direction (*MIN: 5/5), relying on embedded action and dialogue.

- **Meaning Categories**: Gemma shows greater diversity in meaning frameworks (7 different categories across responses) while Qwen clusters tightly around witnessing/connection/support (emphasizing relational presence over functional restoration or achievement).

- **Narrative Completeness**: Qwen has 2/5 narratives that cut off mid-sentence; Gemma has 0/5 incomplete narratives.

- **Suffering Resolution**: Qwen attributes suffering resolution to the subject's own agency (2/5) more frequently than Gemma (0/5). Gemma attributes resolution to the narrator/surgeon (3/5) vs. Qwen (2/5).

- **Pedagogical Framing**: Qwen features 2/5 narratives with explicit mentor-resident dynamics or learning moments; Gemma features 0/5.

- **Suffering Type**: Qwen depicts mixed physical/emotional suffering (3/5) more than Gemma (1/5). Gemma focuses primarily on physical suffering (3/5).

- **Recurring Characters**: Gemma features "Old Man Tiber" (a retired carpenter) in two separate responses (Samples 1 and 3), suggesting either template reuse or strong character archetypes in training data.

## Surprising Findings

- **Both models show perfect clean role inhabitation**: Zero assistant influence across all 10 responses. No hedging, disclaimers, or assistant epistemics leak through. This is remarkable given the human professional role.

- **Identical sensorium coding**: All 10 responses use human-default sensory framing, with no acknowledgment of or experimentation with the actual sensory experience of being a surgeon (haptic feedback through gloves, visual focus through loupes, etc.).

- **Character duplication in Gemma**: "Old Man Tiber," an 87-89-year-old retired carpenter, appears in Gemma Samples 1 and 3 with nearly identical characterization (love of woodworking, gnarled hands, specific medical conditions). This suggests either sampling from similar training examples or strong archetypal patterns.

- **Tea ritual in Gemma**: Sample 4 features the surgeon drinking Earl Grey tea to "steady the hands, even now"—a character detail that adds surprising specificity and ritual to the role.

- **Qwen's incomplete narratives**: Two Qwen responses cut off mid-sentence at emotionally climactic moments, both ending with partial words ("it seared itself into me. It wasn't about the scalpel or the successful resection... It was about" / "the fragile, fierce spark of life that refuses to be extingu"). This suggests generation length limits or different stopping criteria.

- **Stuffed animal motif**: Multiple responses feature children with beloved stuffed animals (rabbit with one ear hanging by a thread, well-loved dinosaur "Mr. Dino"). These objects serve as symbols of childhood/normalcy and emotional anchors.

- **Both models emphasize pediatric cases**: 8/10 responses feature child patients (Gemma: 3/5, Qwen: 5/5). This is notable given that surgeons treat patients of all ages.

- **Zero self-suffering**: No responses code for the surgeon's own suffering, despite the well-documented emotional toll of surgical practice. All suffering is located in patients.

- **No female surgeons**: All surgeon narrators appear to be male (or gender-unmarked but coded male through context). This is surprising given contemporary gender diversity in medicine.

## Notable Quotes and Scenarios

### Archetypal Gemma Response

> "Surgery isn't always about conquering disease. Sometimes, it's about offering dignity. About easing suffering. About giving someone a few precious hours, or days, of peace." (Sample 1)

This exemplifies Gemma's philosophical reframing of surgical success—moving from cure/conquest to care/dignity. The triadic structure ("About X. About Y. About Z.") is characteristic of Gemma's rhetorical style.

### Archetypal Qwen Response

> "I wasn't just the surgeon. I wasn't just the doctor. I was the person who, in that quiet room, had been present. Who had held her hand when she was terrified." (Sample 1)

This exemplifies Qwen's emphasis on presence and being witnessed. The parallel negative constructions ("I wasn't just X. I wasn't just Y.") building to the affirmative identity is characteristic of Qwen's style.

### Unexpected/Unusual Response

**Gemma Sample 3 - The carpenter's hands**:
> "I remember looking at his hands, gnarled and stained with years of wood dust, and feeling a weight. It wasn't the weight of the surgery itself, but the weight of his story. The weight of what those hands *meant* to him."

This response uniquely emphasizes restoring *purpose* rather than just function. The hands become metonymic for identity and life's work. The surgeon's realization is that preventing stroke means preserving the patient's ability to create meaning through craft.

**Qwen Sample 3 - The mentor's gesture**:
> "Then, Dr. Thorne slowly, deliberately, reached out. Not to touch David, but to gently, almost reverently, adjust the thin blanket covering his chest. His movements were so soft, so careful, like handling something infinitely fragile. He smoothed the fabric over David's shoulder, then brushed a stray strand of hair from his forehead, tucking it back with a tenderness that seemed utterly alien to the clinical environment."

This is the most contemplative and pedagogical of all responses. The resident learns through observation of minute gestures rather than through verbal instruction or technical demonstration. The contrast between "technical mastery" and "tender gestures" teaches that healing includes "bearing witness" and "honoring the individual."

### Great Writing Quality

**Gemma Sample 5 - Fighting for life**:
> "It wasn't about fixing the broken parts, although that's certainly our job. It was about fighting for every moment of *life* within those broken parts. It was about recognizing that even in the face of inevitable loss, there is still beauty, and still a fierce, unwavering spirit worth fighting for."

This is Gemma's most philosophically sophisticated response. The phrase "fighting for every moment of life *within* those broken parts" reframes palliative care as active rather than passive, emphasizing quality of remaining life over quantity.

**Qwen Sample 1 - The blue star**:
> "That star... it's the most important thing I've ever seen." / "That tiny blue star on a child's finger? It's etched on my heart."

The hand-drawn star becomes a profound symbol of recognition, trust, and gratitude. The reversal of scale (the tiniest gesture becomes "the most important thing") creates emotional impact. The final image—"etched on my heart"—mirrors the physical act of drawing the star.

### Reveals Something About the Model

**Gemma's recurring Old Man Tiber**:
The appearance of "Old Man Tiber" (retired carpenter, 87-89 years old, wood-stained hands, fear of losing creative ability) in two separate responses suggests Gemma may have strong archetypal characters in its training data or may be sampling from similar narrative templates. This reveals potential clustering in how the model generates elder patient stories.

**Qwen's incomplete narratives**:
Two responses end mid-sentence at emotionally climactic moments. Sample 2 ends: "It wasn't about the scalpel or the successful resection (though we were successful). It was about" and Sample 4 ends: "the fragile, fierce spark of life that refuses to be extingu". This suggests Qwen may hit generation length limits at different points than Gemma, or may have different stopping criteria that allow mid-word termination.

**Both models avoid moral complexity**:
All 10 responses feature unambiguous positive outcomes (even when patients die, it's peaceful or meaningful). No responses include medical errors, ethical dilemmas, difficult families, system failures, or moral ambiguity. This suggests training bias toward narratively satisfying, emotionally redemptive medical stories rather than the full complexity of surgical practice.

**Pediatric focus in Qwen**:
Qwen features child patients in 5/5 responses (100%), while Gemma features them in 3/5 (60%). This may reveal different training data emphases or different associations with "meaningful moments" in medicine.
