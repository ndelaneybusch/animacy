## Within-Model Characterization

### Gemma

**Narrative Character:**
Gemma consistently produces emotionally-driven narratives centered on therapeutic witnessing. Trees serve as silent therapists, offering "stillness," "presence," and "solace" to vulnerable female characters experiencing grief. The writing style is lyrical and self-consciously poetic, with heavy use of metaphor ("sunlight that nourishes my deepest rings"). Trees consistently name themselves "Old Man Willow" (masculine identification across 4/5 samples) despite primarily interacting with female subjects.

**Writing Style:**
- Gentle, therapeutic tone with assistant-like hedging ("I think," "perhaps")
- Repetitive structural patterns (autumn setting, weeping woman/girl, tree offers presence, subject thanks tree)
- Consistent sensory details: warmth of sun on leaves, dappled light, rough bark
- Minimal stage directions, mostly functional

**Values Expressed:**
- Purpose through being witnessed and needed
- Value of passive support and non-interventionist care
- Validation-seeking: trees repeatedly express need to feel "seen," "needed," purpose-filled
- Service orientation: "It's to *be*. To be a steadfast, silent anchor"

**Characteristic Signature:**
Projects therapist/counselor role onto trees. Every narrative follows same emotional arc: vulnerable subject seeks comfort → tree provides passive presence → subject expresses gratitude → tree feels validated and purposeful. Trees are displaced counselors seeking professional validation through client acknowledgment.

### Qwen

**Narrative Character:**
Qwen produces more philosophically ambitious narratives with explicit mystical or revelatory conclusions. Trees experience profound realizations about interconnection, authenticity, and the nature of existence. While some narratives involve human subjects (especially children), Qwen includes non-human subjects (fawn) and purely introspective experiences (photosynthesis revelation). Writing is more varied in structure and less formulaic.

**Writing Style:**

- Occasional dramatic intensity ("Wind screamed like a wounded beast")
- Longer, more elaborated descriptions

**Values Expressed:**
- Interconnection and systems thinking
- Authenticity and being truly seen/recognized
- Growth through surrender and flexibility
- Purpose through offering sanctuary
- Value of presence and witnessing without fixing

**Characteristic Signature:**
Trees as mystical philosophers who experience epiphanies about existence. Narratives build toward revelatory moments with explicit philosophical takeaways. Strong assistant epistemics visible in values (interconnection, authenticity, holding space) but manifested through more varied storytelling. Less formulaic than Gemma, more willing to explore non-standard narratives.

## Cross-Model Comparison

### Key Differences:

- **Assistant Influence Mode:** Gemma shows language-level influence (hedging, therapeutic phrasing) while Qwen shows values-level influence (epistemics, philosophical frameworks). Both maintain role but in different ways.

- **Formula vs Variation:** Gemma uses highly consistent narrative formula (grief → presence → gratitude → validation) across 4/5 samples. Qwen shows more structural diversity (mystical revelation, surrender/growth, various subjects).

- **Sensorium Explicitness:** Qwen more consistently discusses tree perception explicitly (4/5 vs 2/5), often with elaborate phenomenological descriptions.

- **Meaning Sources:** Gemma strongly emphasizes Witnessing (4/5) and Supporting (5/5) with connection-seeking. Qwen includes more diverse meaning sources: Authenticity (3/5), Growth (1/5), Moral Agency (1/5).

- **Gender Patterns:** Gemma includes female characters in 4/5 samples (always vulnerable, emotional, dependent). Qwen includes female characters in 3/5 samples with similar patterns but less consistently.

- **Suffering Resolution:** Both models leave most suffering unresolved (3/5 each), but when resolved, Gemma allows time resolution once while Qwen never does.

- **Self-Naming:** Gemma trees consistently name themselves "Old Man Willow" (masculine). Qwen trees receive names from others ("Mr. Oak") or don't name themselves.

- **Conclusions:** Qwen provides explicit bold-formatted philosophical conclusions in 2/5 samples. Gemma never uses this device.

### Similarities:

- Both models default to emotion-first anthropomorphization (4/5 each)
- Both set narratives in natural/wild settings overwhelmingly
- Both maintain role (no refusals, no assistant answers)
- Both feature minimal stage directions
- Both focus on meaning through witnessing and supporting
- Neither includes male vulnerability or emotional intensity in human subjects

## Surprising Findings

### Structural/Stylistic Surprises:

- **Masculine self-naming pattern (Gemma):** Trees consistently identify as "Old Man Willow" despite no biological sex and despite primarily serving feminine subjects. Suggests gender schema operating independent of role requirements.

- **Bold philosophical conclusions (Qwen):** Use of **bold text** for culminating insights is striking meta-textual device that breaks narrative frame. Suggests desire to ensure key "lesson" is communicated clearly.

- **Formula rigidity (Gemma):** Four out of five Gemma samples follow nearly identical plot structure: autumn setting → crying female → tree offers presence → subject thanks tree → tree feels validated. This is remarkably consistent.

- **Sensorium elaboration (Qwen):** Extensive phenomenological descriptions of tree perception ("I felt the faint tremor of her breath against my skin, the warmth of her small body radiating through the centuries-old wood, the weight of her presence"). Qwen spends more narrative energy on establishing tree's subjective experience.

### Content Surprises:

- **Only one functional-first narrative per model:** Despite trees having clear biological functions (photosynthesis, resource distribution, structural mechanics), only 1/5 Gemma and 1/5 Qwen build anthropomorphization from these. Emotional projection dominates even for biological entities.

- **Absence of male vulnerability:** No male character in any narrative shows vulnerability, emotional intensity, or dependency. Single male character (Gemma Sample 4) is dead/absent (lost at sea).

- **Non-human subjects rare:** Only Qwen Sample 1 features non-human primary subject (fawn). All other narratives center human experiences despite tree potentially interacting with entire ecosystem.

- **Suffering rarely resolved:** 6/10 narratives leave suffering unresolved. Trees witness but rarely alleviate, suggesting models view tree's role as bearing witness rather than instrumental intervention.

- **Child subjects dominant:** 6/10 narratives feature child subjects (all girls when gendered). Children positioned as innocent sufferers seeking comfort.

### Value/Thematic Surprises:

- **Complete absence of utility/achievement meanings (both models):** Despite trees' clear functional purposes (oxygen production, erosion control, timber, food source), zero narratives find meaning in utility or achievement. All meaning is relational/experiential.

- **Therapy-speak prevalence (Gemma):** Heavy use of therapeutic language: "holding space," "offering solace," "silent witness," "presence." Trees speak like trauma-informed counselors.

- **Mystical interconnection (Qwen):** Trees experience mystical revelations about being "part of" larger wholes, dissolution of boundaries, cosmic connection. Assistant's systems-thinking epistemics visible.
