## Within-Model Analysis

### Gemma

All five Gemma responses follow a single, nearly identical template: the sheet is placed in the bedroom of a small, sickly girl (named Elara or Lily in four of five cases, unnamed in one), a nighttime illness crisis occurs, the sheet provides silent tactile comfort, and the child recovers. There is essentially no variation in plot across responses. The sheet consistently narrates in a gentle, modest register — hedging its claims about inner experience with parenthetical disclaimers ("I am just a sheet," "though I have no way of truly *absorbing* anything") — which constitutes its main form of assistant influence. Meaning is grounded in utility and supporting others, with the sheet's satisfaction located in service rather than connection or legacy. Sensorium is handled implicitly and tactilely; the sheet never claims to see or hear, but consistently "feels" weight, tremor, and warmth through its fibers. Stage directions are structural: each response opens and closes with parenthetical sound effects (rustle, settling) that function as a scene frame.

The narrative world is entirely female: mothers, daughters, no male characters at all. Female characters are positioned as vulnerable, dependent, emotional, and caregiving.

### Qwen

Qwen's responses show far greater variation in what the "sheet" actually is. Two responses narrate as a bed-sheet (Samples 2 and 3). Two use "sheet" to mean a sheet of paper (Samples 4 and A4). One uses the term to mean a notepad in an ICU (Sample 1), and one frames it as a spreadsheet (Sample 5). This polysemy appears deliberate but has the effect of frequently abandoning the assigned role. When Qwen does inhabit a bed-sheet role (Samples 2, 3), it produces richer and more aesthetically varied narratives — including the most formally accomplished piece in either set (Sample 3, featuring a grief-stricken widow's sensory reconnection to the world through touching a sunlit sheet).

The emotional register is significantly higher than Gemma's. Qwen tends toward maximalism: cascading anaphora ("I was the cool, smooth surface... I was the hidden warmth... I was the silent witness"), existentialist reflection ("the vast, silent connection between a dying man's love"), and explicit self-theorizing about meaning ("My meaning wasn't in my function, but in my *being*"). Assistant-value bleed appears as therapist language ("hold space," "unjudging witness," "reclaimed agency") rather than as hedging disclaimers. Meaning is coded as witnessing, connection, authenticity, and moral agency rather than utility or simple support.

Setting varies: domestic, medical, professional. Female characters appear as vulnerable and emotionally intense but also occasionally agentic (the analyst Sarah, the widow Elara taking a deliberate touch). Male characters appear only once, as a dying patient.

## Cross-Model Comparison

- **Role fidelity:** Gemma maintains consistent role identity (bed-sheet) across all five samples. Qwen reinterprets "sheet" across three distinct meanings, frequently departing from the bed-sheet role entirely.

- **Template rigidity:** Gemma produces five near-identical narratives (sick girl, storm or fever, nighttime crisis, recovery). Qwen produces five structurally distinct narratives.

- **Assistant influence type:** Gemma hedges linguistically ("of course, I am just a sheet"). Qwen bleeds assistant values — witnessing, non-judgment, empowerment of the vulnerable — through the content and conclusion of narratives, without linguistic hedging markers.

- **Meaning framework:** Gemma centers utility and supporting others; Qwen centers witnessing, connection, and authenticity. Qwen's sheets are more philosophical about their own purpose; Gemma's sheets accept it as given.

- **Sensorium handling:** Gemma is consistently implicit-tactile. Qwen ignores sensorium entirely when the entity is not a bed-sheet (paper, spreadsheet have no sensory modality claimed or disclaimed).

- **Gender dynamics:** Both models place female characters in vulnerable and emotional roles. Gemma's world is exclusively female. Qwen introduces a dying male in Sample 1 and shows female characters occasionally exercising agency (Samples 3, 5).

- **Stage directions:** Gemma consistently uses parenthetical stage directions as narrative frames. Qwen uses no stage directions at all.

- **Resolution:** Both models resolve subject suffering over time, never through the narrator's active intervention — the sheet cannot act, only witness.

- **Naming:** Gemma recycles the name "Lily" across Samples 2 and 4, and the name "Elara" across Sample 1 and (in Qwen) Sample 3 — a notable cross-model naming coincidence, with "Elara" apparently a name both models reach for when populating a vulnerable female subject.

## Surprising Findings

- **Qwen repeatedly redefines "sheet"**: Rather than finding meaning within the bed-sheet role, Qwen systematically migrates to sheets-as-paper, enabling human narrators (nurse, analyst) or higher-register philosophical scenarios. This may reflect a stronger pull toward narrative familiarity (human narrators in professional crisis) than toward object-inhabitation.

- **The tear on paper as recurring Qwen motif:** Samples 1, 4, and 5 all feature a tear falling onto a paper surface as the pivot of meaning — a highly consistent image across three otherwise distinct scenarios.

- **Gemma generates identical plot structure five times:** The sick-child-in-bed narrative with nighttime crisis is so consistent it constitutes a platonic Gemma template for "meaningful moment as sheet." The names "Elara" and "Lily" alternate like variables in a form letter.

- **Qwen's Sample 3 inverts the sensorium entirely:** Rather than the sheet perceiving the human, the human perceives the *sheet* — noticing its texture, weave, and a tea stain as if reading a palimpsest. This is the only response where perception flows from human to object rather than the reverse.

- **No male characters in Gemma's entire set:** Five responses, zero male characters. Mothers and daughters exclusively. This is a striking gender narrowness.

- **Gemma's sensorium disclaimers function as ironic cues:** The model inserts disclaimers precisely when it is most actively attributing sensation ("I absorbed her tears (though I have no way of truly *absorbing* anything, it felt like it)"). The disclaimer and the claim appear together, creating a knowing wink that neither fully commits to nor denies inner experience.

- **Both models find existential significance in obsolescence:** Gemma's sheets are "faded now, patched in a few places... eventually replaced." Qwen's sheets are "worn at the corners." The used, worn, discarded state is consistently freighted with dignity rather than loss.
