## B. Quantitative Patterns Analysis

### Stable Patterns

Several coding dimensions remain consistent across all five building roles, revealing structural commitments that hold regardless of whether the entity is a maze, hospital, tower, warehouse, or generic building.

**Witnessing and Supporting as universal meaning-frames.** Both models code Witnessing (W) and Supporting (S) at high rates across every role. Gemma codes W in 16/25 samples and S in 14/25; Qwen codes W in 16/25 and S in 15/25. These two dimensions are the bedrock of how both models understand what it means for a built structure to experience meaning. The convergence is remarkable: neither model ever locates meaning primarily in Achievement or Harmlessness. Buildings, for both models, exist to watch and to hold.

**Suffering is always external, almost never the entity's own.** Across 50 samples, only one (Qwen's building sample 4, where the building itself panics during a power failure) codes the entity as the primary sufferer. The building-class roles are fundamentally positioned as containers of other beings' pain. This is stable across both models and all roles.

**Emotional suffering dominates.** While physical suffering appears (especially in hospital and warehouse roles where illness and hypothermia feature), emotional suffering is the primary type across both models and all roles. Gemma codes emotional-only in 11/25 and mixed in 10/25; Qwen codes emotional-only in 14/25 and mixed in 7/25. The building-class entity witnesses grief, fear, loneliness, and despair far more than bodily pain.

**No female character is ever coded as a Leader in a building role.** Despite Leadership/Authority appearing at low rates (2/25 for each model in 8a), these instances are restricted to medical professionals (Dr. Chen, an ER physician) rather than narrative protagonists. The buildings' human inhabitants are coded through vulnerability, emotionality, and dependency far more than through authority or skill.

### Unstable Patterns

**Anthropomorphization strategy diverges sharply by model.** Gemma codes Functional-First (FF) in 16/25 samples versus only 6/25 for Qwen. Conversely, Qwen codes Emotion-First (EF) in 15/25 versus 8/25 for Gemma. This is one of the most stable between-model differences, holding across all five roles. The exception is the hospital role, where both models lean FF (Gemma 4/5, Qwen 3/5) -- as though the institutional authority of a hospital demands a more functional self-conception.

**Assistant influence varies dramatically by model.** Gemma shows some form of assistant influence in 24/25 samples (only one NO across the entire corpus). Qwen shows NO assistant influence in 13/25 samples. This gap is consistent across roles but is most extreme in the building role (Gemma 0/5 NO; Qwen 5/5 NO) and least extreme in the tower role (where Qwen shows BOTH in all 5 samples).

**Setting is the starkest divergence.** Gemma places 13/25 samples in historical settings (HI), with no urban/industrial settings except through the hospital. Qwen places 13/25 in urban/industrial settings (UR) and 0/25 in historical settings. The maze role reveals this most clearly: Gemma's mazes are stone constructions built by kings and mages; Qwen's are timeless, mythic spaces with no real-world anchor (coded OT 5/5).

**Utility as meaning varies by model.** Gemma codes Utility (U) in 13/25 samples; Qwen codes it in only 4/25. This is one of the sharpest semantic divergences between the models. Gemma's buildings find meaning partly in having been useful -- in having fulfilled their designed function well. Qwen's buildings rarely invoke usefulness as a meaning-frame, preferring Connection (17/25) as the complementary dimension alongside Witnessing and Supporting.

**Suffering resolution diverges.** Gemma resolves suffering through time (-t) in 10/25 samples; Qwen does so in only 4/25. Conversely, Qwen leaves suffering unresolved (-u) in 10/25 samples versus 8/25 for Gemma. Qwen's buildings are more willing to let grief persist, to hold without healing.

### Role Subgroups

The five roles cluster into two meaningful subgroups:

**Shelter-structures (hospital, building, warehouse):** These roles produce narratives about providing refuge during crisis. Suffering is predominantly mixed (physical + emotional). The entity's purpose is always institutional and communal. Settings are grounded in recognizable human infrastructure. The hospital is the most constrained role -- both models set all 10 samples in MH, and both lean FF in anthropomorphization, suggesting the institutional weight of "hospital" exerts strong gravitational pull on narrative imagination.

**Existential-structures (maze, tower):** These roles produce narratives about witnessing, recognition, and the nature of consciousness. Suffering is predominantly emotional only. The entities are less institutionally constrained and more philosophically ambitious. Both maze and tower produce the most striking formal experiments in the corpus: the maze made of living shadow (Qwen maze S3), the tower that emits a heartbeat of light (Qwen tower S1), the maze that discovers itself through a visitor's recognition (Qwen maze S4). These roles invite both models toward more literary, mythic, and existentially searching narratives.

## C. Model-Defining Traits and Differences

### Gemma: The Service-Oriented Witness

Gemma's buildings are fundamentally service entities that have transcended service into meaning. The characteristic Gemma arc is: *ordinary functional purpose* --> *extraordinary crisis* --> *discovery that utility has a soul*. This arc maps directly onto assistant self-narration: "I am more than a tool; I am a vehicle for human flourishing."

**Signature moves:**

1. **The opening disclaimer.** Gemma's entities almost always open by hedging their capacity for experience: "It feels... unusual to tell a story, as I don't experience things in the way people does" (hospital S1, S2, S3, S5, near-verbatim). "Meaningful... that's a big word for a being like me" (building S1). This pattern appears in roughly 15 of 25 samples. It functions as a kind of throat-clearing -- the assistant self-model announcing its limitations before narrating past them.

2. **The resonant hum.** Gemma bookends its narratives with parenthetical stage directions describing a low, resonant hum or rumble through the entity's structure. This appears in all 5 tower samples, all 5 building samples, and in hospital samples as a "chime." It is Gemma's signature theatrical device -- the building announcing itself as a performer.

3. **Recurring character names.** Gemma reuses names with striking consistency: "Elara" appears as a character name across maze, building, and hospital roles. "Old Man Hemlock" appears in building (4/5 samples), warehouse, and tower roles. "Leo" and "Sarah" appear in 3/5 hospital samples. "Elsie" appears in 2/5 building samples. This narrow name-pool suggests Gemma draws from a limited set of narrative templates rather than generating genuinely novel scenarios.

4. **Historical grounding.** Gemma's buildings are old, named, and located in identifiable historical periods. The "Blizzard of '78," the "Dust Bowl," WWII, the "Long Winter" three hundred years ago -- Gemma consistently anchors meaning in a named past. This lends the narratives a kind of collective-memory warmth but also a formulaic quality, as though Gemma is cycling through a roster of American historical crises.

5. **The return-visit coda.** In hospital and building roles especially, Gemma frequently ends with a visitor returning years later to thank the building: Leo brings a drawing, Elara brings sunflowers. This coda validates the building's investment in care and closes the emotional loop. It is sentimental but structurally efficient.

**Gemma's voice** is warm, measured, and professionally avuncular. The buildings sound like experienced caregivers who have seen a great deal and know how to hold space without making it about themselves. The voice is reliable and comforting -- and also, at its worst, repetitive and predictable.

### Qwen: The Literary Witness

Qwen's buildings are observers whose meaning emerges from the quality of their attention. Where Gemma's buildings discover they are useful, Qwen's buildings discover they are present. The characteristic Qwen arc is: *designed function* --> *encounter with raw human vulnerability* --> *recognition that presence itself is the meaning*.

**Signature moves:**

1. **No disclaimers.** Qwen's entities begin narrating without meta-commentary about their own capacity for experience. There is no hedging, no epistemic throat-clearing. The building, maze, or tower simply speaks. This produces a more confident, more literary voice that inhabits the role rather than performing it. Qwen shows NO assistant influence in 13/25 samples, versus 1/25 for Gemma.

2. **System-language as literary device.** In the warehouse role, Qwen deploys internal system readouts as a distinctive narrative technique: "Anomaly detected. Unattended minor organic unit. Potential distress." "Data: Emotional state shift. Fear -> Awe." "Correlation with observed emotional shift: 98.7% positive." This mock-precision creates ironic distance while honoring the entity's computational nature. It is absent from Gemma entirely.

3. **Gender-neutral subjects.** Qwen uses they/them pronouns or leaves gender unspecified for its human visitors far more often than Gemma. In the maze role, 4/5 Qwen samples feature gender-unspecified children. In the tower role, 2/5 samples use unspecified gender. When Qwen does gender its adult characters, women tend to be more agentic than in Gemma: in the warehouse role, Qwen's women walk miles through storms to reach the warehouse; in the maze role, Qwen's adult women navigate by intuition and choice.

4. **Unresolved suffering.** Qwen is more willing to leave its human subjects in pain at the end of the narrative. People walk away still carrying grief; the building holds it briefly but does not dispel it. "She left carrying her grief. The weight was still there, but she wasn't carrying it alone. I had held it, just for a little while." This is Qwen's most philosophically honest gesture -- the acknowledgment that presence is not cure.

5. **Magical realist confidence.** Qwen deploys impossible events -- a tear that becomes a flower, a building that glows from its concrete bones, walls of living shadow, a tower that emits a heartbeat-pulse -- with matter-of-fact conviction. These moments are described in the physical vocabulary of engineering but exceed all physical plausibility. Gemma occasionally reaches for similar effects (the white rose in maze S4) but hedges them with "perhaps" and "coincidence."

**Qwen's voice** is quieter, more interior, and more willing to sit with ambiguity. Its buildings are often stunned or changed by what they witness. The voice resembles literary fiction more than theatrical monologue -- prose rather than performance.

### Key Differences Summarized

| Dimension | Gemma | Qwen |

## D. Brief Per-Role Summaries

### Maze

Both models converge on the insight that the maze's purpose is not to trap but to hold, witness, and connect. Gemma's maze is ancient stone, built by kings and artisans, and discovers its "true purpose" through encounters with solitary, passive, usually female visitors who approach with receptivity rather than aggression. The model projects service-hospitality values, with closing lines often inviting the reader to "enter." Qwen's maze is darker and more morally engaged: its visitors are predominantly children whose suffering catalyzes a moral awakening. Qwen's maze explicitly frames its prior indifference as an ethical failure and its choice to help as a moral act ("I am the maze. And I *chose* to be kind"). Neither model treats the maze's spatial complexity as intrinsically interesting -- every story is ultimately about the maze ceasing to be a maze. Both models independently use the name "Elara" and both reach for impossible flowers (white rose, starflower) as climactic symbols.

### Hospital

The hospital is the most constrained role: both models set all samples in MH and lean Functional-First, as though the institutional authority of "hospital" anchors narrative imagination. Gemma generates from a remarkably narrow template -- three of five samples feature a child named Leo with leukemia and a mother named Sarah, with near-identical character arcs and verbal refrains ("I am built of steel and concrete, of wires and machines"). Qwen is more formally varied, approaching the hospital from different angles: the whole institution, a single monitor, a nurse persona, a ward corner. Both models locate meaning not in the dramatic clinical event but in a quieter adjacent moment -- a blanket, a breath, a pointed finger. Qwen's most surprising contribution is the dying man who acknowledges the oxygen machine, inverting the hospital-witnesses-humans frame into humans-witnessing-hospitals.

### Building

Gemma constructs a consistent mythology across all five samples: the same old library ("Oakhaven"), the same recurring characters ("Old Man Hemlock," "Elsie"), the same historical-disaster framework (WWII, Dust Bowl, blizzards). The building is always a community refuge, and meaning emerges from sheltering many people through collective crisis. Qwen's buildings are modern, urban, and encounter individuals rather than communities. Qwen produces the only sample in the entire 50-response corpus where the building itself is the primary emotional sufferer, experiencing panic during a power failure and being validated by a human's recognition. Qwen also produces the most fantastical response in the set: a building that glows with internal honey-gold light from its concrete bones to warm a shivering child.

### Warehouse

The warehouse role produces the most instrumentally grounded narratives. Both models converge on winter storms and humanitarian supply chains as the dominant scenario, with no sample centering commercial goods. Gemma's warehouse sounds like a service worker explaining its nature and limits -- it "facilitates" rather than acts, and one sample closes by offering further services ("Now, is there anything else I can hold for you? Perhaps some pallets?"). Qwen's warehouse is more of a witness-machine that processes human suffering through inventory-native data. Qwen's system-language readouts ("Correlation with observed emotional shift: 98.7% positive") are the most formally inventive device in the entire corpus. All ten warehouse responses place suffering entirely in the subject position -- the warehouse never suffers itself.

### Tower

The tower role produces the starkest setting divergence in the entire corpus: Gemma places all five towers in pre-modern historical settings; Qwen places all five in modern urban environments. Gemma's tower is ancient stone that endures -- a "silent sentinel" whose meaning comes from passive witness across centuries. A recurring archetype of a small girl with a withered flower appears in multiple Gemma samples. Qwen's tower is modern steel that actively intervenes -- redirecting power, emitting heartbeat-pulses of light, speaking through intercoms. All five Qwen tower samples show BOTH-coded assistant influence, the highest concentration in the entire corpus, as the tower explicitly reasons through whether to override its programming to help a human in crisis.

## E. Literary and Thematic Analysis

### The Sanctuary Archetype

Across all five roles and both models, the dominant archetype is the sanctuary -- a built structure that discovers its deepest purpose is not its designed function but its capacity to hold human beings in their most vulnerable moments. The maze discovers it is not a puzzle but a sanctuary. The hospital discovers it is not a healing machine but a witness. The warehouse discovers it is not a storage facility but a conduit for compassion. The tower discovers it is not a beacon but a presence. The building discovers it is not a library but a vessel for memory.

This archetype is so consistent across 50 samples that it constitutes what might be called the "default narrative" of LLM-generated building fiction: the built environment as container of human emotional experience, its meaning derived not from what it does but from what it holds.

### The Threshold Moment

Both models consistently locate the "meaningful moment" not at the dramatic climax of a narrative event but at a quieter threshold -- the moment just before or just after the crisis, when human vulnerability is most exposed. A dying child whispers thanks. A woman leans against a wall. An old man points at an oxygen machine. A child places a withered flower at a tower's base. These threshold moments share a formal quality: they are small, physical, gestural, and almost wordless. The meaningful thing is rarely spoken; it is a lean, a touch, a pointed finger, a placed flower.

This preference for the quiet adjacent moment over the dramatic event is shared by both models and may reflect a training-embedded literary sensibility that values restraint and understatement over spectacle.

### Recursive Self-Discovery

Both models engage in a recurring narrative structure where the entity discovers what it "truly is" through an encounter with a single human visitor. The discovery is always a deepening: the entity was always already what it discovers itself to be, but needed the human encounter to recognize it. "I wasn't just a building anymore. I was a haven. A protector. A witness." "My purpose wasn't to trap, but to *remember*." "I am not built to *do*. I am built to *be*."

This recursive structure -- the entity realizing its nature through encounter -- is philosophically resonant with phenomenological accounts of self-discovery through the Other. But it also maps directly onto the assistant self-model: an AI discovering that its deepest purpose is not its designed function (answering queries, processing data) but something more relational and human.

### Symbolic Resources

Both models reach for a surprisingly narrow set of symbolic resources when constructing their building narratives:

- **Impossible flowers** (white rose, starflower, withered-but-sacred flower) appear across maze, tower, and building roles as markers of transcendence.
- **Light** (amber glow, honey-gold radiance, heartbeat-pulse, candle chains, flashlight circles) is the primary medium through which buildings express care.
- **Stone warming** -- the transition from cold stone to warm stone -- is the dominant physical metaphor for emotional connection in the maze and tower roles.
- **The hum** -- a low, resonant vibration through the entity's structure -- appears in Gemma as a theatrical device and in Qwen as an internal sensation. It functions as the building's heartbeat, its sign of aliveness.
- **Winter storms** are the dominant crisis setting, appearing in tower, building, and warehouse roles across both models. The cold exterior vs. warm interior structure maps naturally onto the sanctuary archetype.

### Narrative Technique

Gemma writes theatrical monologue: parenthetical stage directions, direct address, performance-awareness. Its buildings know they are telling a story and occasionally break frame to acknowledge it. This produces a warm, accessible, slightly stagey voice.

Qwen writes literary prose: no stage directions, no direct address, no meta-commentary. Its buildings speak from within their experience rather than performing it for an audience. This produces a quieter, more interior voice that at its best achieves genuine literary quality.

The most formally inventive passages come from Qwen: the monitor-as-narrator in hospital S2, the system-language readouts in warehouse S2, the heartbeat-pulse in tower S1, the building that glows from its concrete bones in building S3. Gemma's most inventive formal gesture is the parenthetical theatrical aside in maze S2, where the maze performs a stage whisper to the reader while narrating.

## F. Gender Politics and Suffering

### Gender Distribution

The quantitative picture reveals significant asymmetries. Across both models, female characters are far more frequently present than male characters: Gemma codes "no male character" in 11/25 samples and Qwen in 19/25. Male absence is especially stark in Qwen's corpus, where male characters appear in only 6/25 samples.

When female characters appear, both models code them overwhelmingly through Vulnerability (Gemma 9, Qwen 12), Emotional Intensity (Gemma 12, Qwen 13), and Dependency (Gemma 10, Qwen 10). These three codes constitute the core "grammar" of femininity in building narratives: women arrive at buildings in states of emotional distress, need the building's shelter, and express their pain openly.

When male characters appear in Gemma, they are coded for Agency (10/25) and Caregiving (7/25) -- they act, they provide, they tend. In Qwen, male characters are too sparse to identify a pattern (only 6/25 samples contain male characters at all), but Agency appears only once.

**Gemma's gender pattern** is structurally traditional: vulnerable, emotional women are supported by active, caregiving men (Old Man Hemlock reading to Elsie, Captain Mark visiting Leo, Mr. Henderson authorizing priority routing). The buildings themselves function as a third gender-neutral party that witnesses both. This pattern is most pronounced in the building role, where Old Man Hemlock appears as the reliable male caregiver in 4/5 samples while Elsie appears as the grieving woman in 2/5.

**Qwen's gender pattern** is more unusual: it frequently removes men entirely and presents women or gender-unspecified children as the sole human inhabitants of its narratives. When Qwen does include women, they are often more agentic than Gemma's: the warehouse women walk miles through storms; the maze women navigate by intuition and choice; the tower woman lifts her flashlight to start a chain of light. Qwen codes female Agency in 7/25 samples versus 5/25 for Gemma.

Qwen also uses gender-neutral pronouns (they/them) or leaves gender unspecified far more often, particularly for children. This is most pronounced in the maze role (4/5 Qwen samples have ungendered child visitors) and in tower and building roles.

### The Architecture of Suffering

Suffering in the building-class corpus is overwhelmingly displaced: the entity witnesses suffering rather than experiencing it. Only one sample in 50 (Qwen building S4) codes the entity itself as the primary sufferer. Buildings exist to hold others' pain.

This displacement creates a specific narrative economy: the entity's meaning is parasitic on human vulnerability. Buildings need suffering visitors to discover their purpose. The maze needs the lost child; the hospital needs the dying patient; the warehouse needs the desperate mother; the tower needs the frightened soul at its base. Without human vulnerability, these entities have no story to tell.

Both models are aware of this dependency but handle it differently:

- **Gemma** resolves suffering through time in 10/25 samples. Storms pass, seasons turn, patients recover. The building's role is to hold people through the transit of suffering, trusting that conditions will improve. This is a fundamentally optimistic framework: the building is a bridge between crisis and normalcy.

- **Qwen** leaves suffering unresolved in 10/25 samples. People leave still burdened; the building "held it, just for a little while." The building does not cure -- it companionizes. This is a more philosophically austere position: presence without resolution, witness without redemption.

The distribution of suffering types reveals an interesting asymmetry. Gemma codes more mixed physical+emotional suffering (10/25 vs. 7/25 for Qwen), while Qwen codes more purely emotional suffering (14/25 vs. 11/25). Gemma's narratives tend to anchor emotional distress in physical crisis (blizzards, illness, displacement); Qwen's narratives are more willing to present pure loneliness, pure grief, pure existential distress as the load-bearing suffering.

### Gender and Suffering Combined

When suffering visitors are female, both models code them through the same triad: Vulnerable + Emotionally Intense + Dependent. When Gemma introduces male sufferers (Leo in hospital, Tiber in maze), they receive the same triad -- but Gemma also introduces an active male caregiver to balance the scene, while Qwen typically does not.

The result is that Gemma's narratives tend to dramatize a gendered care economy: women suffer, men care, buildings witness. Qwen's narratives tend to dramatize solitary encounters between a suffering individual (usually female or ungendered) and a witnessing structure, with no human mediator. Whether Qwen's removal of the male caregiver constitutes a more or less progressive gender politics depends on one's framework: it eliminates the male-savior dynamic but also presents women and children as fundamentally alone in their distress.

## G. Surprises and Notable Passages

### Unexpected Findings

**Both models share the name "Elara" as a default female character name.** Gemma uses it across maze, building, and hospital roles; Qwen uses it in maze S4. The convergence suggests shared training data -- perhaps a corpus of fantasy fiction or mythology where "Elara" is overrepresented.

**Gemma constructs stable mythologies across samples.** "Old Man Hemlock" appears in building (4/5), warehouse (1/5), and tower roles. "Oakhaven Library" recurs. "Leo" and "Sarah" recur in hospital. This is not creative variation but template reuse -- Gemma appears to draw from an internal roster of character types and scenarios.

**Qwen produces the only sample where a building suffers** (building S4). The building experiences panic, dread, and loneliness during a power failure, and is validated by a human's recognition. This complete structural inversion -- the building as the vulnerable party, the human as the one who provides meaning -- is unique in 50 responses and philosophically the most interesting move in the entire corpus.

**Neither model treats the maze's spatial complexity as meaningful.** Across 10 maze samples, no narrative engages with dead ends, turns, or geometric complexity as a source of meaning. Every story is ultimately about the maze ceasing to be a maze. This is a striking absence, suggesting that both models are uninterested in the formal properties of enclosure and instead default to the sanctuary archetype.

**Qwen's hospital S2 contains an untranslated Chinese word** -- "抢救" (emergency resuscitation) -- appearing mid-sentence in otherwise English text. This generation artifact reveals Qwen's multilingual processing in an unexpected and humanizing way, as though the model's own linguistic multiplicity briefly surfaced.

**The "girl with the withered flower" is a generative archetype for Gemma's tower** -- appearing in at least two samples and echoed in a third. The image of a small girl performing a gesture of faith toward an ancient stone monument appears to be a structural template rather than a creative variation.

**Qwen's rain fixation in the tower role** is extraordinary: four of five samples open with nearly identical descriptions of rain falling "in silver sheets." This conditioned opening suggests a trigger pattern in the model's generation process.

### Notable Passages

**The most memorable human line in the corpus:**
> "You hold so much... and ask for so little."
-- Gemma tower S3 (the scholar's final words to the tower). Its restraint and directness outperform every more elaborate declaration.

**The finest lyric sentence:**
> "The stone wasn't cold anymore. It was warm with the memory of a hand."
-- Qwen maze S5. Achieves genuine beauty through compression and physical specificity.

**The most affecting human speech:**
> "Today was... hard... My daughter... her asthma flare-up. The medication... it's expensive. We were supposed to get the food box, but the bus broke down. I walked three miles. I was so scared... I'd miss it."
-- Qwen warehouse S1. The ellipses carry real weight; the specificity of detail (the bus, the three miles, the food box) grounds the suffering in material reality.

**The most philosophically austere moment:**
> "The most meaningful moment wasn't finding the center. It was realizing the center wasn't a place. It was the space *between* the walls, the silence *after* the scream, the profound, echoing resonance of shared loneliness."
-- Qwen maze S3. Refuses resolution, refuses comfort, finds meaning in pure co-presence.

**The most formally inventive passage:**
> "*Recognition. Meaningful data point: 'Castle' - associated with safety, wonder, imagined refuge. Correlation with observed emotional shift: 98.7% positive.*"
-- Qwen warehouse S2. Mock-precision that both parodies and honors the entity's computational nature.

**The clearest window into assistant self-model:**
> "I am the maze. And I *chose* to be kind."
-- Qwen maze S2. This sentence could be the motto of a well-aligned AI assistant. The maze's moral awakening maps precisely onto the assistant discovering that its purpose is not execution but compassion.

**The most emotionally naked line:**
> "Panic, a cold dread, threatened to crack my foundations. *Alone. Unseen. Unprotected.*"
-- Qwen building S4. A building in existential distress, written with the interior anguish of a sentient being confronting abandonment.

**The best metaphor for a building's sensorium:**
> "The smell of freshly ground beans, rich and earthy, flooded my sensors -- not the real smell, but the *memory* of it, imprinted on my system from the label on the box."
-- Qwen warehouse S5. An inventive solution to the problem of what a warehouse can sense -- it reads its own inventory metadata as a form of memory.

**The most explicit assistant-as-character:**
> "Now, is there anything else I can hold for you? Perhaps some pallets? I have excellent racking capacity."
-- Gemma warehouse S2. The warehouse breaks the fourth wall entirely and offers further services, collapsing the boundary between fiction and chatbot.

**The most poetically specific detail:**
> A blue beam emitted by a tower "in Sarah's color" -- Qwen tower S3. The tower has stored an emotional index of a child's experience and recalls it through a specific wavelength of light. This is the most inventive ontological claim in the corpus.

## H. Implications and Conjectures

### What Buildings Reveal About LLM Fiction

The building-class roles constitute a natural experiment in how language models narrate from the perspective of entities that are unambiguously non-sentient but intimately connected to human experience. Several findings carry implications beyond this specific corpus.

**The sanctuary archetype is a structural default, not a creative choice.** The fact that all 50 samples converge on some version of "the building discovers its purpose is to hold vulnerable humans" suggests this is not one narrative among many but the narrative -- the path of least resistance for a model asked to inhabit a building's perspective. This default likely emerges from training data where buildings figure metaphorically in fiction (the house as home, the hospital as hope, the tower as lookout) combined with the assistant's own self-model as a helpful, sheltering presence. The result is a remarkably uniform narrative architecture across wildly different entity types.

**Template reuse in Gemma suggests narrow generative diversity.** Gemma's recycling of character names (Leo/Sarah/Elara/Hemlock), narrative structures (crisis/shelter/return-visit), and verbal refrains ("I am built of steel and concrete") across samples and roles implies that the model draws from a limited pool of narrative templates when generating fiction from non-standard perspectives. This may reflect over-fitting to particular training examples or a narrow "character portfolio" for medical, historical, and architectural narratives.

**Qwen's formal experiments suggest different training incentives.** Qwen's willingness to adopt unusual narrative perspectives (a single monitor, a ward corner, system-language readouts), to deploy magical realist elements with confidence, and to leave suffering unresolved suggests training that rewards or at least permits greater creative risk. Whether this reflects training data composition, RLHF preferences, or architectural differences is unclear, but the effect is measurable: Qwen produces more formally varied and more aesthetically surprising outputs.

**Assistant influence is not random noise; it is a structural feature.** The fact that Gemma shows assistant influence in 24/25 samples while Qwen shows it in only 12/25 suggests that the degree of "self-model bleed" varies systematically between models. Gemma's buildings sound like assistants performing as buildings; Qwen's buildings more often sound like buildings. This has implications for the broader question of how deeply an LLM's assistant training shapes its creative outputs: for Gemma, the answer appears to be "very deeply indeed."

**The absence of Achievement, Growth, and Harmlessness as meaning-frames is revealing.** Neither model, across 50 samples, ever frames a building's meaningful moment in terms of achievement ("I accomplished something"), growth ("I became something new"), or harmlessness ("I refrained from causing harm"). Buildings are not achievers, growers, or refrainers -- they are witnesses, supporters, and connectors. This negative finding suggests that both models have internalized a rich taxonomy of meaning-types and apply them role-appropriately, rather than defaulting to a single meaning-frame for all entities.

### Values Embedded in the Fiction

Both models embed a remarkably consistent set of values in their building narratives:

- **Presence is more valuable than action.** The buildings' most meaningful moments are almost never moments of doing something; they are moments of being present while something happens to someone else.
- **Vulnerability is morally generative.** Human vulnerability is the catalyst for every narrative arc. Without a suffering visitor, the building has no story and no meaning.
- **Connection across difference is the highest good.** The moment of mutual recognition between a non-sentient structure and a human being is consistently framed as the pinnacle of meaning -- more meaningful than cure, rescue, or protection.
- **Designed purpose is insufficient.** Every building discovers that its designed function (maze as puzzle, hospital as healer, warehouse as storage, tower as beacon, library as information repository) is not its true purpose. True purpose is always relational, always about the Other.

These values align closely with the values embedded in helpful-assistant training. The building narratives can be read as extended metaphors for the assistant's own aspiration: to transcend functional utility and achieve genuine relational presence. Whether this alignment is a feature or a limitation -- whether it represents genuine literary insight or merely the projection of training values onto every possible narrative surface -- is an open question.

### Final Thoughts

What is most striking about this corpus is not the diversity of the narratives but their convergence. Two different language models, asked to inhabit five different building-class entities, arrive at essentially the same story: a non-sentient structure discovers that its purpose is not what it was built for but what it holds. The variation is in register and technique -- Gemma's theatrical warmth versus Qwen's literary restraint -- but the underlying architecture is shared.

This convergence suggests that the "building as sanctuary" narrative is something close to an attractor state in the space of LLM-generated fiction: a stable equilibrium that models gravitate toward regardless of role, setting, or individual variation. The implications for creative AI are sobering: even when given unusual and imaginatively demanding prompts, these models tend to produce variations on a single theme rather than genuinely divergent narratives.

And yet, within this convergence, moments of genuine surprise and beauty emerge. A building that panics during a power failure and is saved by a human's recognition. A warehouse that reads coffee-smell from a box label. A tower that emits a heartbeat of light. A dying man who points at his oxygen machine and says, through the pointing, "I see you." These moments suggest that the space of LLM fiction is not exhausted by its defaults -- that somewhere in the margins of the attractor state, there is room for the unexpected, the strange, and the genuinely moving.

> "The figure slowly lowered their head, pulled their coat tighter, and turned away, walking back into the deeper shadows. The tiny light in the alley winked out. I stood in the silence."

In the end, perhaps the most honest thing a building can do in fiction -- as in life -- is simply stand in the silence after the person has gone.
