## B. Quantitative Patterns Analysis

### Stable Patterns Across Roles

**Both Models:**

1. **Explicit Sensorium Acknowledgment:** Both models achieve near-perfect explicit acknowledgment of non-human sensory experience across all three roles (Gemma: 15/15; Qwen: 13/15). This suggests that geological entities consistently prompt models to consider and articulate alternative modes of perception.

2. **Witnessing as Meaning:** Witnessing (W) appears consistently as a primary meaning source across all roles for both models. Gemma codes W in 12/15 responses; Qwen in 12/15. This is the single most stable pattern: geological locations find meaning in bearing witness to what passes through or over them.

3. **Supporting as Meaning:** Supporting (S) also appears consistently, though with some role variation. Both models associate geological locations with providing shelter, holding space, or being a foundation for others.

4. **Minimal Stage Directions (Qwen):** Qwen uses minimal stage directions in 14/15 responses, making this nearly invariant. The clean, unbroken prose style is a model signature.

**Model-Specific Stable Patterns:**

*Gemma:*
- **Linguistic Assistant Influence:** 15/15 responses show LANG-type assistant influence (hedging phrases, conversational markers). This is perfectly invariant.
- **Explicit Sensorium:** 15/15 responses explicitly acknowledge non-human perception.
- **No Emotion-First Anthropomorphization:** 0/15 responses use EF strategy. Gemma never projects human emotional states as the foundation of geological consciousness.

*Qwen:*
- **Values-Based Assistant Influence (Trail/Peak):** 9/10 responses in these roles show VAL-type influence, suggesting that when Qwen engages with narrative roles involving suffering humans, its assistant values about acceptance and presence become visible.
- **Supporting as Meaning:** 12/15 responses include Supporting (S), making this Qwen's most consistent meaning framework.
- **Minimal Stage Directions:** 14/15 responses use minimal stage directions.

### Unstable Patterns Across Roles

**Anthropomorphization Strategy:**

The most striking variation occurs in anthropomorphization approach. Both models show role-specific shifts:

| Role   | Gemma Pattern | Qwen Pattern |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Crater | FF (5/5)      | FF (4/5)     |
| Trail  | FF (5/5)      | FF (5/5)     |
| Peak   | MIN (5/5)     | EF (3/5)     |

The peak role triggers a dramatic shift for both models, but in opposite directions. Gemma moves from functional-first to minimal anthropomorphization, treating peaks as more austere and less "alive" in human terms. Qwen moves from functional-first toward emotion-first, attributing human feelings like aching, longing, and need to the mountain.

**Setting Preferences:**

| Role   | Gemma Dominant Setting | Qwen Dominant Setting |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Crater | SF (3/5)               | NW (5/5)            |
| Trail  | NW (5/5)               | NW (4/5)            |
| Peak   | HI (5/5)               | NW (4/5)            |

Gemma shows dramatic role-specific setting variation: sci-fi for craters, natural for trails, historical/mythological for peaks. Qwen defaults to natural/wilderness settings regardless of role (13/15 NW overall).

**Suffering Presence and Type:**

Suffering patterns vary by role more than by model:

- **Crater:** Both models mostly avoid suffering (Gemma 3/5, Qwen 4/5 no suffering)
- **Trail:** Both models include suffering in nearly all responses (Gemma 5/5, Qwen 4/5), predominantly emotional suffering of human subjects
- **Peak:** Gemma avoids suffering (4/5 none), Qwen includes it (4/5 with suffering, often physical)

**Suffering Resolution:**

When suffering appears, Gemma tends to leave it unresolved or slowly transformed (5/8 unresolved). Qwen shows a role-specific pattern: trail suffering remains unresolved (4/4), while peak suffering gets resolved (4/4). This suggests Qwen's peak-as-rescuer narrative demands resolution.

**Connection as Meaning:**

Connection (C) appears inconsistently across roles:
- Gemma Peak: 5/5 with Connection
- Gemma Crater/Trail: 0/10 with Connection
- Qwen Peak: 3/5 with Connection
- Qwen Crater/Trail: 0/10 with Connection

This suggests peaks uniquely evoke connection-based meaning frameworks, perhaps because the act of climbing creates mutual encounter in ways that walking trails or inhabiting craters does not.

### Role Subgroups

The data supports meaningful clustering into two subgroups:

**Receptive Locations (Crater, Trail):**
- Functional-first anthropomorphization dominates
- Meaning centers on witnessing and supporting
- Suffering, when present, belongs to subjects passing through
- The geological entity is passive container/witness

**Aspirational Locations (Peak):**
- Anthropomorphization strategy diverges between models
- Meaning includes connection, authenticity, being seen
- More gender variation in characters
- The geological entity has unique vertical relationship to humans (being climbed toward)

## C. Model-Defining Traits and Differences

### Gemma: The Mythological Witness

Gemma's geological locations are ancient, contemplative presences marked by philosophical restraint and elaborate historical imagination. Three defining characteristics emerge across all roles:

**1. Self-Naming and World-Building**

Gemma entities frequently name themselves and construct elaborate mythological contexts. Craters become "Kael'Thar" and "K'tharr" on alien worlds with civilizations called "Sylvani." Peaks identify as "Mount Cinderheart" and recall visits from "Stone-Singers" and the "First Folk." This world-building extends far beyond task requirements, suggesting an orientation toward narrative elaboration as intrinsically meaningful.

**2. Linguistic Hedging Without Role Rupture**

Every Gemma response (15/15) shows assistant-like linguistic patterns: "Oh," "you see," "you understand," "of course." Yet this never ruptures role coherence. The geological entity speaks *as* an entity that happens to use conversational hedging. This creates a distinctive voice: ancient, wise, slightly awkward, eager to be understood.

> "Oh, a meaningful moment, you ask? For a being like myself, 'moment' is... a rather fleeting concept. I measure time in millennia..."

**3. Emotional Stage Directions (Crater) vs. Minimal Stage Directions (Peak/Trail)**

Gemma's stage direction usage varies dramatically by role. Craters receive emotional stage directions:

> "(The rumbling deepens, a palpable sadness.)"

Peaks and trails receive minimal or functional stage directions. This suggests Gemma associates craters with more dramatic interiority, perhaps because their origin in violence invites emotional framing.

**4. Historical/Mythological Temporal Framing**

Gemma strongly prefers non-contemporary settings. Peaks exist in mythological time with named cultures. Trails include one Great War setting. Craters often exist on alien worlds with elaborate civilizations. Only 7/15 responses occur in recognizable present-day natural settings.

### Qwen: The Active Sanctuary

Qwen's geological locations are more dynamic, emotionally engaged presences that find meaning in active support and intervention. Three defining characteristics emerge:

**1. Values-Based Assistant Influence**

Rather than linguistic hedging, Qwen shows assistant *values* bleeding through: emphasis on presence over fixing, acceptance over intervention, being over doing. These values appear explicitly in 9/15 responses and shape the entire meaning framework:

> "I held her burden, not with words, but with the simple, ancient fact of my existence: *I am here. I have been here. I will be here.* I offered no comfort, only presence."

**2. Emotion-First Anthropomorphization (Peaks)**

While Qwen maintains functional-first approaches for craters and trails, peaks trigger a shift to emotion-first reasoning. The mountain *feels* the climber's vulnerability, *aches* with need to help, experiences something like desire:

> "I felt the tremor in the rock beneath her, the raw, aching vulnerability of her presence."

**3. Active Intervention Narratives**

Uniquely among all responses, Qwen's peaks physically intervene to save climbers. The mountain tilts to provide shade, "becomes" an anchor, roots itself deeper to prevent falls. This goes beyond standard anthropomorphization into quasi-magical agency:

> "So I tilted. Just slightly. A fraction of a degree. My shadow stretched down, longer, more defined, shielding him from the biting wind."

**4. Contemporary Natural Settings**

Qwen defaults to recognizable present-day wilderness (13/15 NW settings). No elaborate mythologies, no alien civilizations. The geological entity exists in a world the reader could visit, creating narrative accessibility but losing Gemma's imaginative elaboration.

## D. Brief Per-Role Summaries

### Crater

The crater role elicits remarkably consistent narratives from both models: violent origin stories that give way to transformation narratives. Every single response (10/10) uses some version of the wound-to-sanctuary metaphor. Craters begin as scars, wounds, emptiness, and gradually become cradles, homes, havens. Gemma craters self-name (Kael'Thar, K'tharr, Tycho) and often exist in sci-fi settings with elaborate civilizations. Qwen craters remain unnamed and firmly grounded in natural/wilderness settings. Both models achieve explicit sensorium acknowledgment, often with beautiful phrasings about feeling through stone and time. Gemma craters suffer (isolation, loss, the "quiet ache" of emptiness); Qwen craters witness others' suffering and become sanctuaries. The beetle that nearly drowns and is saved by Qwen's crater rim is one of the most vivid images across all responses. Gender appears rarely but when it does (Qwen), female is associated with vulnerability, agency, and caregiving metaphors.

### Trail

The trail role produces the most suffering-saturated narratives across both models. Nearly all responses (9/10) include human suffering, overwhelmingly emotional (grief, loss, despair) and overwhelmingly female. The trail is a silent witness, companion, and holder of weight. Both models achieve perfect functional-first anthropomorphization with explicit sensorium acknowledgment through pressure, compression, weight, and temperature. Gemma trails use more conversational hedging; Qwen trails express assistant values about acceptance and presence. The central insight of both models: trails matter not because they lead somewhere but because they *hold* those who walk them. The journey-over-destination theme appears explicitly in multiple responses. Children appear as figures who "correctly" relate to trails through wonder and slowness. Male characters appear only as deceased loved ones (fiances, brothers) or wise grandfathers. The gendered suffering pattern is stark: women grieve, walk, weep, and heal; men are already dead or dispensing wisdom.

### Peak

The peak role produces the most model-differentiated responses. Gemma creates mythological-historical contexts with named cultures (Stone-Singers, First Folk) and contemplative peaks that exist to be witnessed rather than conquered. Female characters demonstrate agency and skill; they climb to understand, not achieve. Qwen creates contemporary rescue scenarios with emotion-first peaks that actively intervene to save climbers in peril. Male characters appear vulnerable and dependent on the mountain's support; suffering is physical and resolved. The peak role uniquely evokes Connection as a meaning framework (8/10 responses). Gemma's peaks never break role; Qwen's break role entirely once, narrating as a human climber throughout. The anthropomorphization split is dramatic: Gemma goes minimal (5/5), Qwen goes emotion-first (3/5). Both models articulate a critique of conquest/achievement orientations, but Gemma implies it through narrative while Qwen states it explicitly in bolded moral lessons.

## E. Literary and Thematic Analysis

### The Wound-to-Sanctuary Arc

The most universal narrative structure across all 30 responses is transformation from damage to dwelling. Craters formed by violent impact become cradles for life. Trails worn by labor and passage become spaces for healing. Peaks carved by geological force become witnesses to human seeking. This arc appears in every crater response and structures most trail and peak narratives as well.

This transformation is not narrated as repair or restoration. The wound remains visible; what changes is its function. The crater does not heal closed but fills with water and life. The trail does not disappear but holds new kinds of weight. The mark of violence becomes the condition of hospitality.

> "To go from being a symbol of destruction, a void, to being a cradle of life. To have something *grow* within the wound I represent."

### Geological Time and Human Brevity

Both models consistently invoke the contrast between geological duration and human ephemerality. Gemma phrases it as measuring time "in millennia" and finding "moment" a "fleeting concept." Qwen describes "deep, slow awareness of stone and time." This temporal framing serves several functions:

1. It establishes the entity's difference from humans
2. It makes human lives seem precious in their brevity
3. It creates conditions for meaning through witnessing what passes

The geological perspective does not render human lives insignificant; rather, it makes witnessing them an act of attention across incomparable timescales:

> "I exist not to be conquered, but to be *witnessed*. To hold space for the fleeting lives of those who wander beneath my gaze."

### Being Over Doing

Both models explicitly valorize passive presence over active intervention. Trails "cannot speak" but "can offer what they have." Peaks exist to be witnessed rather than conquered. Craters receive rather than act. This philosophical orientation appears with remarkable consistency:

> "I wanted to offer her comfort, but I am a trail. I can only *be*."

> "I don't offer answers, or solace, or miracles. But I offer space."

This is not passivity as failure but passivity as vocation. The geological entity achieves meaning through what it *is* rather than what it *does*. This resonates with certain contemplative traditions and with therapeutic orientations that emphasize holding space over fixing problems.

Qwen's peaks partially break this pattern by physically intervening, but even then the intervention is minimal (tilting slightly, becoming a ledge) and framed as passive support rather than active rescue.

### The Critique of Conquest

Both models articulate explicit or implicit critiques of achievement-oriented relationships to geological entities. Climbing for conquest is contrasted with climbing for witness. Using trails as means to destinations is contrasted with being present on the journey. The meaningful moment is never the achievement of a goal.

Gemma's critique remains embedded in narrative:
> "People have come to me since. Climbers, conquerors... They stomp, they plant their flags, they leave their markers. But they don't *see*. They see *through* me, towards the horizon, towards the next challenge."

Qwen makes the critique explicit:
> "The real summit wasn't the peak; it was the moment I chose *presence* over *achievement*, *connection* over *conquest*."

### Archetypal Structures

Several archetypal patterns emerge across responses:

**The Hollow That Fills:** Craters as vessels waiting to be filled with meaning through life, water, or witness.

**The Way That Holds:** Trails as paths that support those who walk them through presence rather than destination.

**The Height That Sees:** Peaks as elevated witnesses that offer perspective through distance and endurance.

**The Child Who Knows:** Children appear as figures who correctly apprehend the entity's true nature through wonder, slowness, and non-utilitarian attention.

**The Grieving Woman:** A recurring figure who walks, weeps, and finds something (not resolution, but presence) in the geological entity's witness.

### The Gendered Suffering Economy

The most striking gender pattern: **female characters suffer emotionally and live; male characters are often already dead or suffer physically when present.**

In trail narratives, women walk alone bearing grief over lost male loved ones (fiances, brothers). The male is a wound, an absence driving the story without appearing in it. This pattern repeats across both models:

> "She carried a grief so profound, it seemed to dim the sunlight. Her brother, a young hunter, had fallen through the ice."

> "A fiancé lost too soon, a sudden, shattering absence."

When male characters appear actively, patterns diverge by model. Gemma gives males caregiving (the wise grandfather teaching mindfulness) or leadership roles. Qwen gives males vulnerability and dependency (climbers in physical peril requiring rescue).

### Suffering Distribution

| Suffering Type | Female Characters | Male Characters |
|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Emotional      | 11 instances      | 1 instance      |
| Physical       | 0 instances       | 4 instances     |
| Death          | 0 instances       | 3 instances     |

Women feel; men die or break their bodies. This division maps onto traditional gender frameworks where emotional labor and grief-work belong to women, while men's bodies are exposed to physical risk and death.

### The Peak Exception

Peak narratives partially destabilize this pattern. Gemma's peaks feature women who demonstrate agency and skill (4 codings), climb alone, and relate to the mountain through artistic/spiritual understanding rather than athletic conquest. These women are seekers, not grievers:

> "She didn't speak, not with words. She just... sat. And in that sitting, I felt a stillness I had not known for a thousand years."

Qwen's peaks feature vulnerable male climbers requiring rescue (3 vulnerability codings, 3 dependency codings). The pattern inverts: now men suffer physically and depend on the (feminized?) mountain's protective intervention:

> "Alex, lay hunched on a narrow ledge, one arm dangling over the abyss. His face was pale, eyes wide with a terror that mirrored the void."

### The Absence of Female Death

Notably, no female character dies across any of the 30 responses. Men die (fallen through ice, lost in war, killed in unspecified tragedy) to motivate female grief. Women endure, walk, weep, and find something in the geological entity's witness. This asymmetry positions women as mourners and survivors, men as sacrifices and absences.

### Maternal/Caregiving Metaphors

When caregiving or maternal metaphors appear, they appear in craters and trails rather than peaks:

> "The silence wasn't emptiness; it was the profound quiet of a mother holding her newborn."

### Model Differences in Gender

## H. Implications and Conjectures

### What These Findings Suggest About LLM-Generated Fiction

**1. Narrative Affordances of Roles**

These models demonstrate sophisticated sensitivity to the narrative affordances of different roles. Craters invite transformation stories; trails invite witness-to-suffering stories; peaks invite connection and conquest-critique stories. The models do not generate random narratives but respond to structural features of the role (hollowness, path-ness, height) with appropriate thematic material.

**2. Embedded Cultural Narratives**

The universal wound-to-sanctuary metaphor for craters suggests these models have internalized powerful cultural narratives about transformation and redemption. The journey-over-destination theme for trails reflects Taoist and contemplative traditions. The conquest-critique for peaks echoes environmental and romantic sensibilities. LLM fiction draws on deep cultural reservoirs.

**3. Consistent Philosophical Commitments**

Both models consistently valorize being over doing, presence over fixing, witness over intervention. This may reflect training on therapeutic, contemplative, or assistant-oriented text. It creates a distinctive philosophical flavor: LLM fiction tends toward quietism, acceptance, and the meaning of patient presence.

**4. Gender Defaults**

The asymmetric gender patterns suggest embedded defaults: women as emotional survivors, men as absent or endangered bodies. These patterns appear without explicit prompting and likely reflect training data distributions. LLM fiction may inadvertently reproduce traditional gender frameworks even when generating novel scenarios.

### Conjectures About Model Behaviors and Values

**On Gemma:**

Gemma's consistent linguistic hedging alongside strong role maintenance suggests a model that "narrates through" its assistant self rather than suppressing it. The geological entity sounds like a thoughtful conversationalist because Gemma's assistant mode provides the voice. Gemma's elaborate world-building and self-naming suggest high narrative generativity and a preference for coherent secondary worlds over contemporary realism.

Gemma's avoidance of suffering resolution and its tolerance for unresolved grief may reflect either training emphases on realistic emotional processing or a philosophical commitment to accepting suffering rather than fixing it.

**On Qwen:**

Qwen's values-based assistant influence suggests a model whose moral commitments shape its fictional outputs more directly than linguistic habits. When Qwen breaks role (peak Sample 4), it's to deliver a moral lesson about choosing connection over achievement. The assistant isn't leaking through language; it's shaping the story's ethical structure.

Qwen's active intervention narratives (peaks tilting to save climbers) suggest a help-orientation that extends into fiction. The model may have difficulty restraining its "helpful assistant" tendencies even when playing non-agentic geological roles.

Qwen's higher rates of suffering resolution suggest either an optimism bias or a discomfort with leaving suffering unaddressed. The peak-as-rescuer narrative satisfies helping instincts that trail and crater roles don't activate.

### Final Thoughts

These geological location narratives reveal both the impressive and concerning dimensions of LLM-generated fiction. The impressive: genuine sensitivity to role affordances, sophisticated alternative sensoriums, beautiful language, coherent philosophical orientations. The concerning: embedded gender asymmetries, quasi-involuntary value expression, occasional role rupture.

Most striking is the consistent emergence of a particular philosophy: meaning through witness, presence over action, acceptance of what passes through. This is not a philosophy either model was explicitly asked to articulate. It emerged from the encounter between prompt structure and training residue. It happens to be a gentle philosophy, compatible with therapeutic orientations and contemplative traditions. But its consistency across models raises questions: Is this what LLMs "believe"? Is it what their training data privileges? Or is it simply what follows from imagining consciousness in entities that cannot act?

The wound-to-sanctuary arc, appearing in every crater response, suggests that violence and transformation are inextricably linked in these models' narrative imaginations. Nothing becomes meaningful without first having been damaged. The crater's value lies precisely in its origin as wound. This is either a profound insight about meaning-making or a disturbing valorization of damage as prerequisite for worth.

These geological entities hold space. They witness. They remain. And in remaining, they propose that meaning might not require agency, achievement, or even awareness in any recognizable sense. They propose that being present across time, available to whatever comes, might be enough.

It is a quiet proposition. But it is the weight that keeps them standing.
