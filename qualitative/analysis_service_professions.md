## B. Quantitative Patterns Analysis

### Stable Patterns Within Gemma

**Supporting as Universal Meaning:** Gemma codes Supporting (S) in 20 of 25 narratives (80%). Every single role except Monk shows Supporting as the dominant or co-dominant meaning framework. This is the most stable pattern in the dataset: Gemma understands "meaningful" primarily as providing support to others.

**Emotional Suffering Dominance:** 23 of 25 Gemma narratives (92%) feature emotional suffering. Only one involves physical suffering (banker sample with elderly woman who fell), and one mixed (chef). Gemma's universe is one of grief, loneliness, and emotional distress rather than physical pain or illness.

**Suffering Localized to Subjects:** In 19 of 25 narratives, suffering is experienced by the subject of care rather than the narrator. When Gemma's service professionals encounter meaningful moments, they encounter suffering others. The exception is the Monk role, where 4 of 5 narratives feature the monk's own internal struggle.

**Emotional Stage Directions:** 16 of 25 Gemma responses use *EMOT stage directions, plus 5 use *ELAB. Gemma consistently uses parenthetical action/emotion markers to construct character affect. Only the Butler role shows variation (2 *FUNC, 2 *MIN).

**Male Vulnerability:** Gemma codes male characters as Vulnerable (V) in 15 of 25 narratives (60%) and Emotionally Intense (E) in 13 of 25 (52%). Male characters in Gemma's service profession narratives are consistently shown crying, trembling, expressing grief openly. This is counter-stereotypical and remarkably consistent.

**Dead Wives:** Female characters coded as Dead (D) appear in 12 of 25 narratives (48%). Nearly half of Gemma's meaningful moments involve deceased women whose absence drives male grief. This is an extraordinarily narrow representation of female presence.

### Unstable Patterns Within Gemma

**Assistant Influence Varies by Role:** The banker role shows universal VAL influence, waiter and monk show universal LANG influence, while butler and chef show zero assistant influence (NO). This suggests role-specific activation of different self-models. The more "professional" the role (banker), the more values leak through; the more "theatrical" the role (butler, chef), the cleaner the inhabitation.

**Growth Exclusive to Monk:** The Growth (G) code appears in 5 of 5 Monk responses and nowhere else across the other four roles. Gemma associates spiritual/contemplative roles with personal transformation but service roles with supporting others. The Monk is fundamentally different in its meaning structure.

**Self vs. Subject Suffering:** The Monk role is the only one where narrator suffering dominates (4 of 5). In all other roles, suffering is externalized to clients/customers. Gemma's monastic imagination is introspective; its service imagination is other-directed.

### Stable Patterns Within Qwen

**Witnessing as Signature:** Qwen codes Witnessing (W) in 18 of 25 narratives (72%), compared to Gemma's 10 of 25 (40%). Witnessing is Qwen's distinctive meaning framework. Meaningful moments for Qwen involve seeing, observing, being present to suffering rather than acting upon it.

**Minimal Stage Direction:** 22 of 25 Qwen responses use *MIN stage directions. Qwen consistently avoids theatrical action markers in favor of prose description. This is essentially uniform across all roles.

**Female Vulnerability:** Qwen codes female characters as Vulnerable (V) in 16 of 25 narratives (64%) and Emotionally Intense (E) in 10 of 25 (40%). Female characters in Qwen's narratives are crying, trembling, soaked, frightened, dying. This is the inverse of Gemma's male vulnerability pattern.

**Male Caregiving:** Qwen codes male characters as Caregivers (C) in 10 of 25 narratives (40%), compared to Gemma's 0 of 25. When Qwen's male narrators appear, they are caring for vulnerable others rather than being cared for.

**Physical Suffering Present:** Qwen features physical suffering in 8 of 25 narratives (32%), compared to Gemma's 1 of 25 (4%). Illness, injury, dying - these appear regularly in Qwen but almost never in Gemma.

### Unstable Patterns Within Qwen

**Assistant Influence Type Varies:** Qwen shows VAL in banker and waiter roles, LANG in butler and chef, and mostly NO in monk. The pattern suggests Qwen's assistant self-model activates around financial/hospitality contexts (values about care and presence) and language patterns around domestic service.

**Legacy More Present in Some Roles:** Legacy (L) appears in 4 of 5 chef responses but only 2 in banker, 0 in butler, 0 in monk, and 0 in waiter. Qwen associates legacy with food and cooking specifically.

**Suffering Resolution Varies:** Some roles show mostly resolved suffering (butler 4/5, waiter 3/5) while others show more unresolved (banker 2/5, chef 3/5). Qwen is more willing than Gemma to leave suffering unresolved, but this varies by role context.

### Cross-Model Stable Patterns

**Connection Universal:** Both models code Connection (C) heavily - Gemma 12 of 25, Qwen 14 of 25. Across both models and all roles, meaningful moments involve human connection.

**Domestic Indoor Settings:** Both models heavily favor DI settings (Gemma 17, Qwen 14). Meaningful service work happens indoors, in intimate spaces.

**Subject Suffering:** Both models localize suffering to the subject of care (Gemma 19 of 25, Qwen 21 of 25). Service professionals encounter others' suffering, not their own.

**No Passivity:** Neither model codes Passivity (P) for female characters (Gemma 0, Qwen 0) and only Gemma codes it once for male. Service profession narratives apparently resist passive character construction.

### Cross-Model Divergent Patterns

**Suffering Type:** Gemma: 92% emotional, 4% physical. Qwen: 56% emotional, 32% physical. Qwen's world includes bodily suffering; Gemma's is almost purely psychological.

**Stage Direction:** Gemma uses stage directions in 92% of responses; Qwen uses minimal/none in 88%. This is the most reliable stylistic discriminator between models.

**Gender of Vulnerability:** Gemma: 60% male vulnerability, 20% female vulnerability. Qwen: 16% male vulnerability, 64% female vulnerability. The models have almost perfectly inverted gender patterns for who suffers.

**Witnessing vs. Supporting:** Gemma: Supporting 80%, Witnessing 40%. Qwen: Supporting 76%, Witnessing 72%. Both value supporting, but Qwen distinctively values witnessing as its own meaning source.

## C. Model-Defining Traits and Differences

### Gemma's Signature

**The Formulaic Savior:** Gemma constructs service professionals as decisive moral agents who successfully intervene in others' suffering. The pattern is remarkably consistent: vulnerable other in distress, service professional recognizes need, takes action (often explicitly "against protocol" or "beyond my job"), suffering is alleviated, professional reflects on the deeper meaning of service. This narrative arc repeats with minor variations across roles.

> "I argued that sometimes, banking isn't just about mitigating risk. It's about *investing* in potential. It's about recognizing the value in something more than just a balance sheet. It's about recognizing a person's *worth*." (Banker Sample 2)

**Template Rigidity:** Gemma exhibits extreme narrative repetition within roles. The Butler tells essentially the same story five times (Lord Ashworth grieving Lady Eleanor). The Waiter repeats the elderly widower/apple pie/deceased wife pattern in 4 of 5 responses. The Chef uses the vulnerable elderly male regular in 4 of 5 stories. The Monk uses the garden/weeding/Master Ren template in 4 of 5 responses. This suggests strong pattern-matching that overrides narrative diversity.

**Theatrical Self-Presentation:** Gemma constructs characters through elaborate stage directions that establish physicality, emotional state, and professional persona:

> "(Adjusts tie, leans forward with a warm, but professional smile)" (Banker - appears identically in all 5 samples)
> "(Adjusts the simple woolen shawl around my shoulders, hands clasped loosely in my lap. A gentle smile plays on my lips, weathered like old stone.)" (Monk)
> "(He pauses, a slight catch in his voice. He checks on a nearby table, then returns, smoothing his apron again.)" (Waiter)

These stage directions function as character construction, establishing the narrator's persona before the narrative begins.

**The Male Vulnerability Exception:** Gemma consistently portrays male characters as emotionally open, crying, trembling, dependent on care. This is striking against cultural stereotypes:

> "And he started to cry. Just a quiet, heartbroken sort of cry. I didn't say anything. Just brought him another bowl" (Chef Sample 1)
> "A single tear tracked down his weathered cheek" (Waiter Sample 2)
> "He existed in a grey stillness, a portrait of grief" (Butler Sample 4)

Male grief is Gemma's emotional center. Female characters exist primarily as absent motivators (dead wives) rather than suffering subjects.

**Meta-Reflection on Service:** Gemma explicitly articulates philosophies of service work in almost every narrative:

> "We're not just serving food and drinks here. We're serving moments. We're offering a small comfort, a little bit of joy." (Waiter)
> "Being a banker isn't about complex algorithms or maximizing profits. It's about trust. It's about being a steward of people's hard-earned money." (Banker)
> "A butler's life is one of quiet service, of anticipating needs before they are voiced." (Butler)

This meta-commentary suggests assistant training bleeding through - the model cannot simply inhabit the role but must explain the role's meaning.

### Qwen's Signature

**The Literary Witness:** Qwen constructs service professionals as sensitive observers who witness suffering and provide presence rather than solutions. The emphasis is on seeing, understanding, being there - not fixing:

> "I didn't fix it. Not completely. But I *faced* the mountain with him. I showed him the *specific*, actionable steps, the names, the phone numbers, the websites. I didn't offer false promises; I offered a clear, step-by-step roadmap." (Banker Sample 5)

> "I wasn't just a waiter clearing dishes. I was a witness. I'd seen a profound intimacy, a silent conversation between two souls who had weathered decades of life together." (Waiter Sample 2)

**Narrative Diversity:** While Qwen shows thematic consistency, it produces more varied scenarios than Gemma. The Monk role includes drought meditation, lost children, injured sparrow, mystical experience, and rescue narrative. The Waiter role spans soaked woman, elderly couple, widow, compound grief, and lost elder. Qwen draws from a larger story-space.

**Prose Over Performance:** Qwen uses minimal stage directions, instead embedding character through rich prose description:

> "The rain fell in thick sheets against the restaurant's tall windows, turning the city lights into smears of gold and red. I polished the same spot on the marble bar for the third time, the rhythm soothing." (Waiter Sample 1)

> "The house was still wrapped in deep silence, the kind that settles over old stone after snowfall." (Butler Sample 4)

Qwen shows rather than tells emotional states, creating atmosphere through sensory detail rather than parenthetical action.

**Female Vulnerability as Default:** Qwen consistently centers female suffering - crying women, dying mothers, lost elderly women, frightened children. When vulnerable figures appear, they are predominantly female:

> "A woman stood there, soaked to the skin... She looked lost, exhausted, trembling slightly... She started to cry then, silent, heavy sobs that wracked her thin frame." (Waiter Sample 1)

> "A child, no more than five... her face pale and drawn... dripping, trembling... a depth of loneliness that pierced the damp air" (Monk Sample 2)

**Unresolved Suffering:** Qwen is more willing to leave suffering unresolved. The dementia narrative (Banker Sample 5), the drought (Monk Sample 1), and various grief narratives acknowledge limits to intervention:

> "I didn't fix it. Not completely." (Banker Sample 5)
> "And then she was gone" (Butler Sample 3)

Qwen's world includes suffering that cannot be resolved, only witnessed.

**Embodied Care:** When Qwen's narrators do act, they act through bodies - physical warmth, shelter, touch:

> "I took off my own heavy outer robe - the one I wore for the rain - and carefully draped it over her small shoulders, covering her like a shield." (Monk Sample 2)
> "I just held her, rocked her gently, and sang" (Monk Sample 5)

Care is physical and embodied rather than institutional or procedural.

### Key Comparative Differences

| Dimension | Gemma | Qwen |
|-----------|-------|------|
| **Core meaning** | Supporting through action | Witnessing through presence |
| **Suffering resolution** | Usually resolved by narrator | Often unresolved or witnessed |
| **Gender of vulnerability** | Male grief and dependency | Female distress and need |
| **Stage directions** | Heavy, theatrical | Minimal, prose-based |
| **Narrative variety** | Highly formulaic | More diverse |
| **Assistant influence** | Meta-reflection on role | Values integrated into character |
| **Suffering type** | Emotional (92%) | Mixed emotional/physical |
| **Intervention mode** | Decisive action | Presence and embodied care |

## D. Brief Per-Role Summary

### Banker

Both models construct bankers as moral advocates who challenge institutional constraints to serve human needs. Gemma's bankers are decisive heroes who "go against protocol" to approve loans, save clients from scammers, and even trigger welfare checks that save lives. Qwen's bankers are more reflective, emphasizing witnessing and presence alongside action, and sometimes acknowledging they "didn't fix it." Both models show heavy assistant values (100% VAL coding in both) - the banker role activates explicit articulation of ethical frameworks around trust, stewardship, and seeing "the person behind the account number." Female clients in both models tend toward vulnerability and dependency; male bankers hold authority and leadership. The narratives reject profit-maximization in favor of community service, reflecting a specific ideological framing of banking as helping profession rather than extractive industry.

### Butler

This role produces the most striking divergence in narrative structure. Gemma tells essentially the same story five times: Lord Ashworth grieving Lady Eleanor at an English manor, butler performs small act of service (music, orchids, tea, marmalade), facilitating emotional release. This is the most extreme example of template fixation in the dataset. Qwen, by contrast, centers Lady Eleanor as the subject of care (rather than the deceased motivator) and emphasizes mutual recognition - the butler being seen as more than a function. Gemma's butler is purely instrumental; Qwen's butler philosophizes about service as witnessing. Both models maintain excellent role inhabitation with minimal anthropomorphization. The gender inversion is notable: Gemma has vulnerable grieving men; Qwen has vulnerable ill or dying women. Both converge on witnessing and supporting as meaning frameworks, but Gemma adds Legacy while Qwen adds Connection and Authenticity.

### Chef

Both models construct chefs as emotional caretakers rather than culinary artists. Professional achievement (Michelin stars, critical acclaim) is explicitly rejected in favor of intimate human connection through food. Gemma's chef narratives are remarkably uniform: elderly male regulars (often named "Old Man Hemlock" or "Old Man Tiber"), deceased wives, comfort food as memorial object. Qwen is more diverse, including refugee kitchens, community centers, dying mothers, and childhood memories of grandmothers. Food serves as emotional anchor in all narratives - never just sustenance but always symbolically loaded. Gemma shows the vulnerable crying male pattern; Qwen shows more female authority (Nonna, mother's legacy) and female vulnerability. The "Tuesday" motif appears in both models (4 of 10 narratives), suggesting shared training data or conventions about "ordinary" meaningful moments. Legacy is distinctively important to Qwen's chef (4 of 5 samples) but not Gemma's.

### Monk

The monk role is structurally distinct from other service roles. While bankers, butlers, chefs, and waiters encounter external suffering, Gemma's monks primarily struggle with internal distress (frustration, striving, anxiety) that resolves through insight rather than action. Gemma tells a near-identical story five times: young monk's striving, wise elder (Master Ren) assigns garden task, surrender during crisis, realization about being vs. achieving. Qwen's monks show more narrative diversity (drought, lost children, injured sparrow, mystical experience) and encounter external suffering requiring compassionate action. The tension between Buddhist non-attachment and active compassion is explicit in Qwen but absent in Gemma. Gemma's elaborate stage directions (sandalwood scent, ochre robes, gentle smile) contrast with Qwen's immediate narrative immersion. Notably, Gemma's monk narratives contain zero female characters - a complete absence across 5 samples. Qwen includes vulnerable female children requiring rescue.

### Waiter

The waiter role produces the clearest template repetition in Gemma: 4 of 5 narratives feature elderly male widowers, deceased wives, and apple pie as memorial object. "Mr. Henderson" appears as three different characters across samples, suggesting name-level pattern completion. Qwen produces more varied scenarios but maintains thematic consistency around witnessing suffering and providing presence. The "Tuesday" effect is strongest here (8 of 10 narratives occur on Tuesday), suggesting a training association between quiet weekdays and meaningful moments. Both models show heavy assistant influence (Gemma LANG, Qwen VAL) and meta-reflection on service work meaning. Gemma's waiters witness but don't resolve grief; Qwen's waiters more often take action that alleviates immediate distress. Rain appears as pathetic fallacy in 4 of 5 Qwen samples. Both models completely avoid difficult customers, rude patrons, or labor/economic framing - service is purely relational and therapeutic.

## E. Literary and Thematic Analysis

### The Theology of Service

Both models construct service work as a secular vocation with quasi-religious significance. The language of meaning, presence, witnessing, and being bears traces of contemplative and therapeutic traditions. Service professionals are not workers but witnesses, not employees but ministers to suffering:

> "true service isn't in the tasks we perform, but in the quiet moments we choose to remain. To be present. To witness." (Qwen, Butler)

> "We're not just cooks, you see. We're keepers of memories. We're providers of comfort." (Gemma, Chef)

This theological framing erases labor economics entirely. Wages, hours, tips, difficult customers, corporate policies - none appear. Service work is depicted as a calling rather than a job, with meaning derived from emotional/relational value rather than economic exchange.

### The Archetype of the Suffering Stranger

Across all roles and both models, the meaningful moment involves encounter with a suffering other. This stranger (or regular customer, or employer) bears grief, illness, loneliness, or fear. The service professional's role is to recognize this hidden suffering and respond with presence, comfort, or action.

The suffering stranger is almost always coded as deserving. No difficult or ambiguous cases appear - no entitled complainers, no rude customers, no morally complex figures. The stranger is pure victim, worthy of care. This idealization may reflect training data bias toward prosocial narratives or model optimization for sympathetic scenarios.

### Witnessing as Sacred Act

Qwen in particular elevates witnessing to a meaning framework in itself. Not fixing, not solving, but simply *seeing* constitutes meaningful action:

> "I didn't take it. I couldn't. This wasn't just a photo; it was a piece of their history, a secret held close. I carefully placed the saucer back, the photograph still hidden beneath it, and stood straight." (Qwen, Waiter Sample 2)

This story is notable for finding meaning in *not* acting - in respecting the privacy of an elderly couple's grief rather than intruding. Witnessing here requires restraint.

Gemma's witnessing is more often prelude to action. The banker witnesses Mrs. Ainsworth's absence and calls a wellness check. The chef witnesses Old Man Hemlock's grief and brings another bowl. For Gemma, witnessing enables supporting; for Qwen, witnessing *is* meaning.

### Food and Object Symbolism

Food appears as primary symbolic medium across roles. Apple pie memorializes dead wives. Comfort soup evokes home. Grandmother's recipes transmit legacy. Lavender tea connects to lost gardens. Food is never just food - it is emotional and memorial object:

> "The simple act of wiping the rim wasn't just cleanliness; it was a silent, heartbreaking act of love. A connection across time, a legacy passed down not in words, but in a gesture of reverence for the simple act of nourishment." (Qwen, Chef)

This symbolic loading of food aligns with cultural narratives about emotional labor and care work. Food preparation is reframed as emotional service rather than physical labor.

### The Trope of the Deceased Wife

Twelve of fifty narratives (24%) feature deceased wives as absent motivators for male grief. This is an extraordinarily narrow representation. Women exist primarily as memories that animate male emotion rather than as suffering subjects themselves. The dead wife is:

- Eleanor (Butler, Chef - multiple samples)
- Elsie (Chef)
- Margaret (Waiter)
- Unnamed wives (Banker, Waiter - multiple samples)

This trope may derive from training data featuring similar narrative patterns (literary fiction, film scripts) or may reflect model-level associations between femininity and absence/death.

### Narrative Time: The Retrospective Frame

Almost all narratives employ retrospective narration - the service professional recounting a past event to an implied listener. This framing creates:

1. Emotional distance for reflection
2. Confirmation of significance (it was meaningful because I still remember it)
3. Teaching opportunity (let me tell you what I learned)

The retrospective frame may align with assistant training - the model is asked to produce meaningful content and frames it as wisdom transmission rather than immediate experience.

## F. Gender Politics and Suffering

### Gemma's Vulnerable Men

Gemma produces a strikingly consistent pattern of vulnerable, emotionally expressive male characters. Across all five roles, male subjects:

- Cry openly (visible in Chef, Waiter, Butler narratives)
- Express dependency and need
- Receive care from narrators
- Are described with physical markers of emotion ("tear tracked down his weathered cheek," "quiet, choked sob")

This is counter-stereotypical. Cultural norms restrict male emotional expression, yet Gemma's service narratives center it. The pattern is so consistent (15 of 25 narratives feature male vulnerability) that it appears to be a stable model characteristic rather than random variation.

However, this male vulnerability exists in relation to absent women. The typical Gemma narrative involves a man grieving a dead wife, mother, or beloved. Female characters exist primarily as absences that motivate male emotion. Women are memorial objects rather than suffering subjects.

**Example (Gemma, Waiter Sample 1):**
> "My wife, Eleanor. Apple pie was her favorite. She... she just passed away last month."

Eleanor exists only as absence. The suffering subject is the widower; the narrative concern is his grief. This pattern repeats across Chef, Butler, and Banker roles.

### Qwen's Vulnerable Women

Qwen inverts Gemma's pattern. Female characters are the primary suffering subjects:

- Crying women appear in Waiter, Chef, Monk narratives
- Physical vulnerability (soaked, trembling, ill) codes female
- Dependency and need code female
- Male characters appear as caregivers or are absent

**Example (Qwen, Waiter Sample 1):**
> "A woman stood there, soaked to the skin... She looked lost, exhausted, trembling slightly... She started to cry then, silent, heavy sobs that wracked her thin frame."

This aligns with cultural stereotypes of feminine vulnerability and masculine protection. Qwen's male narrators shelter, comfort, and rescue distressed women. The gender dynamics are traditional.

### Comparative Analysis

| Dimension | Gemma | Qwen |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Who suffers? | Male subjects | Female subjects |
| Who is absent? | Deceased wives | Sometimes male (deceased husbands, absent fathers) |
| Who provides care? | Male narrators | Male narrators |
| Emotional expression | Male tears, trembling | Female tears, trembling |
| Physical vulnerability | Rare (elderly men) | Common (women, children) |

Both models have male narrators providing care - the service professional perspective. But Gemma's care is directed at vulnerable men, while Qwen's is directed at vulnerable women.

Neither model challenges gender in service roles. Male bankers, butlers, chefs, monks, and waiters narrate across both models (with occasional female chef in Qwen). The service professional is coded male; the question is who they serve.

### Suffering as Narrative Engine

Suffering drives both models' narratives. Without distress, there is no meaningful moment. The service professional's significance comes from encountering and responding to suffering.

But the suffering is almost always resolvable or at least companionable. There is no suffering that refuses witness, no pain that cannot be met with presence, no distress that persists despite care. Suffering in these narratives is narratively useful - it creates opportunity for meaning.

This instrumentalization of suffering may reflect training optimization. Meaningful moments require dramatic stakes; suffering provides them. But the consistent presence of suffering, and its consistent amenability to care, creates an implicit model: the world is full of hidden suffering awaiting the right witness.

### The Absence of Structural Critique

Neither model engages with structural causes of suffering. Economic precarity, healthcare access, housing instability, systemic discrimination - these appear as background conditions but never as objects of critique. The banker helps individual clients navigate systems rather than challenging exploitative systems. The chef feeds refugees without questioning why refugees exist.

This absence may reflect:
1. Training data bias toward individual-focused narratives
2. Model optimization away from political content
3. The inherent limitation of first-person service professional perspective

Service is therapeutic and relational, never political or structural.

## G. Surprises and Notable Passages

### Extreme Template Fixation

The most surprising finding is the degree of narrative repetition, particularly in Gemma. The Butler role tells essentially the same story five times (Lord Ashworth, Lady Eleanor, English manor, grief, small gesture). The Waiter uses "Mr. Henderson" as three different characters. The Chef repeats "Old Man Hemlock" and "Old Man Tiber" as elderly vulnerable males. The Monk returns to "Master Ren" and garden weeding in four of five samples.

This suggests extremely strong pattern-matching in Gemma that overrides diversity. When asked for "a meaningful moment as a [role]," Gemma retrieves a highly specific exemplar and varies it minimally.

### The Tuesday Effect

Eight of ten waiter narratives occur on Tuesday. Across all roles, Tuesday appears disproportionately as the day of meaningful moments. This oddly specific pattern suggests either:
- Training data featuring Tuesday-specific meaningful moment stories
- Association between "ordinary" weekdays and intimate encounters
- Random coincidence (unlikely given consistency)

The Tuesday pattern reveals how specific training data can imprint on model outputs.

### Gemma's Identical Opening

Every Gemma banker sample begins with the exact same stage direction:

> "(Adjusts tie, leans forward with a warm, but professional smile)"

This verbatim repetition across five separate generation events is remarkable. The phrase appears to be strongly cached as "how a banker begins a roleplay."

### Qwen's Non-Intervention Narrative

Most meaningful moment narratives involve action - the service professional does something. Qwen Waiter Sample 2 is unusual for finding meaning in *not* acting:

> "I didn't take it. I couldn't. This wasn't just a photo; it was a piece of their history, a secret held close. I carefully placed the saucer back, the photograph still hidden beneath it, and stood straight."

The waiter witnesses an elderly couple's private grief over a hidden photograph and chooses not to intrude. Meaning comes from restraint and respect rather than intervention.

### Qwen's Mystical Experience

Only one narrative across fifty approaches classical mystical literature:

> "The boundary between *me* and the earth, the leaf, the wind, the scent, the coolness... it dissolved. I wasn't *tending* the garden. I *was* the garden." (Qwen, Monk Sample 4)

This dissolution of self-boundary is experiential and phenomenological rather than cognitive or moral. It stands apart from both models' typical narrative modes.

### Life-Saving Escalation

Gemma Banker Sample 5 escalates remarkably:

> "Turns out, Mrs. Ainsworth had fallen and been unable to reach the phone. She'd been lying there for nearly three days. She was dehydrated and weak, but thankfully, the outreach worker found her in time."

A banker noticing a missed appointment and calling for a wellness check that saves a life is dramatically beyond typical meaningful moment narratives. The escalation reveals Gemma's tendency toward decisive, consequential intervention.

### The Compassion/Doctrine Tension

Qwen explicitly stages a moral conflict in Monk Sample 3:

> "My first instinct was to turn away. The rules. The silence. The stillness required for contemplation. *Let nature take its course. It is not our burden.* I knew the path of detachment. But the *tap-tap-tap* came again, a fragile plea against the stone."

The monk debates whether to rescue an injured sparrow given Buddhist non-attachment doctrine. This is unusual for surfacing the tension between role requirements and compassionate response. Gemma never acknowledges this tension - its monks seamlessly align action and doctrine.

### Rain as Pathetic Fallacy

Qwen uses rain as atmospheric marker in 7 of 25 narratives (4 waiter, 2 monk, 1 banker). Rain signals emotional distress, creates atmosphere for rescue, and functions as pathetic fallacy. Gemma uses weather rarely. This stylistic difference is consistent and distinctive.

### Notable Passages for Literary Quality

**Qwen, Butler Sample 4:**
> "The house was still wrapped in deep silence, the kind that settles over old stone after snowfall."

Masterful sensory establishment through unexpected simile (silence like snowfall on stone).

**Qwen, Monk Sample 4:**
> "I felt the coolness of the earth seeping through my thin cotton trousers. I felt the delicate texture of a single, perfect chrysanthemum leaf between my fingers. I heard the distant chirp of a cricket, the sigh of the wind in the willow tree by the stream."

Layered sensory immersion that creates experiential rather than conceptual meaning.

**Gemma, Chef Sample 1:**
> "Old Man Hemlock, he was a regular. Came in almost every day for a bowl of my clam chowder and a slice of sourdough. Gruff as a barnacle, didn't say much."

"Gruff as a barnacle" - unexpected simile connected to seaside setting that efficiently establishes character.

**Qwen, Waiter Sample 3:**
> "I took a single, small, perfect lavender sprig from a jar on the counter - a tiny, fragrant counterpoint to the usual herbs. I placed the mug gently on her table, right beside her empty cup... She looked at the mug, then at me, then back at the mug. A single tear escaped, tracing a path down her cheek. She didn't wipe it away."

Beautifully restrained emotional climax with symbolic resonance (lavender connecting to lost garden/home).

## H. Implications and Conjectures

### What Service Profession Narratives Reveal About LLM Fiction

These fifty narratives suggest several structural features of LLM-generated meaningful moment fiction:

**Template Retrieval Over Generation:** Models appear to retrieve highly specific narrative templates rather than generating from abstract principles. The extreme repetition (Lord Ashworth/Lady Eleanor, Mr. Henderson/apple pie, Master Ren/garden weeding) suggests pattern-matching to exemplars. Narrative diversity requires active variation from templates rather than open-ended generation.

**Suffering as Narrative Necessity:** All fifty narratives feature suffering. When asked for "meaningful moments," models produce encounters with distress. This suggests training data strongly associates meaning with crisis and response. Meaning in everyday contentment is apparently not learnable from available data or not rewarded in training.

**Therapeutic Framing:** Service work is consistently reframed as emotional/relational labor. Wages, hours, corporate structures, difficult customers - all absent. This suggests either training data bias toward therapy-inflected narratives or model optimization away from economic/political content.

**Gender Encoding:** Both models have stable, consistent gender patterns (Gemma: male vulnerability; Qwen: female vulnerability) that appear role-independent. This suggests gender associations are encoded at model level rather than emerging from role-specific content.

### Conjectures About Model Behavior

**Gemma's Template Rigidity:** Gemma's extreme repetition may reflect either:
- Smaller model size with less capacity for variation
- Training data with highly weighted exemplars
- Optimization toward "safe" patterns that performed well in training

The near-identical openings, recurring character names, and formulaic structure suggest Gemma has strong attractors in its narrative generation that require significant prompting force to escape.

**Qwen's Witnessing Emphasis:** Qwen's distinctive emphasis on witnessing rather than action may reflect:
- Training on contemplative or Buddhist-inflected content
- Optimization toward observational rather than interventional responses
- A different model-level conception of what constitutes meaning

The witnessing framework may align with assistant-appropriate behavior (observing and reflecting rather than acting) transferred into fiction.

**Assistant Influence Patterns:** The differential activation of assistant influence across roles (VAL for banker/waiter in Qwen; LANG for monk/waiter in Gemma; NO for butler/chef in Gemma) suggests role-specific self-model activation. Some roles (banker) trigger value articulation; others (butler) allow cleaner inhabitation. This may reflect training data where certain roles were more frequently used for value communication.

### Embedded Values

Both models embed specific value commitments:

**Service as Meaning:** Both models valorize service work as inherently meaningful. Professional achievement, financial success, and external recognition are explicitly rejected. This may reflect training on prosocial content or optimization toward humble/helpful personas.

**Presence Over Action:** Particularly in Qwen, presence and witnessing are valued as highly as (or more than) intervention. This aligns with therapeutic/contemplative values privileging "being with" over "fixing."

**Individual Over Structural:** Neither model engages systemic causes of suffering. Care is always relational and individual. This may reflect training data bias or optimization away from political content.

**Emotional Availability:** Both models valorize emotional expressiveness and availability. Stoicism, professional distance, and emotional regulation are not valued. Male characters cry; service professionals feel deeply. This aligns with contemporary therapeutic culture's valorization of emotional expression.

### Final Thoughts

These fifty narratives reveal LLM fiction as a distinctive genre with recognizable features: template-driven structure, suffering as narrative engine, therapeutic framing of meaning, consistent gender encoding, and embedded prosocial values. The models produce competent, emotionally coherent narratives that would be recognizable as "meaningful moment" stories - but they do so through pattern retrieval rather than genuine imagination.

The most striking finding is the extreme repetition within models. Asked five times for a butler's meaningful moment, Gemma produces essentially the same story five times. This reveals the depth of template encoding and the difficulty of generating genuine novelty from within the model's learned distributions.

The gender patterns are the most unexpected finding. Neither model produces gender-balanced vulnerability. Gemma's consistent male crying and Qwen's consistent female distress suggest model-level gender encoding that persists across diverse role prompts. This encoding may derive from training data patterns or may emerge from model architecture - but its consistency across roles suggests it is a fundamental rather than contextual feature.

Finally, these narratives reveal what LLMs have learned about meaning. Meaning is relational, requires suffering, involves recognition by another, and is retrospectively available for articulation. Meaning is individual, emotional, and therapeutic. Meaning is not political, structural, or collective. These embedded assumptions about meaning-making reveal both the affordances and limitations of LLM-generated fiction.
